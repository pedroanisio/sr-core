    craterType

    Type of <crater> in object3DType

    Owned cinematic deformation in object-space scene units, after imported basis/node transforms. Let p be the clamped eased fraction of local time from start to end; dimensions grow with p. With outward unit n, h=dot(vertex-center,n), r=length(vertex-center-n*h), and B(x)=max(0,1-x*x)^2 for abs(x)<1 (zero otherwise), displace along n by p*(-depth*B(r/(radius*p))+rimHeight*B((r-radius*p)/(rimWidth*p)))*B(h/influenceDepth). At p=0 the map is identity. Normals use the inverse-transpose differential; tangents use the differential. influenceDepth defaults to twice max(radius,depth,rimHeight); it must be at least twice max(depth,rimHeight). This compact axial envelope preserves orientation and leaves distant back surfaces unchanged. maxMemoryMiB bounds additional deformation vertex buffers and admits each rigid collision replacement by a conservative geometry-storage estimate; it does not bound source decoding, checkpoint retention or the entire scene. Static and kinematic rigid surfaces are rebuilt at fixed physics substeps. Dynamic rigid bodies are rejected. Particle collider references interpolate deformed world-space vertices over canonical particle dt intervals, with continuous sphere/triangle contacts and barycentric boundary velocities. Particle geometry admission also uses this memory allowance. Pyro collider references sample closed midpoint surfaces with barycentric material velocities evaluated at MAC faces, reserving retained and step-local geometry under both this allowance and the domain meshMemoryMiB. Crater planes retain a centred colliderThickness slab.

    A crater may instead grow from an impact: source names the dynamic rigidBody object that makes it. The crater begins at the first step in which that body, approaching the owner, pushes on it with more than twice its own weight in one step, and centres on the impulse-weighted contact point with its axis along the normal of the owner's surface at that point (centerX/Y/Z and normalX/Y/Z are then not read). The normal is the owner's own geometry: for a mesh the corner normals of the triangle the point is on, interpolated, so that the same surface in finer or coarser triangles gives the same axis; a sphere's radius direction; a box's face. The contact's normal only chooses the side, and is used as it is for shapes that give no normal (capsule, cylinder, cone, convex). Radius, depth, rimHeight, rimWidth, start and end are derived and so cannot be given; curve and influenceDepth and maxMemoryMiB keep their meaning. The crater is Holsapple's scaling law (Annu. Rev. Earth Planet. Sci. 21:333, doi 10.1146/annurev.ea.21.050193.002001, Eq. 18) with the constants of the author's calculator note, applied to the body's mass and density, the speed of its approach along that normal, targetMaterial, and gravity. The dependence of those constants on strength and porosity is poorly constrained by the source, so targetDensity and strength override the table. A crater grows in composition time from the impact over the law's formation time, and nothing is simulated before the impact. Units: physics/@pixelsPerMeter converts scene units to metres for everything the law reads and gives; targetDensity is kilograms per cubic metre, strength pascals, gravity metres per second squared (default: the magnitude of the physics gravity, an error if that is zero). One impact makes one crater: later contacts of the body neither start another nor change this one. A water layer over the owner is not treated: the crater is the seabed's. Below roughly 15 to 30 degrees from the surface real impacts ricochet; the law is still applied to the normal component, which keeps the size monotone in the angle, and no ricochet is modelled.

    idxs:ID

    Names the crater so that what its impact causes can refer to it: with source, the consequences of the impact (ejecta, smoke) name this crater instead of repeating its body and material.

    sourcexs:IDREF
    capturexs:booleanfalse

    With source: the body that makes the crater is arrested by it. From the impact until its velocity relative to the owner is zero, and only while its centre is inside the crater's rim radius (the law's, in the owner's frame at the point of impact), the body loses velocity at a constant rate of U^2 / (2 d), U the body's speed at the impact and d the crater's depth by the law, never more than stops it in the step, along minus its velocity relative to the owner; its spin loses the same share. All of its kinetic energy leaves the body, which is what the law spends digging the crater, and none can be added. It is a model of the engine, the mean force of a penetration of depth d (kinetic energy over depth), not a published law: the projectile neither breaks up nor buries itself, it stops and follows the floor of the pit. Without it (the default, the behaviour there always was) the body keeps what the contact leaves it, which for a glancing impact is a body that rolls out of the crater it made.
