    craterType

    Type of <crater> in object3DType

    Owned cinematic deformation in object-space scene units, after imported basis/node transforms. Let p be the clamped eased fraction of local time from start to end; dimensions grow with p. With outward unit n, h=dot(vertex-center,n), r=length(vertex-center-n*h), and B(x)=max(0,1-x*x)^2 for abs(x)<1 (zero otherwise), displace along n by p*(-depth*B(r/(radius*p))+rimHeight*B((r-radius*p)/(rimWidth*p)))*B(h/influenceDepth). At p=0 the map is identity. Normals use the inverse-transpose differential; tangents use the differential. influenceDepth defaults to twice max(radius,depth,rimHeight); it must be at least twice max(depth,rimHeight) (CRT4, for a crater without a source; the size of a crater from an impact is not in the document, so its envelope is checked when the crater is made). This compact axial envelope preserves orientation and leaves distant back surfaces unchanged. maxMemoryMiB bounds additional deformation vertex buffers and admits each rigid collision replacement by a conservative geometry-storage estimate; it does not bound source decoding, checkpoint retention or the entire scene. Static and kinematic rigid surfaces are rebuilt at fixed physics substeps. Dynamic rigid bodies are rejected. Particle collider references interpolate deformed world-space vertices over canonical particle dt intervals, with continuous sphere/triangle contacts and barycentric boundary velocities. Particle geometry admission also uses this memory allowance. Pyro collider references sample closed midpoint surfaces with barycentric material velocities evaluated at MAC faces, reserving retained and step-local geometry under both this allowance and the domain meshMemoryMiB. Crater planes retain a centred colliderThickness slab.

    A crater may instead grow from an impact: source names the dynamic rigidBody object that makes it. The crater begins at the first step in which that body, approaching the owner, pushes on it with more than twice its own weight in one step, and centres on the impulse-weighted contact point with its axis along the normal of the owner's surface at that point (centerX/Y/Z and normalX/Y/Z are then not read). The normal is the owner's own geometry: for a mesh the corner normals of the triangle the point is on, interpolated, so that the same surface in finer or coarser triangles gives the same axis; a sphere's radius direction; a box's face. The contact's normal only chooses the side, and is used as it is for shapes that give no normal (capsule, cylinder, cone, convex). Radius, depth, rimHeight, rimWidth, start and end are derived and so cannot be given; curve and influenceDepth and maxMemoryMiB keep their meaning. The crater is Holsapple's scaling law (Annu. Rev. Earth Planet. Sci. 21:333, doi 10.1146/annurev.ea.21.050193.002001, Eq. 18) with the constants of the author's calculator note, applied to the body's mass and density, the speed of its approach along that normal, targetMaterial, and gravity. The dependence of those constants on strength and porosity is poorly constrained by the source, so targetDensity and strength override the table. A crater grows in composition time from the impact over the law's formation time, and nothing is simulated before the impact. Units: physics/@pixelsPerMeter converts scene units to metres for everything the law reads and gives; targetDensity is kilograms per cubic metre, strength pascals, gravity metres per second squared (default: the magnitude of the physics gravity, an error if that is zero). One impact makes one crater: later contacts of the body neither start another nor change this one. A water layer over the owner is not treated: the crater is the seabed's. Below roughly 15 to 30 degrees from the surface real impacts ricochet; the law is still applied to the normal component, which keeps the size monotone in the angle, and no ricochet is modelled.

    Attributes

    AttributeTypeDefaultDescription
    declared here
    idxs:ID

    Names the crater so that what its impact causes can refer to it: with source, the consequences of the impact (ejecta, smoke) name this crater instead of repeating its body and material.

    sourcexs:IDREF

    Id of the dynamic rigid body whose first hard impact on this object makes the crater; its centre, axis, size and timing are then derived from the impact, so those attributes are not given.

    capturexs:booleanfalse

    With source: the body that makes the crater is arrested by it. From the impact until its velocity relative to the owner is zero, and only while its centre is inside the crater's rim radius (the law's, in the owner's frame at the point of impact), the body loses velocity at a constant rate of U^2 / (2 d), U the body's speed at the impact and d the crater's depth by the law, never more than stops it in the step, along minus its velocity relative to the owner; its spin loses the same share. All of its kinetic energy leaves the body, which is what the law spends digging the crater, and none can be added. It is a model of the engine, the mean force of a penetration of depth d (kinetic energy over depth), not a published law: the projectile neither breaks up nor buries itself, it stops and follows the floor of the pit. Without it (the default, the behaviour there always was) the body keeps what the contact leaves it, which for a glancing impact is a body that rolls out of the crater it made.

    targetMaterialone of water drySand drySoil wetSoil softRock hardRock regolith ice

    Kind of ground an impact crater is dug in: water, drySand, drySoil, wetSoil, softRock, hardRock, regolith or ice. It sets the constants of the size law; required with source, and not given without it.

    mantlexs:booleanfalse

    Only with source (CRT10). The ejecta that the law throws out (0.8 of the crater's volume) come down outside the rim as a mantle t0 (R / r)^3 thick, R the rim crest's radius, which is part of the ground (the same deformation as the bowl and the rim) and is cut at twenty crest radii and scaled to hold exactly the ejecta's volume; the bowl is made to excavate exactly the law's volume. The ejecta particles that come to rest on it are removed (they have become the ground). Off, the ground is as it was.

    bulkingxs:double in [1, 1.3]

    Only with mantle="true" (CRT10, CRT11). What is put back, the rim and the mantle together, is this many times the volume that the bowl excavates: broken rock takes more room than the rock did (1 to 1.3). Without it the rim keeps the height of the law and the bulking is what that asks for: the law's rim (0.326 of the volume for the authored rock in soft rock) and its 0.8 of ejecta, 1.126 there, a value derived from the law and inside the physical range. With 1 the rim holds 0.2 of the volume.

    reposexs:double in [10, 60]

    Only with source (CRT10), and not with mantle="true" (CRT12): the angle of repose of the debris, in degrees (10 to 60). The ejecta particles that come to rest on the ground are removed and their volume (mass over the target's density) is poured onto a height field of deposit over the final crater, which relaxes at this angle in a fixed order over sixteen neighbours, without making or losing volume; the deposit is added to the height of the ground in the crater's deformation. Absent (the default), the settled particles are not removed and nothing is added to the ground. The deposit is as faithful as the grid is fine: a pile of fewer than ten cells' radius is not within two degrees of the angle. The particles do not see the deposit, and nor does the rigid world or the ocean.

    targetDensitypositiveDecimal

    Density of the ground in kilograms per cubic metre for an impact crater; it replaces the value that targetMaterial gives. Given only with source.

    strengthnonNegativeDecimal

    Strength of the ground in pascals for an impact crater; it replaces the value that targetMaterial gives. Given only with source.

    gravitypositiveDecimal

    Gravity in metres per second squared used by the crater-size law of an impact; absent, the magnitude of the physics gravity (an error if that is zero). Given only with source.

    centerXxs:double0

    Position of the crater's centre along the X axis of the object, in scene units. Default 0; not read with source.

    centerYxs:double0

    Position of the crater's centre along the Y axis of the object, in scene units. Default 0; not read with source.

    centerZxs:double0

    Position of the crater's centre along the Z axis of the object, in scene units. Default 0; not read with source.

    normalXxs:double0

    Component along the X axis of the crater's outward direction, the axis it is dug along; the three components may not all be 0. Default 0 (the defaults together give -Z); not given with source.

    normalYxs:double0

    Component along the Y axis of the crater's outward direction, the axis it is dug along; the three components may not all be 0. Default 0 (the defaults together give -Z); not given with source.

    normalZxs:double-1

    Component along the Z axis of the crater's outward direction, the axis it is dug along; the three components may not all be 0. Default -1 (the defaults together give -Z); not given with source.

    radiuspositiveDecimal50

    Radius of the crater bowl in scene units at full growth; at least rimWidth. Default 50; derived, so not given, with source.

    depthnonNegativeDecimal10

    Depth of the bowl in scene units at full growth. Default 10; derived, so not given, with source.

    rimHeightnonNegativeDecimal2

    Height of the raised rim in scene units at full growth. Default 2; derived, so not given, with source.

    rimWidthpositiveDecimal10

    Half-width of the raised rim in scene units at full growth: the rim is centred on the bowl's radius and extends this far on each side; at most radius. Default 10; derived, so not given, with source.

    influenceDepthpositiveDecimal

    Distance in scene units, along the axis on each side of the crater's centre plane, at which the deformation has faded out. Default twice the largest of radius, depth, rimHeight; at least twice max(depth, rimHeight).

    startnonNegativeDecimal0

    Local time in seconds at which the crater begins to grow; before it the surface is undeformed. Default 0; derived, so not given, with source.

    endnonNegativeDecimal1

    Local time in seconds at which the crater reaches full size; it must be later than start. Default 1; derived, so not given, with source.

    curveone of linear ease-in ease-out ease-in-out stepease-in-out

    Easing of the crater's growth: linear, ease-in, ease-out, ease-in-out (default) or step. It runs from start to end, or over the impact's own growth time with source; not given in an object of cells.

    maxMemoryMiBxs:positiveInteger ≤ 4096128

    Memory budget in MiB for the deformation's extra vertex buffers and the collision geometry built from the deformed surface, including particle and smoke colliders; 1 to 4096. Default 128.
