    The particles3D emitters whose particles fall into this ocean: each must throw out the ejecta of a crater (a burst with crater), whose particles have a mass (OCN13). Absent (the default) is the behaviour there always was. A particle of a listed emitter whose centre crosses the ocean's rest level (waterLevel) downward inside its domain is removed there, and what it brings is given to the water in the canonical step of the crossing, summed by cell: the volume of the particle (its mass over the density of the crater's target) is taken out of the cell's water and given equally to its eight neighbours, never more than 90 % of the cell's water, and its horizontal momentum, divided by the density of the water, is added to the cell's. The water volume and the horizontal momentum that is applied are conserved; the vertical momentum and the energy of the particle are not (the shallow-water model has no vertical momentum), and a particle that has fallen in is gone, it does not sink. The surface is the water's rest level, not the swell, and the ocean is taken at its pose when the emitter starts. The deposit does not go through the depth filter of bedResponse. An emitter belongs to one ocean.

    bodyCouplingone of none buoyancy fullnone

    What the water does to the rigid bodies in colliders. none (the default) is the existing behaviour: a body displaces the water and nothing acts on the body. buoyancy loads each rigid body with the weight of the water it displaces (water density 1000 kg/m3, the ocean's gravity), upward through the centroid of the submerged volume so that a tilted body is turned by it, and with a quadratic form drag on its vertical motion, scaled by bodyDrag. A body floats if it is lighter than the water it displaces. The submerged part is measured against the free surface under the body: the plane that the ocean fits, in the canonical step before the last one it has completed, through the surface of the wet columns of the body's footprint (the water the body displaces stands above the rest level while it is there, and the plane says so), and against the rest level, waterLevel, while there is none; the body's own motion therefore moves the water it floats on, and the water carries the energy of a bobbing body away, so a body with no drag settles. full adds the horizontal momentum the body gave the water in that step, taken back as a force along the body's centre over one canonical step, so that the body and the water keep their momentum together (to what is in flight, one step late); roll is not damped, there is no added mass, and a body rides the rest level when the ocean has no water under it. The load is held for one rigid step and evaluated halfway through it. A body so light that the waterline's stiffness times the step reaches 1.8 is an error (lower physics/@fixedStep or raise the mass), not a damped motion. The rigid body must be a sphere, box, cylinder, cone, capsule or closed mesh. The ocean and the bodies form a group (see the contract text): the ocean is stepped before the rigid world, and the document cannot be baked into a physics cache.

    bedResponseone of depthFiltered hydrostaticdepthFiltered

    How the free surface answers what the objects in colliders do to the water: the bed that a crater moves and the volume a body occupies. depthFiltered (the default) attenuates it by the depth of the water: a displacement much narrower than the depth does little to the surface, as in linear water-wave theory, where an instantaneous displacement of wavenumber k is carried to the surface by 1/cosh(k h) for the bed (Kajiura 1963; read here only as quoted by the tsunami literature) and by cosh(k z0)/cosh(k h) for a volume at height z0 above the bed (derived by the engine). The displaced volume is conserved. hydrostatic lifts the whole column by the displacement, which is the long-wave answer, right when the displacement is much wider than the depth: the behaviour of the first version, kept for comparison. With depthFiltered the horizontal momentum that a body gives the water is also not the relaxation toward its velocity but the form drag of the body through it, (1/2) C_d A |U - u| (U - u) per unit density (C_d is bodyDrag, U the body's velocity, u the mean velocity of the water under it, A the area its wet part presents to that flow), spread as the lift of the body is and never past its velocity. It has no effect without colliders.

    bodyDragnonNegativeDecimal

    Coefficient of form drag of a body in the water. With bodyCoupling it is the coefficient of the vertical motion: the force is -(1/2) rho C_d A |v| v with A the area the submerged part presents from above, limited to what stops the body within a step. With bedResponse="depthFiltered" it is also that of the horizontal exchange with the water, the same law along the motion of the body relative to the water. Default 1.0. It is an engine parameter, not a value from the impact literature; 0 gives undamped bobbing.
