# SREP 0 — Add tagged formula parts and shape morphing

## Abstract

[SREP 12](srep-0012.md) names, as its MF3 building block, "tagged formula parts carried to glyphs; morph by part
with remaps and glyph-outline fallback; arc-length resampling, start alignment, sub-path matching", with the bar
"tagged parts land on their targets; matrix cells align by column; accents centred over their base within
0.5 px; no self-intersections mid-morph" (line 254); its TeX extension (matrices, accents, `\over`) has landed in
the reference. This SREP is the rest of the row. The TeX subset gains `\part{name}{...}`, a tagged group the
formula asset carries to its glyphs; a `formulaAnimator` moves, scales, turns, fades or recolours one part of a
shown formula; a `formulaMorph` asset turns one formula into another by matching parts, moving the ones whose
TeX is the same, morphing the glyph outlines of the ones whose TeX differs, and fading the rest; and `shape` gains
`morph="true"`, under which path keys of different structure interpolate by resampling, alignment and sub-path
matching rules stated here as pure functions. Everything a 1.7.1 document can say renders as before.

## Motivation

- **The plan depends on it.** The Motion Canvas criterion of SREP 12 (line 124) is "catalogue shots 9, 10 and
  16 are authored with no hand-split formula strings, and tagged formula parts land within 0.5 px of their
  targets (MF3)". Today a formula is one `formula` asset drawn as one path (`crates/sr-text/src/formula.rs:817`,
  `draw`: every glyph into one `Path`), so to move one term an author splits the TeX into several assets by hand
  and positions each: the split is a guess at the typesetter's layout and breaks when the formula changes.
- **Path keys hold today.** `keyType` allows path data as a key value (xsd:432) but `Value::lerp` falls back to
  the earlier key for values it cannot blend (`crates/sr-eval/src/value.rs:385-407`), and the reference's own
  test says so ("string keys hold between keys", `crates/sr-gpu/tests/raster_effects/composite.rs:898`). A
  shape cannot be morphed into another shape at all; a formula's glyph cannot turn into another glyph.
- **Manim's vocabulary.** `MathTex(..., substrings_to_isolate=...)`, `TransformMatchingTex`,
  `TransformMatchingShapes` and `Transform` (with `align_points`: resampling, start alignment, sub-path matching)
  are what the math channel's shots are made of. The rules below are their declarative, deterministic
  counterpart: no random choices, no per-frame state (SREP 14), and the same points on every engine.
- **The layout rules must be stated to be tested.** "Matrix cells align by column" and "accents centred over
  their base" are true of the reference's typesetter but written in no SREP; the kit cannot measure what no
  document states. They are stated here as rules the kit measures on the rendered image.

## Specification

### Syntax

The diff is against `schema/scene-render.xsd` at 1.7.1 and `schema/scene-render.sch`. It is stated here for
the schema editor; the schema files are not changed by this draft.

**1. The tag.** The TeX subset of `formulaAssetType/@tex` (xsd:1825) gains the command `\part{name}{body}`:
`name` is an NMTOKEN (`[A-Za-z0-9_.:-]+`), unique within the formula; `body` is any content of the subset, tags
included. The parser treats the command as the group `{body}`. A tag without its two groups, a name that is
not an NMTOKEN, a duplicate name, or an unclosed group is a parse error of the asset, reported as other TeX
errors are.

**2. `formulaAnimator`.** `layerType` (xsd:3393) gains, beside `textAnimator` (xsd:3405), the child element
`formulaAnimator` of type `formulaAnimatorType`: `animationElements`, the attribute `part` (`xs:NMTOKEN`,
required) and the offset attributes a `textAnimator` has — `x`, `y`, `scale`, `scaleX`, `scaleY`, `rotation`,
`opacity`, `fill`, `stroke`, `strokeWidth`, `blur`, `anchorX`, `anchorY` — with the same types and defaults
(xsd:3160-3290). **FRM1** (Schematron, with R2 at sch:166): a `formulaAnimator` needs a `formula` asset on its
layer. A `part` that names no part of the asset is a render error of the layer.

**3. `formulaMorph`.** `assetsType` (xsd:2387) gains `<xs:element name="formulaMorph" type="formulaMorphType"/>`:
`id` (`xs:ID`), `from` and `to` (`xs:IDREF`, required, each a `formula` asset), `match` (`name` | `tex`,
default `name`), `progress` (`xs:double` 0 to 1, default 0, animatable through `animationElements`), `width`,
`height`, `size` and `color` as the formula asset has them (the morph's image is the union box of the two
formulas typeset at `size`, fitted as a formula is). **FRM2**: `from` and `to` are formula ids. A layer shows a
`formulaMorph` as it shows a `formula`.

**4. Path morphing.** `shapeType` (xsd:3598) gains `morph` (`xs:boolean`, default `false`): with `true`, the
keys of an `animate property="path"` on the shape interpolate by Semantics rules 6 to 9; with `false`, they hold
as today. `morph` is inert without such an animation (SREP 18 reports it).

**5. Version gate.** The shared 1.8 gate of SREP 12 (**V17**: one pattern after `p-srep75`, sch:851, one assert
whose test is the conjunction of every 1.8 draft's clause) gains the clause: documents of version 1.0 to 1.7
contain no `formulaAnimator`, no `formulaMorph`, no `shape/@morph` and no `\part{` in a formula's `tex`. The
last part cannot be the literal `contains(@tex, '\part{')` in a Schematron test: the ISO skeleton copies the
test into `svrl:failed-assert/@test`, an attribute value template, where the unmatched `{` fails to compile.
The clause binds the literal with a rule-level `let`, as the file does elsewhere (56 `sch:let` today):

```xml
<sch:rule context="/scene[not(@version='1.8')]">
  <sch:let name="partTag" value="'\part{'"/>
  <sch:assert id="V17" test="not(.//formulaAnimator | .//formulaMorph | .//shape/@morph | /scene/assets/formula[contains(@tex, $partTag)])">formula parts, animators, morphs and shape/@morph need version="1.8".</sch:assert>
</sch:rule>
```

### Semantics

The reference module is `conformance/tools/semantic_tex_reference.py`; function names in parentheses.

**1. Parts** (`parts`, `strip_parts`). The parts of a formula are its `\part` groups in order of their opening
tag; a part's TeX is its body, tags included; its depth is the number of parts that contain it. The formula is
typeset from the text with every `\part{name}` removed, so a tagged formula lays out exactly as the untagged
one. Each glyph and rule the typesetter emits belongs to the innermost part whose body produced it, or to no
part. A part's box is the union of the boxes of its glyphs and rules, in the formula image's pixels after the
fit (xsd:1835-1838); a part that produces nothing has an empty box, and animating it does nothing.

**2. Matching** (`match_parts`). Between a `from` and a `to` formula, with `match="name"`, parts with the
same name are paired; with `match="tex"`, parts whose TeX is the same up to whitespace are paired in document
order, each part at most once (the first unpaired part of `to` with equal TeX). Unpaired parts of `from` are
*out*, unpaired parts of `to` are *incoming*. Every glyph of either formula that belongs to no part is an
implicit part of its own, named by its position, and matches nothing. Only outermost parts take part in a
morph: a nested part moves with its container.

**3. Moving and morphing a pair** (`pair_kind`, `box_at`, `part_transform`). A pair whose TeX is the same up to
whitespace *moves*: at progress p the source part's glyphs are drawn translated by p times the vector between
the two boxes' centres and scaled, about the source centre, by 1 + (h_to/h_from − 1) p, where h is the box
height; at p = 1 this is the target box (both parts typeset the same TeX at the same size, so the widths agree).
A pair whose TeX differs *morphs*: the source part's glyph outlines turn into the target's by rules 6 to 9 after
each has been translated so that its box centre follows rule 3's centre path, with fill colour interpolated
linearly. A part's box, at p, is the box with the interpolated centre and size.

**4. Fading** (`opacity_out`, `opacity_in`). An out part is drawn at its place with opacity 1 − p; an incoming
part at its place with opacity p. Nothing else about them changes. p is clamped to [0, 1].

**5. The animator.** A `formulaAnimator` applies its offsets to the glyphs of its part about the part's box
centre (or its `anchorX`/`anchorY` from the box's top-left), exactly as a `textAnimator` with a selection
amount of 1 applies them to a selected character (SREP 35, `textAnimatorType`), and in document order when
several animators name the same part. The offsets are added to the layer's own transform, not to the typeset
layout: a part moved by `x="20"` is drawn 20 px right of where the typesetter put it.

**6. Resampling** (`perimeter`, `resample`). A sub-path is flattened to a polygon as the engine flattens a path
(SREP 58 rule 1 gives the pieces; the flatness is the engine's) and resampled to N points evenly spaced by arc
length around it, the first at its first vertex, in its own direction. N = 64 (`MORPH_SAMPLES`). An open
sub-path is closed by its chord for the morph. A sub-path of zero perimeter is a point, repeated N times.

**7. Alignment** (`signed_area`, `align_start`, `align`). With a the source samples and b the target samples,
b is reversed when the sign of its area differs from a's (its first vertex kept first), then rotated by the k
in [0, N) that minimises Σ|a_i − b_{(i+k) mod N}|², the smallest k on a tie. The area is the shoelace sum.

**8. The morph of a sub-path** (`morph_polygon`). The polygon at progress p is a_i + p (b_i − a_i) over the
aligned samples; exactly a at p = 0 and the aligned b at p = 1. Fill rule, stroke and paint are those of the
shape; between keys of different paint the paints interpolate as they do today.

**9. Sub-path matching** (`centroid`, `match_subpaths`, `morph_path`). The sub-paths of the source and of the
target are paired greedily by the distance between their area centroids, closest pair first, each sub-path at
most once. A source sub-path left over morphs to the point at the centroid of the target's nearest sub-path;
a target sub-path left over grows from the centroid of the source's nearest sub-path. The result lists the
source's sub-paths in order, then the target's unpaired ones.

**10. No self-intersections** (`self_intersections`). The rules above do not guarantee a simple polygon at
every p for every pair; they do for the kit's pairs (a square to a circle, a square to a triangle, the table
rows), and the count of properly crossing edge pairs is the measure. An author whose pair crosses mid-morph
reorders sub-paths or splits the morph into two keys.

**11. Layout rules measured by the kit.** In a matrix environment the columns' centres are the same on every
row (the typesetter centres each cell in its column, TeXbook Appendix G rule 20's `\halign` with centred
cells); an accent's glyph is centred over its base's box within 0.5 px (rule 12 of Appendix G, with the MATH
table's `TopAccentAttachment` when the font gives it); and a part under a `formulaAnimator` with offsets x, y
lands within 0.5 px of its box translated by (x, y). The reference typesets as stated at 67a661a
(`formula.rs:600`, matrices, where a cell is shifted by half of the column width minus the cell's own
width; `:647`, accents); this SREP makes it normative. The matrix rule is about the cell's box, not its ink:
a glyph's ink is not centred in its advance (`1` and `100` differ in their side bearings), so the kit
measures the box by taking each cell's ink offset from the same TeX typeset alone.

**12. Time.** `progress` and the animator's offsets are ordinary animated numbers; the parts, boxes, samples and
alignments are functions of the two formulas alone and may be computed once. Nothing keeps state between
frames (SREP 14 rules 1 to 3): a seek to any frame equals the frame played from 0.

## Rationale

- **A tag in the TeX, not a list of substrings.** Manim isolates substrings by text search, which fails on
  repeated substrings and on TeX whose source differs from its meaning (`x^2` twice). A tag names a part once,
  at the place it appears, and nests.
- **One animator per part, like text animators.** Authors know `textAnimator`'s offsets; the same attributes on
  a part keep one mental model and one implementation in the reference.
- **Move when the TeX is equal, morph otherwise.** Equal TeX at the same size gives identical glyphs, and moving
  them is exact; different TeX needs the outline morph, whose quality the rules bound but cannot make perfect.
- **Arc-length resampling with a chosen start.** Linear interpolation of matched samples is the only morph that
  is a pure function of the two polygons, and the start alignment removes the twist that the raw first vertices
  introduce. The winding fix removes the inside-out pass.
- **Centroid matching of sub-paths.** A glyph such as "o" has two sub-paths (outer and hole); "l" has one.
  Pairing by centroid keeps the outer ring with the outer ring, and collapsing the hole to a point is what a
  reader sees as the hole closing.
- **A boolean on the shape, not a new key form.** Path keys already exist and hold; `morph="true"` turns on the
  interpolation without changing any document that has path keys today.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Matching by glyph identity.** Pairs glyphs across parts the author did not mean to connect (every "x" to
  the nearest "x"); the part is the author's statement of intent.
- **Fade through a cross-dissolve of the two formula images.** Readable but not an animation of meaning; the
  catalogue's shots move terms.
- **Dynamic programming over all sub-path pairings.** Optimal in total distance but O(n³) and no better on the
  glyphs of a formula than the greedy rule; may be revisited for `TransformMatchingShapes`-style morphs of large
  drawings.
- **Adaptive sample count by perimeter.** Different counts on different engines; 64 is fixed and enough at the
  sizes formulas are shown.

## Backwards compatibility

- Every 1.7.1 document validates and renders unchanged: `\part` is a new command of the TeX subset, the
  animator and the morph asset are new elements, and `morph` defaults to the current hold.
- A 1.8 document that uses them is refused by a 1.7 validator (unknown element or attribute), and a 1.7
  typesetter rejects `\part` as an unknown command, as V17 states for the schema's own gate.

## Open issues

- **Morph quality.** Rule 10 does not guarantee a simple polygon; a Manim-style `path_arc` (points moving on
  arcs rather than lines) reduces crossings for rotations and is a candidate attribute.
- **Parts across environments.** A `\part` that opens in one matrix cell and closes in another is a parse
  error today; the editor decides whether the tag may span cells.
- **Colour per part at rest.** `\part` carries no colour; a `formulaAnimator` with `fill` and no animation
  colours a part at rest, which is verbose. A `\color{}` in the subset is a separate proposal.
- **Text animators on formulas.** Whether a `textAnimator` may select a formula's glyphs as characters is left
  open; this SREP's animator selects by part only.
