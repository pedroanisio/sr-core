# SREP 0 — Add snow and ice to the terrain grid

## Abstract

SREP 12's T3: one **`snowpack`** child of the terrain-grid draft's `terrain` acts on one of its `snow` layers,
its `water` layer and its `ice` layer. Each step, in this order: **snowfall** (`snowfall` metres of snow per
second, varied by `snowfallVariation` through the random-streams draft's stream at pack(i, j, 0) with the purpose
2³¹ + 2¹⁶); **melt** by the degree-day law `meltRate` · (T − 273.15 K) · Δt where T is the cell's `temperature`
layer or the attribute, the melt joining the `water` layer times `density`; **drift** by the wind of the
wind-field draft named by `wind`: the snow leaving a cell per second is `driftRate` · (|w| − `driftThreshold`)³
(the cubic saltation law of Bagnold, Pomeroy and Gray 1990 for snow) times the **exposure** clamp(1 +
`exposureGain` · ∇h · ŵ, 0, 2) after Fearing (2000) — a slope rising into the wind loses, the **lee** gains —
split between the two downwind neighbours, never more than the cell holds; **avalanches** by the erosion draft's
thermal relaxation on the snow layer at the angle of `repose` with `avalancheRate` (Musgrave, Kolb and Mace 1989,
as Cordonnier et al. 2018 relax snow); and, when `iceTimeScale` is positive, **glaciers**: the `ice` layer flows
by the **shallow-ice** approximation (Hutter 1983) with Glen's law, D = 2A/(n + 2) (ρg)ⁿ Hⁿ⁺² |∇s|ⁿ⁻¹, n = 3,
ρ = 917 kg/m³, A = `iceFlow`, in adaptive explicit substeps bounded per step, the ice never negative and its
volume conserved. The steps replay from the frame's start state at `step`, never more than `maxSteps` steps
(SREP 14). Rules SN1–SN5 and V17; the schema files are unchanged, the diff is inside the draft. Reference module
`conformance/tools/snow_reference.py`; engine card SR-106.

## Motivation

- **SREP 12 names the deltas.** Row T3 of the plan: "layered snow with drift by the wind field (F4), melt and
  avalanches (Cordonnier et al. 2018); exposure after Fearing 2000; glaciers by the shallow-ice approximation.
  Reference tests: no snow above the repose angle; mass balance equals snowfall minus melt." This draft is that
  row.
- **The engine's snow is particles in 2D.** The particle emitter's `preset="snow"` (`particleEmitterType`,
  `schema/scene-render.xsd:3816`; `crates/sr-eval/src/sim.rs:1250`) emits flakes that fall and vanish; nothing accumulates, melts, drifts or slides, and nothing can read a snow
  depth. The terrain-grid draft (SR-100) declares `snow` layers by type and an `ice` layer; this draft gives them
  their processes, on the wind of the wind-field draft (SR-101) and the thermal step of the erosion draft (SR-104).
- **Every frame renders alone.** The pack is stepped; it replays from the frame's start state with a hard bound,
  as the stepped processes of the erosion and surface-water drafts do (SREP 14). The glacier runs on its own clock
  (`iceTimeScale` ice-years per scene second) inside the same steps, with its substeps bounded.
- **Practice.** Houdini's heightfield tools and Gaea's Snow node accumulate snow by altitude and slope, melt it and
  let it slide; the film pipeline asks for drifts in the lee of ridges and cornices. The attributes here map onto
  those controls with the wind supplied by the scene rather than a fixed direction.
- **Conformance needs a reference, not a look.** Four properties are testable to the last digit: no neighbour
  slope of the snow surface above `repose` after relaxation; the **mass balance** of the pack equals snowfall minus
  melt when nothing drifts; drift and avalanches move snow but create and destroy none, what leaves the grid is
  accounted; the ice volume is constant and the ice non-negative.

### Syntax

**`snowpack`**, a new child of `terrain` (the terrain-grid draft), at most one, after `erode`:

| Attribute | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `layer` | `xs:string` | snow | the snow layer the pack is: `snow:<type>`, or `snow` for the layer without a type (SN2) |
| `snowfall` | `nonNegativeDecimal` | 0 | metres of snow per second on every cell |
| `snowfallVariation` | `xs:double` in [0, 1] | 0 | the relative spread of the snowfall across cells and steps |
| `temperature` | `positiveDecimal` | 268.15 | the air temperature, kelvin, where the terrain has no `temperature` layer |
| `meltRate` | `nonNegativeDecimal` | 1e-5 | metres of snow melted per kelvin above 273.15 K per second (the degree-day factor) |
| `density` | `xs:double` in (0, 1] | 0.3 | the water equivalent of a metre of snow, metres |
| `repose` | `xs:double` in (0, 90) | 38 | the angle of repose of the snow, degrees |
| `avalancheRate` | `xs:double` in [0, 1] | 0.5 | the fraction of the half-excess moved per step |
| `wind` | `xs:IDREF` | none | the `wind` (wind-field draft) that drifts the snow (SN3); none means no drift |
| `driftRate` | `nonNegativeDecimal` | 1e-4 | metres of snow per second leaving a cell per (m/s)³ of excess wind speed |
| `driftThreshold` | `nonNegativeDecimal` | 5 | the wind speed below which no snow drifts, m/s |
| `exposureGain` | `nonNegativeDecimal` | 1 | the weight of the slope along the wind in the exposure |
| `iceFlow` | `nonNegativeDecimal` | 1e-16 | Glen's rate factor A, Pa⁻³ per ice-year |
| `iceTimeScale` | `nonNegativeDecimal` | 0 | ice-years of glacier flow per scene second; 0 means no flow |
| `step` | `positiveDecimal` | 0.02 | the step, seconds of scene time |
| `maxSteps` | `xs:positiveInteger` | 100000 | the most steps replayed for one frame |

**Schematron** (`scene-render.sch`):

- **SN1** `snowfall`, `meltRate`, `driftRate`, `driftThreshold`, `exposureGain`, `iceFlow` and `iceTimeScale`
  are ≥ 0; `snowfallVariation` and `avalancheRate` are in [0, 1]; `temperature` and `step` are > 0; `density` is in
  (0, 1]; `repose` is in (0, 90); `maxSteps` is a positive integer.
- **SN2** `layer` names a `snow` layer the terrain declares: `snow:<type>` for a typed layer, `snow` for the
  layer declared without a type.
- **SN3** `wind`, when present, is the id of a `wind` of `physics`.
- **SN4** A `snowpack` with `iceTimeScale` > 0 belongs to a terrain that declares an `ice` layer.
- **SN5** A `snowpack` with `meltRate` > 0 belongs to a terrain that declares a `water` layer.
- **V17** (extended) `snowpack` requires `scene/@version` 1.8 or later.

### Semantics

The grid, the layers, the surface h (the sum of the solid layers, every snow type and the ice included), the D8
neighbourhood, the gradient and the element seed e = E(project, id, seed, "terrain") are the terrain-grid draft's;
the thermal step is the erosion draft's; w(p, t) is the wind-field draft's planar wind. dx is `metersPerCell`; the
cell (i, j) has i to the right and j down; snow and ice depths are metres of snow and ice, water depths metres of
water, temperatures kelvin, time seconds of scene time except the ice's, in ice-years. The reference is
`conformance/tools/snow_reference.py`; function names in parentheses.

1. **Order at a frame.** The layers at scene time t are the generated layers (terrain-grid draft rules 1–5) after
   the erosion draft's fluvial pass, then round(t / `step`) steps of the pack, each step running rules 2 to 7 in
   that order. The state is never carried from one frame to the next (SREP 14). (`run`, `step`)
2. **Snowfall.** Step n adds `snowfall` · Δt · (1 + `snowfallVariation` · (2 S(e, 2³¹ + 2¹⁶, n, pack(i, j, 0)) − 1))
   metres of snow to cell (i, j), S and pack the random-streams draft's stream draw and index packing, e the
   terrain's element seed; the purpose 2³¹ + 2¹⁶ is clear of the terrain-grid draft's generator channels (below
   2²⁶) and of the erosion draft's rain purposes (2³¹ + k). With `snowfallVariation` 0 or `snowfall` 0 no draw is
   made. (`snowfall_depth`)
3. **Melt.** The cell's temperature T is its `temperature` layer where the terrain has one, else the attribute.
   The step melts min(the snow after the snowfall, `meltRate` · max(0, T − 273.15) · Δt) metres of snow (the
   degree-day law, Hock 2003, with the day as the unit of Δt rescaled to seconds); the melt times `density` joins
   the `water` layer. Snow never falls below zero. (`melt_depth`, `cell_temperature`)
4. **Drift.** With a `wind`, cell (i, j) reads the planar wind of the wind-field draft at the physics point
   ((i + ½) dx, −(j + ½) dx) at the step's start time n Δt — the terrain's top-left corner at the physics origin,
   +y up — and takes it in grid space as (w_x, −w_y). The snow leaving the cell is min(what it holds,
   `driftRate` · max(0, |w| − `driftThreshold`)³ · X · Δt) with the exposure X = clamp(1 + `exposureGain` ·
   (∇h · w / |w|), 0, 2), ∇h the gradient of the ground surface (central differences, one-sided at the edges): a
   surface rising along the wind is exposed and loses up to twice the flat rate, one falling away lies in the lee
   and loses nothing at a slope of 1 / `exposureGain`. The amount is split between the downwind x neighbour and
   the downwind y neighbour in proportion to |w_x| and |w_y|; the share pushed over the grid's edge is lost and
   its total is reported. All cells compute from the same surface and the deltas apply at once; a step that moves
   nothing returns the layers unchanged. A still cell on a uniform field receives what it loses, exactly.
   (`wind_grid`, `drift_flux`, `exposure`, `drift_step`)
5. **Avalanche.** The erosion draft's thermal step (its rule 6) on the snow layer with talus = `repose` and
   rate = `avalancheRate`: a cell whose snow surface drops to a neighbour by more than tan(`repose`) times the
   distance moves `avalancheRate` times half its largest excess to its lower neighbours in proportion to their
   excess, never more snow than it holds, edges closed, mass conserved exactly; only the named snow layer moves.
   (`avalanche_step`, `max_slope`)
6. **Glacier.** When `iceTimeScale` > 0 the `ice` layer advances by Δt · `iceTimeScale` ice-years of shallow-ice
   flow on the ground surface s: between two edge neighbours the diffusivity is D = 2A/(n + 2) (ρ g)ⁿ H̄ⁿ⁺² |Δs /
   dx|ⁿ⁻¹ with n = 3, ρ = 917 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s², A = `iceFlow`, H̄ the mean of the two ice depths and Δs the
   surface drop across the face; the face carries D Δs / dx cubic metres per metre of face per ice-year down the
   surface. The time advances in substeps of min(the remainder, dx² / (8 · max D)); each cell's outflows of a
   substep are scaled so that they never exceed its ice; the fluxes apply at once and the ice stays ≥ 0. Flow stops
   when no face has a diffusivity (a flat surface or no ice). More than 1000 substeps in one step is refused — an
   engine reports it (SREP 14) rather than carrying state. The volume of ice is conserved exactly; the snow on the
   glacier counts in the surface but does not flow. (`ice_diffusivity`, `ice_step`)
7. **Steps and the bound.** The number of steps at scene time t is min(round(t / `step`), `maxSteps`); `step` ≤ 0
   is refused. (`steps_for`)
8. **Digest.** The layers' digest is the SHA-256 of the JSON of the layers with sorted keys, no spaces, pairs as
   two-element lists and every value rounded to three decimals. (`digest`)
9. **Layer names.** `layer` resolves as `kind:type`; the value `snow` names the snow layer declared without a
   type (keyed `snow:` in the terrain-grid draft's layer map). A pack whose layer the terrain does not declare is
   refused at parse (SN2), never silently skipped. (`snow_key`)
10. **Validity.** SN1–SN5 and V17 hold or the document is refused; the reference lists the codes.
    (`validate_document`, `bad_document`)
11. **Interplay.** The snow layer is solid in the terrain-grid draft: it raises the ground the erosion draft's
    processes and the surface-water draft's bed read, and the meltwater it adds to `water` is theirs to move. A
    terrain may carry an `erode` thermal child on the same snow layer; it is redundant with rule 5 and allowed.
12. **Units.** Snowfall and melt are depths of snow; `density` converts them to water. The degree-day factor is
    given per second so that the scene clock needs no calendar: a `meltRate` of 1e-5 m K⁻¹ s⁻¹ is 0.86 m K⁻¹ per
    day, a cinematic rate; the glaciological 3–8 mm K⁻¹ per day (Hock 2003) is 3.5e-8 to 9e-8 per second.
13. **Mass balance.** With no `wind`, the snow on the grid after any number of steps is the snow that fell minus
    the snow that melted, exactly up to rounding; with a `wind`, minus what left the grid (reported). (`run`)

## Rationale

- **The thermal step, not a dedicated avalanche model, for the slide.** Cordonnier et al. (2018) relax the snow
  to its angle of repose with the same neighbourhood transfer Musgrave uses for talus, with the stability of the
  pack as a trigger. The erosion draft already specifies that step, synchronous and conserving; one rule reused is
  one rule fewer to implement and test. The trigger (a stability field) is an open issue.
- **A cubic law with a threshold for drift.** Saltation transport grows with the cube of the friction speed once
  the threshold is passed (Bagnold; Pomeroy and Gray 1990 give the snow law), which is why a 9 m/s wind moves an
  order of magnitude more than a 7 m/s one. The two parameters are a rate and a threshold speed; both are observable
  quantities, not tuning knobs.
- **Exposure as the slope along the wind.** Fearing (2000) shades the accumulation by exposure to the wind;
  the dot product of the surface gradient with the wind direction is the cheapest field with the right sign:
  windward faces scoured, lee faces loaded, the flat unchanged. The clamp at [0, 2] keeps a cliff from scouring
  more than twice the flat rate.
- **The shallow-ice approximation.** It is the zeroth-order theory of ice sheets (Hutter 1983), a nonlinear
  diffusion of the surface that every glacier model starts from (Mahaffy 1976 for the finite differences), and it
  has exact solutions (Vialov 1958 steady profile, Halfar 1981 similarity solution) for future kit cases. The
  explicit scheme with adaptive substeps and the per-cell outflow limiter is reproducible across engines and keeps
  the ice non-negative without an implicit solve.
- **Ice-years on their own clock.** Glaciers move metres per year; a scene second cannot be a year for the snow
  and a second for the water. `iceTimeScale` makes the ratio explicit and 0 turns the flow off; the substep bound
  keeps a large ratio from running away (rule 6).
- **Purpose 2³¹ + 2¹⁶.** The snowfall draws must not alias the erosion draft's rain purposes 2³¹ + k (k the
  `erode` index) nor the generator channels; 2¹⁶ erode children is the bound the offset assumes.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Growing the 2D snow particles into a pack.** The emitter's flakes are a rendering of falling snow with no grid
  under them; the terrain-grid draft's layers are the state a pack needs. The particles stay as the 1.7 feature.
- **A full energy balance for melt.** Radiation, turbulent fluxes and albedo need fields the scene does not
  have; the degree-day law needs one temperature and matches energy-balance models to within their own spread
  over a season (Hock 2003).
- **Advecting a suspended-snow field.** A transported concentration with settling would give plumes; it is a
  second state layer and a second solver. Direct cell-to-cell transport is the saltation layer alone and is enough
  for drifts and cornices at the grid's scale.
- **An implicit ice solve.** Unconditionally stable, and not reproducible to the digit across engines; the
  explicit substeps are bounded and the bound is the engine's report, not a hidden cost.
- **Snow drift inside the erosion draft as a fourth `erode` kind.** The pack couples snowfall, melt, drift and
  avalanches around one layer and one wind; splitting it into kinds would need four children agreeing on the layer.

## Backwards compatibility

A 1.7 document has no `snowpack`; the particle emitter and its `snow` preset are untouched. A 1.8 `terrain`
without `snowpack` behaves as in the terrain-grid draft. The element is additive; no attribute, default or rule
of an existing element changes.

## Open issues

1. **The avalanche trigger.** Cordonnier et al. (2018) release a slab when its stability falls under a
   threshold set by the snow's age and temperature; here the pack relaxes every step. A stability layer and a
   release event (with a stream draw) are the extension.
2. **Sublimation and wind-packing.** Drifting snow sublimates and the packed snow drifts less; both change the
   mass balance in the field and neither is here.
3. **Several snow types.** A pack acts on one layer; fresh snow over old snow needs two packs or a type that
   ages. How types interact in the avalanche (the slab over the base) is open.
4. **Snow on the glacier and firn.** The snow that falls on the ice counts in the surface but never becomes ice;
   a densification into the `ice` layer at a rate would close the glacier's mass balance.
5. **Ice beyond the shallow-ice approximation.** Sliding, calving into water and the ice's temperature are out;
   the Halfar similarity solution and the Vialov profile should become reference tests of the scheme's accuracy.
6. **The substep bound.** 1000 substeps per step bounds the work of a frame; a glacier on fine cells at a large
   `iceTimeScale` hits it, and whether an engine should then subcycle across steps or refuse is open.
7. **Open edges.** The drift loses snow over every edge and the avalanche's edges are closed; an edge policy
   shared with the erosion and surface-water drafts (`boundary`) is the candidate.
8. **Rendering the pack.** The snow layer is a height; its look (albedo, the blue of the ice, the cornice's
   overhang that a height field cannot hold) belongs to the terrain-grid draft's materials and is not specified.
9. **Other engines' generators and winds.** The kit cases bind the Rust engine until a second engine reproduces
   the terrain-grid draft's noise and the wind-field draft's turbulence.
