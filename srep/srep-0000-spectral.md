# SREP 0 — Add spectral rendering

## Abstract

Dispersive materials and a spectral path tracer, SREP 12's milestone 6 (OW2): a `dispersionModel` on
materials (`cauchy`, `sellmeier` or a named `glass` from a table with published coefficients: `BK7`, `SF10`,
`SF11`, `fusedSilica`, `water`, `diamond`) that gives n(λ) and derives the existing `dispersion` attribute for
the raster path through the Abbe number; a `spectral` mode of the path tracer that traces each path at a hero
wavelength and its rotations across a band (`bandMin`, `bandMax`, `wavelengths` per path), drops the
companions at a dispersive refraction, and converts radiance to XYZ through the CIE 1931 colour matching
functions and to linear sRGB under D65; emitters by blackbody `temperature` or a tabulated `spectrum`; RGB base
colours uplifted to bounded smooth spectra so that an RGB document renders the same colours spectrally; and a
`wavelength` on the ray-optics bench's sources so that a `prism` draws its spectrum. The reference tests are
the ones SREP 12 names: BK7 n = 1.5168 at 587.56 nm and the prism deviation per wavelength; the draft adds
the Abbe number 64.17, the D65 white point, the Planckian chromaticity and the uplift round trip.

## Motivation

SREP 12 row OW2 asks for "Sellmeier and Cauchy materials, hero-wavelength sampling in the path tracer",
measured by "BK7 n = 1.5168 at 587.6 nm; prism deviation", and makes shot 28 (optical rotation's colour bands)
native together with OW3. scene-render today has one `ior` per material and a `dispersion` strength that the
raster path applies as KHR_materials_dispersion does (`three.wgsl` spreads the red and blue indices by
(ior − 1) × 0.025 × dispersion) and the path tracer ignores; no named glass, no wavelength anywhere, and the
rainbow of a prism is a hand-painted gradient. A physics explainer that shows why glass splits light needs the
index to be a stated function of wavelength from a cited source, and the tracer to carry wavelengths so that
the colours on the screen are the integral of a spectrum and not three indices chosen by hand.

## Specification

### Syntax

A diff for schema 1.8 against `schema/scene-render.xsd` at 1.7.1 (line numbers of that file) and the
ray-optics draft; the schema files are not changed by this draft.

- An attribute group `dispersionAttributes`, added to `materialType` (next to `ior` at 2471 and `dispersion`
  at 2507) and to the ray-optics draft's `lens`, `prism`, `block` and `grin` elements:
  - `dispersionModel` (one of `none`, `cauchy`, `sellmeier`, `glass`, default `none`: with `none` the
    material's `ior` or the element's `n` holds at every wavelength), `glass` (one of `BK7`, `SF10`, `SF11`,
    `fusedSilica`, `water`, `diamond`, required with `dispersionModel="glass"`: rule 1's table), `cauchyA`,
    `cauchyB` (`xs:double`, `cauchy`: n = A + B/λ², λ in micrometres), `sellmeierB`, `sellmeierC`
    (`doubleListType`, `sellmeier`: equal-length lists, C in micrometres squared).
  - With a model other than `none`, `ior` (or `n`) is ignored and `dispersion` is derived (rule 1); a document
    that sets both is valid and the derived value wins.
- `cameraType` (4423) gains, next to `pathSamples` and `maxBounces`: `spectral` (`xs:boolean`, default false),
  `bandMin`, `bandMax` (`xs:double`, nanometres, defaults 380 and 780), `wavelengths` (`xs:positiveInteger`,
  1 to 16, default 4: wavelengths traced per path, rule 3).
- `lightType` and `materialType` (for its emission) gain `temperature` (`xs:double`, kelvin: a blackbody
  emitter, rule 2) and `spectrum` (`xs:IDREF` of a `data` asset with columns `nm` and `value`: a tabulated
  emission spectrum, linear between samples, 0 outside); without either, an emitter's `color` is uplifted
  (rule 4).
- The ray-optics draft's `source` gains `wavelength` (`xs:double`, nanometres: the rays are monochromatic and
  drawn in that wavelength's colour, rule 5); its `spread` of colours across `rays` is `wavelengthSpread`
  (`xs:double`, nanometres, default 0: ray i carries `wavelength` + (i/(rays − 1) − 1/2) × spread).
- Schematron:
  - **SD1** `dispersionModel="glass"` has `glass`; `cauchy` has `cauchyA` at least 1 and `cauchyB` at least
    0; `sellmeier` has `sellmeierB` and `sellmeierC` of equal, non-zero length with every C positive; `ior`
    and `n` are at least 1.
  - **SD2** with `spectral="true"`, `bandMin` is below `bandMax`, both inside 360 to 830 nm, and
    `wavelengths` lies in 1 to 16.
  - **SD3** an emitter has at most one of `temperature` and `spectrum`; `temperature` lies in 1000 to
    40000 K.
  - **SD4** a bench `source` with `wavelength` has it inside 380 to 780 nm, and a bench element with a
    dispersion model other than `none` is hit only by rays that carry a wavelength.
  - **V17** `dispersionModel`, `spectral`, `temperature`, `spectrum`, `wavelength` and their companions need
    `scene/@version="1.8"` or later.

### Semantics

The reference module (`conformance/tools/spectral_reference.py`) names the function of each rule. Wavelengths
are nanometres in the document and in the module's signatures; the Sellmeier and Cauchy forms take
micrometres inside, as their published coefficients do.

1. **Dispersion models.** `sellmeier(λ, B, C)` is n² = 1 + Σ Bᵢ λ²/(λ² − Cᵢ); `cauchy(λ, A, B)` is
   n = A + B/λ². `glass_index(name, λ)` is the Sellmeier form with the coefficients of the table below, valid
   inside each fit's range (an error outside); `refractive_index(material, λ)` dispatches on the model and
   returns `ior` for `none`. The Abbe number is `abbe_number` = (n_d − 1)/(n_F − n_C) at the Fraunhofer lines
   F 486.13, d 587.56 and C 656.27 nm (`LINES`), and the raster path's `dispersion` is derived as
   KHR_materials_dispersion defines it, `material_dispersion` = `khr_dispersion(V_d)` = 20/V_d, so that the
   existing shader keeps its meaning; the raster path refracts red, green and blue with `rgb_indices` =
   n at 610, 550 and 465 nm (`RGB_WAVELENGTHS`) when a model is present. `cauchy_from_abbe(n_d, V_d)` gives the
   Cauchy pair of a glass known only by its catalogue numbers.

   | `glass` | Form | Coefficients (B; C in µm²) | Range | n_d | Source |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | `BK7` | Sellmeier | 1.03961212, 0.231792344, 1.01046945; 0.00600069867, 0.0200179144, 103.560653 | 300–2500 nm | 1.5168 | Schott N-BK7 data sheet |
   | `SF10` | Sellmeier | 1.62153902, 0.256287842, 1.64447552; 0.0122241457, 0.0595736775, 147.468793 | 380–2500 nm | 1.7283 | Schott N-SF10 data sheet |
   | `SF11` | Sellmeier | 1.73759695, 0.313747346, 1.89878101; 0.013188707, 0.0623068142, 155.23629 | 380–2500 nm | 1.7847 | Schott N-SF11 data sheet |
   | `fusedSilica` | Sellmeier | 0.6961663, 0.4079426, 0.8974794; 0.0684043², 0.1162414², 9.896161² | 210–3710 nm | 1.4585 | Malitson (1965) |
   | `water` | Sellmeier (4 terms) | 0.5684027565, 0.1726177391, 0.02086189578, 0.1130748688; 0.005101829712, 0.01821153936, 0.02620722293, 10.69792721 | 182–1129 nm | 1.3334 | Daimon and Masumura (2007), 20 °C |
   | `diamond` | Sellmeier (2 terms) | 0.3306, 4.3356; 0.1750², 0.1060² | 225–10000 nm | 2.4175 | Peter (1923) |

2. **Colour.** The CIE 1931 2° colour matching functions are the multi-lobe fit of Wyman, Sloan and Shirley
   (2013), `cie_xyz(λ)`, whose integrals agree within 1 % and whose ȳ peaks at 555 nm; XYZ of a spectrum is
   the rectangle rule at 1 nm over the band, `xyz_from_spectrum`, normalised so that a constant 1 has Y = 1,
   and `white_point` scales an illuminant to Y = 1. The D65 illuminant is the CIE table at 10 nm (`d65`,
   linear between samples); its chromaticity through the fit is (0.3131, 0.3295) against the standard's
   (0.3127, 0.3290). XYZ to linear sRGB is the inverse of the IEC 61966-2-1 matrix (`xyz_to_linear_srgb`,
   `linear_srgb_to_xyz`). An emitter with `temperature` T radiates Planck's law `blackbody(λ, T)` (W sr⁻¹
   m⁻³; `blackbody_normalised` is 1 at the Wien peak `wien_peak` = 2.897771955 × 10⁶ nm K / T); its `color`
   then scales the result. A `spectrum` asset is read linearly between its samples.
3. **Hero-wavelength sampling.** With `spectral="true"` each path carries `wavelengths` wavelengths: the hero
   λ₀ = `bandMin` + u (`bandMax` − `bandMin`) from the unit draw u = `hero_unit(seed, pixel, sample)` =
   U(seed, WAVE, pixel × 65536 + sample) of SREP 92's path stream (channel `CHANNEL_WAVELENGTH`, index
   `path_index`; the frame's seed is `frame_seed(seed, frame)` = H(seed, FRAM, frame)), and the companions
   are its rotations `hero_wavelengths(u, n)`: λⱼ = `bandMin` + ((u + j/n) mod 1)(`bandMax` − `bandMin`)
   (Wilkie, Nawaz, Droske, Weidlich and Hanika 2014). All wavelengths share the path's geometry until a
   refraction through a material with a dispersion model, `dispersive_event`: the hero's index steers the
   path and the companions are dropped. The pixel estimate is `estimate_xyz`: (band / n_alive) Σ cmf(λⱼ)
   L(λⱼ) over the surviving wavelengths, the balance heuristic of the paper for equal wavelength densities,
   normalised like rule 2; it converges to `xyz_from_spectrum` (the reference averages 256 stratified heroes
   to 2 × 10⁻³), and the pixel's linear sRGB is `xyz_to_linear_srgb` of the accumulated XYZ. Reflectances that
   are not spectral (textures, `baseColor`) are uplifted by rule 4 at each surviving wavelength.
4. **Uplift.** An RGB reflectance is the sigmoid polynomial of Jakob and Hanika (2019),
   `uplift_spectrum(c, λ)` = σ(c₀t² + c₁t + c₂) with t = (λ − 380)/400 and σ(x) = 1/2 + x/(2√(1 + x²)), bounded
   in (0, 1); `fit_uplift(rgb)` solves for c by Gauss–Newton so that the spectrum under D65 renders to the
   given linear sRGB (`uplift_rgb`), with the fit's D65 white scaled onto the sRGB white so that a grey is the
   flat spectrum (c₀ = c₁ = 0). `uplift_error(rgb)` is the round-trip error; the draft's bound
   `UPLIFT_BOUND` is 0.005 per component for colours inside the sRGB cube, which is what "an RGB document
   renders the same colours spectrally" means and what the kit measures.
5. **The bench.** A ray-optics `source` with `wavelength` λ refracts at every dispersive element with
   `bench_index(element, λ)` = `refractive_index` of the element's model and is drawn in
   `wavelength_rgb(λ)` (the matching functions through the sRGB matrix, clipped and scaled to a unit
   maximum); a fan of `rays` with `wavelengthSpread` draws a spectrum. The deviation of a prism per wavelength
   is the OW1 formula with n(λ), `prism_spectrum(glass, apex, incidence, λs)`, and `angular_dispersion` is
   the F-line deviation minus the C-line deviation (0.70° for a 60° BK7 prism at 50° incidence, 2.94° for
   SF10 at 60°).
6. **Animation and the seek check.** The wavelengths of a path are a pure function of (seed, frame, pixel,
   sample), `frame_wavelengths`, and the estimate of a frame depends on nothing but them and the scene at that
   frame (SREP 14; `seek_test` renders 49 frames of paths through a 5000 K emitter and compares frames 0, 11,
   24 and 48 rendered alone with the run). Dispersion models, temperatures and uplifts carry no state.
7. **Validation.** `validate_material` is SD1, `validate_spectral` SD2, `validate_emitter` SD3 and
   `validate_bench_wavelength` SD4.

## Rationale

- **Named glasses with cited coefficients.** The point of OW2 is that n = 1.5168 is a fact with a source, not
  a slider; the table carries the coefficients and the citation, and the kit checks the module's value against
  the Schott catalogue's n_d, n_F and n_C to 5 × 10⁻⁵.
- **Deriving `dispersion`, not replacing it.** The raster shader implements KHR_materials_dispersion; mapping
  the model's Abbe number to 20/V_d keeps one source of truth and keeps glTF round trips meaningful.
- **Hero wavelengths rather than one wavelength per path.** One wavelength per path is unbiased but four
  times noisier for non-dispersive paths; the hero scheme shares the geometry and pays only at dispersive
  events, which is why every production spectral tracer uses it. The draw comes from SREP 92's stream so that
  the seek check holds without any new randomness.
- **A fitted CMF and a 10 nm D65.** The reference stays standard-library and readable; the fit's white error
  is 0.0005 in chromaticity and is removed from the uplift by scaling onto the sRGB white. An engine may use
  the full tables; the kit's tolerances (the numeric table's six digits are the fit's) allow either.
- **The sigmoid uplift.** Jakob and Hanika's spectra are smooth, bounded and cover the sRGB cube; the
  three-coefficient fit is a 3 × 3 Gauss–Newton that an engine can run per texel or cache in a small table.
- **Wavelength on the bench's sources, not on its elements.** A ray carries its wavelength; elements carry
  their material. The same `dispersionAttributes` serve the tracer and the bench.

## Rejected alternatives

- **RGB rendering with three indices per material.** Three indices give three refracted images and no
  spectrum between them; the rainbow stays a banded artefact.
- **Spectral textures and spectral assets everywhere.** Only emitters take a `spectrum` asset; reflectances
  are uplifted. Measured reflectance spectra are an asset problem for a later draft.
- **Fluorescence and polarisation in the tracer.** Out of scope; OW3 covers polarisation on the bench.
- **Metamer-aware uplift (reflectance under the scene's own illuminant).** The uplift is under D65 by
  definition of sRGB; a scene lit by a 3000 K lamp shows the uplifted colour under that lamp, which is the
  physical answer.

## Backwards compatibility

Additive. Documents without a dispersion model, `spectral`, `temperature`, `spectrum` or `wavelength` validate
and render unchanged; the new attributes require version 1.8 (V17). A material with a model and a hand-set
`dispersion` keeps validating and the derived value wins (rule 1), which is stated so that no document can
disagree with its own glass.

## Open issues

1. **Full CMF tables.** The fit's residual is below 1 % of the integrals; whether the engine ships the 1 nm CIE
   tables (and the kit then tightens the colour rows) is for the implementation.
2. **More glasses.** The table has six entries; a `glass` catalogue asset (Schott's AGF format) would let an
   author add any catalogue glass without a schema change.
3. **Spectral textures.** Uplift per texel is exact for sRGB textures but costs a fit per texel; a cached
   3D lookup as in the paper is an engine choice.
4. **Thin-film iridescence and the `iridescence` attributes.** They are spectral by nature and could use the
   same wavelengths; out of scope here.
5. **Channel allocation.** `CHANNEL_WAVELENGTH` and `CHANNEL_FRAME` are requested of the SREP 92 editor as
   named channels of the path stream.
