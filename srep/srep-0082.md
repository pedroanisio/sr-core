# SREP 82 — Mix the sound of video layers across a transition

## Abstract

D19 reports `transition/@audio` as having no effect, on the premise that "visual nodes carry no sound". That premise
is false in sr-core's own schema: a video layer's `volume` and `mute` control the sound of its asset, which is mixed
into the programme. This SREP defines `audio`. At the transition's eased progress p it gives the outgoing and incoming
sides' sound a gain: `crossfade` (the declared default) 1 − p and p, `equal-power` cos(πp/2) and sin(πp/2), `cut` one
side or the other either side of p = ½, and `none` 1 and 1. The gain reaches every video layer that is a side or sits
inside one. A sequence junction cuts its sound when its type is `cut` and crossfades it otherwise. The gain
multiplies the layer's volume.

## Motivation

**sr-core says video layers carry sound.**

- The `layerType` annotation in `schema/scene-render.xsd`: "volume/mute control a video asset's own audio, which is
  mixed into audioMix bus @audioBus (default: master)".
- `videoAssetType` declares `hasAudio` and `audioStream`.
- [SREP 18](srep-0018.md) reports `volume`, `mute` or `audioBus` as inert (I7) only on a layer whose asset has no
  sound. On a video layer they take effect.

**D19 says the opposite.** Its last "Reported" item reads: "Visual nodes carry no sound, so `audio` has nothing to
mix." Under D19, a 0.5 s centred transition between two video layers extends both layers over the window (D19,
Handles) and leaves their sound alone. The outgoing clip keeps sounding at full level for 0.25 s after the cut, and
the incoming clip starts at full level 0.25 s before it.

**What the reference did, as measured.** An internal lab run (G2, wave 1, 2026-10-05) rendered four documents with
two sound-carrying video layers and a 0.6 s transition, one per `audio` value. On `rs-scene-render` of that date,
all four values gave the same mix, and both clips played at full level wherever they overlapped. v0.3.1 (commit
1ae5ce10) still behaves this way. Its model parses `audio` (`m::TransitionAudio`), but `sr-deliver`'s
`audio::mix_scene`, which mixes the sound of every video layer whose asset has `hasAudio="true"`, never reads it.

**What the reference does now.** Branch `fix/transition-audio`, commit 61a85ebf (CI run 38057496158, green):

- `audio::transition_gains` gives the two gains for each value.
- `audio::transition_gain` multiplies them over every active transition whose side is the layer or contains it.
- `mix_scene` multiplies the layer's volume by that product, once per frame.
- Two tests in `crates/sr-deliver/tests/deliver.rs` check the gains:
  `a_transition_mixes_the_sound_of_its_sides_by_its_audio` and
  `a_one_sided_transition_fades_the_sound_of_its_node_and_reaches_into_groups`.

## Specification

### Syntax

No declaration changes. The `audio` attribute of `transitionType` gains an annotation:

```xml
<xs:attribute name="audio" default="crossfade">
  <xs:annotation><xs:documentation>How the transition mixes the sound of the video layers on its sides (a side that is
  such a layer, and such layers inside a side), at the transition's eased progress p: crossfade gives the outgoing side
  1 - p and the incoming side p; equal-power cos(pi p / 2) and sin(pi p / 2); cut the outgoing side while p &lt; 1/2
  and the incoming side from p = 1/2; none leaves both at their own volume. The gains multiply the layers' volume.
  A sequence junction cuts its sound when its type is cut and crossfades it otherwise. SREP 82.</xs:documentation></xs:annotation>
  <xs:simpleType><xs:restriction base="xs:string">
    <xs:enumeration value="crossfade"/><xs:enumeration value="equal-power"/>
    <xs:enumeration value="cut"/><xs:enumeration value="none"/>
  </xs:restriction></xs:simpleType>
</xs:attribute>
```

Schematron: none.

### Semantics

1. **Sound sources.**
   - A sound source is a `layer` whose asset is a video asset with `hasAudio="true"`.
   - A source belongs to a side S of a transition when it is S or a descendant of S: the side's own layer, or the
     layers inside a group or sequence that is the side.
   - Other nodes have no sound, and a transition does nothing for them.
   - On a `transition` element, the picture's `type` does not affect the sound. A `type="cut"` element with no
     `audio` attribute crossfades the sound over its window, since `crossfade` is the attribute's default and the
     element can say `audio="cut"`. A sequence junction cannot, and Semantics 3 gives it its law.
2. **Gains.** Let p be the transition's eased progress at time t (D19, Progress: `curve` over the window). Inside
   the window [w₀, w₁), the outgoing side's sources get the gain g_out(p) and the incoming side's sources get
   g_in(p):

   | `audio` | g_out(p) | g_in(p) |
   |---|---|---|
   | `crossfade` (default) | 1 − p | p |
   | `equal-power` | cos(πp/2) | sin(πp/2) |
   | `cut` | 1 if p < ½, else 0 | 0 if p < ½, else 1 |
   | `none` | 1 | 1 |

   p is clamped to [0, 1]. Outside the window, a transition gives no gain (a factor of 1). D19's handles still decide
   when a source sounds at all: before its handle starts and after its handle ends, it is silent as before.
3. **Sides, junctions and several transitions.**
   - A transition with only `from` applies g_out to its sources. One with only `to` applies g_in.
   - A sequence junction that gets the sequence's `transition` (D19, Default transitions) has no element and no
     `audio`. When that type is `cut`, the junction uses the `cut` law, so its sound switches where its picture
     does (p = ½). Every other type uses `crossfade`. Its progress is that of the junction's transition.
   - When several transitions are active at t, the gain of a source is the product over all of them of the gain each
     gives it, a factor of 1 from those it does not belong to. G(t) is that product.
4. **Volume.** The source's effective volume at t is v(t) · G(t). v(t) is the layer's `volume`, animated or static.
   The gain applies where `volume` does, so `pan`, `mute` and bus routing (`audioBus`) are unchanged, and a muted
   layer stays silent.
5. **Sampling.**
   - G is evaluated at the composition's frame times k / fps and interpolated linearly between them. This is the rate
     at which the layer's volume automation is sampled; SREP 13 calls these the control frames.
   - So a `cut` is a ramp of one frame period. It ends at the first frame time with p ≥ ½.
   - Between two frame times inside the window, a crossfade with a linear `curve` is exact. Elsewhere the
     interpolation differs from G(t) by at most (1/8) · max|G''| / fps² (the error bound of linear interpolation).

### Defaults and the neutral case

`crossfade` is the declared default. This SREP therefore changes the sound of documents that never set `audio`, and
that change is listed under Backwards compatibility. Sound does not change for:

- a document with no transition or sequence junction;
- a document whose transitions have no sound source on either side;
- a document whose transitions all set `audio="none"`.

## Rationale

- **The baseline already says that video layers sound.** The `layerType` annotation, `hasAudio` and SREP 18's I7 all
  treat a video layer's sound as part of the programme. D19's premise contradicts them. Without this SREP, the
  attribute's four declared values have no meaning, and the declared default would be silently ignored.
- **Gains at the eased progress, not at linear time.** The sound follows the same p as the picture's kernel, so a
  transition that eases its picture eases its sound. `cut` switches where the reference's picture `cut` switches:
  `effects.wgsl`, `select(F, T, p >= 0.5)`.
- **The four laws.**
  - `crossfade` keeps the sum of amplitudes constant (1 − p + p = 1). That suits correlated material, such as two
    takes of the same sound.
  - `equal-power` keeps the sum of powers constant (cos² + sin² = 1). That suits uncorrelated material.
  - At p = ½, `crossfade` loses 3.01 dB of power on uncorrelated material: 10·log₁₀(0.5² + 0.5²) = −3.01.
  - At p = ½, `equal-power` gains 3.01 dB of amplitude on in-phase correlated material: 20·log₁₀(2 · cos(π/4)) = +3.01.
  - sr-core already uses the equal-power law for segment joins (SREP 13, Semantics 2) and offers it as an
    `audioTrack` fade curve. This SREP does not change which law is the default.
- **Reaching into containers.** A transition from a group draws the group's whole content through the transition,
  so the sound of the layers inside it follows the picture.
- **A `cut` junction cuts its sound.** A sequence with `transition="cut"` cuts its picture. With `crossfade`, it
  would also crossfade its sound over `transitionDuration`, 0.5 s by default, around each cut. The sequence has no
  attribute to say otherwise, and adding one is not needed: the junction's type already says what the editor
  wants. Other junction types blend their pictures over the window, so their sound blends with them.
- **Product over transitions.** Nested and overlapping transitions compose the way nested opacity does. No
  transition can raise a gain above the layer's own volume.
- **Frame-rate sampling.** It is the rate at which volume automation is defined. It is also what the reference
  does, and it softens the `cut` step into a one-frame ramp, which avoids a click.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Keep D19 as it is.** It contradicts sr-core's own schema text. It also leaves `audio` declared with four values
  and a default that mean nothing.
- **Make `equal-power` the default.** That changes a declared XSD default. It is a separate question from giving the
  attribute a meaning, and this SREP keeps it out.
- **Crossfade every sequence junction, `cut` included.** This was the reference's first choice (61a85ebf). It
  puts a 0.5 s sound crossfade under a picture cut, and the author cannot change it.
- **Add `sequence/@transitionAudio`.** It would let a junction choose its sound law, at the cost of a MINOR syntax
  change. Deriving the law from the junction's type covers the case that needs it without new syntax.
- **Evaluate G at every audio sample.** That would make a `cut` an exact step. Volume automation is not defined at
  sample rate, and the reference does not do it. This is kept as an open issue.
- **Let `none` drop the handles' sound**, so each side sounds only in its own window. D19 extends both nodes over the
  window, and a source's sound follows its node's window. `none` means that the transition does nothing to the sound.

## Backwards compatibility

- **Validity.** No document changes validity, and no picture changes.
- **Sound changes against rs-scene-render v0.3.1 (1ae5ce10) and earlier.**
  - Affected: every document with a transition or sequence junction whose side has a sound source that is not
    muted.
  - Within each window, v0.3.1 played both sides at their own volume over the handles. With the default
    `crossfade`, the outgoing side now gets 1 − p and the incoming side gets p. At the centre of the window, each
    side is 6.02 dB quieter than before (20·log₁₀ 0.5).
  - Documents that set `audio="none"` sound as before. Documents with `equal-power` or `cut` change as their rows
    of the table say. A sequence with `transition="cut"` switches its sound at each junction's centre, where v0.3.1
    played both sides at full volume over the window.
- **No version gate.** The reference applies the change to every document version: `mix_scene` reads `audio`
  unconditionally. D19 listed `audio` as reported, so the conformance contract defined no sound across transitions,
  and this SREP defines what was undefined. A gate would keep the overlap at full level, which no document
  plausibly wants, alive for older versions. Whether to add one anyway is the owner's decision (Open issues).
- **Known affected documents.** The four internal lab documents of the G2 wave 1 run named above. No published
  film is known to place a transition between sound-carrying video layers. This was not checked beyond that lab
  corpus.

## Conformance

The cases are in `conformance/srep_cases/srep-0082.json`. They are written by `conformance/tools/srep82_cases.py`,
whose `--assets` option also writes the two tone clips: `srep_cases/assets/srep82-tone-500.mkv` and
`srep82-tone-1250.mkv`. Each clip is 2.2 s long, with black 64 × 36 video and a sine of 500 Hz or 1250 Hz at
amplitude 0.25 on both channels.

**How the cases are built.**

- Layer `a` plays the 500 Hz tone and ends at the cut (1 s).
- Layer `b` plays the 1250 Hz tone from the cut, with `clipIn="1"`, so that its handle before the cut reads real
  media.
- Each document renders an audio-only WAV output, `mix`.

**How the cases are checked.** They use a new form of `conformance/run.py`, `"audio"`.

- The runner measures each tone's amplitude with one DFT bin over the 40 ms window centred on each listed time. Both
  tones complete a whole number of cycles in that window, so neither leaks into the other.
- The amplitude divided by 0.25 is that side's gain.
- The transitions use `curve="linear"`, so p = (t − w₀) / (w₁ − w₀). The exception is the sequence junction, whose
  ease-in-out is symmetric and is measured only at its centre.
- No time is within a frame of a step (Semantics 5).
- The tolerance is 0.01 in gain.

All cases are pending until the reference's branch reaches main. With `pending` removed, they pass on that branch
(61a85ebf), and all but `srep-0082-none` fail on v0.3.1 (History).

| Case | Checks (gains a, b at the listed times) | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| `srep-0082-crossfade-default` | no `audio`: 1 − p and p at p = ¼, ½, ¾; a alone before, b alone after the window | 1, 2 |
| `srep-0082-crossfade-explicit` | `audio="crossfade"`: the same values | 2 |
| `srep-0082-equal-power` | cos(πp/2) and sin(πp/2) at p = ¼, ½, ¾ | 2 |

| `srep-0082-cut-picture-crossfades-sound` | `type="cut"`, no `audio`: the sound crossfades | 1 |
| `srep-0082-one-sided-group-out` | `from` is a group holding a: 1 − p over an end-aligned window | 1, 3 |

- the product over several transitions (Semantics 3, last item);
- `mute` and `pan` being unchanged (Semantics 4).

## Open issues

1. **Sample-exact gains.** Should G be defined at every audio sample (an exact step for `cut`), with frame sampling
   as an allowed approximation within one frame? The current text follows the volume-automation rate.
2. **Version gate.** None is proposed (Backwards compatibility). The owner decides.
