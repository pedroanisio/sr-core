# SREP 0 — Add cloud layers and the night sky

## Abstract

SREP 12's A3: `cloudLayer` children of a dome light with `sky="physical"`, and three attributes of that light
for the night. A cloud layer is a spherical shell above the planet between `base` and `base` + `thickness`
metres whose shape is the random-streams draft's value noise dilated by Worley billows (Schneider and Vos
2015), shaped by a height gradient blended from the stratus, stratocumulus and cumulus presets by the cloud
`type`, thresholded so that the covered fraction of the layer equals its `coverage` by construction, eroded by
a Worley `detail` noise and carried by an F4 `wind` as a frozen pattern. `coverage`, `precipitation` and `type`
are uniform or read from a `weatherMap` (red, green and blue). Light through a layer follows Beer's law with
the powder term, the media draft's `fog` phase function for `dropletDiameter` (Jendersie and d'Eon 2023) and
three multiple-scattering octaves (Wrenninge). The night sky adds the moon's light by Jensen's Lommel-Seeliger
irradiance with earthshine, a Hapke-Lommel-Seeliger disc, the airglow with the van Rhijn brightening, and the
uniform starlight and galactic background, each as a scale on the physical sky's radiance (`earthshine`,
`airglow`, `starlight`). Every quantity is defined by the reference module `clouds_reference.py`; the schema
files are unchanged, the diff lives in this draft.

## Motivation

- SREP 12 plans clouds as A3 of milestone 8, after the physical sky (A1), the volumes (A2) and the wind (F4),
  and names the weather-map clouds of Schneider and Vos, the Draine phase of Jendersie and d'Eon and the night
  sky of Jensen et al. as the models to adopt; this draft is that plan written as rules.
- The physical-sky draft stops at the sun and the moon's position: its sky is black after dusk, apart from the
  `moonIntensity` scale on a moon disc. Night scenes need the moon's irradiance, its phase and colour, the
  airglow and the starlight, from the same observer and date.
- The 1.7 gradient sky (`sky="gradient"`, `crates/sr-3d/src/sky.rs`) has no clouds at all, SREP 40's
  volumetric effects are local media, not a weather system, and SREP 79's multiple scattering in media is the
  general integral, too costly for a sky-wide layer. Users paint clouds into HDR environment images today,
  which do not move, do not follow the sun and cannot be seeded.
- The wind draft (F4) promised that clouds sample the wind directly; the weather map and the noise lattice drift
  with the wind's displacement, so a time-lapse of a front crossing the sky is one `wind` and one `weatherMap`
  asset.

## Specification

### Syntax

A `cloudLayer` is a child element of `lightType` (`schema/scene-render.xsd:3548`), allowed only when the light
is a dome with `sky="physical"` (the physical-sky draft). A light holds any number of layers; they are rendered
from the lowest `base` up. The attributes:

| Attribute | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | ID | required | the layer's id, part of its seed (rule 1) |
| `seed` | integer | absent | the layer's `@seed` in the element seed (rule 1) |
| `base` | metres | 1500 | the shell's bottom altitude above the planet's surface |
| `thickness` | metres | 2500 | the shell's thickness |
| `coverage` | 0 to 1 | 0.5 | the covered fraction of the layer's plan when no `weatherMap` is set (rule 9) |
| `type` | 0 to 1 | 0.5 | the cloud type: 0 stratus, 0.5 stratocumulus, 1 cumulus (rule 6) |
| `precipitation` | 0 to 1 | 0 | rain: raises the coverage and the type toward cumulonimbus, darkens (rules 7, 11) |
| `weatherMap` | IDREF | absent | an image asset read as the weather map, red coverage, green precipitation, blue type (rule 7) |
| `weatherScale` | metres | 20000 | the ground extent of one tile of the weather map |
| `density` | per metre | 0.02 | the extinction coefficient at normalised density 1 (rule 11) |
| `scale` | metres | 4000 | the size of one cell of the base noise lattice |
| `detailScale` | metres | 500 | the size of one cell of the detail lattice |
| `detail` | 0 to 1 | 0.3 | the strength of the detail erosion (rule 10) |
| `octaves` | 1 to 3 | 3 | the octaves of the base and detail noises |
| `wind` | IDREF | absent | the F4 `wind` that carries the layer (rule 8) |
| `dropletDiameter` | micrometres | 20 | the droplet diameter of the `fog` phase function, 5 to 50 (rule 13) |
| `albedo` | 0 to 1 | 0.95 | the single-scattering albedo (rule 11) |
| `ambient` | number | 1 | a scale on the sky's ambient light inside the layer (rule 16) |

Three attributes of the dome light, scales on the night sky's terms (rules 17 to 21):

| Attribute | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `earthshine` | number | 1 | the earthshine term of the moon's irradiance |
| `airglow` | number | 1 | the airglow layer's radiance |
| `starlight` | number | 1 | the integrated starlight; the galactic and cosmic light is not scaled |

Rules the Schematron would carry; the reference's `validate_document` returns their codes:

- **CN1** `coverage`, `type`, `precipitation`, `detail` and `albedo` are 0 to 1; `scale`, `detailScale` and
  `weatherScale` are positive; `density` and `ambient` are at least 0; `octaves` is an integer 1 to 3.
- **CN2** `base` is at least 0, `thickness` positive, and `base` + `thickness` at most the light's
  `atmosphereHeight` (100 km by default).
- **CN3** `dropletDiameter` is 5 to 50 micrometres, the range of the media draft's fit.
- **CN4** `weatherMap` names an image asset and `wind` names a `wind` of `physics`.
- **CN5** a light with `cloudLayer` children has `sky="physical"`.
- **CN6** `earthshine`, `airglow` and `starlight` are at least 0.
- **V17** a document of version 1.7 or earlier has no `cloudLayer` and none of the three light attributes.

### Semantics

All positions are in the physical-sky draft's frame: metres, the planet's centre at the origin, +z up at the
observer, x east and y north; the altitude of a point p is |p| − R with R the planet radius (6 360 000 m by
default), its map point the horizontal position projected to the ground, (p_x, p_y) · R / |p|. Radiance is in
units of the light's `sunIrradiance`. e is the layer's element seed, U and pack the random-streams draft's
uniform draw and lattice index.

1. **Seed.** e = E(project, `id`, `seed`, "clouds").
2. **Remap.** remap(v, lo, hi, a, b) is the clamped linear map of [lo, hi] onto [a, b]; lo < hi.
3. **Value noise.** value(e, q, n) = Σₖ ½ᵏ (V₃(e, 1024 + k, 2ᵏ q) + 1) / 2 over k < n, divided by Σ ½ᵏ, where V₃
   is the wind draft's three-dimensional value noise on the lattice index pack(i, j, k) with the quintic fade.
4. **Worley noise.** The feature point of cell (i, j, k) on channels (c₀, c₁, c₂) at octave k is the cell's
   corner plus (U(e, c₀ · 1024 + k, pack(i, j, k)), U(e, c₁ · 1024 + k, …), U(e, c₂ · 1024 + k, …)). F1(q) is the
   distance from q to the nearest feature point of the 27 cells around it, at most 1. worley(e, c, q, n) =
   Σₖ wₖ (1 − F1(2ᵏ q)) / Σ wₖ with w = (0.625, 0.25, 0.125). The base billows use the channels (2, 3, 4), the
   detail the channels (5, 6, 7).
5. **Base shape.** base(q) = remap(value(q), worley₂₃₄(q) − 1, 1, 0, 1), the value noise dilated by the
   billows (Schneider and Vos 2015, replacing their Perlin with the random-streams value noise).
6. **Height.** h = (altitude − `base`) / `thickness`; the layer is empty outside 0 ≤ h ≤ 1. A band(h, a, b, c,
   d) = remap(h, a, b, 0, 1) · (1 − remap(h, c, d, 0, 1)); the presets are stratus (0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3),
   stratocumulus (0, 0.15, 0.45, 0.6) and cumulus (0, 0.1, 0.7, 1). gradient(h, type) blends stratus to
   stratocumulus for type 0 to 0.5 and stratocumulus to cumulus for 0.5 to 1, linearly.
7. **Weather.** Without a `weatherMap`, (coverage, precipitation, type) are the attributes everywhere. With one,
   they are the red, green and blue of the asset, 0 to 1, sampled bilinearly and tiled at ((m − D(t)) /
   `weatherScale`) · (width, height) for the map point m, pixel (i, j) sitting at coordinates (i, j). The
   effective weather raises coverage to at least 0.7 · precipitation and type to at least precipitation: rain
   falls from cumulonimbus that cover at least 70 % of their region (Schneider and Vos 2015).
8. **Drift and lattice.** D(t) is the wind draft's displacement of the layer's `wind` at t (its x along east,
   its y along north; `speedZ` is not read), zero without a wind. The lattice point of p is ((m − D(t)) /
   `scale`, altitude / `scale`): the clouds are frozen in the wind, like the wind draft's turbulence.
9. **Coverage.** The threshold θ(c) of a coverage c is 1 for c ≤ 0, 0 for c ≥ 1, else the ⌊(1 − c) · 1024⌋-th
   of the 1024 base-shape values sorted ascending at the points ((i + ½) / 32 · 8, (j + ½) / 32 · 8, (`base` +
   `thickness` / 2) / `scale`) of the lattice, i and j from 0 to 31. A column is covered where base(q) > θ(c);
   so the covered fraction of a layer with uniform weather equals c to within the sampling of the calibration,
   whatever the noise's distribution.
10. **Density.** d(p) = remap(base(q), θ(c), 1, 0, 1) · gradient(h, type), then eroded: with w = worley₅₆₇(q ·
    `scale` / `detailScale`) and u = min(1, 10 h), a = `detail` · (w (1 − u) + (1 − w) u) and d ← remap(d, a, 1,
    0, 1) (wispy bottoms, billowy tops); d is 0 when a ≥ 1. 0 ≤ d ≤ 1 and erosion never raises d.
11. **Extinction and albedo.** σ(p) = `density` · d(p) · (1 + precipitation) per metre; the single-scattering
    albedo is `albedo` · (1 − precipitation / 4).
12. **Shell and optical depth.** A ray meets the layer where its parameter lies inside the sphere of radius R +
    `base` + `thickness` and outside the sphere of radius R + `base`, clipped to [0, 35 000 m] (the draw range of
    Schneider and Vos). The optical depth is the midpoint sum of σ ds over those intervals; the transmittance
    is exp(−τ).
13. **Phase.** The phase function is the media draft's `fog` fit for `dropletDiameter`: (1 − w_D) HG(g_HG) +
    w_D Draine(g_D, α). It integrates to 1 over the sphere. phase(μ, d, c) scales both asymmetries by c.
14. **Beer and powder.** The light that reaches a point through optical depth τ toward the sun and scatters
    toward the eye is weighted 2 e^{−τ} (1 − e^{−2τ}): Beer's law times the powder term of Schneider and Vos,
    which darkens the lit surface of a thick cloud and peaks half an optical depth inside it.
15. **Multiple scattering.** The in-scattered sunlight is Σₖ bᵏ e^{−aᵏ τ} phase(μ, d, cᵏ) over three octaves
    with a = b = c = ½ (Wrenninge, Kulla and Lundqvist 2013): the higher octaves are dimmer, less attenuated and
    less forward-peaked.
16. **Ambient.** The sky's irradiance (the physical sky's `sky_irradiance`) lights the cloud by `ambient` ·
    (1 + h) / 2: half at the base, full at the top.
17. **Moon irradiance.** For the moon's phase angle φ and distance d (km) from the physical sky's
    `moon_from_document`, E_m = (2 C r_m² / 3 d²) (`earthshine` · E_em(φ) + E_sun · L(φ)) with C = 0.072 the
    moon's albedo, r_m = 1737.4 km, E_sun = 1361 W/m², L(φ) = 1 − sin(φ/2) tan(φ/2) ln cot(φ/4) the Lommel-Seeliger
    phase law (1 at full moon, 0 at new moon) and E_em(φ) = 0.19 · 0.5 · L(π − φ) the earthshine (Jensen et al.
    2001, equations 1 and 5). The moon's fraction f_m = E_m / E_sun scales the moon's sky; the physical-sky
    draft's `moonIntensity` multiplies it further.
18. **Moon disc.** The disc subtends π (r_m / d)² sr and has the mean radiance E_m over that solid angle, tinted
    by the moon's spectrum relative to the sun's, a ramp from 70 % at 340 nm to 135 % at 740 nm read at 650, 550
    and 450 nm and normalised to the green channel. Across the disc, a point lit at incidence θᵢ and seen at θᵣ
    has the Hapke-Lommel-Seeliger BRDF f = (2 / 3π) B(φ, g) S(φ) / (1 + cos θᵣ / cos θᵢ) with g = 0.6 and t = 0.1
    (Jensen et al. 2001, equations 2 to 4): B = 2 − (tan φ / 2g)(1 − e^{−g / tan φ})(3 − e^{−g / tan φ}) below
    90°, else 1; S = (sin|φ| + (π − |φ|) cos|φ|) / π + t (1 − cos|φ| / 2)². f multiplies the normal irradiance,
    so the full moon's disc is uniform from the centre to the limb (no limb darkening).
19. **Airglow.** The airglow is a layer at 80 km of radiance `airglow` · 5.1 · 10⁻⁸ / (π E_sun) at the zenith
    (Jensen et al. 2001), times the van Rhijn factor 1 / √(1 − (R / (R + 80 km))² sin² z) at the zenith angle z,
    seen through the atmosphere's transmittance.
20. **Background.** The uniform background is (`starlight` · 3 · 10⁻⁸ + 10⁻⁸) / (π E_sun): the integrated
    starlight and the diffuse galactic and cosmic light (Jensen et al. 2001), through the transmittance. The
    star-fields draft (SP2) draws the catalogue stars on top of it.
21. **The night frame.** The sky in a direction is the sum of the physical sky's `sky_radiance` lit by the sun,
    the same function lit from the moon's direction scaled by f_m and the moon's tint, the airglow and the
    background. Directions into the ground see none of them.
22. **Rendering a layer.** Along the view ray, within rule 12's intervals, a renderer accumulates at each sample
    the transmittance so far times σ ds times the sum of the sunlight (E_sun · albedo · the transmittance from
    the sample toward the sun through the layer and the atmosphere · rule 14 · rule 15) and the ambient light
    (rule 16 · albedo); the moon lights the layer the same way with f_m and its tint. What the ray then sees is
    rule 21's sky times the layer's transmittance. The sample counts (view, sun cone) are the engine's choice;
    the kit cases bound the error. The layer's positions, seeds and weather are functions of t, so a frame
    rendered alone equals the played frame (SREP 14).

## Rationale

- **Calibrated coverage.** Schneider and Vos remap the base shape by 1 − coverage, which makes the covered
  fraction depend on the noise's distribution, so "coverage 0.5" is whatever the noise gives. The quantile
  threshold (rule 9) makes the covered fraction equal the number the user set, and the calibration is a fixed
  1024-point sample so every engine computes the same threshold.
- **Value noise, not Perlin.** The random-streams draft defines one value noise with the quintic fade; the wind
  draft already extends it to three dimensions with analytic gradients. Reusing it keeps one noise in the
  SREPs and keeps the clouds seedable from the project seed. Perlin's gradient noise (Schneider and Vos's
  Perlin-Worley) would add a second lattice primitive for a visual difference that the Worley dilation and the
  erosion dominate.
- **Fog phase.** The media draft fit the Mie phase of water droplets (HG + Draine, Jendersie and d'Eon 2023)
  for 5 to 50 µm; cloud droplets sit in that range, so one `dropletDiameter` gives the forward peak, the fog
  bow and the glory without a second phase function. The multiple-scattering octaves scale that fit's
  asymmetries rather than a fixed HG.
- **A spherical shell.** Flat layers meet the horizon at infinity; the shell gives the horizon's cloud band and
  the dip of distant layers for free, and the 35 km draw range bounds the march.
- **Frozen clouds.** The layer rides the wind's displacement and does not evolve otherwise; evolution needs a
  time axis in the noise (the wind draft's `turbulenceTime` slide) and is an open issue, since a cloud that
  evolves while it drifts is not reproducible from a seek without the whole history unless the time is in the
  address, which the lattice already allows.
- **The night in the sun's units.** Jensen's irradiances are absolute (W/m²); dividing by 1361 W/m² states them
  in the physical sky's radiance units so one `sunIrradiance` scales day and night together, and the moon's
  light reuses the sky function with a different light direction instead of a second scattering integral.
- **Lommel-Seeliger, not a magnitude table.** The irradiance law (rule 17) gives the phase curve from the
  phase angle the physical sky already computes; Jensen's table of lunar magnitudes would be a second source
  for the same quantity.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Clouds as volumes (A2).** A `cloudLayer` could be a `medium` with a procedural density. The volumes draft's
  media are local and voxelised; a weather system spans the sky, needs the shell geometry, the weather map and
  the calibrated coverage, and a cheaper lighting model. The two share the phase functions and Beer's law.
- **Cirrus as a 2D texture layer.** Schneider and Vos scroll 2D textures above 4 km. A thin high layer with a
  small `thickness` and `type` 0 renders the same; a second element kind is not needed.
- **A full weather simulation.** Out of scope; the weather map is the user's or a generator asset's.
- **Jensen's moon as geometry with elevation maps.** The disc BRDF (rule 18) and a uniform albedo give the
  phase and the limb; the maria need an albedo map of the moon, which a user may supply later as an asset.
- **Zodiacal light.** Jensen et al. read it from a table (Roach and Gordon) that the paper does not print; it is
  an open issue rather than a guessed number.

## Backwards compatibility

- Every document valid under 1.7 is valid under 1.8: `cloudLayer` and the three attributes are new and optional.
- Documents of version 1.7 or earlier may not use them (V17); their skies render as before.
- A physical sky without layers and with the three attributes at their defaults gains the moon's sky, the airglow
  and the background at night, radiances of order 10⁻⁸ of the sun's irradiance, invisible at daytime exposure.

## Open issues

1. **Evolution.** The layer is frozen in the wind; a time term in the lattice (as the wind draft's
   `turbulenceTime`) would let billows grow and dissolve. It needs a rate attribute and a decision on whether the
   detail or the base evolves.
2. **Zodiacal light.** Jensen et al. take it from Roach and Gordon's table, which this draft does not have; a
   term of order 10⁻⁸ W/m² toward the ecliptic is missing.
3. **Rain.** Precipitation only darkens (rule 11) and shapes (rule 7); falling rain, virga and the rain shaft's
   scattering are not modelled, nor is snow (A8 reads `precipitation` for its own purposes).
4. **Layers and the physical sky's aerial perspective.** The sky function is evaluated to the top of the
   atmosphere; a layer's in-scattering is not attenuated by the atmosphere between the observer and the cloud
   except through the transmittance toward the sun (rule 22). The physical sky's aerial perspective (its rule
   10) applied at the layer's distance would be more exact.
5. **The ambient term.** Rule 16 is a height ramp on the sky's irradiance; an octave-aware ambient (Schneider
   2017) and the ground's albedo bounce are candidates.
6. **The moon's maria.** An albedo map of the moon as an asset, applied over rule 18's BRDF.
7. **Draw range.** 35 km is a constant; very high layers (cirrus at 10 km) are visible further. A `range`
   attribute or a range derived from the layer's altitude is an option.
8. **Conformance in other engines.** Terragen, Unity HDRP and UE5 render Schneider-style layers with their own
   noise textures; a document's layer renders with the same coverage and height band there but not the same
   billows, which the kit cases accept by measuring fractions, not pixels.
