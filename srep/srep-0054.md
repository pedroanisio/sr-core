# SREP 54 — Add a pole target and a soft reach to two-bone IK

## Abstract

`transformConstraint type="ik"` solves two bones (or two node joints) analytically and picks the bend side with the boolean
`bendPositive`. Two optional attributes improve it: `pole` names a node or bone the middle joint bends toward, and `softness`
(0 to 1) eases the chain's reach toward full extension so it does not snap straight when the target leaves its reach. A
constraint without them is unchanged. Stretch (lengthening bones beyond reach) is not part of this SREP.

## Motivation

The NOLN motion research (§1.5 and item E10): "IK: pole target and stretch/soft limit; a walk after K5". With the Rust
reference the bend side is a fixed sign, which flips the whole limb when the character turns, and a target at the
edge of reach straightens the limb in one frame.

## Specification

### Syntax

```xml
<!-- transformConstraintType gains -->
<xs:attribute name="pole" type="xs:IDREF">
  <xs:annotation><xs:documentation>type="ik" on a chain of two bones or joints: a node or bone whose position the middle joint
  bends toward; it replaces @bendPositive (which stays the choice when the pole lies on the line to the target).</xs:documentation></xs:annotation>
</xs:attribute>
<xs:attribute name="softness" type="unitDecimal" default="0">
  <xs:annotation><xs:documentation>type="ik" on a chain of two bones or joints: the fraction of the chain's full reach, counted
  from its end, over which the reach eases toward full extension, so the chain does not snap straight as the target
  leaves its reach.</xs:documentation></xs:annotation>
</xs:attribute>
```

```xml
None.
```

New attributes with neutral defaults, accepted in every version (SREP 0, Versioning).

### Semantics

For a chain of two bones of lengths `l1` and `l2` with its root joint at `A`, a target `T` and `R = l1 + l2`:

**1. Soft reach.** With `d = |T − A|` and `σ = softness`: `e = σR`, `s = R − e`; the distance used is
`d' = d` if `d ≤ s`, else `d' = s + e·(1 − exp(−(d − s)/e))`. (Continuous, slope 1 at `s`, monotone, below `R` for every `d`.)
Then `d'` is clamped to `[|l1 − l2|, R]` as in the analytic solution, and the middle joint is placed for distance `d'`; the end bone
still aims at `T`. With `σ = 0` (and for any `d ≤ s`) the solution is the existing one.

**2. Pole.** With a pole at `P`, let `side = (T − A) × (P − A)` (the z component of the cross product, in the solver's space). The
middle joint is on the side of `P`: it replaces the sign chosen by `bendPositive` when `|side|` is not 0; when `P` lies on the line
`A T`, `bendPositive` decides. The pole is a node or a bone: its position is its anchor (nodes) or its joint (bones), as for the IK target.

**3. Longer chains.** Chains of three or more bones (the FABRIK solver) ignore `pole` and `softness`; an engine SHOULD report
them as inert (SREP 18).

### Defaults and the neutral case

Neither attribute: the existing solution.

## Rationale

- **The side of the pole**, not an angle: the pole is a point in the scene that moves with the rig (a knee target in front of a
  leg), which is how animation tools express it.
- **An exponential ease for the reach**: it has slope 1 where it starts, so there is no visible knee at `s`, and it never
  reaches `R`, so no frame is exactly straight (the instability of the analytic solution at full extension).

## Rejected alternatives

- **Pole angle (twist) instead of a pole target**: a different feature for chains with a twist axis; two-bone 2D chains need a side only.
- **Stretch in this SREP**: it changes bone lengths, which the skeleton model treats as authored data.

## Backwards compatibility

Class: Added attributes with neutral defaults, MINOR in effect, accepted in every version. No valid document changes.
