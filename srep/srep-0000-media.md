# SREP 0 — Define the participating-medium model and its phase functions

## Abstract

[SREP 12](srep-0012.md) names, as its F3 building block, "one description of participating media (absorption,
scattering, emission, phase function) used by the atmosphere, fog, clouds, volumes, water and smoke, so the
raster renderer and the path tracer agree". This SREP is that description. It states, as pure functions, the
coefficients a `medium` has at a point (extinction, scattering, absorption, emission), how overlapping media
combine, the transmittance and the weight of one march step, the absorption inside a transmissive surface as the
same kind of coefficient, and the phase functions: Henyey–Greenstein (what `anisotropy` selects today), Rayleigh,
Draine (a two-parameter family that contains Henyey–Greenstein, Rayleigh and Cornette–Shanks) and the fog phase
function of Jendersie and d'Eon (a Henyey–Greenstein and Draine mixture fitted to Mie scattering by water
droplets, selected by droplet diameter). Three attributes are added to `medium`: `phase`, `phaseAlpha` and
`dropletDiameter`. Everything a 1.7.1 document can say renders as before.

## Motivation

- **The plan depends on it.** SREP 12's F3 row (line 244) records that `medium` with extinction, albedo, a
  one-parameter anisotropy, emission and multiple scattering landed (SREPs 40 and 79) and that "the Rayleigh and
  Draine phase functions are open". A1 (atmosphere "through the media model (F3)"), A2 (volumes), A3 (clouds,
  "Draine–HG phase (Jendersie and d'Eon 2023)"), A4, MF6 (transfer-function volumes "through the media model"),
  S2 and SP8 all read this model; milestone 5 "needs only F3 from the building blocks" (SREP 12, line 365).
- **The model exists but is stated in three places and no SREP defines it as a whole.** SREP 40 gives the
  medium's attributes and the integration (lines 315-377); SREP 79 gives the march step and the walk (Semantics,
  rules 4 to 7); the absorption inside glass and water is a third statement, with its own convention
  (`-ln(colour) / distance`, SREP 40 line 2413). The reference implements the three in four places:
  `crates/sr-volume/src/medium.rs:65-80, 433-438, 481-510` (CPU), `crates/sr-gpu/src/volume.wgsl:133-136`
  (phase), `pathtrace.rs:80-87` and `three.wgsl:581-585` (surface absorption, once per renderer).
- **One phase function is not enough for the plan's media.** Henyey–Greenstein with |g| ≤ 0.99 is the only phase
  function a document can ask for (XSD `mediumType/@anisotropy`). Air (Rayleigh), cloud and fog droplets (Mie,
  with the fogbow's shape) and dust (Draine's family) need the others; SREP 12's A1 and A3 name them.
- **Raster and path tracing must agree.** SREP 40 (line 383) already routes every pass with volumes through the
  transport pipeline, so there is one volume implementation; the surface absorption is the one medium term each
  renderer computes on its own. Stating the model once, with numeric tests, is what lets a second implementation
  (a raster volume path, another engine) be checked against it.

### Semantics

The reference implementation of this section is `conformance/tools/media_reference.py` (standard-library Python);
its function names are given in parentheses. Directions are propagation directions: ω_i is the direction the
light travels when it arrives at the point, ω_o the direction it travels when it leaves, and μ = cos θ = ω_i · ω_o,
so μ = 1 is light that continues straight on (forward scattering) and μ = −1 is light turned back. This is the
reference's convention (`crates/sr-volume/src/medium.rs:433-434`; `crates/sr-gpu/src/volume_grid.wgsl:133`,
where the light's direction is negated before the dot product).

#### 1. Coefficients at a point

1. **Density.** At a point x inside a medium's domain, ρ(x) = `densityScale` × the sampled density × the object's
   opacity (SREP 40, line 379: "Object opacity multiplies density, including emission density"). Outside
   the domain ρ = 0.
2. **Extinction.** σ_t(x) = ρ(x) · `extinction`, a scalar per scene unit (`coefficients(...).sigma_t`;
   `medium.rs:65-66`: "Scalar extinction preserves exact RGBA compositing").
3. **Scattering and absorption.** Per channel c, σ_s,c = σ_t · α_c and σ_a,c = σ_t · (1 − α_c), with α the RGB
   `albedo`, each channel in [0, 1] (`sigma_s`, `sigma_a`). A medium with `scatterBounces="0"` has σ_s = 0 for
   the purpose of in-scattering and keeps σ_t (SREP 79 rule 2).
4. **Emission.** E(x) = ρ(x) · `emissionScale` · (`emissionColor` + thermal RGB), radiance per scene unit, where
   thermal RGB is SREP 40's blackbody colour at the point's scaled temperature and black when `blackbody` is
   false (`emission`; SREP 40 lines 343-356). Emission is not scaled by `extinction` or `albedo`.
5. **Overlap.** Where media overlap, σ_t, σ_s, σ_a and E are the sums over the media (`combine`). The result does
   not depend on the order of the media (SREP 40, line 335; test `conformance/tools` row "overlap").
6. **Validation.** Every input is finite; densities, scales and `extinction` are nonnegative; opacity and albedo
   channels lie in [0, 1]. The reference raises on anything else; a renderer MUST refuse the frame with an error
   that names the attribute, never clamp (SREP 40, "Invalid values fail before transport").

#### 2. Transmittance and the march step

7. **Transmittance.** Over a straight segment of length ℓ with constant σ_t, T = e^(−σ_t ℓ); per channel when the
   coefficient is RGB (`transmittance`). Over a path, the product of its segments' transmittances; equivalently
   e^(−∫σ_t). A renderer that marches MUST use steps no longer than the smallest `stepSize` of the media the ray
   is inside, split at every domain boundary (SREP 40, line 368; `medium.rs:454-472`).
8. **The step weight.** The light a step of length Δ with constant σ_t contributes to the ray is T_before · w · S
   with w = (1 − e^(−σ_t Δ)) / σ_t, and w = Δ when σ_t = 0 (`step_weight`; SREP 79 rule 4; `medium.rs:505`,
   which computes 1 − e^(−σ_t Δ) with `exp_m1`). A renderer MAY use the series Δ (1 − σ_t Δ / 2 + (σ_t Δ)² / 6)
   below an optical depth of 10⁻³ per step, as the reference's GPU does (`pathtrace_water.wgsl:160-162`); the two
   agree to 10⁻¹² relative there.
9. **The source term.** S = E + Σ_m σ_s,m L_m, where L_m is the light arriving at the point weighted by medium m's
   own phase function (section 4) and the sum runs over the media with `scatterBounces` > 0 (SREP 79 rule 4;
   `medium.rs:481-499`). Each medium weights the light with its own phase; overlapping media of different phases
   therefore add their in-scattering separately.

#### 3. The interior of a transmissive surface

10. A material with `transmission` above 0 and a finite positive `attenuationDistance` d is, inside the surface, a
    medium with σ_s = 0, E = 0 and σ_a,c = max(0, −ln(clamp(c_c, 10⁻⁴, 1)) / d) per channel of `attenuationColor`
    c (`surface_absorption`; `pathtrace.rs:80-87`; `three.wgsl:581-585`). Without a distance, or with a white
    colour, σ_a = 0. The clamp is what both of the reference's renderers apply; it makes a black channel
    attenuate as 10⁻⁴ does.
11. Both renderers MUST apply the same coefficient. In the reference they do, each in its own code: the
    rasteriser along the material's `thickness` (`three.wgsl:584`), the path tracer along the refracted path
    (`pathtrace.wgsl:582`; SREP 40, line 2413). The water of SREP 79 (σ_w, rule 6) is this coefficient.

#### 4. Phase functions

Every phase function p(μ) is a density over the sphere of outgoing directions: ∫ p dω = 2π ∫₋₁¹ p(μ) dμ = 1.

12. **`hg`** (default). p(μ) = (1 − g²) / (4π (1 + g² − 2gμ)^(3/2)) with g = `anisotropy` (`phase_hg`;
    Henyey and Greenstein 1941; `medium.rs:435-438`, `volume.wgsl:133-136`). Its mean cosine is g.
13. **`rayleigh`.** p(μ) = 3 (1 + μ²) / (16π) (`phase_rayleigh`; Bruneton and Neyret 2008, the reference code's
    `RayleighPhaseFunction`). It has no parameter: `anisotropy` MUST be absent or 0 (MED3).
14. **`draine`.** p(μ) = (1 − g²) / (4π (1 + α (1 + 2g²) / 3)) · (1 + α μ²) / (1 + g² − 2gμ)^(3/2) with g =
    `anisotropy` and α = `phaseAlpha` ≥ 0 (`phase_draine`; Draine 2003, equation 5). α = 0 gives `hg`; g = 0 and
    α = 1 give `rayleigh`; α = 1 gives Cornette and Shanks (1992), the Mie approximation of Bruneton's
    `MiePhaseFunction`. Its mean cosine is g (1 + α (3 + 2g²) / 5) / (1 + α (1 + 2g²) / 3)
    (`mean_cosine_draine`; Draine 2003, equation A2); the second moment is Draine's equation A3.
15. **`fog`.** p(μ) = (1 − w_D) · p_hg(μ; g_HG) + w_D · p_draine(μ; g_D, α) with, for the droplet diameter d in
    micrometres, 5 ≤ d ≤ 50 (`phase_fog`, `fog_parameters`; Jendersie and d'Eon 2023, equations 3 to 7):

    - g_HG(d) = exp(−0.0990567 / (d − 1.67154)),
    - g_D(d) = exp(−2.20679 / (d + 3.91029) − 0.428934),
    - α(d) = exp(3.62489 − 8.29288 / (d + 5.52825)),
    - w_D(d) = exp(−0.599085 / (d − 0.641583) − 0.665888).

    The fit holds for 5 ≤ d ≤ 50 (the paper's range); the schema refuses other diameters. The paper's
    supplemental gives fits for 0 < d < 5 (Open issues).
16. **Domain.** μ ∈ [−1, 1]; |g| < 1 (the schema's ±0.99 is inside); α ≥ 0. The reference raises outside these.
17. **Light grids** (SREP 40, Light grids). The environment's radiance is pre-integrated over the dome's fixed
    directions only when every asking medium's phase is isotropic, which means `hg` with `anisotropy` 0; a
    medium whose phase is `rayleigh`, `draine` or `fog` is anisotropic for this purpose, whatever its g, and
    costs one grid per fixed direction (`volume_grid.wgsl:146`, which weights each direction by the phase). The
    per-light grids are unchanged: they hold transmittance and visibility, and the phase is applied at lookup
    (`volume_grid.wgsl:133`).

#### 5. Sampling in the multiple-scattering walk

SREP 79 rule 6 samples the new direction "from Henyey–Greenstein with the medium's g, by inversion". With this
SREP, the direction is sampled from the phase function of the medium the collision is in (the m′ of SREP 79 rule 6),
exactly, so that the phase function and the pdf cancel and β is unchanged:

18. **`hg`**: SREP 79's inversion, unchanged (`sample_hg`): u ~ U[0, 1); μ = 1 − 2u if |g| < 10⁻³, else
    s = (1 − g²) / (1 − g + 2gu) and μ = clamp((1 + g² − s²) / (2g), −1, 1).
19. **`draine`** and **`rayleigh`** (= `draine` with g = 0, α = 1): the μ with F(μ) = u, where F is the
    cumulative of p in μ (`cdf_draine`): with t(μ) = 1 + g² − 2gμ, a = 1 + g² and
    K = (1 − g²) / (2 (1 + α (1 + 2g²) / 3)),

    F(μ) = K · (G(t(μ)) − G(t(−1))) / (−2g),  G(t) = −2 / √t + α / (4g²) · (−2a² / √t − 4a √t + (2/3) t √t),

    and for g = 0, F(μ) = K ((μ + 1) + α (μ³ + 1) / 3). The reference inverts F by 64 bisection steps on
    [−1, 1] (`sample_draine`); Jendersie and d'Eon's supplemental (section 2.2) gives the closed-form inverse
    through a quartic. A renderer MAY use either: they agree within 10⁻¹² in μ, which is below the conformance
    tolerance of a Monte Carlo estimate (SREP 79 rule 10).
20. **`fog`**: with a first draw u_s, the Draine lobe when u_s < w_D, else the HG lobe; then rule 18 or 19 with a
    second draw (`sample_fog`; supplemental section 2). The reference does not rescale the first draw.
21. **Bounds.** Nothing here changes SREP 79 rule 7: the walk still has at most min(32, L) collisions, and the
    bisection has a fixed step count.

#### 6. Agreement between renderers

22. A pass with a volume uses the transport pipeline whichever renderer the camera asks for (SREP 40, line 383;
    srep-0079 rule 9). An implementation with a raster volume path of its own MUST agree with the model above
    in expectation: the transmittance of a homogeneous slab to 10⁻⁶ relative (rule 7 is closed-form), and the kit
    cases under Conformance within their stated tolerance.
23. The surface absorption of rule 10 is the same coefficient in both renderers (rule 11); the thermal table of
    SREP 40 (1025 samples at T_i = 50000 (i / 1024)²) is the same table in both.

24. The three attributes carry "Version 1.8" in their documentation, as the schema marks every attribute's

## Rationale

- **Draine's family rather than four unrelated functions.** One formula gives Henyey–Greenstein, Rayleigh and
  Cornette–Shanks as parameter values (Draine 2003, equation 5; Jendersie and d'Eon 2023, equation 2), has
  closed-form moments (Draine, appendix A) and a closed-form cumulative, so sampling is exact. `rayleigh` is kept
  as its own value because air has no parameter to author.
- **A droplet diameter rather than four fitted parameters.** Jendersie and d'Eon fitted the four-parameter model
  to Mie reference data and then "produced a second fit to map d to the parameters, to provide a meaningful and
  intuitive single parameter" (section 2.1). Authoring g_HG, g_D, α and w_D by hand would let a document ask for a
  mixture that fits no droplet; the diameter cannot.
- **The surface absorption stated as a medium.** It is the one medium term each renderer of the reference
  computes separately (`three.wgsl:583`, `pathtrace.rs:84`). Stating it with the same clamp and sign is what
  makes "the raster renderer and the path tracer agree" checkable, and it is the coefficient SREP 79's water
  walk already uses.
- **No change to `anisotropy`.** The existing attribute is Draine's g as well as Henyey–Greenstein's, so `draine`
  needs one new number, not two.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Mie lookup tables per droplet size** (tabulated phase functions as assets). Exact for one wavelength and
  size, but a pinned data file per medium, no closed-form sampling, and the paper's fit is within its own
  measured error of the tabulated reference for the forward 95 % of the scattered light (Jendersie and d'Eon
  2023, figure 2). A later SREP MAY add tables as an asset kind without changing this model.
- **Cornette–Shanks alone for Mie.** It is Draine with α = 1 (rule 14) and has no droplet-size dependence; the
  fog fit needs α up to about 32 (table row "fog 50").
- **Two Henyey–Greenstein lobes.** Jendersie and d'Eon measured 2×HG and 3×HG against the same reference and
  found them worse than HG + Draine across 5 to 50 µm (figure 3).
- **Phase functions on lights instead of media.** Scattering is a property of the particles, not of the light;
  SREP 40 puts the medium's properties on `medium`.
- **Animating `phase`, `phaseAlpha` or `dropletDiameter`.** A change of phase function mid-shot has no physical
  reading; the scales SREP 40 animates stay animatable.

## Backwards compatibility

- Every 1.7.1 document validates and renders unchanged: the new attributes have defaults that reproduce rule 12
  with the existing `anisotropy`, and no existing rule changes.
- A 1.8 document with `phase="rayleigh"`, `draine` or `fog` is refused by a 1.7 validator (unknown attribute),
  as every added attribute is.
- SREP 79's walk is unchanged for `hg`; for the other phases its direction sampling follows rules 19 and 20.
  Its bounds (rule 7) and its invariants (rule 8: no change to transmittance or alpha) hold for every phase.

## Open issues

1. **Droplets below 5 µm.** Jendersie and d'Eon's supplemental (section 3) gives three more fits (d ≤ 0.1 µm,
   0.1 to 1.5 µm, 1.5 to 5 µm) with α = 250 for the smaller ranges. This SREP takes the paper's 5 to 50 µm range;
   extending `dropletDiameter` below 5 is a later amendment once a scene needs it (haze, A1's aerial perspective).
2. **Spectral Rayleigh.** Rule 13 gives the shape only; the λ⁻⁴ dependence of the coefficient is A1's (SREP 12),
   through `extinction` and `albedo` per channel, and is not stated here.
3. **The closed-form Draine inverse.** The reference bisects; the engine may transcribe the supplemental's
   quartic. Whether the two agree to 10⁻¹² in μ over the schema's whole range (|g| ≤ 0.99, α up to 32) is to be
   measured when SR-62 lands, not assumed.
4. **Light-grid cost of `fog`.** Rule 17 makes every `fog` medium anisotropic for the dome grids (one grid per
   fixed direction). The memory bound of SREP 40 applies; no new budget is added.
