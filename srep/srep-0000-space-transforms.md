# SREP 0 — Add space transforms

## Abstract

[SREP 12](srep-0012.md) names, as its MF2 building block, "matrix, complex-function and nonlinear warps applied
to any shape after adaptive subdivision", with the bar "a linear map moves grid nodes exactly; conformal maps
keep right angles" (line 253). This SREP is that block. The `deform` element that `shape` and `layer` carry
today gains three modifier types: `matrix` (an affine map about a centre), `map` (two expressions of the data
coordinates) and `complex` (a named complex function, Möbius included), all three stated as point maps in the
frame of the plots SREP (MF1); `group` gains `deform`, so a grid of shapes warps as one. Before the map, every
flattened segment is subdivided adaptively until its mapped chord is within a tolerance of the mapped curve,
with a fixed depth bound, and a point the map sends to infinity breaks the path as in SREP 70. The stereographic
projection of the 3-sphere and the Penrose compactification are stated as the maps the catalogue's shots 11 and
16 need. Everything a 1.7.1 document can say renders as before.

## Motivation

- **The plan depends on it.** SREP 12 counts shots 10 (matrix grid), 11 (stereographic projection) and 16
  (Penrose diamond) under MF2, and SP6's Minkowski and Penrose diagrams (line 286) build on it. The SRC-17
  measurement (2026-10-24) scored 11 as a gap and 16 as custom: "the compactification of each grid line is a
  hand-written expression" (probe 16 writes one `parametricPath` per grid line with `Math.atan` in each).
- **The deformer exists; the general map does not.** The reference applies fourteen `modifier` types as point
  maps to a node's flattened outline after a uniform subdivision (`crates/sr-vector/src/deform.rs`,
  `crates/sr-gpu/src/vector.rs:724`): bend, twist, wave, bulge, spherize, corner-pin and the rest are each one
  fixed function. A matrix, an arbitrary expression and a complex function are the three the catalogue asks for
  and none is expressible: `corner-pin` is a homography of the box, not a linear map about a point, and no
  modifier reads an expression.
- **Subdivision must be adaptive and bounded.** A uniform step draws z² of a straight grid line as a visible
  polygon where the map stretches and wastes points where it does not; the step must follow the map. It must
  also stop: a map with a pole, or one that oscillates, would otherwise subdivide forever. The rule below splits
  where the mapped chord error exceeds a tolerance and never more than ten times along a branch, so two engines
  produce the same points and the same count.
- **Right angles are the test.** A conformal map keeps angles, so a grid under z² or exp shows curved lines
  meeting at right angles; that is what 3Blue1Brown's complex-function shots show, and it is the bar SREP 12
  states. The numeric table measures the angle between the images of two directions at a grid node.

### Semantics

The reference module is `conformance/tools/space_transforms_reference.py`; function names in parentheses.

**1. `matrix`** (`matrix_map`). With centre c = (cx, cy), the point p maps to
(cx + a (px − cx) + c (py − cy) + e, cy + b (px − cx) + d (py − cy) + f). The six numbers are SVG's
`matrix(a, b, c, d, e, f)`; the identity is a = d = 1 and the rest 0. The map is exact in 64-bit floats, so a
grid node lands exactly where the arithmetic puts it (the bar of SREP 12).

**2. `map`** (`expr_map`). The point p is read in data units as (u, v) = ((px − cx)/unit, (cy − py)/unit):
+v is up, as the y axis of an `axes` is (the plots SREP, rule 1), so an expression written for the plane reads
as in a textbook. The expressions `x` and `y` give (u', v') and the point maps to (cx + unit u', cy − unit v').
They use the expression language of `expressionType` (xsd:522) with the variables `u` and `v` and every
built-in it lists, in 64-bit floats, evaluated at each frame (SREP 70 rule 1).

**3. `complex`** (`complex_function`, `complex_map`). With z = u + i v in the frame of rule 2, w = f(z) and
(u', v') = (Re w, Im w). The functions, on the principal branch where one is needed:
`square` z², `cube` z³, `inverse` 1/z, `exp` eᶻ, `log` Log z (imaginary part in (−π, π]), `sqrt` the root
with non-negative real part, `sin`, `cos`, `joukowski` z + 1/z, `mobius` (a z + b)/(c z + d). A pole (1/z at
0, the Möbius at z = −d/c) gives a non-finite w; the branch cut of `log` and `sqrt` is the negative real axis,
where the map is discontinuous but finite.

**4. Adaptive subdivision** (`subdivide_segment`, `chord_error`). Let F be the composition of the node's
modifiers in document order, and let the node's outline be flattened to segments as the engine flattens a
path before any modifier (the reference: `Path::flatten`). For each segment (p, q): let m be its midpoint;
the segment is *kept* when F(p), F(q) and F(m) are finite and |F(m) − (F(p) + F(q))/2| ≤ `tolerance`;
otherwise it is split into (p, m) and (m, q), and the rule applies to each, at most 10 times along any branch
(2¹⁰ = 1024 sub-segments of one segment at most). The kept sub-segments' ends, in order from p to q, are the
segment's samples. The split point is always the midpoint, so the samples are p + k (q − p)/2¹⁰ for integers k,
and two engines produce the same points. A segment with a non-finite mapped end or midpoint is split down to
the bound, so the break of rule 5 lands within 2⁻¹⁰ of the segment of the point the map sends to infinity. A
modifier of one of the fourteen existing types takes part in F but does not change this rule.

**5. The mapped path** (`deform_polyline`). The samples of every segment are mapped by F; a mapped point with
a non-finite coordinate is dropped and breaks the path (SREP 70 rule 2): the finite points between two drops
form one subpath each, drawn open. A closed outline maps its closing segment and, when unbroken, stays closed.
The node then draws exactly as it would with that path data, with its fill, stroke, stroke style, trim and
markers; the fill of a broken subpath is the fill of its open polygon, as for a `path` that lacks `Z`.

**6. Conformality** (`tangent_angle`). The maps of rule 3 are conformal away from their poles and branch cuts:
the angle between the images of two directions at a point equals the angle between the directions. An engine
is not asked to compute this; the numeric table checks it by central differences at a node, and a kit case
checks it on a rendered grid (Conformance).

**7. The stereographic projection** (`stereographic`, `inverse_stereographic`). The point (x, y, z, w) of the
unit 3-sphere projected from the pole w = 1 onto the hyperplane w = 0 is (x, y, z)/(1 − w); the pole itself is
non-finite. The inverse of (x, y, z) is (2x, 2y, 2z, r² − 1)/(r² + 1) with r² = x² + y² + z². A circle of the
sphere through the pole projects to a line, and any other circle to a circle. The projection is written in a
document as a `parametricSurface` or `parametricPath` of SREP 70 whose expressions are these formulas; it is
stated here so the catalogue's shot 11 has one definition, not a modifier (the 3D case is out of a 2D deform's
reach; see Open issues).

**8. The Penrose compactification** (`penrose`). With u = t − x and v = t + x, T = (atan v + atan u)/2 and
X = (atan v − atan u)/2. Light rays t = ±x + k map to T ∓ X = const, so they stay at 45°; t → ±∞ and x → ±∞ map
to the edge |T| + |X| = π/2 of the diamond. In a document the diamond is a `map` modifier with
`x="(Math.atan(v + u) - Math.atan(v - u)) / 2"` and `y="(Math.atan(v + u) + Math.atan(v - u)) / 2"` on a group
of grid lines drawn in (x, t) = (u, v); the subdivision of rule 4 puts the samples where the arctangent bends.

**9. Order and composition.** A node's modifiers compose in document order: F = Fₙ ∘ … ∘ F₁. A node's own
`deform` applies before its parent group's (Syntax 2). The box used for `centerX`/`centerY` defaults is the
node's undeformed box; a group's box is the union of its children's undeformed boxes.

**10. Time.** Nothing in this SREP keeps state between frames (SREP 14 rules 1 to 3): the samples and the
mapped points are a function of the document and the frame number, so a seek to any frame equals the frame
played from 0. An animated matrix (shot 10) is six animated numbers; the subdivision follows the map at each
frame, and the sample count may change between frames, which is correct and visible only as the curve bending.

## Rationale

- **Three types on the existing `deform`, not a new element.** The reference already treats every modifier
  as a point map over a subdivided outline; the three new types are three more maps, and they compose with
  bend, twist and the others in the order the author writes.
- **Named complex functions rather than complex expressions.** The expression language is scalar; writing
  Re and Im of exp or a Möbius map by hand is error-prone and hides the function's name from the reader. The
  ten functions cover the catalogue; `map` covers everything else.
- **The frame of the plots SREP.** With +v up and a `unit`, a `map` written for the plane reads as in a
  textbook and composes with an `axes`: the same (u, v) a plot uses.
- **Chord error, not segment length.** A length criterion ignores the map; a chord-error criterion is the
  only one that tracks curvature where the map creates it, and it is what SREP 12's 0.5 px bar is about.
- **A midpoint split with a depth bound.** Splitting at the midpoint makes the sample set a function of the
  segment and the map alone (no engine-chosen parameters), and the bound makes it finite for every map,
  including one with a pole on the segment.
- **Break at infinity, as SREP 70.** 1/z of a line through the origin is two rays; drawing a joining segment
  across the screen is wrong, so the non-finite point breaks the path. The same rule serves poles of the
  Möbius map and the overflow of exp.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A `warp` expression per shape (`x="..." y="..."` on `shape`).** Each shape would carry the map; a grid of
  forty lines would repeat it forty times, and the group-level deform of Syntax 2 does it once.
- **Adaptive subdivision by angle (flatness of the mapped polyline).** An angle criterion is scale-free and
  cannot state a pixel bar; the chord error states it directly.
- **Unbounded subdivision with a minimum segment length.** A length floor depends on the node's scale and on
  the camera; the depth bound is a count and the same at every scale.
- **A `stereographic` modifier.** A 2D modifier cannot read the fourth coordinate; the projection is a
  parametric geometry, and rule 7 fixes its formula instead.
- **Complex expressions in the language (`z * z`).** A complex type in `expressionType` touches every
  built-in; out of scope and not needed for the catalogue.

## Backwards compatibility

- Every 1.7.1 document validates and renders unchanged: the three types are new enumeration values, the
  attributes are new, `group/deform` is new, and the fourteen existing modifiers keep the reference's current
  subdivision.
- A 1.8 document that uses them is refused by a 1.7 validator (unknown value, attribute or element), as V17
  states for the schema's own gate.

## Open issues

- **3D maps.** `object3D` has no `deform`; a `map` of (x, y, z) on a mesh after subdividing its edges by
  rule 4 is the natural extension and is what shot 11's hypersphere needs for an animated projection. This
  draft states the projection as a formula (rule 7) and leaves the 3D modifier to the editor.
- **Fill under a broken path.** Rule 5 fills a broken subpath as an open polygon; for 1/z of a filled disc
  through the origin the right answer is the complement region, which no rule here draws.
- **Branch cuts.** `log` and `sqrt` are discontinuous on the negative real axis; a segment that crosses it is
  split to the bound and then drawn across the jump. A break at the cut, as at a pole, is a candidate rule.
- **Raster content.** The reference deforms a layer's raster through a grid mesh (`deform.rs`, `grid`); the
  three new types apply to that mesh as any modifier does, but rule 4 is stated for outlines only.
