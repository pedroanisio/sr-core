# SREP 0 — Add stylised characters

## Abstract

[SREP 12](srep-0012.md) names, as its CL2 building block, "blob characters with squash-and-stretch rigs and
eyes, in 2D and 3D", with the bar "volume preserved under squash and stretch" (line 264), and its Characters
row records what exists: "2D rigs, skinned glTF; no agent behaviours" (line 46). This SREP is that block. It
states the area rule of the 2D `squash` and `stretch` modifiers that the engine already keeps but the schema
does not say, extends `deform` to `object3D` with a volume rule (the factor `s` along the axis and
`1 / sqrt(s)` across), lets a modifier's axis be the node's velocity so a body stretches into its motion by
a gain, and adds eyes: an `eye` node in 2D and a clay blob `shape="eye"` in 3D, with a pupil that looks at a
target within its travel, a lid, and seeded blinks. A bounce rig is given as the worked application. Every
rule is a function of the document and of `time`, so the seek check of [SREP 14](srep-0014.md) holds.

## Motivation

- **Shots 33 and 34** (SREP 12 lines 621 and 622, Primer's natural-selection and aggression videos) are blob
  creatures running, eating and pairing off, with eyes and a squash when they land. Their behaviour is CL3;
  their bodies are this SREP.
- **What exists.** 2D nodes take `deform` with `modifier type="squash"` and `"stretch"`
  (`schema/scene-render.xsd:2776`); the engine scales by `(1 / s, s)` (`crates/sr-vector/src/deform.rs:86`),
  which keeps the area, but the schema says only "a fraction for squash, stretch" and an engine that scaled one
  axis alone would conform. `object3D` has no `deform` (`schema/scene-render.xsd:4112`); a clay character
  ([SREP 8](srep-0008.md), `blob` of sphere, box, capsule and torus) cannot squash at all. No element is an
  eye; `transformConstraint type="look-at"` turns a whole node (`crates/sr-eval/src/rig.rs:549`), which is a
  head, not a pupil.
- **The bar** is numeric: the area of a squashed shape and the volume of a squashed solid are those of the
  rest shape, to the kit's tolerance.

### Semantics

The reference implementation is `conformance/tools/characters_reference.py`; the names in parentheses are its
functions. `amount` is `a`, the factor `s` is `1 − a` for `squash` (`0 ≤ a < 1`) and `1 + a` for `stretch`
(`0 ≤ a ≤ 100`).

1. **Area in 2D** (`scales_2d`, `deform_points`, `polygon_area`). A `squash` or `stretch` on a 2D node
   scales the node's points about the centre `(centerX, centerY)` by `s` along the axis and `1 / s` across
   it. The determinant is 1: the area of every closed path is kept exactly. This is what the Rust reference
   does today; it is now a rule. With `a = 0` the points are unchanged.
2. **Volume in 3D** (`scales_3d`, `deform_vertices`, `squashed_sdf`, `mesh_volume`). A `squash` or
   `stretch` on an `object3D` scales its local geometry about `(centerX, centerY, centerZ)` (default the
   object's origin) by `s` along the axis and `1 / sqrt(s)` along each other axis. The determinant is 1: the
   volume of every closed mesh is kept, and a clay object's signed distance field is sampled at the
   inverse-mapped point so its blobs keep their volume too. Shading normals are those of the deformed surface
   (the inverse-transpose), not the rest normals.
3. **Contact** (`squash_on_floor`). A body that lands squashes about a centre on the floor
   (`centerY` at the contact line): the points on the floor stay on it and the body widens, as rule 1
   requires. This is a consequence of the centre, not a separate rule.
4. **Velocity** (`velocity`). With `axis="velocity"` the axis is the node's velocity in its parent's frame,
   `v = (p(t) − p(t − dt)) / dt`, where `p` is the node's own animated position (its `x`, `y` after keys,
   expressions and constraints, before this modifier) and `dt` the composition's frame step. At the first frame
   (`t − dt` before the composition's start) `v = 0`. The amount is `min(amount, gain · |v|)`
   (`velocity_stretch`), the angle is `atan2(v_y, v_x)`, and the modifier rotates the points so that angle lies
   on x, scales by rule 1 (or rule 2 in 3D, the axis being `v` itself), and rotates back (`stretch_along`).
   `|v| = 0` leaves the points unchanged. A `squash` with `axis="velocity"` squashes along the motion.
5. **The 2D eye** (`pupil_offset`, `lid_line`, `visible_area`). An `eye` is a disc of `radius` at `(x, y)` in
   its parent's frame, filled with `color`, with a pupil disc of radius `pupil` at the centre plus the offset
   toward the target (`lookAt`'s pivot, or `(lookX, lookY)`, in the same frame): the vector to the target, or
   that vector scaled to `(radius − pupil) · travel` when longer. A lid of the parent's background (the eye is
   simply not painted there) covers the eye from the top down to `y − radius + (1 − open) · 2 · radius`; the
   visible region is the circular segment of height `open · 2 · radius`, whose area is
   `r² acos((r − h) / r) − (r − h) sqrt(2rh − h²)`: all of the disc at `open` 1, half at 0.5, nothing at 0.
6. **The 3D eye** (`pupil_center_3d`). A `blob shape="eye"` is a sphere blob of `radius` in the clay union
   with a pupil: a sphere of radius `pupil` centred `radius − pupil / 2` from the eye's centre along the gaze,
   painted `pupilColor` where it breaks the surface. The gaze is the unit direction to the target (`lookAt`'s
   world position, or `(lookX, lookY, lookZ)` in the object's frame), turned back toward the eye's forward
   axis (the blob's local `+z`) until it lies within `travel · 90°` of it; a target straight behind, with no
   plane to turn in, gives the forward gaze. Without a target the gaze is forward. The lid of rule 5 applies
   as a cap of the eye's colour grown from the top over `(1 − open)` of the diameter.
7. **Blinks** (`blink_start`, `blink_open`, `eye_open`). With `blinkPeriod` `P`, `blinkDuration` `D` and
   `seed` `σ`, blink `k` (`k ≥ 0`) starts at `k · P + U(σ, 0, k) · (P − D)` from the composition's start, with
   `U` the unit draw of D24, so one blink falls somewhere in each period and never overlaps the next. During a
   blink the opening is `|2u / D − 1|` for `u` the time since its start (closed at the middle, triangular), and
   1 otherwise; the eye's opening at a frame is `open` times that. Without `blinkPeriod` or without `seed` the
   eye never blinks. `D < P` is required.
8. **The bounce rig** (`hop`, `bounce_rig`). A hop of `height` `h` and `period` `T` is
   `y = 4 h u (1 − u)`, `u = (t mod T) / T`, with `v_y = 4 h (1 − 2u) / T`. Below `contact · h` the body
   squashes by `squashMax · (1 − y / (contact · h))` about the floor (rule 3); above it, it stretches by
   `min(stretchMax, gain · |v_y|)` along the vertical (rule 4). This is the worked application; a document
   writes it with keys or expressions on `amount` and `y`.
9. **Determinism and bounds** (`seek check`). Rules 4, 7 and 8 are functions of `time` and the frame step
   only; a frame rendered alone equals the frame played from the start (SREP 14 rule 5). `amount` of a squash
   is below 1 (a squash of 1 has no inverse); a stretch is at most 100; `travel` and `open` are in [0, 1].

## Rationale

- **Keep the area and the volume, state it.** Lasseter (1987) names squash and stretch the first principle
  and volume preservation its rule; the engine already keeps the 2D area, so the SREP writes it down and
  carries the same determinant-1 rule into 3D with `1 / sqrt(s)` across.
- **Velocity as an axis, not a new modifier.** Stretch-into-motion is the same scale with a different
  frame; one attribute value and one gain cover it, and the backward difference of the node's own position
  keeps it a function of time.
- **Eyes as elements, not shapes.** An eye needs a pupil that moves without moving the white and a lid that
  clips: three shapes and a mask per eye, repeated. One element with `open`, `lookAt` and `travel` is what
  every blob needs and what the engine can draw in one pass.
- **Seeded blinks from D24.** One draw per period places the blink; the triangular opening is one line and
  needs no easing curve; the eye without a seed is deliberately still.
- **The 3D pupil as a sphere in the clay union** reuses SREP 8's smooth union and SREP 39's finish, so a
  clay creature's eye is clay.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Uniform scale for squash** (`s` along, `s` across): loses volume in proportion to `s²`; fails the bar.
- **A `character` element** with body, eyes and rig in one: every creature differs; the parts compose.
- **Random blinks per frame**: fail SREP 14 rule 1 and differ between a frame alone and the frame played.
- **Spring-based squash** (a damped oscillator on `amount`): stateful; this SREP keeps a closed form and
  leaves springs to a later reactive-values draft (SREP 12 MF4).

## Backwards compatibility

The 2D rule is the engine's existing behaviour, now normative; any engine that kept the area conforms. The
3D `deform`, `axis="z"` and `"velocity"`, `gain`, `centerZ`, `eye` and `blob shape="eye"` are new and gated
by V17. A clay blob of another shape is unchanged.

## Open issues

- **Bone-driven squash.** A 2D `skeleton` bone that scales (`scaleX`, `scaleY`) does not keep area; whether
  a bone's scale should be split by this rule, or a `preserveArea` attribute added, is the editor's call.
- **Eyelids of other shapes.** The lid is a straight horizontal line; a curved lid and lower lids are one
  attribute each.
- **Pupil dilation and eye highlights** are presentation and can follow as attributes.
- **Mouths.** Primer's blobs have none; Kurzgesagt's birds do. A `mouth` element with a few shapes keyed by
  a phoneme or an expression is a separate SREP.
