# SREP 0 — Add computed tracking

## Abstract

A computed form of `trackData`, SREP 12's milestone 7 (LA1): a `provider="tracks"` with one `solve` child
makes the document state how a `point` track, a `planar` track or a `camera` solve is computed from a
`footage` asset, and a resolve step, never rendering, runs it and writes the result into the track cache at
`src` in the engine's JSON track format, pinned by `sha256` (the pattern SREP 9 set for tiles: resolve
fills the cache and pins its digest, rendering only reads). Point tracks are a normalised cross-correlation
search of a template `window` within `search` pixels, refined to sub-pixel precision by Lucas-Kanade's
Gauss-Newton iteration, with a `confidence` and a `lost` state. Planar tracks follow a `grid` of points
inside four `corners` and carry the corners by a homography fitted with the normalised DLT under RANSAC.
Camera solves recover the pose from a planar `target` of known `width` and `height` by Zhang's decomposition
of that homography, or from `survey` points of known position by the DLT for the projection matrix, both
refined by Gauss-Newton on the re-projection error, and write the engine's `[tx, ty, tz, rx, ry, rz, fov]`
row. The reference tests are the ones SREP 12 names: synthetic plates with known motion, error below 0.5 px;
the draft adds the deterministic cache, the seek check of SREP 14 and the validation rules CT1–CT4.

## Motivation

SREP 12 row LA1 asks for "point and planar tracks and camera solves made by resolve providers into pinned
track caches", measured by "synthetic plates with known motion: error below 0.5 px", and makes shots 1 and 6
native with imported tracks only. At 67a661a `trackData` reads a file in one of six formats
(`crates/sr-vector/src/track.rs`), interpolates it and drives constraints, stabilisation and attachments; the
file always comes from another tool. A documentary cut that needs a title to follow a hand, a replacement
plate on a screen or a camera move on a still has to leave the document to get it. This draft keeps the
document the source: it states the solve, the resolve step computes the cache once, and every render of the
document reads the same pinned bytes, so the renders stay reproducible (SREP 14) and offline (SREP 9).

## Specification

### Syntax

A diff for schema 1.8 against `schema/scene-render.xsd` at 1.7.1 (line numbers of that file); the schema
files are not changed by this draft.

- `trackDataType` (6192) gains:
  - `provider` (one value, `tracks`, optional). Without it a `trackData` is an imported file as today. With
    it the element is a computed track: `footage` (6214) becomes required, `src` (6196) names the cache the
    resolve step writes, in the engine's `json` format (`format` must be absent or `json`), and `sha256`
    (6220) is the pin the resolve step fills; `kind` is one of `point`, `planar`, `camera` (`mask` and `face`
    stay imported).
  - `fps` (`positiveDecimal`, optional): the frame rate written into the cache; the default is the footage's
    frame rate.
  - a `solve` child, exactly one when `provider` is present and none otherwise, with `kind` equal to the
    track's `kind` and:
    - common: `window` (`xs:positiveInteger`, odd, 5 to 63, default 15: the template's side in pixels),
      `search` (`xs:positiveInteger`, 1 to 64, default 8: the largest motion between frames in pixels),
      `template` (one of `first`, `previous`, default `first`: whether the template is cut from the first
      frame or follows the track).
    - `point`: `x`, `y` (`xs:double`, pixels of the footage on frame 0, required).
    - `planar`: four `corner` children with `x`, `y` (`xs:double`, required: TL, TR, BR, BL on frame 0, a
      convex quadrilateral), `grid` (`xs:positiveInteger`, 3 to 16, default 4: points per side tracked inside
      the plane).
    - `camera`: `target` (one of `planar`, `survey`, required), `fov` (`xs:double`, horizontal degrees,
      strictly between 0 and 180, required: the camera's field of view, as `cameraType/@fov`). With
      `target="planar"`: the four `corner` children and `grid` of a planar track and `width`, `height`
      (`positiveDecimal`, metres: the target's size). With `target="survey"`: six or more `point` children
      with `x`, `y` (pixels on frame 0) and `X`, `Y`, `Z` (`xs:double`, metres: the point's position).
- Schematron:
  - **CT1** a `trackData` with `provider` has `footage`, `kind` in `point`, `planar`, `camera`, `format`
    absent or `json`, and exactly one `solve` whose `kind` equals its own; a `trackData` without `provider`
    has no `solve`.
  - **CT2** a `point` solve has `x` and `y`; `window` is odd and 5 to 63; `search` is 1 to 64.
  - **CT3** a `planar` solve has four `corner`s that form a convex quadrilateral in order and `grid` 3 to 16.
  - **CT4** a `camera` solve has `target` and `fov` strictly between 0 and 180; a `planar` target has four
    `corner`s, positive `width` and `height`; a `survey` target has at least six `point`s.
  - **V17** `provider`, `fps` and `solve` on `trackData` need `scene/@version="1.8"` or later.

### Semantics

The reference module (`conformance/tools/computed_tracking_reference.py`) names the function of each rule.
Pixels are the footage's, x right and y down, with the centre of pixel (i, j) at (i, j); the camera looks
down +z with x right and y down; metres are the target's or the survey's.

1. **The resolve step.** `resolve(doc, frames)` runs the solve of a computed `trackData` on the frames of its
   `footage` and returns the cache text and its digest; the resolve step writes the text to `src` and sets
   `sha256` to the digest, as SREP 9's resolve step fills `cache` and `cacheSha256` for tiles. The cache is
   the engine's JSON track format, `{"fps", "points", "planes", "cameras"}` with one series named by the
   track's `id`, written by `cache_json`: keys sorted, no spaces, every value rounded to `CACHE_DECIMALS` = 4
   decimals, one line ended by a newline, so that the same document and footage give the same bytes.
   Rendering never runs a tracker: it reads the cache at `src` and checks `sha256` (`verify_cache`; a
   different digest is error A02 as for every pinned asset), and a computed `trackData` whose cache is absent
   is an error of the same class as an unreadable imported file. The frames read from the cache follow the
   engine (`frame_sample`): linear between the two nearest rows, clamped outside them, a plane read as a point
   being its centre.
2. **Point tracks.** `track_point` cuts the template `window` around (`x`, `y`) on frame 0 and, on every later
   frame, finds the integer offset within ±`search` pixels whose window has the highest zero-mean normalised
   cross-correlation with the template (`ncc_search`, `ncc`; Lewis 1995), then refines it by Lucas-Kanade
   (`lucas_kanade`; Lucas and Kanade 1981): Gauss-Newton steps on the translation that minimise the squared
   difference between the template and the bilinearly sampled image, with central-difference gradients, at
   most `MAX_ITERATIONS` = 20 steps or until the step falls below 10⁻⁴ px. The row's `confidence` is the
   correlation at the refined position. The point is `lost` when the best correlation is below
   `LOST_THRESHOLD` = 0.6, when the refined position lies more than 1.5 px from the integer match, or when the
   window would leave the frame; a lost point keeps its last position with `status` `lost` for the rest of the
   track and the cache carries that position. With `template="previous"` the template is cut again at every
   tracked position. On synthetic plates (`synthetic_plate`: value noise warped by known homographies) the
   error against the truth is below 0.5 px: measured 0.038 px for a translation of (1.7, −0.9) px per frame
   over 16 frames and 0.42 px for 1.5° and 1 % of scale per frame over 16 frames.
3. **Planar tracks.** `track_plane` seeds `grid` × `grid` points spread 15 % inside the quadrilateral
   (`grid_points`), tracks each as rule 2 with previous templates, and on every frame fits the homography from
   the seeds to their tracked positions by the normalised direct linear transform (`fit_homography`; Hartley
   and Zisserman 2003, algorithm 4.2) inside RANSAC (`ransac_homography`; Fischler and Bolles 1981): samples of
   four pairs drawn from the document's random stream (SREP 92, channel RNSC, index by frame and draw), the
   inlier threshold `RANSAC_THRESHOLD` = 1 px, at most `RANSAC_ITERATIONS` = 200 samples, the model refitted on
   the inliers of the best sample. The frame's `corners` are the four corners carried by that homography, with
   `inliers` and the root mean square residual `error`. When fewer than four seeds are tracked the corners
   hold their last position with `inliers` 0; on frame 1 that is an error. Measured corner error 0.19 px under
   a perspective drift of 0.4 px per frame over 12 frames.
4. **Camera solves.** `intrinsics(width, height, fov)` is the pinhole matrix with focal length
   (width/2)/tan(`fov`/2) px and the principal point at the frame's centre, `fov` being horizontal as
   `cameraType/@fov`. With `target="planar"` the target's corners are `planar_target_points(width, height)`,
   TL, TR, BR, BL on the plane z = 0 centred at the origin, and the frame's homography from them to the
   tracked corners of rule 3 is decomposed by Zhang (2000, section 3.1): K⁻¹H = λ [r₁ r₂ t], λ from the mean
   norm of the first two columns, r₃ = r₁ × r₂, the nearest rotation by the polar iteration, the sign chosen
   so that t_z is positive (`planar_pose`). With `target="survey"` the six or more `point`s are tracked as rule
   2 and the projection matrix is the direct linear transform on the normalised image points (`pnp_dlt`;
   Hartley and Zisserman 2003, section 7.1); fewer than six tracked points or coplanar points are an error
   (a planar set is the planar target). Both poses are refined by Gauss-Newton on the rotation vector and the
   translation with Levenberg damping (`refine_pose`), minimising the root mean square re-projection error
   (`reprojection_error`) of the target's corners or the survey points. The cache row is
   `[frame, tx, ty, tz, rx, ry, rz, fov]`: t the camera translation, (rx, ry, rz) the degrees of
   R = R_z(rz) R_y(ry) R_x(rx) (`euler_to_rotation`, `rotation_to_euler`), `fov` the solve's. Measured
   re-projection error 0.20 px over 8 frames of a moving and turning camera, and 0.38 px for a survey of 12
   points with 0.4 px of jitter.
5. **Frames as pure functions.** The cache is written once; a frame of the document reads only the cache, so
   frame n rendered alone equals frame n of a run (SREP 14). The seek check `seek_test` resolves the same
   document twice, compares the bytes and reads every tracked frame back from the cache within 10⁻⁴ px.
6. **Validation.** `validate_track_data` applies CT1 and `validate_solve` CT2–CT4; invalid documents raise
   `TrackingError` with the rule's wording.

## Rationale

- **Resolve, not render.** Running a tracker during rendering would make a frame depend on every earlier
  frame of the footage and on the tracker's version; the SREP 9 pattern (resolve fills, pins, rendering
  reads) keeps SREP 14's seek semantics and SREP 9's offline guarantee at the cost of one explicit step.
- **The cache in the engine's own format.** The engine already reads `{"fps", "points", "planes",
  "cameras"}` with linear interpolation; writing that format means no new reader, and a computed track and
  an imported one are the same thing to the renderer.
- **NCC then Lucas-Kanade.** Cross-correlation finds the match within a large motion without a good start;
  Lucas-Kanade needs a start within about a pixel and gives the sub-pixel position. Each alone fails where the
  other works.
- **Four decimals.** 10⁻⁴ px is far below any tracker's precision and keeps the bytes stable across
  platforms' last-bit differences.
- **Zhang for planar, DLT for survey.** A single homography carries exactly the information a planar target
  gives, and Zhang's decomposition is its closed form; a survey needs the full projection matrix, and the DLT
  is its linear solution. Gauss-Newton after either removes the linearisation bias.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A `track` node computed per frame.** Rejected: it violates SREP 14 (frames would not be pure functions of
  the index) and makes every render re-run the tracker.
- **A sidecar document with the tracks.** Rejected: the document would again read a file it cannot
  reproduce; the solve belongs in the document and the cache is derived.
- **Feature-based trackers (KLT corners, ORB).** Rejected for this draft: the document states where to track;
  automatic feature selection is a later provider.
- **Bundle adjustment over all frames.** Rejected: a per-frame solve with a known target or survey is exact to
  the measurement error; a multi-frame adjustment is for the unknown-structure case, out of scope here.

## Backwards compatibility

A 1.7 document is valid and unchanged in meaning: `provider`, `fps` and `solve` are new and optional, and a
`trackData` without `provider` is read as today. Documents using them require version 1.8 (V17). The draft
depends on SREP 9 for the resolve pattern and on SREP 92 for the RANSAC draws.

## Open issues

1. **The camera row's field of view.** `track.rs` reads a camera row's `fov` as vertical degrees while
   `cameraType/@fov` and this draft are horizontal; the editor decides whether the resolve step writes the
   vertical angle 2 atan(tan(fov/2) · height/width) into the cache or the engine's reader changes.
2. **The engine's rotation convention.** The draft fixes R = R_z R_y R_x in degrees; the engine's camera
   track reader must be checked against it before SR-97 lands, and the kit's camera cases pin it.
3. **The RNSC channel.** SREP 92 reserves channel names; "RNSC" is requested here and the number the editor
   assigns replaces `CHANNEL_RANSAC` in the reference.
4. **Masks and faces.** `mask` and `face` stay imported; a computed mask (LA2) is its own draft.
5. **Lens distortion.** The camera solves assume a pinhole; a distortion model on `cameraType` would enter
   `intrinsics` and the re-projection.
6. **Survey points as an asset.** Six or more `point` children keep the document self-contained; a survey of
   hundreds of points would read better from an asset with a `sha256`.
