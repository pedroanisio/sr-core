# SREP 0 — Add scalar 3D: isosurfaces and transfer-function volumes

## Abstract

[SREP 12](srep-0012.md) names, as its MF6 building block, "isosurfaces (marching cubes), transfer-function
volumes through the media model (F3)", with the bar "sphere area error falls as O(h²)" (line 257). This SREP
is that block. An `isosurface` object draws the level set f = `level` of an expression in x, y, z and t, or
of a channel of a volume asset, inside a box cut into a grid: every cell is split into the six tetrahedra on
its main diagonal and each tetrahedron is cut by linear interpolation (marching tetrahedra, the
decomposition of marching cubes that has no ambiguous case), the crossings are welded into one mesh facing
the side where f rises, and the mesh is lit like any mesh with smooth, gradient or flat normals. A
`transfer` child of `medium` maps a scalar channel of the volume through colour and opacity stops, and the
medium renders the opacity as its density and the colour as its emission through SREP 40's model, so Beer's
law and the existing marches hold. Both are functions of the document and the frame, so the seek check of
[SREP 14](srep-0014.md) holds; the mesh and the transfer function are stated as pure functions in
`conformance/tools/scalar3d_reference.py`, and the sphere's measured area error falls with order 1.85 and
2.04 between 6, 12 and 24 cells per axis.

## Motivation

- **Shots 14 and 31** (SREP 12 lines 612 and 622, the Kerr disk's isodensity shells and the plasma sheath)
  need a surface at a value of a 3D field and a volume whose colour and opacity follow a scalar, as every
  scientific-visualization tool draws them ([ParaView, VisIt](srep-0012.md) at line 147 of the plan).
- **What exists.** Volume assets ([SREP 40](srep-0040.md)) render as media through `medium`: density is an
  extinction multiplier, kelvin glows through `blackbody`, and the path tracer marches them
  (`crates/sr-volume/src/medium.rs`, `sample_density`, `integrate`; `crates/sr-gpu/src/volume.wgsl`). No
  element draws a level set: `parametricSurface` and `heightfield` ([SREP 70](srep-0070.md),
  `schema/scene-render.xsd:3532` and `3574`) mesh explicit surfaces, and SREP 70 ruled implicit surfaces out
  of its scope as "a different algorithm with its own resolution and ambiguity rules" (line 201). No
  attribute maps a scalar to a colour: `medium/@albedo` and `@emissionColor` are constants
  (`schema/scene-render.xsd:7744`).
- **The ambiguity rules** are the reason to state the algorithm exactly. Marching cubes' 15-case table has
  faces whose two diagonals are both consistent, and two engines choosing differently produce holes between
  cells. The tetrahedral decomposition has one answer per tetrahedron and neighbouring cells agree on their
  faces by construction, so one mesh follows from one document.
- **The bar** is exactness with a known rate: the area of a sphere's mesh must converge to 4π as O(h²), the
  rate of linear interpolation, which the numeric table measures.

The object's `primitive` is `isosurface`; it takes `material` like a mesh. `volume` names a `volume` asset
(SREP 40) whose `channel` exists (validator error `E1`, as VOL5 for `temperatureGrid`); the box then defaults
to the medium domain of the asset (SREP 40, "derive the finite medium domain"). `transfer/@channel` must
exist on the medium's object's volume asset (`E2`); `transfer` on a medium whose object has no volume asset
(a pyro child) reads the simulation's channel of that name.

### Semantics

The reference implementation is `conformance/tools/scalar3d_reference.py`; the names in parentheses are
its functions. Resolutions `Rx, Ry, Rz` are `xResolution`, `yResolution`, `zResolution` or `resolution`; the
grid corners are `x_i = x0 + i·(x1 − x0)/Rx` and likewise for y and z; the field is `F(x, y, z) = f(x, y, z,
t)` at the frame's time, or the trilinear sample of the asset's channel (rule 7).

1. **The cell** (`CORNERS`, `TETS`, `cell_corners`, `tet_volume`). Cell `(i, j, k)` has corners 0 to 7 in
   Lorensen and Cline's order, 0 at `(x_i, y_j, z_k)` and 6 at `(x_{i+1}, y_{j+1}, z_{k+1})`, and is the six
   tetrahedra `(0,5,1,6) (0,1,2,6) (0,2,3,6) (0,3,7,6) (0,7,4,6) (0,4,5,6)` on its main diagonal (Doi and
   Koide 1991). Every face of the cell is split by the diagonal from its lowest corner to its highest, so the
   two cells on a face hold the same two triangles of it; the six fill the cell.
2. **A crossing** (`edge_point`). A corner is *inside* when `F ≥ level`. On an edge with one inside and one
   outside corner the crossing is `a + t·(b − a)`, `t = (level − F_a)/(F_b − F_a)`, computed from the inside
   corner `a`; an edge whose inside corner is at the level crosses at that corner.
3. **A tetrahedron** (`tet_triangles`, `tet_gradient`, `triangle_normal`). With no or four inside corners
   it holds nothing; with one or three, the triangle of the three crossings on the edges at the odd corner;
   with two, the quadrilateral of the four crossings on the edges between the pairs, as two triangles
   sharing its diagonal from the first crossing. Each triangle is ordered so that its normal has a positive
   dot with the gradient of the linear function through the corners, which is exact on a tetrahedron; a
   triangle of zero area is dropped.
4. **The mesh** (`isosurface`). `F` is sampled once per corner; a non-finite sample is a render error (`E3`,
   as SREP 40 rejects non-finite volumes). Cells are cut in slice order, z outermost, then y, then x, and
   their tetrahedra in rule 1's order; cells with all corners inside or all outside hold nothing. Vertices
   are welded by their point: every tetrahedron on an edge computes the same crossing (rule 2), and a crossing
   on a corner is one vertex however many edges reach it. A surface that lies on a grid plane is held by the
   cells below it alone (their corners on the plane are inside, the cells above are all inside). The result
   is the vertex list in order of first use and the face list of index triples.
5. **Measures** (`mesh_area`, `mesh_volume`, `euler`, `is_closed`, `convergence_order`). The area is the sum
   of the triangles'; the volume `Σ a·(b × c)/6` over faces is the enclosed volume, positive for outward
   faces; a mesh of a closed surface has `V − E + F = 2` and every edge in two faces, once each way. The
   observed order between two resolutions is `log₂(e_h/e_{h/2})`.
6. **Normals** (`vertex_normals`, `gradient_normal`). `smooth` (the default) averages each vertex's face
   normals weighted by area; `gradient` takes `∇F` at the vertex by central differences with step
   `h = min(cell side)/1024`, and is an error where it vanishes (`E4`); `flat` lights each face by its own
   normal. Normals follow rule 3's side, so the material's front faces the rising side; `level` on a signed
   distance (negative inside) therefore faces outward.
7. **Grid functions** (`grid_function`). A volume channel is the trilinear interpolation of its samples at
   their voxel centres in the asset's world space (SREP 40's sampling), clamped to the domain; `F` for an
   `isosurface` of a `volume` is that function, and the grid of rule 1 is the box's own.
8. **A transfer function** (`validate_stops`, `transfer`). Stops are two to 256 `(at, colour, opacity)`
   with strictly rising `at`; for a channel value `s`, colour and opacity are linear between the stops around
   `s` and equal to the first or last stop outside them, in linear light.
9. **The medium under a transfer** (`medium_sample`). At a point with channel value `s`, the medium's
   density is `opacity(s)·densityScale` in place of the density channel, its extinction coefficient that
   density times `extinction`, and it emits `colour(s)·density·emissionScale`; `albedo`, `anisotropy`,
   `scatterBounces` and the rest of SREP 40 and [SREP 79](srep-0079.md) apply unchanged, and `blackbody`
   is an error together with `transfer` (`E5`).
10. **The march** (`transmittance`, `march_transmittance`). Transmittance along a ray is `exp(−∫σ)` (Beer's
    law); a renderer estimates the integral by midpoint steps of at most `stepSize` within `maxSteps`
    (SREP 40), and its estimate converges to the exact value as the step falls. The numeric table shows the
    one-step case exact on a constant medium and the 64-step case within 10⁻⁴ on a quadratic one.
11. **Determinism and bounds.** The mesh at a frame is a function of the document and the frame's time,
    with no state between frames, so the seek check (SREP 14 rule 5) holds for an animated `f` or a volume
    sequence. `resolution` ≤ 1024 per axis and `Rx·Ry·Rz ≤ 2²⁴` (IS4); at most 256 stops; `maxSteps` ≤
    65536.

## Rationale

- **Marching tetrahedra rather than the 256-case table.** The table has ambiguous faces (Lorensen and Cline
  1987; the asymptotic decider is one of several fixes) and engines that resolve them differently leave
  holes between cells. The six-tetrahedra split needs no table, has no ambiguity and keeps neighbours
  consistent by construction, at the cost of about twice the triangles, which rule 11's bounds cover.
- **Inside is `F ≥ level`** so a corner at the level belongs to one side and no edge is ambiguous; a surface
  on a grid plane is then drawn once.
- **Crossings computed from the inside corner** make the welded mesh exact: two tetrahedra never disagree on
  a vertex by rounding.
- **Linear interpolation** bounds the vertex error by O(h²) on a smooth field, which is the area bar.
- **Transfer as opacity and emission** keeps SREP 40's optical model and estimators: the transfer function
  changes what the march samples, not how it marches. Levoy (1988) and Max (1995) are the model.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Dual contouring or surface nets**: sharper features, but they need Hermite data and a QEF solve whose
  rounding differs between engines.
- **Ray-marching the implicit surface** without a mesh: exact silhouettes, but no mesh for the material,
  shadows and the path tracer's BVH, and no area to measure.
- **Marching cubes with the asymptotic decider**: exact for trilinear fields but still a table, and the
  decider's ties are another rule.
- **A transfer function of two channels** (density and temperature): one channel keeps the stops a line;
  `blackbody` already covers the kelvin colour.

## Backwards compatibility

A new object element and a new `medium` child, gated by V17; every existing document renders the same.

## Open issues

- **Adaptive grids** (octree marching) for thin features; one `resolution` per axis is the plan's control.
- **Two isosurfaces of one field** with different levels are two elements today; a `levels` list is an
  easy extension once the cost of re-sampling is measured.
- **Transfer functions on `pyro` channels** without a volume asset (rule 9's last sentence) need the
  simulation's channel names listed in SREP 40's terms.
- **Normals of a volume isosurface**: `gradient` by central differences of a trilinear field is C⁰ across
  voxels; a smoother estimate is a later rule.
