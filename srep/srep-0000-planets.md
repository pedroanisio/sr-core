# SREP 0 — Add planets and the Sun as bodies

## Abstract

A `primitive="planet"` for `object3D`, the body of SREP 12's milestone 6 (SP3): an oblate spheroid of the
IAU radii of its `body` (the time-frames draft's attribute) under `pixelsPerMeter`, dressed with an
equirectangular day texture (`baseColorMap`, the prime meridian at the centre), night lights (`emissiveMap`)
masked to the night side with a twilight band, a drifting cloud shell that casts its shadow on the ground, a
ring strip that shadows the planet and is shadowed by it, the Hestroffer–Magnan limb darkening of the Sun, and
the physical-sky draft's atmosphere as a scattering shell seen from orbit. The limb, the terminator and their
cusps are closed-form circles on the sphere, so a frame's positions are measurable against geometry; spin and
cloud drift are pure functions of the frame's time, so the seek check (SREP 14) holds by construction.

## Motivation

SREP 12 row SP3 asks for "planets and the Sun: textures, clouds, night lights, rings, limb darkening;
atmosphere from ground to orbit (A1)", measured by "terminator and limb positions against geometry". At
67a661a `object3D` has a `sphere` (prim.rs `sphere(r, segs)`: a UV sphere with its poles on y and `u = 0` at
+x) and a `globe` (SREP 10: a sphere wrapped in a `<map>`), a material with `baseColorMap` and `emissiveMap`
(xsd 2511, 2526), and the dome sky's sun disc (sky.rs). None of them knows a body's radius, where the prime
meridian of a texture falls on the mesh, which side is night, or that the Sun's disc is darker at its edge. The
time-frames draft places a body at the ephemeris position and turns it by the IAU rotation elements; this draft
gives the body its surface.

## Specification

### Syntax

A diff for schema 1.8 against `schema/scene-render.xsd` at 1.7.1 (line numbers of that file); the schema
files are not changed by this draft.

- `planet`, a new value of `object3DType/@primitive` (the enumeration at 4136): an oblate spheroid. With
  `body` (the time-frames draft) its equatorial and polar radii are the IAU 2009 values of `RADII` (rule 1)
  in scene units through `pixelsPerMeter` (SREP 4); without `body`, `radius` (4233) is the equatorial radius
  in scene units and `flattening` the oblateness. `segments` (4203) tessellates it as a sphere. The object's
  material (4146) is read as follows: `baseColorMap` is the equirectangular day texture, `emissiveMap` the
  night-lights texture (rule 3), both with longitude −180 at the left edge, the prime meridian at the centre
  and the north pole at the top; `baseColor` and `emissive` multiply them as for any object.
- New attributes of `object3DType` (4112), read with `primitive="planet"` (PL2):
  - `flattening` (`nonNegativeDecimal`, default 0: (a − c) / a; ignored, with a warning, when `body` gives
    the radii), `nightLights` (`xs:boolean`, default true: `emissiveMap` is masked to the night side by rule 3;
    false leaves it an ordinary emissive map), `twilight` (`nonNegativeDecimal`, degrees, default 6: the band
    of the Sun's elevation over which the night lights fade).
  - `cloudMap` (`xs:anyURI`: an equirectangular RGBA texture of a cloud shell), `cloudHeight`
    (`positiveDecimal`, a fraction of the equatorial radius, default 0.004: 25 km on the Earth),
    `cloudRotation` (`xs:double`, degrees of longitude per scene second, eastward, default 0), `cloudShadow`
    (`xs:boolean`, default true: the shell's alpha darkens the ground by rule 4).
  - `ringMap` (`xs:anyURI`: a one-row RGBA strip from the inner to the outer edge), `ringInner` and
    `ringOuter` (`positiveDecimal`, in equatorial radii, defaults 1.239 and 2.269: Saturn's C ring's inner and
    A ring's outer edges), read by rule 5.
  - `limbDarkening` (one of `none`, `solar`; default `none`): `solar` darkens the emissive map toward the limb
    by rule 3's Hestroffer–Magnan law.
  - `atmosphere` (one of `none`, `earth`; default `none`) and `atmosphereHeight` (`positiveDecimal`, metres,
    default 100000): `earth` wraps the body in the physical-sky draft's atmosphere (rule 6).
- Schematron:
  - **PL1** `body` on a `planet` is one of the bodies with IAU radii in this draft: 10, 199, 299, 399, 499,
    599, 699, 799, 899 and 301; any other `body` needs `radius`.
  - **PL2** `flattening`, `nightLights`, `twilight`, `cloudMap`, `cloudHeight`, `cloudRotation`,
    `cloudShadow`, `ringMap`, `ringInner`, `ringOuter`, `limbDarkening`, `atmosphere` and `atmosphereHeight`
    are written only with `primitive="planet"`.
  - **PL3** `flattening` is below 1, `twilight` at most 90, `cloudHeight` at most 1, `ringOuter` above
    `ringInner` and `ringInner` above 1, `atmosphereHeight` positive.
  - **PL4** `cloudHeight`, `cloudRotation` and `cloudShadow` are written only with `cloudMap`; `ringInner` and
    `ringOuter` only with `ringMap`.
  - **PL5** `limbDarkening="solar"` needs a material with `emissiveMap` or `emissive` (the Sun lights itself)
    and is written with `body="10"` or without `body`.
  - **V17** `primitive="planet"` and the attributes above need `scene/@version="1.8"` or later.

### Semantics

The reference module (`conformance/tools/planets_reference.py`) names the function of each rule. Angles are
degrees unless a rule says radians; the body frame has x toward the prime meridian and z toward the north pole,
and the scene's axes follow the time-frames draft (scene (x, y, z) = body (x, −z, y), `scene_from_body`).

1. **The body.** `RADII` holds the IAU 2009 equatorial and polar radii (km) of the Sun, the eight planets and
   the Moon from `pck00010.tpc`; `flattening(body)` is (a − c) / a (Earth 0.003353, Saturn 0.097962) and
   `scene_radius(body, pixelsPerMeter)` the equatorial radius in scene units (the Earth at 10⁻⁶ px/m is
   6.378 units). `surface_point(a, f, lon, lat)` is the spheroid's point at a geodetic longitude and latitude
   and `surface_normal` its outward normal (the gradient of the implicit equation); the geodetic and geocentric
   normals differ by under 0.2° on the Earth. With `body`, the time-frames draft turns the body frame by its
   rotation elements and places it at its ephemeris position; without `body` the object's `rotation*`
   attributes apply. The primitive takes a uniform scale (its `scale.x`): it is never a general ellipsoid.
2. **Textures and the mesh.** `texture_uv(lon, lat)` is the texel of an equirectangular map: u = lon / 360 +
   1/2 wrapped into [0, 1) and v = (90 − lat) / 180, so longitude −180 is the left edge, the prime meridian
   the centre and the north pole the top. The engine's UV sphere (prim.rs: n = (sin πw cos 2πu, −cos πw,
   sin πw sin 2πu), u = 0 at +x, the north pole at w = 0) reads the map at u + 1/2 (`engine_uv`,
   `engine_normal`): a texture's centre lands on the scene's +x at the body's equator and 90° E on the
   scene's +z. The texture is not mirrored: east is to the right when the north pole is up. A surface point's
   texel is read at its geodetic latitude, `geodetic_latitude(p, f)` = atan2(z, (1 − f)² √(x² + y²)) in the
   body frame (the elevation of `surface_normal`, the inverse of `surface_point`; with f = 0 the geocentric
   latitude), which on the Earth exceeds the geocentric latitude by 0.19° at 45° (Open issue 6).
3. **Shading, night lights and limb darkening.** The day side is lit by the scene's lights as any surface
   (`day_weight`: Lambert's cosine of the sun direction). With `nightLights="true"` the emissive map is
   multiplied by `night_weight(normal, sun, twilight)`: 1 when the Sun is `twilight` degrees or more below the
   horizon, 0 when as far above it, 1/2 on the terminator and linear in the Sun's elevation between; the sun
   direction is the scene's physical sun when the dome light has `sky="physical"`, else the first directional
   light, else the Sun's ephemeris direction when a `space` element is present (from the body's ephemeris
   position with `positionFrom="ephemeris"`, else from the space's origin); without any of the three the
   whole surface is night and the map is unmasked (weight 1). The Sun itself (`body="10"`) has no night side:
   `nightLights` is ignored on it. With `twilight="0"` the band is a step: 1 below the horizon, 0 above, 1/2
   on it. With `limbDarkening="solar"` the emission (`emissive`, with or without a map) is multiplied per
   channel by `limb_darkening(mu, alpha)` = μ^α, where μ is the cosine of the angle between the surface normal
   and the line of sight (`disc_mu`; clamped to [0, 1]; under an orthographic camera the line of sight is the
   projection's axis for every pixel) and α is `limb_alpha(nm)`, Hestroffer
   and Magnan's (1998) eq. 5 fit: α = −0.023 + 0.292 / λ for 1 / λ ≤ 2.4 µm⁻¹ and α = −0.507 + 0.441 / λ for
   1 / λ ≥ 2.8 µm⁻¹ (λ in µm, valid from 303 to 1100 nm; the gap between 357 and 417 nm is Open issue 2),
   at 610, 550 and 465 nm for the red, green and blue channels (`CHANNEL_WAVELENGTHS_NM`): the limb is redder
   than the centre. The disc-averaged intensity over the central one is `disc_average(alpha)` = 2 / (α + 2)
   (0.797 at 550 nm), so an author who sets `emissive` to the Sun's mean radiance divides it by this factor.
   A body with `limbDarkening="solar"` has no night mask (PL5 binds the attribute to the Sun or to a body
   without IAU radii).
4. **Clouds.** The cloud shell is the body's spheroid scaled by 1 + `cloudHeight` (semi-axes a (1 + h) and
   c (1 + h): the shell is `cloudHeight` × c over the pole, 25.4 km on the Earth, where a sphere of a (1 + h)
   would float 47 km up), drawn with `cloudMap` as an alpha-blended white diffuse surface (the map's colour
   times its alpha, roughness 1, no specular) lit by the scene's lights, turned eastward by
   `cloud_longitude(seconds, cloudRotation)` degrees (`clouds_uv`). With `cloudShadow="true"` the
   contribution of rule 3's sun (under a physical sky, the sky's sun) to the ground at a lit point p is
   multiplied by 1 − alpha of the cloud texel at `cloud_shadow_point(p, sun, radius, cloudHeight,
   flattening)`: the point where the ray from p toward the Sun leaves the shell, which displaces the shadow
   away from the Sun by about `cloudHeight` × tan(zenith angle) radians (0.13° at a zenith angle of 30° on the
   Earth); on the night side (the geodetic normal facing away from the Sun) there is no shadow. The shell
   casts no other shadow and the term is independent of the light's own shadow casting.
5. **Rings.** The ring is an annulus in the body's equatorial plane from `ringInner` to `ringOuter`
   equatorial radii, both faces drawn, its colour and alpha `ring_sample(strip, rho, inner, outer)`: the
   linear interpolation of `ringMap` across the radius. The ring is an alpha-blended diffuse surface (the
   strip's colour times its alpha, roughness 1, no specular) lit on both faces alike by the scene's lights with
   the cosine `ring_cosine(sun)` = |n · s|, n the plane's normal (the unlit face shows the same strip: Open
   issue 5). A ring point is in the planet's shadow when its ray toward the Sun hits the spheroid
   (`ring_shadowed_by_planet(q, sun, radius, flattening)`): there rule 3's sun (under a physical sky, the
   sky's sun) contributes nothing, and no other light is shadowed by the planet; the shadow is
   the half of the ellipse with semi-axes `planet_shadow_on_rings` = (R √(sin² β + (1 − f)² cos² β) / |sin β|,
   R) on the side away from the Sun, β the Sun's elevation above the ring plane: with flattening f the
   meridian ellipse x² + z² / (1 − f)² = R² has its tangent of slope tan β at that distance from the centre
   on the plane (Saturn at its solstice, β 26.7°: 2.053 radii along the Sun's direction; a sphere of the
   equatorial radius would cast 2.226, and the ring at 2.1 radii is lit, not shadowed). The planet is
   shadowed by the ring where the ray from a surface point toward the Sun crosses the ring plane ahead of it at
   a radius inside the annulus (`ring_crossing`); the light through is 1 − alpha there (`ring_shadow`), a
   factor on rule 3's sun alone, independent of the light's own shadow casting, as rule 4's cloud shadow is.
   Points with the Sun on their own side of the plane are not shadowed.
6. **Atmosphere.** With `atmosphere="earth"` the body carries the physical-sky draft's atmosphere
   (`atmosphere(planet_radius, top_radius)` there, with the body's equatorial radius in metres and
   `atmosphereHeight` above it); its transmittance and in-scattering apply to every ray toward or past the
   body, so the shell is seen from orbit as a band between the planet's limb and the top of the atmosphere:
   `shell_band(distance, radius, height)` gives both angles from the nadir (from 400 km above the Earth,
   70.19° and 72.87°), and the physical-sky draft's `limb_direction` agrees with the first. The shell's sun
   is rule 3's sun and its irradiance the light's colour times its intensity in the scene's linear units (as
   that draft's `effective_sun_irradiance` scales `sunIrradiance` by `sunIntensity`; the kit's white light
   at intensity 1 is the reference's default `sun_irradiance`): the air scatters the light that shades the
   ground. With only the ephemeris direction (no directional light) the irradiance is zero and the shell has
   transmittance alone. A document composes one atmosphere: under a dome with `sky="physical"` the planet's
   shell is not drawn and the engine reports it. From inside the atmosphere the shell has no outer edge and
   the sky of that draft is what is seen.
7. **Limb, terminator and phase.** From an eye at distance d the limb is the circle where the sphere's
   tangent lines through the eye touch it: angular radius asin(R / d), centre R² / d along the eye direction,
   radius R √(1 − (R / d)²) (`limb`, `limb_points`). The terminator is the great circle normal to the sun
   direction (`terminator_points`), or for a point Sun at a finite distance the small circle of half-angle
   `terminator_half_angle` = 90° − asin(R / d☉) about it; a surface point is lit when its normal faces the Sun
   (`lit`). The cusps are where the two circles meet (`cusps`), two points symmetric about the plane of the
   eye and the Sun, absent when the Sun is within the limb's angular radius of the line of sight or its
   opposite. `cusps(eye, sun, radius, weight, cone)` generalises both circles: the terminator to the
   isophote of Lambert weight `weight` (`isophote_points`, the small circle acos(weight) from the subsolar
   point: a kit reads the 0.1 isophote, where a frame's shading is still above its noise) and the limb to the
   cone of half-angle `cone` degrees about the line of sight (a circle inside the limb, clear of the edge's
   pixels); it refuses a cone outside the limb. `pixel_surface_point(cam, eye, radius, x, y)` is the surface
   point seen at a pixel offset from the frame's centre (the first hit of the pixel's ray; `disc_mu` reads
   it), and `limb_pixel_extents(cam, eye, a, c)` the spheroid's silhouette half-extents from an eye in the
   equatorial plane: F a / √(d² − a²) across and F c / √(d² − a²) along the pole, the Earth from 4 radii
   143.108 × 142.629 px. The phase angle is the Sun–body–eye angle (`phase_angle`) and the lit fraction of the disc
   (1 + cos i) / 2 (`illuminated_fraction`); `lit_fraction` samples it through the pinhole camera of
   `camera`, `project` and `limb_pixel_radius`, and agrees to 0.2 % from 200 radii. Spin and cloud drift are
   `frame_state(frame, fps, rate, cloudRotation, body)`, pure functions of the frame index, the frame rate and
   the clock's rate (`spin_degrees(body, seconds)` is the IAU prime-meridian rate: one sidereal day,
   86164.0962 s, is one turn of the Earth); a frame rendered alone equals the frame reached by playing
   (`seek_test`: 600 frames at 60 scene seconds per real second).

## Rationale

- **Why a new primitive and not `globe`.** SREP 10's `globe` draws a `<map>` projected over a sphere of
  `radius`; a body has physical radii, an oblateness, a night side, clouds and rings, and its textures are
  photographic. Keeping the two apart leaves SREP 10's map rendering untouched.
- **Why the radii come from `body`.** The time-frames draft already names bodies by NAIF id for position and
  rotation; one attribute gives position, orientation and size from the same source (`pck00010.tpc`), and the
  texture's prime meridian is the IAU prime meridian that draft turns.
- **Why the prime meridian is the centre of the texture.** It is the convention of the common equirectangular
  maps (the Blue Marble, the Black Marble, the Mars and lunar global mosaics), so authors use them as
  distributed; the engine's `u = 0` at +x is a half turn away, which rule 2 states once.
- **Why a twilight band for night lights.** A hard cut at the terminator flickers as the terminator sweeps
  texels; 6° is the civil twilight after which lights are on, and the band is linear so that it is a one-line
  rule to measure.
- **Why Hestroffer and Magnan.** Their fit is a one-parameter power law in μ per wavelength with a stated
  accuracy (±0.02 in α) over the visible and near infrared, whose disc average has a closed form; a
  five-coefficient polynomial per channel would be fitted from data this draft does not ship.
- **Why ring shadows are ray tests.** The planet's shadow on the rings and the ring's shadow on the planet are
  each one ray against a sphere or a plane; the half-ellipse is their closed form, so a frame's shadow edge is
  measurable against geometry as SP3 asks.
- **Why the atmosphere is the physical-sky draft's.** One model from ground to orbit (SREP 12's A1) keeps the
  colours of the sky at the ground and of the limb from orbit from the same coefficients.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A `sphere` with a planet material.** The radii, the night mask, the cloud shell and the rings are
  properties of the object, not of the surface; a material cannot carry the ring geometry.
- **Procedural planets** (noise terrain and generated clouds): nothing to measure against, and the photographic
  maps exist for every body in `RADII`.
- **Full limb darkening per material.** Only the Sun needs it here; gas giants' limb darkening is the
  atmosphere's job and the subject of Open issue 4.
- **Rings as a mesh the author supplies.** The shadow geometry needs the annulus' plane and edges; the two
  radii and a strip give them exactly.

## Backwards compatibility

Every document at 1.7.1 keeps its meaning: `planet` is a new primitive value and the attributes are new. Both
are refused below version 1.8 (V17).

### Kit cases

The cases are described here and added to `conformance/srep_cases/` once the SREP has a number.
`conformance/tools/planets_kit.py` writes them (as `srep-0000-…`, `--write DIR`) with their synthetic textures
(`assets/planet-marks.png`, a 1024 × 512 map with a 4 × 4 mark per channel; `planet-white.png`;
`planet-cloud.png`, 4096 × 2048 with one opaque texel; `planet-strip.png`, `STRIP` quantised to 8 bits,
`QSTRIP`) and renders and measures an engine's frames of each (`--engine BIN`, `--only CASE`). A case that
compares two bodies, phases or views has a companion document named with a suffix: `planet-limb-saturn`,
`planet-phase-60`, `planet-phase-90` and `planet-phase-120`, `planet-sun-centre`, `planet-rings-lit`,
`planet-rings-ground`. Every
document is 640 × 360 over a black background with `segments="256"` (the schema's most: the mesh's sag is
7.5 × 10⁻⁵ of the radius); the planet stands at the scene's origin, the camera is placed in the body frame
(x toward the prime meridian, z toward the pole, km, scaled by `pixelsPerMeter`) and aimed by yaw and pitch as
the physical-sky kit aims its cameras, so the reference's `camera` and `project` give the expected pixel of a
surface point. Lit cases use a Lambert material (`metallic="0"`, `roughness="1"`, `specular="0"`: the engine's
specular weight is `specular` mixed to 1 by `metallic`) under one directional light of intensity 1 whose +z is
the scene direction away from the Sun; emissive cases set `emissive="#FFFFFFFF"`, which the engine multiplies
by the map; the Sun is seen through an orthographic camera (`orthoHeight` for a disc radius of 1500 px). The
terminator, phase, night and cloud cases set `radius` without `body`, a sphere on which the shading is exact;
the limb, texture, rings, atmosphere and seek cases set `body`, the spheroid. Status: run on a release build of
scene-render feat/sr-67-fields e107893 (0.8.0, llvmpipe; SR-89's schema half, spheroid mesh, night mask, limb
darkening, the orthographic line of sight fixed, the cloud shell and its shadow, the ring and its shadows, a
material's diffuse keeping the light its specular layer does not reflect, the atmosphere shell in the raster
pass): all ten cases pass (the sun case failed on 2fc3024, where μ was taken toward the camera's position under
the orthographic camera; the rings case's first protocol misread 6e4723d by 1.1 px and 2.1 % of its own doing,
and its lit companion then read the Fresnel share a `specular="0"` material lost before ca2c8d0).

## Open issues

1. **The textures' terms.** NASA's Blue Marble and Black Marble and the USGS planetary mosaics are US-government
   public domain; textures from other sources carry their own terms, and no texture ships with this draft. The
   kit cases use synthetic test textures.
2. **The limb-darkening fit's gaps.** Hestroffer and Magnan's two branches leave 357 to 417 nm uncovered and
   stop at 303 nm; the blue channel's 465 nm is inside the long branch, but a spectral renderer would need the
   gap closed from their Table 2.
3. **The Sun's colour.** Rule 3 scales an emissive map; the Sun's spectrum, its 5,778 K colour (the star-fields
   draft's `colour(temperature(0.65))`) and its radiance in the scene's units are the author's `emissive`.
4. **Gas giants and Venus.** Their limb darkening and their clouds' own shading are atmospheric; this draft
   has only the Earth's atmosphere (`atmosphere="earth"`) and flat-lit cloud maps.
5. **Rings from the unlit side.** Rule 5 gives the unlit face the same strip; the forward scattering that
   brightens Saturn's rings seen against the Sun is not modelled.
6. **Oblate bodies and geodetic textures.** Rule 2 reads textures by geodetic latitude on the spheroid; a map
   drawn in geocentric latitude would be displaced by up to 0.19° on the Earth.
