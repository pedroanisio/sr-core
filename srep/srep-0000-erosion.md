# SREP 0 — Add erosion processes to the terrain grid

## Abstract

SREP 12's T1: `erode` children of the terrain-grid draft's `terrain`, each of one `kind`. **`fluvial`** is the
linear stream power law ∂h/∂t = U − K Aᵐ S (n = 1) solved in closed form along the D8 flow paths of the generated
surface with a frozen drainage area: a cell's **travel time** τ is the sum of dx / (K Aᵐ) down its path to base
level, and its height at the geological time T is the initial surface at the point of the path with travel time
τ − T plus U T, or the steady profile h_base + U τ once T ≥ τ (the **characteristic** solution of Royden and
Perron 2013, the analytical erosion of Tzathas et al. 2024). T = `time` + `timeScale` · t, so any time is reached
without stepping and every frame renders alone (SREP 14). **`thermal`** is the talus relaxation of Musgrave, Kolb
and Mace (1989) on one solid layer: a cell steeper than `talus` moves `rate` times half its largest excess to its
lower neighbours in proportion to their excess, **mass** conserved exactly. **`hydraulic`** is the **virtual
pipes** model of Mei, Decaudin and Hsu (2007) on the `water`, `sediment`, `flow` and `loose` layers: `rain`
(varied by `rainVariation` through the random-streams draft's stream at pack(i, j, 0)), pipe fluxes bounded by the
**Torricelli** discharge of the cell's column, the outflow scaling, the water update, the velocity, a `capacity`
of Kc sin(tilt) |v| with the `minTilt` floor and Šťava's shallow-water limiter at `maxDepth`, erosion of the top
solid layer (`loose` first, then `bedrock` scaled by 1 − `hardness`) at `erodeRate`, deposition into `loose` at
`depositRate`, the sediment carried by the water fluxes (so the ground plus the suspended sediment is conserved),
`evaporation`, an `open` or `closed` `boundary` and `pipeArea`. The stepped processes replay from the frame's start
state at `step`, never more than `maxSteps` steps. Rules ER1–ER5 and V17; the schema files are unchanged, the diff
is inside the draft. Reference module `conformance/tools/erosion_reference.py`; engine card SR-104.

## Motivation

- **SREP 12 names the deltas.** Row T1 of the plan: "layered erosion on the terrain grid — fluvial (stream power),
  thermal (talus) and hydraulic (virtual pipes) processes over the F2 layers, deterministic and bounded, so the
  2D droplet erosion of today has a layered successor". This draft is that row.
- **The engine's erosion is a baked 2D droplet model.** `erosionType` (`schema/scene-render.xsd:5220`,
  `crates/sr-sim/src/erosion.rs`) is the particle method of Beyer (2015): droplets walk a single height field once
  at parse time and the result is a picture. It has no layers, no water or sediment state, no notion of time, and
  nothing a `snow`, `water` or `wind` process can read. The terrain-grid draft (SR-100) gives the grid its layers;
  this draft gives the layers their processes.
- **Every frame renders alone.** SREP 14 forbids a frame from depending on the frames before it. A stream-power
  solver stepped through geological time cannot meet that at a geological scale; the closed form along flow paths
  can, at any T, in one pass. The stepped processes (thermal, hydraulic) meet it by replay from the frame's start
  state with a hard bound, as the other 1.8 drafts do.
- **Practice.** Houdini's heightfield Erode nodes and Gaea's Erosion nodes are the layered, stepped tools artists
  use: debris and sediment layers, flow and water outputs, a rain field. The attributes here map onto theirs.
- **Conformance needs a reference, not a look.** Three properties are testable to the last digit: the ground
  plus the suspended sediment is conserved by thermal and closed hydraulic steps; after convergence no neighbour
  slope exceeds the talus angle; at steady state S Aᵐ = U / K on every cell with a receiver.

Each attribute is read only by the kinds listed; the schema allows all of them on every `erode` and the rules
below check the ones the kind reads.

**Schematron** (`scene-render.sch`):

- **ER1** `kind` is `fluvial`, `thermal` or `hydraulic`.
- **ER2** A hydraulic `erode` has `rain`, `evaporation` and `capacity` ≥ 0, `rainVariation`, `erodeRate`,
  `depositRate` and `minTilt` in [0, 1], `pipeArea`, `maxDepth` and `step` > 0, `boundary` one of `open` and
  `closed`, and `maxSteps` a positive integer.
- **ER3** A thermal `erode` has `talus` in (0, 90), `rate` in [0, 1], `step` > 0, and `layer` naming a solid layer
  (`bedrock`, `loose`, `lava`, `ice`, `snow:<type>`) the terrain declares.
- **ER4** A fluvial `erode` has `uplift`, `time` and `timeScale` ≥ 0, `erodibility` > 0, `m` in [0, 1], and `layer`
  naming a solid layer the terrain declares.
- **ER5** A terrain with a hydraulic `erode` declares `loose`, `water` and `sediment` layers; all the thermal and
  hydraulic `erode` of one terrain share one `step`.
- **V17** (extended) `erode` requires `scene/@version` 1.8 or later.

### Semantics

The grid, the layers, the surface h (the sum of the solid layers), the D8 neighbourhood, the flow accumulation and
the element seed e = E(project, id, seed, "terrain") are the terrain-grid draft's. dx is `metersPerCell`; the cell
(i, j) has i to the right and j down; lengths and layer values are metres, fluvial time is years, stepped time is
seconds of scene time. The reference is `conformance/tools/erosion_reference.py`; function names in parentheses.

1. **Order at a frame.** The layers at scene time t are: the generated layers (terrain-grid draft rules 1–5);
   then each fluvial `erode`, in document order, at its geological time; then round(t / `step`) steps, each step
   running the thermal and hydraulic `erode` in document order, from the state the fluvial pass left. The stepped
   state is never carried from one frame to the next (SREP 14). (`run`)
2. **Routing.** Each cell's receiver is its steepest strictly lower neighbour among the eight, the drop divided by
   the neighbour's distance (1 or √2 cells); ties take the first in the D8 order (right, left, down, up, then the
   diagonals); a cell with no lower neighbour — a pit or an outlet — has none and is a base-level cell. The
   distance to the receiver is in metres; the drainage area A is the terrain-grid draft's flow accumulation times
   dx², cells drain to their receiver only (O'Callaghan and Mark 1984). (`routing`)
3. **Travel time.** τ(cell) = τ(receiver) + distance / (K Aᵐ), and 0 at a base-level cell: the time a change at
   base level needs to propagate up the path to the cell under the linear stream power law. (`travel_time`)
4. **The characteristic solution.** With U = `uplift`, K = `erodibility`, m = `m`, n = 1 and the drainage area
   frozen at the generated surface's routing, ∂h/∂t = U − K Aᵐ S along a flow path is a linear advection of the
   profile toward the divide at the celerity K Aᵐ (Royden and Perron 2013; Tzathas et al. 2024). At geological
   time T a cell with travel time τ has h(T) = h₀(p) + U T where p is the point of its path with travel time
   τ − T, h₀ interpolated linearly in τ between the two cells around p, while T < τ; once T ≥ τ the cell is on the
   steady profile h_base + U τ, whose slope satisfies S Aᵐ = U / K. A base-level cell keeps its height (τ = 0). At
   T = 0 the surface is h₀ exactly. (`fluvial_surface`)
5. **Geological time and the layer.** T = `time` + `timeScale` · t. The named solid layer changes by h(T) − h₀;
   the other layers are untouched. (`geological_time`, `fluvial`)
6. **Thermal step.** For the named solid layer with tan θ = tan(`talus`): a cell's excess over a neighbour is
   its drop minus tan θ times the neighbour's distance in metres; a cell with positive excesses moves
   min(`rate` · (largest excess) / 2, what the layer holds) out, split among those neighbours in proportion to
   their excess; all cells compute from the same surface and the deltas apply at once; the grid's edges are
   closed. Nothing crosses to a layer of another name. Mass is conserved exactly; a step that moves nothing
   returns the layers unchanged (Musgrave, Kolb and Mace 1989). (`thermal_step`, `max_neighbour_slope`)
7. **Hydraulic step.** On the ground b (the sum of the solid layers), water d, sediment s, the pipe fluxes f to
   the four edge neighbours (left, right, up, down; the state starts at zero fluxes) and the step Δt = `step`,
   following Mei, Decaudin and Hsu (2007) with the three bounds named below:
   1. *Rain.* d₁ = d + r, r the cell's rain depth of the step (rule 8).
   2. *Fluxes.* For each neighbour, Δh = b + d₁ − (b' + d₁'); the new flux is max(0, f + Δt · `pipeArea` · g ·
      Δh / dx), g = 9.81 m/s², and never more than the **Torricelli** discharge d₁ · dx · √(2 g Δh) of the cell's
      water column leaving at the free-fall speed of its head (zero when Δh ≤ 0). If the four outflows would move
      more than the cell holds, they are scaled by K = min(1, d₁ dx² / (Δt Σ f)), and the scaled fluxes are stored.
      (`torricelli`)
   3. *Water.* d₂ = d₁ + Δt (inflows − outflows) / dx², the inflows being the neighbours' fluxes toward the cell.
   4. *Velocity.* v = (W_x, W_y) / (dx · d̄), d̄ = (d₁ + d₂) / 2, with W_x = (f_in,left − f_out,left + f_out,right −
      f_in,right) / 2 and W_y likewise; v = 0 where d̄ < 10⁻⁹ m.
   5. *Capacity and exchange.* tilt = atan |∇b| (the terrain-grid draft's gradient); the capacity C = `capacity` ·
      max(sin tilt, `minTilt`) · |v| · l(d̄), l(d) = min(1, d / `maxDepth`) the shallow-water limiter of Šťava et
      al. (2008). If s < C the column loses `erodeRate` · (C − s): from `loose` first, then from `bedrock` scaled by
      (1 − `hardness`) where a `hardness` layer exists; the amount removed joins s. If s ≥ C, `depositRate` ·
      (s − C) leaves s and joins `loose`. (`capacity`, `depth_factor`, `erode_cell`)
   6. *Transport.* Each outflow carries the fraction f Δt / (d₁ dx²) of the cell's sediment to its neighbour (the
      scaling of step 2 keeps the fractions' sum at most one); the sediment crossing an open edge leaves. The
      ground plus the suspended sediment is therefore conserved on a closed grid.
   7. *Evaporation.* d₃ = d₂ · max(0, 1 − `evaporation` Δt).
   8. *Flow layer.* Where the terrain has a `flow` layer it is set to v d̄ (m²/s, two channels).
   Water, sediment and `loose` never fall below zero. (`hydraulic_step`, `initial_state`)
8. **Rain.** The rain depth of step n on cell (i, j) is `rain` · Δt · (1 + `rainVariation` · (2 S(e, p, n,
   pack(i, j, 0)) − 1)), with S and pack the random-streams draft's stream draw and index packing, e the terrain's
   element seed and p = 2³¹ + k for the k-th `erode` child of the terrain (document order, from 0). The purposes
   from 2³¹ are clear of the terrain-grid draft's generator channels, which lie below 2²⁶. With `rainVariation` 0
   no draw is made. (`rain_depth`)
9. **Boundary.** `closed`: no flux crosses the grid's edge. `open`: the cell outside the edge has the same ground
   height and no water, so water and sediment leave down any head. The thermal step's edges are always closed.
10. **Steps and the bound.** The number of steps at scene time t is min(round(t / `step`), `maxSteps`); the
    terrain's thermal and hydraulic children share one step (ER5) and interleave step by step in document order;
    `step` ≤ 0 is refused. An engine that cannot replay the steps of a frame within its budget reports it (SREP 14)
    rather than carrying state. (`steps_for`)
11. **Digest.** The layers' digest is the SHA-256 of the JSON of the layers with sorted keys, no spaces, pairs
    as two-element lists and every value rounded to three decimals (a millimetre). (`digest`)
12. **Validity.** ER1–ER5 and V17 hold or the document is refused; the reference lists the codes. An `erode`
    whose named layer the terrain does not declare is refused at parse (ER3, ER4), never silently skipped.
    (`validate_document`, `bad_document`)

## Rationale

- **The closed form, not a stepped solver, for fluvial.** Braun and Willett (2013) solve the stream power law
  implicitly in O(n) per step; it is the right tool for an interactive sculpting session, and the wrong one for a
  renderer that must produce frame 400 without frames 1–399. The characteristic solution evaluates any T in one
  pass over the paths. Its price is the frozen drainage (the network does not reorganise as the surface changes),
  stated as an open issue; Tzathas et al. (2024) take the same trade for the same reason.
- **n = 1.** The linear law is the only one with a closed form along paths that needs no iteration; m = 0.5 with
  n = 1 is the classic choice (Whipple and Tucker 1999). The exponent n is an open issue.
- **Three bounds on Mei's pipes.** The pipe model with the water column as a film on a steep slope reaches the
  numerical hop speed dx / Δt (50 m/s at the defaults), which is not a water speed; a frictionless model has no
  mechanism to stop it. The Torricelli discharge bounds a pipe by the physics it lacks (the energy of the head); the
  outflow scaling is Mei's own; Šťava's l(d) keeps a film from carrying metres of sediment. With the three, the
  example bowl fills symmetrically and its sediment stays of the order of its water (numeric table); without them
  the reference's first run eroded fifteen metres of bedrock under a millimetre of rain.
- **Sediment moves with the fluxes, not semi-Lagrangian.** Mei advects the sediment field along v with a
  back-trace; that interpolation neither conserves mass nor is reproducible across engines to the digit. Carrying
  the sediment with the same fluxes that carry the water is conservative and bit-for-bit reproducible.
- **Erosion into `loose` and `hardness` on `bedrock`.** The two-layer column (debris over rock) is what Houdini's
  Erode and Gaea expose; `hardness` is the terrain-grid draft's layer in [0, 1] and scales the bedrock's loss only.
- **Purposes from 2³¹.** The rain's stream purposes must not alias the generator's lattice channels at step 0,
  where S(e, p, 0, i) = U(e, p, i).

## Rejected alternatives

- **Making the 2D droplet `erosion` the layered process.** Beyer's droplets are sequential and order-dependent;
  the same scene on two thread counts differs. They stay as the 1.7 element, unchanged.
- **Carrying the stepped state across frames.** Forbidden by SREP 14; the replay with `maxSteps` is the bound
  every stepped draft of 1.8 uses.
- **A friction or Manning term on the pipes.** Physically truer than the Torricelli bound, and one more parameter
  (a roughness) artists would have to learn; the bound needs none. Kept as an open issue.
- **A separate `erosion` element referencing the terrain.** The processes read and write the terrain's layers
  and nothing else; as children they are validated against the layers they name (ER3–ER5) without cross-references.
- **Depression filling before routing.** Priority-flood (Barnes, Lehman and Mulla 2014) would route through
  pits; it also changes the generated surface before the first frame. Pits are base level here; an open issue.

## Backwards compatibility

A 1.7 document has no `erode`; the 1.7 `erosion` element and `erosionType` are untouched. A 1.8 `terrain`
without `erode` behaves as in the terrain-grid draft. The three processes are additive; no attribute, default or
rule of an existing element changes.

## Open issues

1. **Frozen drainage.** The fluvial routing is the generated surface's; a river that captures another's basin
   under uplift is not represented. Re-routing at intervals of T would need the surface at those intervals and
   breaks the one-pass form.
2. **The exponent n.** n ≠ 1 has no closed form along paths; Royden and Perron give series solutions. Whether the
   draft gains `n` with an iterative reference is open.
3. **Pits as base level.** A generated pit holds its height and the cells draining into it steepen under uplift.
   Depression filling (priority-flood) or lakes (water level in pits) are candidates.
4. **Friction.** The Torricelli bound stands in for the friction the pipe model lacks; a Manning roughness would
   give realistic film speeds on gentle slopes too.
5. **Hydraulic reproducibility across engines.** The pipes are stable with the three bounds, but the state after
   thousands of steps differs between float orders; the kit cases' millimetre tolerance covers the reference's
   step counts and must be re-measured for longer runs.
6. **Deposition into standing water, and into `snow` or `ice`.** The sediment deposits into `loose` wherever it
   settles; a lake bed and a snow pack are not distinguished.
7. **Several solid layers under water.** Erosion takes `loose` then `bedrock`; `lava`, `ice` and `snow` are not
   eroded by water in this draft.
8. **GPU order.** The thermal and hydraulic steps are synchronous by construction so a parallel engine matches;
   the fluvial interpolation along paths is sequential per path and parallel across paths.
9. **Other engines' generators.** A second engine implementing the terrain grid has to reproduce the terrain-grid
   draft's noise to feed these processes; until then the kit cases bind the Rust engine only.
