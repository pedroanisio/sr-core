# SREP 0 — Add particle liquids and granular media in 3D

## Abstract

[SREP 12](srep-0012.md) names, as its S3 building block, "APIC/FLIP (Jiang et al. 2015) with exact transfers;
MLS-MPM for granular snow and sand (Hu et al. 2018); whitewater (Ihmsen et al. 2012); surface meshing", with
the bars "Martin and Moyce dam-break front within 10 %; volume drift below 1 %" (line 296), and records that
the format has no liquid: "the `ocean` is a height field, not a liquid solver" (line 186). This SREP adds a
`liquid3D` node to `object3D`: a closed box of cells filled from `fill` children with particles on a lattice
and stepped by APIC, FLIP or MLS-MPM on a MAC grid with quadratic B-spline transfers; a projection over the
cells holding particles with a density correction; a particle separation; `material` children for elastic,
snow and sand bodies; a `whitewater` child that turns the trapped-air, wave-crest and kinetic-energy potentials
into spray, bubbles and foam; and a surface, the Zhu–Bridson level set meshed by the marching tetrahedra of
the scalar 3D draft. `conformance/tools/liquids_reference.py` restates the rules in the standard library and
measures the bars: a layer at rest stays at rest within 10⁻¹¹ with no volume or density drift; the dam break
reaches the three measured front positions within 6 % of Martin and Moyce's times with the bulk density within
1 % at the end and the particle count exact; a sand column collapses to a pile whose flank rises with the
friction angle and stays below its tangent; the level set of a ball of particles sits within 0.04 h of its
radius; and a run is a function of the step count.

## Motivation

- **Shot 35** (SREP 12 line 623), particles pouring into a box and sloshing, coloured by speed, is a gap: "no
  particle liquid (S3): particles3D fall and bounce off the planes but carry no pressure, and nothing colours
  them by speed". A `liquid3D` with `surface="particles"` makes it native.
- **The peers** (SREP 12 line 186): Houdini FLIP, Blender Mantaflow and Unreal Niagara Fluids all have a
  particle liquid; the plan asks for parity plus deterministic transfers and the dam-break curve as the
  acceptance test.
- **Granular media** are the same machinery with a constitutive model: MLS-MPM moves snow and sand on the grid
  the liquid uses, so one node and one cache serve both.
- **The engine today** has the shallow-water `ocean` (`crates/sr-sim/src/ocean.rs:1-5`), whose whitewater
  tracers "do not exchange mass or momentum with the shallow-water solver" (`ocean/whitewater.rs:2`), and a
  `Water` plane that absorbs falling particles (`particles3d.rs:119-129`); neither is a volume of liquid.

### Semantics

The rules state the solver in d = 2 or 3 dimensions: the document's liquid is three-dimensional, and the dam
break of rule 6 is a two-dimensional slice of it (Martin and Moyce's channel is uniform along the third axis),
so the reference measures it in two. `liquids_reference.py` states every rule as a function. Positions are in
scene units; cell (i, j, k) spans [i h, (i + 1) h) along x and so on; the solver's y points up from the floor
and is the document's −y.

1. **The kernel** (`bspline`, `stencil`, `weights`). Transfers use the quadratic B-spline N(x) = 3/4 − x² for
   |x| < 1/2, (3/2 − |x|)² / 2 for 1/2 ≤ |x| < 3/2, else 0; a sample at x on nodes at the integers takes the
   three nodes ⌊x − 1/2⌋ + k, k = 0, 1, 2. The weights sum to 1, their first moment about x is 0 and their
   second is 1/4, so the APIC inertia tensor is D = h² / 4 · I at every position (Jiang et al. 2015 §5.3).
2. **The liquid** (`spec`, `liquid`, `material`). The grid has `cells` cells of size h in a closed box; the
   component a of the velocity sits on the faces normal to axis a, at integer multiples of h along a and at
   cell centres along the other axes, in arrays with two ghost layers (`PAD`) on every side so the stencil of a
   particle at a wall has its nodes. Each `fill` box seeds particles on a lattice of k = round(perCell^(1/d))
   per axis in every cell it covers (those inside the box), at rest, with mass ρ h^d / k^d and the affine
   velocity C = 0; with a `material`, the deformation gradient F = I and the plastic volume Jp = 1. The
   particle count is bounded by `maxParticles`; a box outside the domain, a grid over 2²¹ cells or `mpm`
   without a material is an error.
3. **The transfers** (`p2g`, `g2p`, `interpolate`). Particles to faces: each face of axis a holds the
   mass-weighted mean of v_a + C_a · (x_f − x_p) over the particles in reach (C_a the row a of C), with the
   stress term of rule 7 for a material. Faces to particles, from the new faces: the PIC velocity v = Σ w u,
   and for APIC the row a of C = (4 / h²) Σ w u_a (x_f − x_p) over the faces of axis a; for FLIP, v = r (v_p +
   Σ w (u_new − u_old)) + (1 − r) Σ w u_new with r = `flipRatio` and C = 0. The particle then moves by dt × its
   PIC velocity and is clamped to the box. The transfers are exact for affine fields: a rotation field v = A x
   returns v and C = A at every particle whose stencil lies in the fluid, within 10⁻¹², and a uniform field is
   returned unchanged, so mass and momentum are conserved by the pair (the sum of the face momenta equals the
   particle momentum).
4. **Walls and the projection** (`enforce_walls`, `mark`, `divergence`, `project`, `extrapolate`). Faces at
   and beyond a wall normal to it are zero. `walls="slip"` mirrors the interior face across the wall onto the
   ghost faces tangential to it; `walls="noslip"` holds every face within one cell of the wall at rest (the
   sticky boundary of Stomakhin et al. 2013). The fluid cells are those holding a particle. The projection
   solves −∇² q = target − div u over the fluid cells (q = dt p / ρ) by Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate
   gradients from q = 0, with reductions in cell order: a wall is a Neumann face, an empty cell is at q = 0.
   Iteration stops when dt × the RMS residual is at most `pressureTolerance`; reaching `pressureIterations`
   first is an error, as is a recomputed error above 1.01 × the tolerance. The faces of fluid cells then lose
   (q_hi − q_lo) / h with q = 0 in empty cells. The faces not touching a fluid cell take, two layers deep, the
   mean of their valid axis neighbours (one Jacobi sweep per layer), so a particle near the free surface reads
   projected velocities and not the air's unprojected gravity. **The bar:** the kit's expanding 4 × 4 block
   reaches an error of 10⁻⁹ in 3 iterations with every cell's divergence below 10⁻⁷.
5. **The step** (`apply_gravity`, `density_target`, `separate`, `substep`, `step`, `run`). A substep of dt /
   `substeps` is: particles to faces; gravity (faces with mass take −g dt along y); walls; for a liquid, the
   projection with the density target, the extrapolation and the walls again; faces to particles; for a liquid,
   the separation. The density target is `densityCorrection` × (n − k^d) / k^d / dt in every interior fluid
   cell (its axis neighbours inside the domain all hold particles) with n particles, positive or negative, and
   0 in the free-surface cells, so the bulk density returns to the seeded one while the surface is not pulled
   in. The separation moves each particle by half the sum, over the particles within `separation` lattice
   spacings, of the shortfall along the pair direction, computed from the positions before the move (one Jacobi
   sweep; Ando, Thürey and Wojtan 2012). The step count rises by one; the state after n steps is a function
   of n and the document alone: `run(12)` equals `run(6)` continued by `run(6)`, so a frame rendered alone
   equals the frame played (the seek check of [SREP 14](srep-0014.md) rule 1). **The bar:** a layer at rest
   under gravity stays at rest, every particle within 3 × 10⁻¹² of its seat after 60 steps with no volume or
   density drift.
6. **The dam break** (`dam_break`, `nondimensional_time`, `front_position`, `crossing_time`, `MARTIN_MOYCE`).
   The kit's acceptance test is Martin and Moyce's n² = 2 column: a column a wide and 2a high at the left wall
   of a channel 4.5a long and 2.5a high, released at rest with slip walls, 8 cells per a and 4 particles per
   cell, dt = 1/120 s. With T = t √(2g / a) and Z = x_front / a (the farthest particle), the front crosses
   Z = 2.11, 3.00 and 3.89 at T = 1.73, 2.36 and 2.95 against the measured 1.83, 2.51 and 3.11: within 6 %
   (the bar is 10 %), with FLIP at 3.5 %. The inviscid front leads the measurement more as the grid is refined
   (1.8 % at 6 cells per a, 8.8 % at 10): the experiment has a gate and a wet bed. The particle count is
   exact over the run and the bulk density (rule 5's interior cells) is within 1 % at its end; the volume
   counted from the particles per cell undercounts a spread-out liquid by its partly filled surface cells
   (−5 % at T = 3.4), which is what the surface of rule 9 is for.
7. **MLS-MPM** (`svd`, `polar`, `corotated_stress`, `hencky_kirchhoff`, `drucker_prager`, `plasticity`,
   `momentum`, `sand_collapse`, `repose_test`). With a material the step has no projection: the face momentum
   takes, per particle, the term −dt (4 / h²) V₀ P Fᵀ (x_f − x_p) with V₀ = h^d / k^d (Hu et al. 2018), and
   after the transfer F ← (I + dt C) F followed by the material's plastic update. `elastic` is the fixed
   corotated model P = 2μ (F − R) + λ (J − 1) J F⁻ᵀ with R from the polar decomposition; `snow` the same with
   μ, λ scaled by e^(hardening (1 − Jp)) and the singular values of F clamped to [1 − compression, 1 + stretch],
   the clamped volume moving into Jp (Stomakhin et al. 2013); `sand` the Hencky elasticity τ = U (2μ ε + λ tr ε)
   Uᵀ on ε = log Σ with the Drucker–Prager return mapping of Klár et al. 2016: tr ε > 0 returns to the origin,
   δγ = |dev ε| + α (dλ + 2μ) / (2μ) tr ε ≤ 0 stays, else ε loses δγ along dev ε, with α = √(2/3) · 2 sin φ /
   (3 − sin φ). The SVD is the Jacobi eigen-decomposition of FᵀF with det V > 0 and the sign of det F on the
   last singular value. **The bars:** a translating elastic block conserves its momentum over 10 steps within
   10⁻⁹ and keeps F = I; a block at rest stays exactly in place; a sand column 1 × 1 on a no-slip floor
   collapses to a pile whose flank (its angle of repose) is below tan φ and rises with φ (15°, 30°, 45°).
8. **Whitewater** (`trapped_air`, `wave_crest`, `kinetic`, `clamp_potential`, `emission_count`,
   `diffuse_position`, `classify`, `emit`). Per liquid particle and step, with W(r) = 1 − r / h over the
   neighbours within h: the trapped-air potential Σ |v_ij| (1 − v̂_ij · x̂_ij) W, the wave-crest potential
   Σ (1 − n̂_i · n̂_j) W over the neighbours below the tangent plane when v̂_i · n̂_i ≥ 0.6 (else 0), the
   kinetic energy m |v|² / 2, each clamped by Φ(I) = (min(I, max) − min(I, min)) / (max − min) (Ihmsen et al.
   2012). The particle emits ⌊(Φ_ta rate_ta + Φ_wc rate_wc) Φ_k dt + carry⌋ diffuse particles, the fraction
   carried to the next step, so the count is a function of the potentials alone; the j-th diffuse particle of
   the liquid draws its position on the cylinder of `radius` around the particle's velocity from the D24 hash
   H(seed, c, j), c = 0, 1, 2 (the random-streams draft), and takes the particle's velocity and the
   `lifetime`. Its class is spray with fewer than 6 liquid particles within h, foam with more than 20, else a
   bubble. The normals are the unit vectors from the kernel-weighted neighbour centroid to the particle. The
   total is bounded by the whitewater's `maxParticles`. **The bar:** the kit's two crashing blocks emit 300
   diffuse particles of all three classes, the same on a second call.
9. **The surface** (`isotropic_kernel`, `level_set_at`, `surface_mesh`, `surface_test`). φ(p) = |p − x̄| − r
   with x̄ the average of the particle centres within h weighted by k(s) = max(0, 1 − s²)³, s = |p − x_i| / h,
   h = 2 lattice spacings and r = `surfaceRadius` lattice spacings; φ = h where no particle is within reach
   (Zhu and Bridson 2005). `surface="mesh"` cuts φ = 0 by the marching tetrahedra of the scalar 3D draft on
   `surfaceResolution`; `surface="particles"` draws the particles as the `particles3D` sprites coloured by
   speed. **The bar:** a ball of particles of radius 1 at spacing 0.25 gives a closed mesh of Euler
   characteristic 2 whose mean vertex radius is within 0.04 h of 1 and whose volume is within 10 % of the
   ball's.
10. **Bounds.** 2²⁰ particles, 2²¹ cells, 65536 steps per run, 10000 conjugate-gradient iterations, 64 substeps,
    64 particles per cell; every loop is owned by the document and the solver, none by convergence.

## Rationale

- **APIC by default.** Its transfers are exact for affine fields and conserve angular momentum, so a vortex in
  a box neither dissipates as PIC nor rings as FLIP; FLIP stays as the peers' default for authors who want its
  look, with the same grid and projection.
- **A MAC grid with per-component affine rows.** Each component's C row is built from the faces of its own
  axis, so the pyro solver's staggered projection (SREP 40) is reused unchanged and the transfers still satisfy
  the moment identities of rule 1.
- **A density correction on interior cells only.** Particle clustering is the source of volume drift in FLIP;
  a divergence target proportional to the excess returns the bulk to its seeded density within a few steps,
  and leaving the free-surface cells alone keeps the surface where the particles are. A correction on every
  cell inflates the liquid (the surface cells are always under-filled); a one-sided one inflates it too.
- **A sticky no-slip wall.** Mirroring with the opposite sign makes a velocity gradient across the wall that
  the MPM deformation picks up; holding the band of one cell at rest is what granular solvers do.
- **Whitewater by potentials, deterministic by carry and hash.** Ihmsen's rates are expectations of a random
  draw; the carried fraction and the hashed positions give the same particles at every seek.
- **Zhu–Bridson for the surface.** One pass over the particles, a closed form, and the scalar 3D mesher
  already in the plan; anisotropic kernels (Yu and Turk 2013) are a later refinement.

## Rejected alternatives

- **SPH.** No grid, but its pressure needs stiffness tuning or an implicit solve (DFSPH) and its neighbour
  search is the cost; the plan names APIC/FLIP.
- **A level-set liquid without particles.** Volume loss at thin sheets is what particles fix; the level set is
  rebuilt from the particles per frame instead.
- **Collocated nodes for the liquid.** MLS-MPM's node grid is natural for solids, but the liquid's projection
  needs the staggered divergence; the solids share the faces instead.
- **Adaptive time steps by CFL.** A step that depends on the velocity makes the frame depend on the history
  of the run; `substeps` is fixed by the document (SREP 14 rule 1).
- **Reseeding.** Adding and removing particles changes the count the seek check keys on; the separation and
  the density correction keep the count fixed.

## Backwards compatibility

A new node at schema 1.8 (V17); documents at 1.7.1 are unchanged, and `ocean`, `particles3D` and the `Water`
plane keep their meaning. The particle IDs and the whitewater's hash follow the random-streams draft, so a
document's liquid is the same in every engine that implements this SREP.

## Open issues

1. **The Martin and Moyce values.** The three (Z, T) pairs are a secondary transcription of Table 2 (the
   paper, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A* 244, was not accessible when drafting); the editor should check them
   against it before the kit case is numbered.
2. **The bar's resolution.** The inviscid front leads the measurement as the grid is refined; the bar holds
   up to 10 cells per a. A no-slip bed or a viscosity term would bring a finer run back; whether the kit fixes
   the resolution or the bed is the editor's call.
3. **Moving colliders.** The box is static; rigid bodies of SREP 11 inside the liquid (two-way coupling) need
   the collider faces in the projection.
4. **Anisotropic surfaces.** Zhu–Bridson gives a bumpy sheet at low particle counts; Yu and Turk's kernels
   smooth it.
5. **Two-dimensional reference, three-dimensional node.** The reference runs the dam break and the pile as
   slices; a 3D run of either is within the module's reach but slow in pure Python.
