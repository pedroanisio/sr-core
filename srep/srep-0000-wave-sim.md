# SREP 0 — Add wave simulations

## Abstract

Three simulated 2D nodes for the wave explainers, SREP 12's milestone 6 (OW4): a `rippleTank` that steps the
scalar wave equation on a grid by finite differences in time and space (FDTD) with a document-stated Courant
number checked against the CFL bound, `source`s that are points or lines, continuous or pulsed, `wall`s with
`slit`s, `speedRegion`s of a different wave speed and a `boundary` that is `absorbing` (a `sponge` of damped
cells), `reflecting` or `periodic`; a `string` with `fixed` or `free` ends that is plucked, struck or driven and
damped, whose nodes sit at nλ/2 and whose modes are n c/2L; and a `chladniPlate` whose nodal lines are the
closed form sin(mπx/a) sin(nπy/b) when `simplySupported` and, when `free`, the modes of Ritz's method with the
free-free beam functions, as Gander and Wanner (2012) retrace Ritz's 1909 paper, with `sand` walking to the
nodal lines. Every frame is a pure function of the frame index (SREP 14), and the `hits` of shot 15 are drawn
from the pattern's intensity with the document's random stream (SREP 92). The reference tests are the ones SREP
12 names: standing-wave nodes at nλ/2 and CFL-stable steps; the draft adds the measured pulse speed, the
conserved discrete energy, the plate's three rigid modes and the twist mode's single-term bound 18(1 − ν).

## Motivation

SREP 12 row OW4 asks for a "2D ripple tank (finite differences with absorbing boundaries), strings, Chladni
plates from a plate eigen-solver (Ritz; Gander and Wanner 2012)", measured by "standing-wave nodes at nλ/2;
CFL-stable steps", and makes shot 15 (the double slit, with OW3) native: probe 15 finds no wave passing the
slits and no sampled hits building the pattern dot by dot. scene-render has no time-stepped field of any kind
at 67a661a: a ripple tank, a vibrating string or a Chladni figure is a painted texture or an imported video. An
explainer that shows why a wave bends round a slit, why a string held at a third sounds the twelfth, or why
sand on a bowed plate gathers on lines, needs the field to be computed from stated parameters so that the
caption's numbers (the Courant number, the mode frequency, the eigenvalue) are facts the kit can measure.

### Semantics

The reference module (`conformance/tools/wave_sim_reference.py`) names the function of each rule. Grid
quantities are in cells, the string in metres along x, the free plate on the square [−1, 1]²; the rules hold
at every `stepsPerFrame` and are checked by the module's tests.

1. **The CFL condition.** The tank's time step is `cfl_dt(dx, c, courant)` = courant · dx/c with c the
   fastest speed on the grid, the Courant number being c dt/dx, and the scheme is stable when
   `courant_stable(courant, 2)`, courant ≤ 1/√2 (`COURANT_LIMIT_2D`; Courant, Friedrichs and Lewy 1928;
   Strikwerda ch. 2); the string's step is the same with the 1D limit 1 (`COURANT_LIMIT_1D`). A document
   above the limit is refused (WS1, WS2; `validate_tank`, `validate_string`); the reference shows that a step
   1.5 times the one of courant 0.5 grows the field past 10³ in 120 steps.
2. **The ripple tank.** The field u on `nx` × `ny` cells advances by the leapfrog `tank_step`:
   u⁺ = 2u − u⁻ + (c dt/dx)² `laplacian`(u), the five-point Laplacian under the boundary rule
   (`absorbing`: zero outside the grid; `reflecting`: the outside copies the edge cell, a hard wall;
   `periodic`: wrap). A `source` adds `source_value`(t) = A sin(2πft + φ) to its cell, or to every cell of its
   column for a `line`, and a `pulse` stops after `cycles` periods; `wall` cells (`add_wall`) stay at 0 every
   step; `speedRegion`s set c per cell (`set_speed`), and dt follows the fastest cell. In an `absorbing`
   tank the `sponge` multiplies both time levels of a cell d cells from the edge by
   `sponge_factor(d, width)` = e^(−0.1 (1 − d/width)²) per step (Israeli and Orszag 1981), which is the damped
   wave equation u_tt + 2γu_t = c²∇²u with γ rising quadratically into the sponge. The discrete `energy`
   ½Σ((u − u⁻)/(c dt))² − ½Σ u · laplacian(u⁻)/dx² is conserved by the reflecting and periodic schemes to
   round-off; the reference measures a pulse travelling at 0.994 c on the grid and at 0.496 c in a half-speed
   region, a 16-cell sponge keeping 0.25 % of a pulse's energy, the field behind a slit above five times the
   field behind the wall, and two point sources d = 3λ apart making fringes with the central maximum on the
   axis and the first order near L λ/d. `run` averages the field's square over the closing quarter of the
   steps, cut to whole periods of the slowest continuous source, for the RMS maps (`rms_region`,
   `rms_column`) that captions and `hits` read.
3. **Frames and seeking.** The tank at frame k is `frame_state(spec, k)`: `stepsPerFrame` · k steps from rest
   with the document's sources, a pure function of k (SREP 14); `seek_test` renders frame 7 alone and as one
   frame after frame 6 and compares the fields bit for bit. The string and the plate seek the same way: the
   string by replaying its steps, the plate by evaluating its standing wave w(x, y) cos(2πf t) at the frame's
   time. The field is drawn through the wave-optics draft's `display` (sign as phase 0 or π, value as
   amplitude).
4. **Hits.** With a `hits` child the tank draws `count` dots per frame from the RMS profile of `column`:
   `sample_hits(profile, count, seed, first)` inverts the profile's cumulative sum at `hit_unit(seed, k)`
   = U(seed, HITS, k), the k-th draw of the document's stream on channel `CHANNEL_HIT` ("HITS", requested of
   SREP 92), so that frame k shows hits 0 to count · k − 1 and seeking to it draws the same dots as playing
   to it; the reference's 4000 draws from a 1 : 2 : 1 profile land 1003, 2008 and 989 in its cells.
5. **Strings.** A `string` of n `segments` (n + 1 points) advances by `string_step`:
   u⁺(1 + g) = 2u − (1 − g)u⁻ + (c dt/dx)² (u_{i−1} + u_{i+1} − 2u_i) with g = damping · dt, the near end at 0
   or at the `drive`'s A sin(2πft) and the far end at 0 (`fixed`) or with a mirrored neighbour (`free`). A
   `pluck` starts from a triangle at rest, a `strike` from rest with a cosine-squared bump of velocity, a
   `mode` from sin(nπx/L) at rest (`set_shape`); the start uses u⁻ = u − dt v + ½(c dt/dx)² lap u. The
   closed forms are `mode_frequency(n, L, c, ends)` = n c/2L for fixed ends and (2n − 1) c/4L with a free end,
   `mode_wavelength` = 2L/n, `node_positions` at k L/n (nλ/2 apart; `showNodes` marks them) and the standing
   wave `standing_wave` = A sin(nπx/L) cos(2πf_n t); the reference's 200-segment string started in mode 2
   keeps its midpoint below 10⁻⁶ and oscillates at 0.99997 of n c/2L, its discrete `string_energy` dx[½Σv² +
   ½(c/dx)² Σ Δu Δu⁻] is conserved to round-off without damping and decays as e^(−2 damping t) with it (0.670
   after t = 2 at damping 0.1 against 0.670).
6. **Plates.** A `simplySupported` a × b plate's (m, n) mode is `plate_mode_ss` = sin(mπx/a) sin(nπy/b) at
   `plate_frequency_ss` = (π/2) √(D/ρh) ((m/a)² + (n/b)²) with nodal lines `nodal_lines_ss` at k a/m and
   k b/n (Leissa ch. 4). A `free` square plate uses Ritz's method (Ritz 1909; Gander and Wanner 2012):
   `beam_functions(basis)` are the orthonormal free-free beam functions on [−1, 1], the constant, x, and
   cosh kx/cosh k + cos kx/cos k for the even roots of tan k + tanh k = 0 interleaved with
   sinh kx/sinh k + sin kx/sin k for the odd roots of tan k − tanh k = 0 (`beam_roots`: 2.36502, 3.92660,
   5.49780, 7.06858, …); `ritz_plate(basis, poisson)` assembles the stiffness matrix of the products
   u_i(x) u_j(y), A ⊗ I + I ⊗ A + ν(Cᵀ ⊗ C + C ⊗ Cᵀ) + 2(1 − ν) B ⊗ B with A = ⟨u_i″, u_k″⟩, B = ⟨u_i′, u_k′⟩,
   C = ⟨u_i, u_k″⟩, splits it by parity in x and y and, for equal parities, by symmetry under the swap of x
   and y, and diagonalises each block by Jacobi rotations (`jacobi_eigen`), so that every mode has a definite
   symmetry (`mode_symmetry`, `first_mode_with_symmetry`) even where eigenvalues repeat. The spectrum has
   three zero eigenvalues (translation and two rotations); the first flexible mode is the twist, odd in x and
   y, whose Ritz value 12.58 with 4 functions and 12.49 with 6 lies below its single-term bound 18(1 − ν)
   = 13.95 and whose nodal lines are the medians; the first even–even mode antisymmetric in the swap is the
   saddle, vanishing on the diagonals. `plate_mode_free(res, mode)` is the mode as a function, `nodal_points`
   finds its zeros by sign change on a grid, and `chladni_sand` walks each grain down |w| by a Newton step
   capped at `stepSize` until it rests on a nodal line (Chladni 1787; the reference's 100 grains settle
   below 5 % of the mode's amplitude).
7. **Validation.** `validate_tank` and `validate_source` are WS1 (and WS4 for `stepsPerFrame`),
   `validate_string` WS2 and `validate_plate` WS3.

## Rationale

- **The Courant number in the document.** A tank that silently picks a step is a tank whose caption cannot
  quote it; a stated `courant` with the CFL bound enforced by validation makes stability a declared fact and
  instability a refused document rather than a blown-up frame.
- **A sponge, not a PML.** The quadratic sponge is a dozen lines, reflects little at the wavelengths the
  explainers draw, and is what Falstad's and PhET's tanks use; a perfectly matched layer is a follow-up
  (open issue 1).
- **Leapfrog with the conserved energy.** The second-order leapfrog is the simplest scheme whose discrete
  energy is exactly conserved, so the reference can test conservation to round-off instead of to a
  tolerance; the strings use the same scheme for the same reason.
- **Ritz with beam functions, as Ritz did.** The free-free beam functions satisfy the free-edge conditions
  one axis at a time and give Ritz's own figures with six functions per axis; a finite-element plate would
  need a mesh in the document. Splitting by symmetry makes every mode's figure well defined where the square's
  spectrum repeats.
- **Hits from the stream.** Shot 15's dots must build the same pattern at every seek; drawing them from the
  document's stream (SREP 92) on a named channel is what makes frame k alone equal frame k played.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Deep-water dispersion in the ripple tank.** Water waves are dispersive; the tank solves the
  non-dispersive scalar wave equation that the explainers' ripple tanks solve, and a dispersive tank is an
  open issue (2).
- **A 3D membrane or a drum.** The plate eigen-solver covers the square; a membrane with Bessel modes is a
  small addition once the plate has a number (open issue 3).
- **Audio from the string.** The string's modes could sound; sound is outside the schema.
- **Implicit time stepping.** Unconditionally stable schemes damp and disperse the waves the explainers want
  to show; the explicit scheme with a checked step is the honest choice.

## Backwards compatibility

Additive. Documents without the three nodes validate and render unchanged; the nodes and their attributes
require version 1.8 (V17). The draft depends on the wave-optics draft for `display` and on SREP 92 for the
hits' stream channel.

## Open issues

1. **A perfectly matched layer.** The sponge reflects long wavelengths (a 3-cell pulse against a 16-cell
   sponge leaves 5 % of its amplitude); a PML would absorb them. Whether the explainers need it is for the
   editor.
2. **Dispersive water waves.** The deep-water relation ω² = g k is what a real ripple tank obeys; the scalar
   wave equation is what the explainers' tanks draw.
3. **Other plate shapes.** Circular and rectangular free plates need other basis functions or a mesh; the
   square is what Chladni's and Ritz's figures show.
4. **Ritz's published values.** Ritz's 1909 eigenvalues (12.43 for the twist) were computed with his own
   roundings; the draft's 12.49 with six functions per axis is a Ritz upper bound that falls with the basis,
   and the kit's tolerance on the caption should allow for it until the editor fixes a basis.
5. **The HITS channel.** SREP 92 reserves channel names; "HITS" is requested here and the number the editor
   assigns replaces `CHANNEL_HIT` in the reference.
6. **Grid units.** Sources, walls and regions are placed in cells, which keeps the document integral; metres
   through `dx` would read better in captions and is a choice for the editor.
