# SREP 0 — Add shallow-water flow to the terrain grid

## Abstract

SREP 12's T2: one `shallowWater` child of the terrain-grid draft's `terrain` integrates the two-dimensional
shallow-water equations on the terrain's own grid. The bed is the sum of the solid layers, the `water` layer is
the depth and the `flow` layer the discharge, so floods, rivers and the runoff of a storm run over the same
layers the erosion, snow and vegetation drafts read. The scheme is the **central-upwind** flux of Kurganov and
Petrova (2007) on the **hydrostatic reconstruction** of Audusse et al. (2004), **well-balanced** (a lake at rest
with dry islands is kept exactly) and **positivity** preserving at Courant numbers up to one half, in first
order (`order` 1: piecewise constant, forward Euler) or second order (`order` 2: minmod reconstruction and Heun).
Manning friction (`manning`) is semi-implicit; `rain` and `source` children add depth; a fixed `step` is
subdivided into the fewest equal substeps that satisfy `cfl`, so frame t is the state after round(t / `step`)
steps from the start (SREP 14), never more than `maxSteps`; `dryTolerance` names a dry cell and `boundary` is
`open` or `closed`. The reference holds Ritter's dry dam break, Thacker's planar oscillation in a parabolic bowl
and the SWASHES subcritical flow over a bump (Delestre et al. 2013), implemented from the papers; the SWASHES
code is CeCILL and is not used. Rules SW1–SW5 and V17; the schema files are unchanged, the diff is inside the
draft. Reference module `conformance/tools/surface_water_reference.py`; engine card SR-105.

## Motivation

- **SREP 12 names the delta.** Row T2: "the shallow-water `ocean` of SREP 40 (first- or second-order,
  bathymetry, colliders) moved onto the terrain grid; shallow-water equations, central-upwind and well-balanced
  (Kurganov and Petrova 2007; hydrostatic reconstruction, Audusse et al. 2004); rain and source terms", with the
  reference tests "Ritter dam break; lake at rest; Thacker bowl; the SWASHES analytic solutions". This draft is
  that row.
- **The engine's water lives on its own grid.** SREP 40's `ocean` (`crates/sr-sim/src/ocean/`) is a shallow-water
  solver with its own cell size, its own bathymetry asset and its own boundary; nothing of it reads or writes a
  terrain layer. A flood over the F2 terrain, a river fed by a source on a slope, the runoff that the erosion
  draft's hydraulic process approximates with pipes, all want the real equations on the terrain's cells.
- **Well-balanced or useless.** A still lake over an uneven bed must stay still to the last digit, including
  where the bed breaks the surface; a naive discretisation of the bed slope sends spurious currents around every
  island (Audusse et al. 2004 state the problem and the cure).
- **Every frame renders alone.** SREP 14: the state at frame t is replayed from the start with a fixed step and
  a hard bound, as the other stepped drafts of 1.8 do; an adaptive time step would make the sequence of frames
  depend on which frames were asked for, so the step is fixed and the Courant condition is met by subdividing it.
- **Conformance needs analytic solutions, not pictures.** The dam break, the oscillating bowl and the steady
  bump have closed forms; the errors of the scheme against them at a stated resolution are the test.

## Specification

### Syntax

the editor applies the diff on acceptance.

**`shallowWater`**, a new child of `terrain` (the terrain-grid draft), at most one, after `material` and the
`erode` children of the erosion draft:

| Attribute | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `order` | `1` \| `2` | `1` | the scheme's order (SW1); 1 matches SREP 40's default |
| `manning` | `nonNegativeDecimal` | 0.03 | Manning's n, s m^(−1/3) (SW1) |
| `cfl` | `xs:double` in (0, 1] | 0.5 | the Courant number the substeps keep under (SW1) |
| `rain` | `nonNegativeDecimal` | 0 | metres of water per second on every cell (SW1) |
| `dryTolerance` | `positiveDecimal` | 1e-6 | the depth under which a cell is dry and carries no momentum (SW1) |
| `step` | `positiveDecimal` | 0.02 | the step, seconds of scene time (SW1) |
| `boundary` | `open` \| `closed` | `closed` | whether water leaves across the grid's edge (SW5) |
| `maxSteps` | `xs:positiveInteger` | 100000 | the most steps replayed for one frame (SW1) |

**`source`**, any number, children of `shallowWater`: `x`, `y` (`xs:double`, metres from the terrain's top-left
corner, inside the grid), `radius` (`positiveDecimal`, metres), `rate` (`nonNegativeDecimal`, metres of water
per second over the disk), `start` (`nonNegativeDecimal`, default 0) and `end` (`xs:double`, optional, after
`start`), seconds of scene time. SW3.

**Schematron** (`scene-render.sch`):

- **SW1** `order` is 1 or 2; `manning` and `rain` ≥ 0; `cfl` in (0, 1]; `dryTolerance` and `step` > 0;
  `maxSteps` a positive integer.
- **SW2** A `terrain` has at most one `shallowWater`, and none when it has a hydraulic `erode` (the erosion
  draft): one process owns the `water` layer.
- **SW3** Every `source` has `radius` > 0, `rate` ≥ 0, `x` and `y` inside the grid, `start` ≥ 0 and, when
  present, `end` > `start`.
- **SW4** The terrain declares a `water` layer; a `flow` layer, when declared, receives the discharge.
- **SW5** `boundary` is `open` or `closed`.
- **V17** (extended) `shallowWater` requires `scene/@version` 1.8 or later.

### Semantics

The grid, the layers and the surface are the terrain-grid draft's; dx is `metersPerCell`, the cell (i, j) has
i to the right and j down, lengths are metres and time is seconds of scene time; g = 9.81 m/s². The state per
cell is the bed b (the sum of the solid layers), the depth h, the discharge (hu, hv) in m²/s and the surface
w = h + b. The reference is `conformance/tools/surface_water_reference.py`; function names in parentheses.

1. **State and order at a frame.** The generated layers (terrain-grid draft rules 1–5) give b; the `water`
   layer's generated value is h at t = 0 and the `flow` layer, when present, (hu, hv) at t = 0, else zero. The
   layers at scene time t are those after round(t / `step`) steps from that start, never more than `maxSteps`
   (SREP 14); the `water` layer receives h and the `flow` layer (hu, hv). The erosion draft's fluvial and thermal
   processes run before the first step, as that draft's rule 1 orders. (`initial_state`, `run`, `steps_for`)
2. **Substeps.** A step is divided into n = ⌈`step` · s / (`cfl` · dx)⌉ equal substeps, at least one, where s is
   the largest |u| + √(g h) or |v| + √(g h) over the wet cells at the step's start; each substep is one update
   of the scheme followed by the friction and the added water. (`substeps`, `step`)
3. **Dry cells.** A cell with h < `dryTolerance` has zero velocity and keeps no momentum; a depth within
   10⁻¹⁴ m below zero after an update is floating-point residue and reads as zero, and a depth further below
   zero is an error of the implementation, never clamped. (`velocity`, `_advance`)
4. **Hydrostatic reconstruction.** At the interface between two cells with surfaces w and beds b, each side
   presents the depth h* = max(0, w − max(b_left, b_right)) to the flux and keeps its own velocity; its pressure
   term is corrected by (g/2)(h² − h*²) on its own side (Audusse et al. 2004). A still surface therefore gives
   equal h* on both sides and no net force, also against a dry cell above the water. (`reconstruct`)
5. **Central-upwind flux.** With the one-sided speeds a⁺ = max(u_l + c_l, u_r + c_r, 0) and a⁻ = min(u_l − c_l,
   u_r − c_r, 0), c = √(g h*), the flux of (h, hu, hv) through an x interface is (a⁺ F(U_l) − a⁻ F(U_r)) / (a⁺ − a⁻)
   + a⁺ a⁻ (U_r − U_l) / (a⁺ − a⁻), F = (hu, hu² + g h²/2, huv), and zero when both sides are dry; the y
   direction exchanges the roles of u and v (Kurganov and Petrova 2007). (`flux_x`)
6. **Order 1.** Piecewise-constant cells; U ← U + Δt L(U), L the flux divergence of rules 4–5 over the four
   interfaces. (`_rhs`, `_advance`)
7. **Order 2.** h, hu, hv and w are reconstructed linearly in each direction with the minmod limiter of the
   neighbour differences (so the face depths stay non-negative), the face bed is w − h, rule 4 applies to the face
   values, and the centred bed source (g/2)(h₋ + h₊)(b₋ − b₊)/dx is added to the momentum, where ∓ are the cell's
   two faces along the direction; time advances by Heun's method, the average of the state and two Euler updates.
   (`minmod`, `_rhs`)
8. **Friction.** After each substep, hu ← hu / (1 + Δt g n² |u| / h^(4/3)) and hv likewise, n = `manning`,
   u the velocity before the correction; nothing in a dry cell. (`friction`)
9. **Rain and sources.** After the friction, every cell gains `rain` · Δt, and every cell whose centre lies
   within `radius` of a `source` whose `start` ≤ t < `end` (t the substep's start time) gains `rate` · Δt.
   (`_add_water`, `source_cells`)
10. **Boundary.** `closed`: the cell across the edge mirrors the edge cell with its normal discharge reversed,
    so the mass flux through the edge is exactly zero. `open`: the cell across the edge copies the edge cell, so
    water flows out down its own slope and never in. (`_ghost`)
11. **Conservation and balance.** On a closed grid without rain, sources or friction, the water volume is
    conserved to rounding; a lake at rest over any bed, with any dry cells above it, is kept exactly in both
    orders (the numeric table's lake rows show 0 surface error and 7 · 10⁻¹⁶ m²/s of discharge after 40 steps).
12. **Digest.** The layers' digest is the SHA-256 of the JSON of the layers with sorted keys, no spaces, pairs
    as two-element lists and every value rounded to three decimals. (`digest`)
13. **Validity.** SW1–SW5 and V17 hold or the document is refused; the reference lists the codes.
    (`validate_document`, `bad_document`)

## Rationale

- **Hydrostatic reconstruction over Kurganov–Petrova's bilinear bed.** Kurganov and Petrova keep the bed
  continuous and piecewise bilinear from vertex values; the terrain-grid draft stores beds at cell centres and
  its layers change under the erosion draft, so a centre-based balance is the one that costs nothing to keep
  consistent. Audusse's reconstruction is centre-based, well-balanced for the lake at rest including wet/dry
  fronts, positivity preserving, and second-order with the centred source of rule 7.
- **The central-upwind flux.** It needs no Riemann solver, no entropy fix and no characteristic decomposition,
  handles dry states by its one-sided speeds, and is the flux SREP 40's engine already uses; the two water
  solvers of 1.8 share a numerical core.
- **A fixed step subdivided by the Courant number.** An adaptive step makes frame 400 depend on whether frame
  399 was asked for; a fixed step with substeps makes the sequence a function of t alone (SREP 14) and keeps
  the Courant condition. The engine may cache; the result may not depend on the cache.
- **Manning, semi-implicit.** The explicit friction term stiffens in shallow fast water; the implicit form is
  unconditionally stable and never reverses the flow (the numeric table's friction row).
- **`water` and `flow` as the state.** The terrain-grid draft already types `water` in metres and `flow` in
  m²/s with two channels: a discharge. Other drafts read them (snow melt feeds `water`; vegetation reads wetness
  from it), and the erosion draft's hydraulic process writes the same two, which is why SW2 forbids both on one
  terrain.
- **Sources as disks in metres.** SREP 40's impulses are points in the ocean's frame; a terrain source is an
  inflow (a spring, a breach, a pipe) with a footprint and a schedule, and the mass it adds is exact by rule 9.
- **Three analytic cases at stated resolutions.** Ritter at 400 cells and 1 s (L1 0.8 % at order 1, 0.27 % at
  order 2), Thacker at 200 cells over one period (2.5 % and 0.16 %), the subcritical bump at 80 cells at 60 s
  (0.19 %): the tests bound these with headroom (2 %/1 %, 5 %/1 %, 1 %) so another implementation of the same
  scheme passes and a different scheme that is worse does not.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Moving SREP 40's `ocean` onto the terrain as is.** Its bathymetry asset, periodic boundary, colliders, bed
  response, body coupling and splash live in the 3D scene's frame and in the pyro and particle drafts; the
  terrain needs the solver, not the ocean. The ocean keeps all of it; this draft takes the equations.
- **An adaptive time step.** Breaks SREP 14's seek (rationale).
- **Kurganov–Petrova's vertex bed.** Needs a second bed representation that the erosion draft would have to
  update (rationale).
- **Semi-Lagrangian or pipe water.** The pipes of the erosion draft are a transport model, not the equations;
  semi-Lagrangian advection does not conserve mass. Both stay where they are.
- **Inflow and outflow boundaries as attributes.** The bump case needs a fixed inflow discharge and a fixed
  outflow depth; the reference provides them as ghost cells to its own solver, but a terrain's edge is a scene
  boundary and a source is the inflow this draft offers. Open issue 2.

## Backwards compatibility

A 1.7 document has no `shallowWater`; SREP 40's `ocean` is untouched. A 1.8 `terrain` without `shallowWater`
behaves as in the terrain-grid draft. No attribute, default or rule of an existing element changes.

The analytic solutions, as the reference implements them from the papers: **Ritter** (dry bed, dam at x₀
holding h_l): with c₀ = √(g h_l) and ξ = (x − x₀)/t, h = h_l and u = 0 for ξ ≤ −c₀, h = 0 beyond ξ = 2 c₀, and
h = (4/9g)(c₀ − ξ/2)², u = (2/3)(ξ + c₀) between (Stoker 1957 gives the wet-bed case, not used here).
**Thacker** (planar surface in the bowl z = h₀((x − L/2)²/a² − 1)): the surface η = A(t)(x − L/2) + C(t) with
A = A₀ cos ωt, ω = √(2 g h₀)/a, C = g A₀² sin²(ωt)/(2 ω²), velocity u = −(g A₀/ω) sin ωt where wet, h = η − z
where positive; the momentum equation gives u' = −g A and the continuity equation A' = 2 u h₀/a², C' = −u A,
whose solution this is; the exact wet volume ∫(η − z)⁺ is constant in t (the table's two volume rows).
**Bump** (SWASHES 3.1.1, subcritical): z = 0.2 − 0.05(x − 10)² on 8 < x < 12 m, q = 4.42 m²/s, h = 2 m at the
outlet, and the steady depth is the largest root of h³ + (z − q²/(2 g h_out²) − h_out) h² + q²/(2g) = 0
(Bernoulli), by Newton from h_out − z.

The reference's tests also hold: the lake at rest with the island is exact to 10⁻¹² in both orders over 40
steps; the dam break keeps its mass to 10⁻⁹ on a closed grid, its front within 1 m of 2 c₀ t and its depth
non-negative; order 2 is closer than order 1 on the dam break; the Thacker solution starts at rest, is dry at
the rim, and the bowl holds more than half h₀ at its centre after one period; the bump's steady discharge is
within 5 % of q over the interior; rain and sources add exactly their volume on a closed grid; an open edge lets
water leave; `run` writes `water` and `flow`, counts its steps and is deterministic.

## Open issues

1. **Coupling with erosion.** SW2 keeps the pipes and the equations apart; a hydraulic erosion driven by this
   solver's depth and velocity (the capacity of the erosion draft's rule 7.5 on this state) is the natural
   successor and would retire the pipes.
2. **Inflow and outflow edges.** A fixed discharge in and a fixed depth out, as the bump case needs, are not
   attributes; `source` and `open` cover springs and runoff, not a river crossing the terrain's edge.
3. **Infiltration and evaporation.** Rain only adds; a loss term (a rate, or the terrain's `wetness` layer) is
   missing.
4. **Colliders and bodies.** SREP 40's `colliders`, bed response, body coupling and splash stay on the ocean;
   whether a terrain's water needs them is open.
5. **Rendering.** The terrain-grid draft's `display` shows layers; the water surface of a terrain as a mesh
   with SREP 40's material and whitewater (SREP 79) is not specified here.
6. **Periodic boundary.** SREP 40 has it; a terrain is a finite rectangle and does not.
7. **Second-order time with friction.** The friction is applied after the Heun step, which is first order in
   the friction term; a Strang splitting would restore second order where friction dominates.
8. **Reproducibility across engines.** The scheme is deterministic and synchronous, but the order of floating
   operations differs between engines; the kit cases' millimetre tolerances cover the reference's cases and must
   be re-measured for long runs on large grids.
