# SREP 0 — Add agent behaviours

## Abstract

[SREP 12](srep-0012.md) names, as its CL3 building block, "declared rules (seek, eat, reproduce, pair off)
on seeded agents, with charts bound to live counts (MF4)", with the bar "seeded populations reproduce
exactly" (line 265). This SREP is that block. An `agents` node is a population of named kinds that starts on
the rim of an arena and lives in days, each day one fixed step: the day's pellets are placed by a D24 stream,
every agent runs for the pellet it would reach first, the first arrival eats (one pellet may feed two, by a
payoff table: hawk and dove), each agent then survives and reproduces by its food and two draws, children
inherit their kind with a mutated speed, and everyone takes a new home on the rim. The population's counts
are live values that expressions, links and chart series read through `prop`. Every rule is a function of
the document, the seed and the day, in gather form, so the seek check of [SREP 14](srep-0014.md) holds and
two engines agree on every count.

## Motivation

- **Shots 33 and 34** (SREP 12 lines 621 and 622, Primer's natural-selection and aggression videos) are
  populations of blobs running for food, dying, reproducing and, in shot 34, pairing off at pellets as hawks
  and doves, with a population chart ticking beside the arena. The probes record "no agent behaviour (seek
  food, eat, die)" and "no agent model … and no stacked-area chart bound to it".
- **What exists.** `flock` ([SREP 73](srep-0073.md), `schema/scene-render.xsd:4959`) steers agents by
  separation, alignment and cohesion and `slime` (5150) deposits trails; neither seeks a target, eats, dies or
  reproduces, and neither exposes a count. The engine's `crates/sr-sim/src/flock.rs` keeps positions and
  velocities in arrays and steps them with a grid; the population is fixed.
- **The bar** is exactness: the same document, seed and day give the same population on every engine. A
  continuous steering simulation cannot promise that across floating-point paths; a day of discrete events
  decided by arrival times and integer indices can.
- **Charts bound to counts** are the MF4 reactive-values draft (rule 7, `series` with a linked `values`);
  this SREP supplies the values.

### Semantics

The reference implementation is `conformance/tools/agents_reference.py`; the names in parentheses are its
functions. `e = E(project, id, seed, "agents")` is the element seed of D24 section 4, and
`S(e, p, k, i) = U(e, p + k·2³², i)` the stream draw of the random-streams draft (section 5), with the
purposes `P_HOME` 0, `P_FOOD` 1, `P_SURVIVE` 2, `P_REPRODUCE` 3 and `P_MUTATE` 4. Day `k` (`k ≥ 0`) runs from
`start + k·day` to `start + (k + 1)·day` in composition time.

1. **Kinds** (`kind_of`). Agent `i` of the initial `count` has the kind whose cumulative share bracket holds
   `(i + ½) / count`, in document order: with shares ½ and ½ and `count` 6, agents 0 to 2 are the first kind.
2. **The rim** (`rim_point`). `rim(u)` is the point at fraction `u ∈ [0, 1)` of the arena's perimeter,
   clockwise from the origin along the top, right, bottom and left edges.
3. **The start** (`init`). Agent `i` starts at `rim(S(e, P_HOME, 0, i))` with speed `speed`.
4. **Pellets** (`pellets`). Day `k` has `pellets` pellets, pellet `j` at
   `(S(e, P_FOOD, k, 2j) · width, S(e, P_FOOD, k, 2j + 1) · height)`.
5. **The race** (`claims`). Each agent heads, from its home at time 0, for the free pellet it would reach
   first at its speed (the smallest arrival time, ties to the lower pellet index; a pellet is free while it has
   fewer than `share` claimants). The earliest arrival among all agents (ties to the lower agent index) eats
   that pellet, and heads for its next free pellet from there unless it has had `meals`. When a pellet fills,
   every other agent heading for it turns, from where it is at that moment (its home moved toward the pellet
   by its speed times the elapsed time), toward its next free pellet. The day's race ends when no agent has a
   pellet to reach. The result is the list of pellets each agent ate, in order, and the claimants of each
   pellet; it depends on positions, speeds and indices only.
6. **Food** (`food`, `payoff`). A pellet's sole claimant eats its `units`. Two claimants take
   `(aFood, bFood)` of the `payoff` of their kinds, or `units / 2` each without one. With the hawk–dove
   table (hawk–dove 1.5/0.5, hawk–hawk 0/0, dove–dove 1/1 of a 2-unit pellet), shot 34 is Maynard Smith and
   Price's game.
7. **Fate** (`fate`). With food `f`, agent `i` survives day `k` when `f ≥ 1` or `S(e, P_SURVIVE, k, i) < f`;
   a survivor has `⌊f − 1⌋` children plus one more when `S(e, P_REPRODUCE, k, i)` is below the fraction of
   `f − 1`. So food 0 dies, 1 lives, 2 lives and reproduces, 0.5 lives half the time, 1.5 reproduces half the
   time, all from draws indexed by the day and the agent.
8. **The next day** (`day`, `day_report`, `mutated_speed`). The population after day `k` is the survivors, in
   index order, followed by the children, in parent order, each child with its parent's kind and speed
   `parent · (1 + mutation · (2 S(e, P_MUTATE, k, c) − 1))` clamped to `[minSpeed, maxSpeed]`, `c` the child's
   index among the day's children; then agent `i` of the new list takes the home `rim(S(e, P_HOME, k + 1, i))`.
   A population that would exceed 100000 is a render error (`E2`), not a truncation; an empty population
   stays empty. The day is a function of the population before it and `k` alone (gather form, SREP 14 rule 3).
9. **Live values** (`counts`, `count`, `mean_speed`). At any composition time the node's `count` is the size
   of the population of the current day (the day containing the time; before `start`, day 0's), `count.<kind>`
   the number of that kind and `mean.speed` the mean speed (0 of an empty population). A chart `series` whose
   `values` links to them (the reactive-values draft, rule 7) ticks once a day.
10. **Within a day** (`day_path`, `along`, `position_at`). Between the day's start and end, agent `i` moves
    along the polyline home → its pellets in the order eaten → home at its speed, and rests at home once the
    polyline is walked; an agent that ate nothing rests at home. This is presentation: it does not feed back
    into rule 5. The sprite follows `flock`'s `shape`, `size` and the kind's `color`; the squash and eyes of
    the characters draft apply as children.
11. **Determinism and bounds.** The state at a frame is the population after the last day at or before the
    frame, by checkpoints or by stepping from the start: the seek check (SREP 14 rule 5) is exact within an
    engine and, since every quantity is an integer, a draw or a sum of a few distances, exact between engines
    whose arrival-time ties are decided by the same indices. `count`, `pellets` ≤ 100000; `meals` ≤ 16; days
    ≤ 1000000 (`E3`).

## Rationale

- **Days, not steering.** Primer's populations are decided by who gets to the food, not by the path taken; a
  day of events on arrival times is exact and cheap (`O(agents · pellets · meals)`), where a steering
  simulation at 60 steps a day is neither exact across engines nor what the shot shows.
- **Draws by (purpose, day, index).** The random-streams draft's `S` makes every decision a function of the
  element seed, the day and an integer, with no generator state; two engines, or one engine at two seeks,
  draw the same numbers.
- **Children after survivors in parent order** fixes the indices that the next day's draws use, so the
  population is reproducible without sorting by anything floating.
- **Food as a number, fates as thresholds.** One rule (`f ≥ 1` lives, `f − 1` reproduces, fractions by a
  draw) covers Primer's "one pellet to live, two to reproduce" and the hawk–dove fractions.
- **Counts as live values** reuse MF4 instead of a chart-specific binding.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Extending `flock`** with `seek` and `eat`: the flock is a continuous simulation of fixed size; adding
  births and deaths breaks its arrays and its SREP 73 behaviour.
- **A general rule language** (a scripting of behaviours): SREP 14 rule 8 forbids external code and the
  expression language has no loops; the four rules of CL3 are stated directly instead.
- **Simultaneous rounds** (every agent picks, then pellets resolve, then the losers pick again): a fast agent
  that has eaten cannot beat a slow one to the next pellet even when it would arrive earlier; the event race
  gives the physically right answer with the same determinism.
- **Energy carried across days**: a second state variable; the shots need none, and the editor can add
  `energy` later without changing the day's order.

## Backwards compatibility

A new node gated by V17; `flock` and `slime` are unchanged. The reactive-values draft is required for the
chart binding; without it the counts are still readable by `prop`.

## Open issues

- **Energy and lifespan.** Agents that carry food across days and age out are one state variable each; the
  day order here leaves room for both.
- **Sense radius.** Every agent sees every pellet; a `perception` radius (as `flock`'s) would make unseen
  pellets unreachable and is one attribute.
- **Stacked-area charts** (shot 34) are a chart kind of the plots draft (MF1), not of this SREP.
- **Heritable kinds.** A child is its parent's kind; a mutation between kinds (dove to hawk) is a `mutationKind`
  probability the editor may add.
