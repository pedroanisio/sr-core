# SREP 0 — Add orbits and N-body motion

## Abstract

Two ways to move bodies under gravity for SREP 12's milestone 6 (SP4): an `orbit` child of `object3D` that
places the object on a Keplerian orbit about a central object from its classical elements at an epoch of the
space clock, propagated by universal variables so that a two-body orbit closes on itself to 1e-12; and an
`nbody` child of the `space` element whose `member`s and Plummer `cluster` move under their mutual attraction
by one of three integrators, `leapfrog`, `wisdomHolman` and `ias15`, with a fixed `step` that divides the
frame interval so that every frame's state is a pure function of the frame index (the seek check of SREP 14).
A `trail` child draws a fading ribbon of an object's past positions. The reference tests are the ones SREP 12
names: two-body closure, bounded energy error and time reversibility.

## Motivation

SREP 12 row SP4 asks for "Kepler propagation; leapfrog, Wisdom–Holman and IAS15 (Rein and Spiegel 2015)
integrators; trajectory ribbons", measured by "two-body closure 1e-12; bounded energy error; time
reversibility". At 67a661a a 3D object moves by keyed transforms, by the rigid-body solver of `rigidBody`
(contacts and joints under a uniform `physics/@gravity*`), or by the time-frames draft's ephemeris; nothing
moves an object on an orbit the author writes as elements, and nothing integrates mutual gravitation. The
time-frames draft gives the clock and the frames; the random-streams draft gives the deterministic draws a
cluster needs; this draft gives the motion.

### Semantics

The reference module (`conformance/tools/orbits_reference.py`) names the function of each rule. Positions
are metres and seconds in the space frame of the time-frames draft until rule 6 maps them to pixels; angles
are degrees unless a rule says radians.

1. **Kepler's problem.** `state_from_elements(mu, a, e, i, raan, argp, nu)` is the perifocal state
   (r = p / (1 + e cos ν), p = a(1 − e²), v = √(μ/p)(−sin ν, e + cos ν)) rotated by R₃(Ω) R₁(i) R₃(ω);
   `elements_from_state` inverts it from h = r × v, the node ẑ × h and the eccentricity vector, with the
   angles as atan2 about ĥ so that retrograde orbits (i above 90°) and equatorial ones (Ω = 0, the node
   taken as x̂) come back unchanged; the round trip holds to 1e-12. Kepler's equation is solved by Newton's
   iterations from Danby's start (`kepler_elliptic`: E − e sin E = M; `kepler_hyperbolic`: e sinh H − H = M),
   bounded by `MAX_KEPLER_ITERATIONS`. `propagate(mu, r, v, dt)` is the universal variable χ (Vallado's
   algorithm 8) with the Stumpff functions `stumpff_c2` and `stumpff_c3` (their series below |z| = 10⁻³), the
   Lagrange f and g functions and at most `MAX_UNIVERSAL_ITERATIONS` Newton steps that stop at rounding; an
   ellipse propagated by its `period` returns to its state within 1e-12 in position and velocity (`closure`),
   a hyperbola 10 days out and back within a millimetre, and energy is kept to 1e-12 at any time. The GM
   table (`GM`, m³/s²) holds the DE440 header values (Park et al. 2021) of the ten bodies of the planets
   draft; `orbit_mu(mass, body)` is G × mass or that GM.
2. **The orbit element.** `orbit(mu, a, e, i, raan, argp, anomaly, epoch)` is the state at the epoch;
   `orbit_state(orbit, t)` propagates it to the time t of the space clock, forward or back, with no state
   carried between frames; the object's position is its centre's place at that time plus the state's position
   mapped by rule 6, so an orbit about a `member` or about another orbit's object moves with it. The Earth's
   circular 1 au orbit has a period of 365.257 days from `GM_SUN`; an
   orbit of e 0.3 is at true anomaly 122.543° a quarter period after pericentre.
3. **The system and the integrators.** `system(masses, positions, velocities, G, softening)` validates the
   members (positive masses, no two coincident without softening, at most `MAX_BODIES`); `accelerations`
   is a_i = Σ_j G m_j (r_j − r_i) / (|r_j − r_i|² + ε²)^(3/2) (Plummer softening ε); `energy`, `momentum`,
   `angular_momentum` and `centre_of_mass` are the usual sums and `at_rest` moves a system to its barycentric
   frame. `integrate(system, integrator, step, t)` applies `steps_to(t, step)` steps of one scheme:
   - `leapfrog` (`step_leapfrog`): kick-drift-kick, second order, symplectic and time-symmetric; the energy
     error of a circular pair is 2.4 × 10⁻⁷ at 200 steps per orbit and does not grow over 50 orbits; the
     position error falls by 4 when the step halves.
   - `wisdomHolman` (`step_wisdom_holman`): Wisdom and Holman (1991) in Jacobi coordinates (`_jacobi`: r_i =
     x_i − X_{i−1}, X_i the centre of mass of bodies 0..i), kick-drift-kick with the interaction kick of
     H_int = Σ_i G m_i M_{i−1} / |r_i| − Σ_{pairs} G m_i m_j / |x_i − x_j| for half a step on each side of
     the exact Kepler drift of every Jacobi coordinate about the mass M_i inside it (`propagate` with
     μ = G M_i). A two-body system is exact (energy to 1e-9 at 20 steps per orbit where leapfrog loses
     2 × 10⁻³); three bodies keep energy to 1.3 × 10⁻⁶ over 50 orbits. Body 0 is the central mass; softening
     is not admitted (OR3).
   - `ias15` (`step_ias15`): Everhart's Gauss–Radau scheme as described by Rein and Spiegel (2015). The
     acceleration over a step is the polynomial a(τ) = a₀ + Σ_k b_k τ^(k+1) in τ = t / dt, k = 0..6; the
     eight Gauss–Radau nodes `RADAU_H` (0 and the seven roots of P₇ + P₈ on (−1, 1) mapped to (0, 1), found
     by bisection, not copied: h₁ = 0.0562626, h₇ = 0.977521, whose weights `RADAU_W` integrate every
     polynomial to degree 14 exactly) give the positions where the force is evaluated; the Newton
     divided differences g_j of the accelerations at the nodes (`RADAU_R`) are converted to the b_k by the
     monomial coefficients of ∏(τ − h_i) (`RADAU_C`, `b_from_g`), and the predictor–corrector iterates the
     substeps until the b change by less than 10⁻¹⁶ of the acceleration scale or `MAX_RADAU_ITERATIONS`
     passes; the step then advances position and velocity by the integrated polynomial. The position error
     of a circular pair is below 1e-8 at 20 steps per orbit and below 1e-12 at 40, energy is kept to 1e-10
     over 20 orbits, and the Chenciner–Montgomery figure eight (Simó's initial conditions, period 6.32591398)
     closes within 1e-6 at 400 steps. With `tolerance`, `integrate_adaptive` rejects a step whose error
     estimate max|b₆| / max|a| exceeds it and retries at dt (ε / error)^(1/7), grows an accepted step by at most
     1.4, lands the last step on the frame's time, and stops with an error after `MAX_STEPS`.
   Every scheme is time-reversible to rounding: `reversibility` integrates forward, reverses the velocities
   and integrates back, and the pair returns within 1e-10 of its start under all three.
4. **The cluster.** `plummer(seed, n, mass, scale, G)` draws n equal masses from the random streams of the
   F1 draft (`U(seed, channel, index)`: channel 0 the radius by the inverse cumulative mass
   `plummer_radius`, r = a / √(u^(−2/3) − 1); channels 1 and 2 the direction; channels 3 and 4 the speed
   fraction q by Aarseth, Hénon and Wielen's rejection against q² (1 − q²)^(7/2) in at most `MAX_TRIES`
   draws of consecutive indices (`plummer_q`); channels 5 and 6 the velocity direction; the speed is q times
   the escape speed `plummer_escape_speed` = √(2GM/a) (1 + r²/a²)^(−1/4)) and recentres the cluster at rest
   (`at_rest`). A body's draw depends on the seed and its index alone, so a larger cluster keeps a smaller
   one's bodies up to the recentring shift. A cluster of 400 has virial ratio 2K / |W| = 0.975 and median
   radius 1.33 scale radii (the Plummer half-mass radius is 1.305).
5. **Trails.** `trail_times(t, length, samples)` are the sample times evenly spaced over the last `length`
   seconds and clamped at the epoch; the ribbon joins the object's places at those times: an orbit's is its
   centre's place at that time plus the state (rule 2), a member's is the system walked along the step grid
   from the epoch then one step of the remainder to the sample time (`member_trail(system, integrator, step,
   times)`; under `tolerance` the adaptive integration from the epoch to each time), any other object's is
   its place at the frame. Each sample's alpha is `trail_alpha(age, length, fade)` = 1 − age / length when
   fading. The ribbon is a tube of radius `width` / 2 in scene units with eight segments about the path,
   unlit, blended and double-sided, in the trail's `color` or the object's base colour, the alpha varying
   along it per vertex; seen through both of its walls a sample's apparent opacity is 1 − (1 − α)².
6. **Frames, seek and the scene.** `frame_state(system, integrator, step, frame, fps, rate)` integrates from
   the space epoch over frame / fps × `rate` seconds, which OR3 makes a whole number of steps; the state at a
   frame is therefore a pure function of the document and the frame index and a frame rendered alone equals
   the frame reached by playing (`seek_test`: 240 frames, 600 steps). With `tolerance` the state at a frame
   is `integrate_adaptive` from the epoch to the frame's time and the engine integrates from the epoch at
   every frame, so the frame is again a pure function of the document and the frame index (the step sequence
   to a frame does not depend on the frames rendered before it). Without `tolerance` the engine may cache
   the state of the last frame and step from it, since the step sequence is the same.
   `scene_position(p, origin, pixelsPerMeter)`
   maps metres to scene units by the time-frames axes (scene (x, y, z) = space (x, −z, y) × `pixelsPerMeter`,
   SREP 4) about the space origin.

## Rationale

- **Why elements and an N-body block, not one or the other.** An author who wants a satellite on a given
  orbit writes six numbers and gets an exact conic; an author who wants a cluster or a resonance needs
  mutual gravitation. Both read the space clock, so the two compose (a moon on an `orbit` about a `member`).
- **Why universal variables.** One formula covers ellipses and hyperbolas, the Stumpff series removes the
  singularity at the parabola's edge, and closure to 1e-12 is a property of the algorithm rather than of a
  tolerance; a position from mean anomaly needs two code paths and a separate hyperbolic solver.
- **Why these three integrators.** Leapfrog is the smallest symplectic scheme and the one a shader can run;
  Wisdom–Holman is the planetary-system scheme (exact Kepler drifts, errors scaled by the planet masses);
  IAS15 is the general high-order scheme with an error bound, for close encounters and the figure eight.
  SREP 12 names exactly these.
- **Why Jacobi coordinates for Wisdom–Holman.** The democratic heliocentric splitting is simpler to write but
  not exact for two bodies; the Jacobi one is, so the reference test "two-body closure" holds for it as well.
- **Why a fixed step that divides the frame interval.** The seek check (SREP 14) needs the state at a frame
  to be independent of the frames rendered before it; with the step dividing the interval, playing and
  seeking apply the same sequence of steps. The adaptive IAS15 keeps the property by landing each frame's
  last step on the frame time.
- **Why the Gauss–Radau nodes are computed.** They are the roots of a known polynomial; computing them in the
  reference (and checking them by the quadrature's exactness) avoids copying tables from a GPL code base.
- **Why the cluster uses the random streams.** The draws are deterministic across engines (F1), and a
  per-index sample means the cluster does not change when `count` grows.

## Rejected alternatives

- **REBOUND or another library.** REBOUND is GPL-3; the draft's scope is three schemes a renderer can carry
  in a few hundred lines, written from the papers.
- **A velocity-Verlet with adaptive step.** Adaptivity breaks the symplectic property; IAS15 is the adaptive
  scheme of the draft.
- **Orbits by mean motion only** (a circle with a period): the elements cost nothing more and give
  eccentric and hyperbolic cases.
- **Trails as particle emitters.** A trail is the object's own path; an emitter would carry a second
  simulation with its own seed and seek rules.

`conformance/tools/orbits_kit.py` writes them (as `srep-0000-…`, `--write DIR`), renders them with an engine

at the scene origin with `orthoHeight` equal to the frame's height, so one scene unit is one pixel and a

## Open issues

1. **The GM table's provenance.** Rule 1's values are the DE440 header constants as published; the
   ephemeris excerpt of the time-frames draft carries no constants, so the table is not pinned by a digest.
   A `gm_de440.tpc` asset with a digest would make it so.
2. **Relativity and non-gravitational forces.** No post-Newtonian term, radiation pressure or drag; Mercury's
   perihelion advance is not reproduced.
3. **Collisions and close encounters.** Two members that meet are not merged; the softening is the only
   regularisation, and `wisdomHolman` has none.
4. **The adaptive step's cost.** With `tolerance` every frame integrates from the epoch (rule 6), so the work
   grows with the frame index; a frame at a different `fps` lands on a different last step and differs
   within the tolerance; a fixed step is exact across frame rates and steps from the cached frame.
5. **Hierarchical systems in Wisdom–Holman.** The Jacobi chain follows document order; a moon listed before
   its planet is treated as orbiting the Sun. An explicit hierarchy attribute may follow.
6. **Trails of N-body members.** Rule 5's walk along the step grid with one step of the remainder is a
   convention: a sample between grid times is reached by one step of another size, whose error differs from
   the grid's; a trail whose samples fall on grid times has none.
