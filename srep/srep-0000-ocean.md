# SREP 0 — Add spectral seas, caustics and flow maps to the ocean

## Abstract

SREP 12's A4: one `sea` child of `ocean`, a statistical wave field laid over SREP 40's shallow-water solver. Its
`model` is one of `phillips`, `piersonMoskowitz`, `jonswap` (the default) and `tma`, driven by `windSpeed`,
`windDirection`, `fetch`, `gamma`, `spread` and `cutoff`; it is sampled on a periodic grid of `size` metres and
`resolution` cells, its initial amplitudes drawn once from the random-streams draft's normal draw on the lattice
index pack(i, j, 0), and its height, `choppiness` displacement, slopes, normal and Jacobian are written in closed
form in time (Tessendorf 2001), so every frame renders alone (SREP 14). Foam appears where the Jacobian falls
under `foamThreshold` and fades over `foamDecay`, feeding the `whitewater` albedo of SREP 79. The ocean gains its
water's optics: `absorption` and `scattering` per metre (Pope and Fry 1997), `ior` (1.333), the exact Fresnel
reflectance (0.020 at normal incidence, total beyond Snell's window of 48.6°), `caustics` on the bed and a
`flowMap` with `flowSpeed` and `flowCycle` for rivers and shores (Vlachos 2010). The statistics are testable: the
field's variance equals the spectrum's integral m₀ and the significant wave height is 4 √m₀. Rules OC1–OC6 and
V17; the schema files are unchanged, the diff is inside the draft. Reference module
`conformance/tools/ocean_reference.py`; engine card SR-103.

## Motivation

- **SREP 12 names the ocean's deltas.** Row A4 of the plan: directional spectra "JONSWAP, TMA, Pierson-Moskowitz,
  Phillips (Tessendorf 2001; Horvath 2015) as functions of time, so every frame renders alone; foam from the
  displacement Jacobian; water medium; caustics in the path tracer; flow maps", with the reference tests "wave-
  height variance equals the spectrum integral; Fresnel 0.020 at normal incidence; Snell's window 48.6 degrees"
  and the quality bar "parity plus with the Houdini and Blender ocean spectra and HDRP water".
- **The ocean of SREP 40 has waves but no sea state.** Its `wave` children are single sinusoids (`wavelength`,
  `amplitude`, `direction`, `phase`, `speed`; at most 64; `crates/sr-sim/src/ocean/waves.rs`, `Wave` and
  `apply`), tapered to half the local depth. A sea is a continuum of thousands of components with a measured
  distribution of energy over frequency and direction; a user who wants "a 10 m/s wind over 100 km of fetch"
  has no way to say it, and the result of 64 sinusoids is periodic and regular.
- **SREP 77 and SREP 79 touched the water without its optics.** SREP 77 gave the ocean a `density`; SREP 79 gave
  `whitewater` a foam albedo. The water's colour is still a material (`material` with IOR 1.333 by default,
  SREP 40), with no absorption length, so a deep ocean and a puddle look alike, and the path tracer has no
  caustics on the bed.
- **Random streams make the sea deterministic.** The random-streams draft (D24) gives every element a seed and
  a lattice draw; the amplitudes drawn from it are the same in every engine, so two renderers agree on the
  waves, not only their statistics.

## Specification

### Syntax

A `sea` is an optional child of `oceanType` (`schema/scene-render.xsd:8116`), at most one per ocean, beside the
`wave` children, which remain. The attributes:

| Attribute | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | ID | required | the sea's id, part of its seed (rule 1) |
| `seed` | integer | absent | the sea's `@seed` in the element seed (rule 1) |
| `model` | enumeration | `jonswap` | `phillips`, `piersonMoskowitz`, `jonswap` or `tma` (rules 3, 5) |
| `windSpeed` | m/s | 10 | the wind speed U₁₀ at 10 m |
| `windDirection` | degrees | 0 | the direction the wind blows toward, from +x toward +z (SREP 40's `wave@direction`) |
| `fetch` | metres | 100000 | the fetch F of `jonswap` and `tma` |
| `gamma` | number ≥ 1 | 3.3 | the JONSWAP peak enhancement γ |
| `spread` | number > 0 | 10 | the spreading exponent s of rule 4 |
| `cutoff` | metres | 0 | waves shorter than this wavelength are dropped; 0 keeps every resolved wave |
| `size` | metres | 1024 | the period of the grid in x and z |
| `resolution` | integer | 256 | the grid's cells per side, a power of two from 16 to 2048 |
| `choppiness` | number ≥ 0 | 1 | the horizontal displacement scale λ (rule 9) |
| `foamThreshold` | 0 to 2 | 0.8 | the Jacobian under which foam appears (rule 11) |
| `foamDecay` | seconds | 0.5 | the e-folding time of the foam (rule 11) |

Seven attributes of `ocean`, its water's optics (rules 14 to 17):

| Attribute | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `absorption` | three numbers, per metre | `0.34 0.0565 0.0092` | the water's absorption at 650, 550 and 450 nm (Pope and Fry 1997) |
| `scattering` | three numbers, per metre | `0.003 0.003 0.003` | the water's scattering coefficient |
| `ior` | number ≥ 1 | 1.333 | the water's index of refraction, replacing the material's for the surface |
| `caustics` | boolean | false | the path tracer traces light through the surface onto the bed (rule 16) |
| `flowMap` | IDREF | absent | an image asset whose red and green channels are the flow velocity (rule 17) |
| `flowSpeed` | number ≥ 0 | 0 | the flow speed in texture widths per second at full channel deflection |
| `flowCycle` | seconds | 2 | the period after which a flow phase resets (rule 17) |

Rules the Schematron would carry; the reference's `validate_document` returns their codes:

- **OC1** `model` is one of the four names.
- **OC2** `windSpeed` and `fetch` are positive, `gamma` is at least 1, `spread` is positive, `cutoff` is at
  least 0, `windDirection` is a number.
- **OC3** `size` is positive, `resolution` is a power of two from 16 to 2048, `choppiness` is at least 0,
  `seed` when present is an integer in [0, 2⁶⁴).
- **OC4** `foamThreshold` is 0 to 2 and `foamDecay` at least 0.
- **OC5** every component of `absorption` and `scattering` is at least 0 and `ior` is at least 1.
- **OC6** `flowMap` names an image asset, `flowSpeed` is at least 0 and `flowCycle` positive.
- **V17** a document of version 1.7 or earlier has no `sea` and none of the seven ocean attributes.

### Semantics

The sea lives in the ocean's local x/z plane of SREP 40, with scene y increasing downward and the surface at
`waterLevel` minus the water's rise. Lengths are metres, times seconds on the ocean's local clock, g the ocean's
`gravity` and d its `bottomDepth`. e is the sea's element seed, G and pack the random-streams draft's normal
draw and lattice index. Angles of the wave plane are measured from +x toward +z.

1. **Seed.** e = E(project, `id`, `seed`, "sea").
2. **Dispersion.** ω(k) = √(g k tanh(k d)); the group velocity dω/dk = g (tanh(kd) + kd sech²(kd)) / (2ω). The
   reference takes d = none as deep water, ω = √(g k).
3. **Frequency spectra.** With U = `windSpeed`, F = `fetch`, γ = `gamma`:
   - `piersonMoskowitz`: S(ω) = α g² ω⁻⁵ exp(−β (ω₀/ω)⁴), α = 0.0081, β = 0.74, ω₀ = g / U₁₉.₅, U₁₉.₅ = 1.026 U
     (Pierson and Moskowitz 1964). Its peak is at ω_p = 0.877 g / U₁₉.₅ (Horvath 2015; (4β/5)^¼ = 0.8772) and
     its variance over all frequencies is m₀ = α U₁₉.₅⁴ / (4 β g²).
   - `jonswap`: S(ω) = α_J g² ω⁻⁵ exp(−5/4 (ω_p/ω)⁴) γ^r with ω_p = 22 (g² / (U F))^⅓, α_J = 0.076 (U² / (F g))^0.22,
     r = exp(−(ω − ω_p)² / (2 σ² ω_p²)), σ = 0.07 below the peak and 0.09 above it (Hasselmann et al. 1973).
   - `tma`: the JONSWAP spectrum times the depth factor Φ(ω_h) of Kitaigorodskii et al. 1975 at ω_h = ω √(d/g):
     ω_h²/2 for ω_h ≤ 1, 1 − (2 − ω_h)²/2 for 1 < ω_h < 2, 1 above (Bouws et al. 1985).
4. **Spreading.** D(θ) = C(s) cos²ˢ((θ − θ_w)/2) with θ_w = `windDirection`, s = `spread` and C(s) =
   Γ(s+1) / (2√π Γ(s+½)), so that D integrates to one over the circle; D is zero where cos((θ − θ_w)/2) ≤ 0,
   against the wind (Longuet-Higgins, Cartwright and Smith 1963).
5. **Wavenumber spectrum.** For the three frequency models P(k) = S(ω(k)) D(θ_k) (dω/dk) / k, the change of
   variables from (ω, θ) to the plane. For `phillips`, P(k) = A exp(−1/(kL)²) k⁻⁴ (k̂ · ŵ)² with L = U²/g
   (Tessendorf 2001) and A = 2 m₀_PM / (π L²), the scale that gives it the Pierson-Moskowitz variance of the same
   wind (the unit spectrum integrates to π L²/2 over the plane). P(0) = 0, and P(k) = 0 for k > 2π / `cutoff`
   when `cutoff` is positive. A `model` outside the four is an error (OC1).
6. **Grid.** n = `resolution`, Δk = 2π / `size`; cell (i, j) with 0 ≤ i, j < n carries k = Δk (i′, j′) with
   i′ = i for i < n/2 and i − n otherwise, so that (−i mod n, −j mod n) carries −k. The cell (0, 0) and the
   Nyquist row and column i = n/2 or j = n/2 carry nothing.
7. **Initial amplitudes.** h₀(k) = (ξ_r + i ξ_i) √(P(k) Δk²) / 2 with ξ_r = G(e, 1, pack(i, j, 0)) and
   ξ_i = G(e, 2, pack(i, j, 0)): one draw per cell, never per frame.
8. **Time.** h̃(k, t) = h₀(k) e^{iωt} + h₀*(−k) e^{−iωt}, Hermitian in k, so the height h(x, z, t) =
   Re Σ_k h̃(k, t) e^{i(k_x x + k_z z)} is real, periodic in `size`, and a closed function of t: the frame at t is
   computed without the frames before it (SREP 14). Engines evaluate the sum by an inverse FFT of n × n.
9. **Displacement.** D(x, z, t) = −i λ Σ_k (k / |k|) h̃(k, t) e^{ik·x} with λ = `choppiness`; the surface point of
   grid position (x, z) is (x + D_x, `waterLevel` − taper(h), z + D_z), where taper clamps the rise to half the
   local water depth, as SREP 40 does for `wave`. λ = 0 gives no displacement.
10. **Slopes and normal.** ∂h/∂x = Re Σ i k_x h̃ e^{ik·x}, ∂h/∂z likewise; the up normal of the surface is
    (∂h/∂x, −1, ∂h/∂z) normalised (y points down).
11. **Jacobian and foam.** J = (1 + ∂D_x/∂x)(1 + ∂D_z/∂z) − (∂D_x/∂z)², from the analytic derivatives
    ∂D_x/∂x = λ Re Σ (k_x²/|k|) h̃ e^{ik·x} and their kin; J < 1 where the surface compresses and J ≤ 0 where it
    folds. The instantaneous foam f = clamp((`foamThreshold` − J) / 0.3, 0, 1); the foam seen at t is
    F(t) = max over the frames τ in [t − 3 `foamDecay`, t] of f(τ) e^{−(t − τ)/`foamDecay`}, a bounded window so the
    frame is still computed alone. F is the sea's coverage for SREP 79's `foamMode="albedo"`: where the ocean has
    `whitewater`, the surface's foam coverage is the maximum of F and the tracers' coverage.
12. **The solver.** The sea adds to the solver's surface after the solver's step and does not feed back into
    it: the solver's height, the `wave` children and the sea's tapered rise sum. Bodies, colliders and splashes
    of SREP 40 read the solver's surface; the sea is the rendered surface, the normal of rule 10 shading it.
13. **Statistics.** m₀ = ∫ S(ω) dω = ∬ P(k) d²k is the variance of the height; the grid's Σ P Δk² equals it up
    to the band the grid resolves, the generated field's spatial variance Σ |h̃(k, t)|² equals it by Parseval,
    and the significant wave height is H_s = 4 √m₀. The reference integrates S over 0.2 to 8 rad/s by Simpson's
    rule in 4000 steps.
14. **Fresnel.** The surface reflects the unpolarised Fresnel fraction for the index ratio n = `ior` (from above,
    1/`ior` from below): R = (r_s² + r_p²)/2 with r_s = (cos θ_i − n cos θ_t)/(cos θ_i + n cos θ_t), r_p =
    (n cos θ_i − cos θ_t)/(n cos θ_i + cos θ_t), R = 1 beyond the critical angle. At normal incidence R =
    ((n − 1)/(n + 1))² = 0.0204 for 1.333; from below, the light leaves only within Snell's window of half-angle
    asin(1/n) = 48.6°. The refracted direction scales the tangential component by n_i/n_t.
15. **Water medium.** Below the surface the ocean is a homogeneous medium of the media draft with σ_a =
    `absorption`, σ_s = `scattering` per channel and the Henyey-Greenstein phase with g = 0.924 (Petzold's
    average particle phase function, Mobley 1994). A colour seen through L metres is attenuated by
    exp(−(σ_a + σ_s) L) directly; the defaults keep 3 % of the red, 55 % of the green and 89 % of the blue
    after 10 m.
16. **Caustics.** With `caustics="true"` the path tracer carries light through the surface to the bed and the
    bodies in the water (a caustic photon map, a caustic grid or bidirectional sampling, the engine's choice).
    The reference check is a vertical section: vertical rays through the surface a sin(2πx/λ) over one period,
    refracted by rule 14, weighted by 1 − R and binned where they reach a flat bed at depth; the mean over the
    bins equals the mean transmitted fraction exactly and the bins under the crests are brighter. With
    `caustics="false"` the bed receives the transmitted light unfocused.
17. **Flow maps.** `flowMap` is read at the surface's texture coordinate; its red and green channels give the
    flow velocity v = (2 r − 1, 2 g − 1) `flowSpeed` in texture widths per second. A texture T of the surface
    (the normal detail, the foam) is sampled at two phases, p₀ = (t / `flowCycle`) mod 1 and p₁ = (p₀ + ½) mod 1,
    at uv − v `flowCycle` p, and blended as w₀ T(p₀) + (1 − w₀) T(p₁) with w₀ = 1 − |2 p₀ − 1|, so a phase resets
    where its weight is zero (Vlachos 2010). Without `flowMap` nothing flows.
18. **Version gate.** Under version 1.7 or earlier, `sea` and the seven ocean attributes are errors (V17); the
    ocean renders as SREP 40, 77 and 79 define.

## Rationale

- **Closed form in time, not a stepped simulation.** Tessendorf's h̃(k, t) is an analytic function of t for a
  fixed set of draws; a sea of 2048² cells at frame 10 000 costs one FFT, and SREP 14's seek rule holds without
  a cache. The solver of SREP 40 is stepped because it reacts to bodies; the sea does not.
- **Four spectra.** Pierson-Moskowitz is the fully developed limit, JONSWAP the fetch-limited sea most tools
  default to, TMA its shallow-water form, Phillips the historical graphics spectrum that Blender's ocean
  modifier and older pipelines use. Horvath 2015 reviews the first three for graphics; the Houdini Ocean
  Spectrum and HDRP water expose the same parameters (wind speed, fetch or "swell", directional spreading,
  chop), so a document's `sea` maps onto theirs.
- **The Phillips scale.** Tessendorf leaves A to the artist; tying A to the Pierson-Moskowitz variance of the
  same wind makes `model` a shape choice with the same energy, and keeps rule 13's variance test meaningful for
  all four.
- **A normal draw per cell from the lattice.** The amplitudes are Gaussian by the theory of linear waves; drawing
  them from G at pack(i, j, 0) of the sea's seed reproduces the identical sea in every engine, the point of the
  random-streams draft. Channels 1 and 2 leave channel 0 and the channels above 2 to later uses (a swell sea, a
  second octave).
- **Foam from the Jacobian with a bounded memory.** Tessendorf's criterion is J < 0; graphics practice fades the
  foam in below a threshold near 1 and lets it linger. A maximum over a window of three decay times keeps the
  lingering and the seekability; an unbounded accumulation would need the frames before.
- **The sea over the solver.** Letting the spectrum drive the solver's water would turn 65 536 modes into
  boundary conditions of a shallow-water grid with its own dispersion; the sum of the two keeps SREP 40's
  interactions and the sea's look, as the production pipelines do (an FFT ocean with a simulated splash layer).
- **Exact Fresnel and the Pope-Fry absorption.** Schlick's approximation is 1 % off at normal incidence; the
  exact form is three lines. The absorption triple is the measured pure-water spectrum at the three channel
  wavelengths; the scattering default is of the order of clear ocean water and is an authoring knob, the
  reference does not fix it from a source.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Gerstner waves as the sea.** A sum of a few Gerstner waves is SREP 40's `wave` already; it does not carry a
  spectrum's energy distribution.
- **A smooth small-wave damping instead of `cutoff`.** Tessendorf's exp(−k² ℓ²) blurs the band's edge; a
  wavelength floor states what is dropped and is what the Houdini spectrum exposes.
- **The Mitsuyasu or Donelan-Banner frequency-dependent spreading.** More faithful to measured seas and in
  Horvath's review; one exponent is the parameter the tools expose. Open issue 2.
- **Per-frame foam particles.** SREP 40's whitewater tracers exist; the sea's foam is a coverage so that it is
  seekable and cheap, and it joins the tracers through SREP 79's albedo mode.
- **Caustics by a fixed method.** Photon maps, caustic grids and light tracing all conserve the transmitted
  energy, which is what the reference checks; the method is the engine's.

## Backwards compatibility

- Every document valid under 1.7 is valid under 1.8: `sea` and the seven attributes are new and optional.
- Documents of version 1.7 or earlier may not use them (V17); their oceans render as before.
- An ocean without `sea` and with the seven attributes at their defaults changes in one way: the water below the
  surface is a medium with the default absorption and scattering, so deep water darkens and turns blue where it
  was the material's uniform colour. SREP 40's `material` still shades the surface; `ior` replaces its index.

## Open issues

1. **Two seas.** A swell from a distant storm and the local wind sea have different spectra and directions;
   Houdini layers several spectra. Allowing several `sea` children, summed, is the natural extension; it needs
   a rule for the shared foam and the channels of the second sea.
2. **Frequency-dependent spreading.** The Mitsuyasu, Hasselmann and Donelan-Banner spreadings in Horvath 2015
   narrow at the peak and widen away from it; `spread` is one exponent.
3. **Capillary waves.** The spectra end at the grid's Nyquist; the wind's ripples below a few centimetres,
   which carry the glitter, are not modelled. A second small grid or an analytic roughness is a candidate.
4. **Depth variation.** TMA uses the ocean's single `bottomDepth`; a sea over a bathymetry shoals and refracts
   toward the shore, which a periodic spectrum cannot do. The solver of SREP 40 does, and the blend of the two
   near the shore is an open design.
5. **The water's phase function.** g = 0.924 is a fixed constant; measured waters range from clear to turbid
   and a `phase` or turbidity attribute may follow once the media draft settles its parameters.
6. **Caustics on bodies and from the sun only.** Rule 16 names the bed and the bodies in the water; caustics
   from area lights and the moon, and caustics seen from under water on the surface itself, are left to the
   engine's integrator.
7. **Flow-map authoring.** A `flowMap` is painted; a flow derived from the solver's velocity field (SREP 40
   has it) would animate rivers without an asset.
8. **Conformance in other engines.** Blender's ocean modifier (Phillips with its own scale), Houdini's ocean
   spectrum (JONSWAP, TMA and Phillips with Horvath's spreadings) and HDRP's water system draw their amplitudes
   from their own generators; a document's sea renders there with the same spectrum and statistics but not
   the same waves, which the kit cases accept by measuring variance and fractions.
