# SREP 0 — Add computed masks and depth

## Abstract

A computed form of the `imageSequence` asset, SREP 12's milestone 7 (LA2): `provider="masks"` with one
`segment` child, or `provider="depth"` with one `estimate` child, states how a matte sequence or a depth
sequence is computed from a `footage` asset, and a resolve step, never rendering, runs it and writes one 16-bit
PGM per frame under the asset's `src` pattern plus a manifest that lists every frame's `sha256`, the manifest
being pinned by the asset's own `sha256` (the pattern SREP 9 set for tiles and the computed-tracking draft
reused for tracks). Mattes come from the engine's `chroma` key and `difference` key, from `region` growing out
of `seeds` with a linear colour model on the boundary (rotoscoping that follows the object), and from `roto`
polygons keyed in time; `expansion` and `feather` apply as on `maskType`. Depth comes from the `plane` of a
tracked target through a solved camera (the computed-tracking draft) and from `stereo` block matching between
two footage assets with a known `baseline`. The reference tests are the ones SREP 12 names, intersection over
union against synthetic mattes, with the floor 0.95 per frame; the draft adds the depth within 2 % of a
synthetic truth, the deterministic cache, the seek check of SREP 14 and the validation rules MD1–MD4.
Monocular depth from a learned model is left as an open issue because the conformance suite cannot pin it.

## Motivation

SREP 12 row LA2 asks for "segmentation masks for rotoscoping and monocular depth as resolved assets", measured
by "mask IoU against synthetic mattes". At 67a661a a matte is a node drawn by hand (`maskType`, a track matte
through `matte`) or a keyed layer (`chroma-key`, `luma-key`, `difference-key` effects, `effects.wgsl` cases 71
to 73), and depth exists only inside the renderer. A documentary cut that wants a person isolated from a plate,
a title behind them, or a fog that thickens with distance has to bring a matte or a depth pass from another
tool as an image sequence. This draft keeps the document the source and the cache derived: the segmentation
or the estimate is stated once, the resolve step computes the frames, and every render reads the same pinned
bytes, offline (SREP 9) and as a pure function of the frame (SREP 14).

## Specification

### Syntax

A diff for schema 1.8 against `schema/scene-render.xsd` at 1.7.1 (line numbers of that file) and the
computed-tracking draft; the schema files are not changed by this draft.

- `imageSequenceAssetType` (1232) gains:
  - `provider` (one of `masks`, `depth`, optional). Without it the sequence is imported as today. With it the
    sequence is computed: `footage` (`xs:IDREF`, the footage asset it is computed from) becomes required, `src`
    is the frame pattern the resolve step writes (printf padding or `#` digits, as today), `first` numbers the
    first written frame, `last` is filled by the resolve step, `fps` defaults to the footage's rate, and the
    frames are 16-bit PGM files (`P5`, maxval 65535).
  - `sha256` (`sha256Type`, optional): the digest of the manifest the resolve step writes next to the frames
    (`manifest_path`: the pattern's number field replaced by `manifest`, extension `.json`); the manifest lists
    every frame file and its own `sha256`. A different digest, of the manifest or of a frame, is error A02.
  - `depthScale` (`positiveDecimal`, metres at full scale, default 65.535: one unit is a millimetre) for a
    `depth` sequence.
  - a `segment` child, exactly one when `provider="masks"`, with `method` (one of `chroma`, `difference`,
    `region`, `roto`, required), `expansion` (`xs:integer`, −64 to 64, default 0) and `feather`
    (`xs:nonNegativeInteger`, at most 64, default 0), and:
    - `chroma`: `keyColor` (`colorType`, default green), `tolerance` and `softness` (`unitDecimal`, defaults
      0.2 and 0.1), the attributes of the `chroma-key` effect.
    - `difference`: `clean` (`xs:IDREF`, the clean-plate image asset, required), `tolerance`, `softness`.
    - `region`: one or more `seed` children with `x`, `y` (`xs:double`, pixels on the first frame) and
      `tolerance` (`unitDecimal`, above 0, default 0.1).
    - `roto`: one or more `key` children with `frame` (`xs:integer`, increasing) and three or more `point`
      children with `x`, `y`, every key with the same number of points.
  - an `estimate` child, exactly one when `provider="depth"`, with `method` (one of `plane`, `stereo`,
    required), `fov` (`xs:double`, horizontal degrees strictly between 0 and 180, required), and:
    - `plane`: `camera` (`xs:IDREF`, a `trackData` of kind `camera`, required).
    - `stereo`: `right` (`xs:IDREF`, the right footage asset, required), `baseline` (`positiveDecimal`,
      metres, required), `window` (`xs:positiveInteger`, odd, 3 to 31, default 7), `maxDisparity`
      (`xs:positiveInteger`, 1 to 256, default 64).
- Schematron:
  - **MD1** an `imageSequence` with `provider` has `footage`, a `src` with a frame number field, exactly one
    `segment` when `provider="masks"` and exactly one `estimate` when `provider="depth"`; without `provider`
    it has neither child.
  - **MD2** a `segment` has `tolerance` and `softness` in [0, 1], `expansion` in [−64, 64], `feather` at most
    64; `region` has at least one `seed`; `roto` has keys in increasing frame order with the same three or
    more points each.
  - **MD3** an `estimate` has `fov` strictly between 0 and 180; `plane` names `camera`; `stereo` names
    `right`, has a positive `baseline`, an odd `window` 3 to 31 and `maxDisparity` 1 to 256.
  - **MD4** `depthScale` is positive and `sha256` is 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits.
  - **V17** `provider`, `depthScale`, `segment` and `estimate` on `imageSequence` need
    `scene/@version="1.8"` or later.

### Semantics

The reference module (`conformance/tools/computed_masks_reference.py`) names the function of each rule.
Pixels are the footage's, x right and y down, with pixel (i, j) spanning i − 0.5 to i + 0.5; a matte is a
coverage in [0, 1] per pixel; a depth is the camera-space z in metres; the camera is the computed-tracking
draft's (`intrinsics`, `euler_to_rotation`).

1. **The resolve step.** `resolve(doc, frames)` runs the `segment` or the `estimate` on the frames of the
   `footage` and returns the manifest, its digest and the frame files; the resolve step writes the frames to
   `frame_path(src, first + k)`, the manifest to `manifest_path(src)`, and sets `sha256` to the manifest's
   digest, as SREP 9's resolve step fills `cache` and `cacheSha256` for tiles. A frame is `pgm16`: a binary
   PGM, maxval 65535, big-endian, every value rounded to the nearest unit of the full scale (1 for a matte,
   `depthScale` for depth) and clipped; the manifest is `{"fps", "frames": [{"file", "sha256"}], "kind",
   "scale"}` with sorted keys, no spaces, one line ended by a newline, so that the same document and footage
   give the same bytes. Rendering never segments or estimates: it reads the frames at `src`, checks the
   manifest against `sha256` and each frame against the manifest (`verify_cache`; a different digest is error
   A02), and a computed sequence whose cache is absent is the error of a missing frame under `missingFrame`.
2. **Keys.** `chroma` keeps, per pixel, `chroma_key_coverage` = smoothstep(`tolerance`, `tolerance` +
   `softness`, d) with d the distance between the pixel and `keyColor` in the Cb-Cr plane divided by 0.7
   (`chroma_distance`: Cb = −0.1146 R − 0.3854 G + 0.5 B, Cr = 0.5 R − 0.4542 G − 0.0458 B on the encoded
   values), the formula of the `chroma-key` effect (`effects.wgsl` case 71). `difference` keeps
   `difference_coverage` = smoothstep(`tolerance`, `tolerance` + `softness`, |c − clean|) with the RGB
   distance to the clean plate's pixel (case 73). A keyer reads a mixed edge pixel as foreground once it is
   past the tolerance, so a keyed matte is a fraction of a pixel fat; measured on synthetic plates
   (`synthetic_matte_plate`: a textured or flat shape over the key colour or a textured plate, the truth matte
   by 4 × 4 supersampled coverage) the lowest intersection over union over four frames is 1 for the chroma key
   on a 64 × 48 plate and 0.971 for the difference key on a 128 × 96 plate.
3. **Region growing.** `region_matte` grows the 4-connected set of pixels within `tolerance` (RGB distance)
   of the mean colour of the `seeds` (`region_grow`), then gives every pixel next to the region the coverage
   of a linear colour model (Smith and Blinn 1996): α = ((c − b) · (f − b)) / |f − b|² clamped to [0, 1], with
   f the region's colour and b the colour two pixels further out from the region. On every later frame the
   seeds move by the shift of the matte's `centroid`, so the region follows the object; a region that vanishes
   is an error. Measured lowest IoU 0.951 over four frames of a flat disc of radius 10 px moving 3 px per
   frame over a textured plate.
4. **Roto.** `roto_polygon` interpolates the key polygons linearly between the two nearest `key`s and clamps
   outside them; `rasterise` gives the polygon's coverage by 4 × 4 supersampling under the even-odd rule.
   Measured IoU 1 against the same polygon drawn by the plate generator.
5. **Expansion and feather.** `expand` is the grayscale dilation (positive) or erosion (negative) by a square
   of half-side |`expansion`|; `feather` is the box blur of radius `feather` with zero outside the frame.
   They apply in that order after every method, as `maskType` applies them.
6. **Depth from a plane.** `plane_depth` casts the ray of every pixel through the camera of the frame (the
   row of the named camera track: `tx`, `ty`, `tz`, `rx`, `ry`, `rz`) and returns the camera-space depth of
   its intersection with the target plane z = 0, or 65.535 (`DEPTH_FAR`, the full scale) where the ray misses
   or the plane is edge-on. A frontal plane at 2 m reads 2 everywhere.
7. **Depth from stereo.** `stereo_disparity` matches, for every left pixel, the `window` against the right
   frame at disparities 0 to `maxDisparity` by the sum of absolute differences, refines the best integer by a
   one-dimensional Lucas-Kanade step on the bilinearly sampled right row (Lucas and Kanade 1981), and marks a
   pixel unmatched (disparity 0) where the right frame's own match disagrees by more than one pixel, the
   left-right consistency check of Scharstein and Szeliski (2002), which catches occlusions; depth is
   `disparity_to_depth` = f `baseline` / d with f the focal length from `fov`, far where unmatched. On the
   synthetic pair (`synthetic_stereo`: a frontal plane at 4 m and a disc at 2 m, textured in world
   coordinates, baseline 0.3 m) the 95th percentile of the relative error over matched pixels away from the
   border and from the disc's edge is 0.57 % (`depth_error`), within 2 %, and no such pixel is unmatched
   (`unmatched_fraction`).
8. **Frames as pure functions.** The cache is written once and a frame reads only its file, so frame n
   rendered alone equals frame n of a run (SREP 14). The seek check `seek_test` resolves the same document
   twice, compares the bytes, and reads every frame back from its PGM within half a unit of the segmentation.
9. **Validation.** `validate_asset` applies MD1 and MD4, `validate_segment` MD2 and `validate_estimate` MD3;
   invalid documents raise `MaskError` with the rule's wording.

## Rationale

- **Resolve, not render.** As for tracks: a frame must not depend on a segmentation of every earlier frame
  or on the version of a model; the SREP 9 pattern keeps SREP 14's seek semantics and the offline guarantee.
- **PGM frames and a manifest.** A matte or a depth is one channel at 16 bits; PGM is the smallest format
  every reader has, needs no library, and a per-frame digest in a manifest lets rendering verify one frame
  without hashing the sequence. The asset's `sha256` pins the manifest, so one attribute pins everything.
- **The engine's own key formulas.** A `chroma` matte and the `chroma-key` effect must agree pixel for pixel,
  or the same document would key one way as an effect and another as a matte.
- **A linear colour model on the boundary.** Region growing alone gives a binary matte and the IoU of a small
  shape falls below 0.95; the two-colour model gives the mixed pixels their coverage from the two colours the
  frame shows, with no training and no parameter.
- **Plane and stereo before monocular.** Both are exact or near-exact on synthetic data, so the conformance
  suite can measure them; a learned monocular estimate is neither reproducible across platforms nor
  verifiable against a truth the document defines (open issue 1).

## Rejected alternatives

- **A `matte` node that keys at render time.** Rejected: it exists already as the key effects and a track
  matte; what the row asks for is a resolved asset that other nodes read, including the depth that no effect
  can produce.
- **EXR float frames.** Rejected for the draft: a 16-bit PGM with a millimetre unit covers 65 m and keeps the
  reference free of libraries; EXR is open issue 2.
- **Trimap matting (Bayesian or closed-form).** Rejected: the shape's boundary band is already given by region
  growing and the linear model needs no solver; a trimap solver is a later provider.
- **Semi-global matching.** Rejected: block matching with a consistency check meets the 2 % target on the
  synthetic pair; smoothness terms would be tuning without a measurable gain here.

## Backwards compatibility

A 1.7 document is valid and unchanged in meaning: `provider`, `depthScale`, `segment` and `estimate` are new
and optional, and an `imageSequence` without `provider` is read as today. Documents using them require version
1.8 (V17). The draft depends on the computed-tracking draft for the camera track of the `plane` method and
on SREP 9 for the resolve pattern.

## Open issues

1. **Monocular depth.** LA2 names monocular depth; a `method="model"` naming an external network and the
   `sha256` of its weights fits the resolve pattern, but no conformance case can state its truth, and its
   output differs across platforms and library versions. The editor decides whether to admit it as an
   unverified provider, pinned only by the cache's digest, or to keep depth to measurable methods.
2. **Float depth.** 16-bit PGM at a millimetre unit covers 65.535 m; astronomy and landscape shots need
   float EXR frames and a `far` beyond the full scale.
3. **Where the manifest lives.** The draft derives its path from the pattern; a `manifest` attribute would be
   explicit at the cost of one more pinned path.
4. **Edge quality.** The linear colour model takes the background colour two pixels out; textured or
   motion-blurred edges need a trimap solver, and the IoU floor on shapes under 30 px is tight (0.951 measured
   for a 10 px disc).
5. **Occlusion filling.** Unmatched stereo pixels read far; filling them from the nearer neighbour (the usual
   background fill) is a choice for the editor.
6. **Frame rate.** `fps` defaults to the footage's rate; a `video` footage with a variable rate needs the
   resolve step to state the rate it sampled at.
