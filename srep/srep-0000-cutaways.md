# SREP 0 — Add cutaways, exploded views and surface flow to 3D objects

## Abstract

Three children of `object3D` for the explanatory shots of SREP 12's milestone 4 (S5): `section`, a plane that
removes the half of the object in front of it and caps the cut with a face that lies on the plane, hatched if
asked; `explode`, an exploded view that moves the parts of an assembly away from its centre by an animatable
amount, so that amount 0 is the assembled object exactly; and `flow`, flow lines of a vector field drawn over
the object's surface by the fields draft's evenly spaced streamlines, the field's tangent part integrated on
the surface. Every construction is a pure function of the document's values at the frame, so the seek check
(SREP 14) holds by construction. The reference module `conformance/tools/cutaways_reference.py` states each
rule as code and prints the numeric table of the Conformance section.

## Motivation

- **The catalogue has the shot.** SREP 12 line 298 lists S5 as "section planes with capped cuts, exploded
  assemblies, flow lines over 3D parts (MF5)" with the reference tests "cut faces lie on the plane; exploded
  parts return exactly" (the two bars of this draft: cut faces lie on the plane, exploded parts return exactly),
  and shot 30 (line 618, the J58 nacelle cutaway of Real Engineering, with flow and
  shock lines through a moving inlet spike) is scored a gap. The schema at 1.7.1 has no clip plane, section,
  cap, explode or surface-flow construct (grep of `schema/scene-render.xsd` and of the engine's `crates/`).
- **An author writes them by hand today.** A cutaway is modelled as two meshes, one for each state, and the
  cap is a third; an exploded view is one animated transform per part, with no notion of a centre; flow lines
  over a part are `parametricPath` elements on the surface's projection. None of these animate as one value.
- **The constructions have closed-form checks.** A capped cut of a closed mesh is closed and its two sides
  sum to the whole volume; an exploded view at amount 0 is the identity exactly; the tangent flow of a uniform
  field on a sphere is a meridian of known length, and a rotational field's line is a latitude circle that
  closes. These are the numeric tests below.

### Semantics

The reference is `conformance/tools/cutaways_reference.py`; the function named in each rule is the normative
statement. Meshes are tuples of triangles wound counter-clockwise seen from outside; the object's own space is
used throughout, so the object's transform applies after every rule here.

1. **The section plane** (`plane`, `signed_distance`). The plane through (`x`, `y`, `z`) with unit normal n,
   moved by `offset` along n; the signed distance of a point p is (p − point) · n − `offset`, positive on the
   removed side. `keep="front"` negates both n and `offset`. A vertex whose distance is within 10⁻¹² of the
   mesh's bounding-box diagonal of the plane counts as on it (`_snap`). The planes of one object apply in
   document order, each to the result of the one before, so the removed region is the union of their fronts
   (rule 7 of the Rationale states the corner cutaway this does not express).
2. **The cut** (`section`, `clipped`). Every triangle is clipped by Sutherland–Hodgman to its kept polygon
   (`_clip_triangle`): a vertex is kept when its distance is at most 0; the crossing point of an edge whose
   ends are strictly on opposite sides is computed from the edge's lexicographically ordered ends
   (`_crossing`), so the two triangles of the edge get the identical point; the polygon is fanned from its
   first vertex, zero-area triangles dropped. A triangle lying in the plane is kept only when its normal has a
   positive component along n (it is the solid's own face there). The cap loops are the kept polygons' edges
   whose ends are both on the plane, after every edge that two kept polygons share in opposite directions has
   cancelled (`_clip_mesh`, `_loops`); they are chained from the lexicographically least start, and reversed,
   so the cap faces the removed side like the walls face outward. With `cap="true"` the loops are triangulated
   on the plane (`triangulate`): in the plane's basis (U, V) with U × V = n, a loop of positive area is an
   outer boundary and one of negative area a hole; each hole is bridged into the smallest outer loop that
   contains its first vertex (even-odd) by Eberly's visible-vertex rule (`_bridge`: the ray from the hole's
   rightmost vertex, the nearest outer edge it meets, that edge's rightmost end, or the reflex vertex of least
   angle inside the triangle they span), and the merged polygon is ear-clipped (`_ear_clip`: an ear is a
   strictly convex vertex whose triangle contains no other vertex, boundary included, so a collinear vertex
   on an edge is never spanned). The cut of a closed mesh is closed and its two sides sum to the whole
   volume (`volume_test`, `plane_test`); a plane that touches the mesh only from outside returns the mesh
   unchanged, one with the whole mesh in front returns nothing (`identity_test`). An open mesh gets no cap.
3. **Hatching** (`hatch`). Lines in the plane at `hatchAngle` degrees from U, at signed offsets (k + ½) ·
   `hatchSpacing` from the plane's point along the in-plane normal, for every integer k whose line meets the
   loops' extent; each line is clipped to the cap by the even-odd rule over the loops' edges (an edge counts
   when exactly one end is at or below the line's offset), the crossings sorted along the line and paired.
   `cross` adds the family at `hatchAngle` + 90°. Lines are drawn with `strokeStyle` on the cap; at most 100 000
   lines per cap.
4. **Parts and the centre** (`part_centre`, `assembly_centre`). A part is a node of the model with its
   triangles in the object's space; its centre is the centre of its bounds; the assembly centre is the centre
   of the bounds of all parts, or (`centerX`, `centerY`, `centerZ`) when given.
5. **Displacement** (`displacements`, `explode`). With r the part's centre minus the assembly centre (or its
   `direction` when given) the part moves by `amount` × `factor` × r in `radial` mode, by `amount` × `factor` ×
   (r · a) a along the unit axis a in `axis` mode, and by `amount` × `factor` × `spacing` × rank × r̂ in `rank`
   mode. The displacement is linear in `amount`, so amount 0 is the identity exactly (`return_test`); the test
   `separation_test` states that two touching boxes separate at the first amount above the closed-form one.
6. **Rank** (`ranks`). Parts are ranked by the distance of their centres from the assembly centre, densely:
   equal distances share a rank and the nearest part has rank 0, so it does not move.
7. **Order** (`cutaway`). Sections apply after the explosion, to each displaced part; a part wholly in front
   of a plane is not drawn.
8. **The tangent field** (`tangent`). At a surface point p with unit normal n the flow's direction is v − (v ·
   n) n, where v is the field at p (an error when it is not finite). The surface is the object's own mesh;
   the reference's surfaces (`sphere_surface`, `plane_surface`, `cylinder_surface`) give the nearest point
   and the normal in closed form, and the engine uses the nearest triangle and its normal.
9. **A flow line** (`flow_line`, `_grow`). From the seed's nearest surface point, each step moves `step` along
   the unit tangent direction by classical Runge–Kutta, every stage point projected to the surface and the
   step's end projected (`_advance`); when a stage finds the tangent part vanished or pointing against the
   first stage, the step falls back to the Euler step into that stagnation point. The line ends at a
   stagnation point (tangent part below 10⁻¹² in length), where the direction reverses between two steps, when
   a point comes within `step` / 2 of one of its own points at least three steps earlier (a closure), or at
   `maxSteps`. A uniform field on the unit sphere runs down a meridian from 30° to the south pole in arc
   length 5π/6 within 2 × 10⁻³ (`meridian_test`); a rotational field's line on the sphere is a latitude circle
   that closes with its height kept within 10⁻¹¹ (`latitude_test`); a uniform field on a plane or along a
   cylinder's axis runs straight to 10⁻¹² (`straight_test`).
10. **Evenly spaced lines** (`flow_lines`, Jobard and Lefer). The first line grows from the seed forward and
    backward; a line stops when its next point is within `test` of a point of another line. Candidate seeds
    lie to either side of every point of every line in order, at the straight distance `separation` from the
    point along n × t (the offset along the surface found by bisection, `_offset`, since the projection pulls
    a curved surface's point back); a candidate is taken when no point of any line is nearer than
    `separation` (within 10⁻⁹ relative). A line of fewer than two points is not kept; at most `maxLines`
    lines. On the plane with a uniform field and the box −1 to 1 this is the family of 11 parallels at
    spacing 0.2 (`spacing_test`); on the sphere the meridians keep at least `test` − `step` apart.
11. **Bounds.** `MAX_SECTIONS` 8, `MAX_TRIANGLES` 4 194 304 per object, `MAX_PARTS` 4096, `MAX_HATCH` 100 000,
    `MAX_STEPS` 1 000 000 and `MAX_LINES` 1 000 000 are `CutawayError`s; a negative `amount`, a zero normal, a
    zero hatch spacing, a non-positive step, `test` above `separation` and a field that is not finite are
    errors, never silently clamped.
12. **The seek check.** Sections, explosions and flow lines are functions of the frame's `offset`, `amount`,
    `time` and the document's values, with no state between frames; `seek_test` renders the same cutaway twice
    with another in between and compares the results exactly.

## Rationale

- **Sequential planes, not a CSG tree.** A sequence of half-space cuts keeps every construction a clip of a
  triangle by a plane, with the closedness proof in rule 2; a boolean tree needs a BSP and robust predicates.
  The classic quarter cutaway (remove the intersection of two fronts, not the union) is the corner case left
  to open issue 1, with its construction sketched there.
- **Cap faces on the plane by construction.** The cap's vertices are the loop points themselves, which are
  crossing points or on-plane vertices, so "cut faces lie on the plane" is not a tolerance but an identity;
  the only tolerance is `_snap`'s classification of vertices.
- **Canonical crossing points.** Computing a crossing from the edge's ordered ends makes the two polygons that
  share the edge agree bit for bit, which is what makes the cut closed and the loops chain without a
  tolerance.
- **Ear clipping with bridges, not a Delaunay cap.** The cap must share its boundary edges with the walls
  exactly; ear clipping uses the loop's vertices and no others, and bridging holes keeps that property, since
  the two copies of a bridge cancel in the edge count.
- **Amount 0 is the identity by arithmetic.** The displacement is amount × a vector, and adding 0 × r to a
  coordinate returns the coordinate; no epsilon and no special case.
- **Dense ranks.** With ties sharing a rank, a symmetric assembly explodes symmetrically; a sparse rank would
  move two equal parts by different amounts.
- **Runge–Kutta on the projected field.** A tangent-and-project Euler step drifts a latitude circle by a few
  per cent per turn; the midpoint rule by 10⁻⁴; classical Runge–Kutta with every stage on the surface keeps
  the height to 2 × 10⁻¹² (the table's `latitude drift`), which is what makes the closure test meaningful.
- **Seeds at the chord.** Jobard–Lefer's spacing is a straight distance in the plane; on a curved surface the
  natural offset along the tangent lands closer than `separation` after projection and every candidate would
  be refused, so the offset is solved for the chord.
- **Object units for the flow.** The fields draft measures `separation` in pixels because its lines live in a
  2D box; on a surface the lines are geometry and animate with the object, so the units are the object's.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Clip planes in the shader only.** A fragment-side clip gives the hole but no cap, hatch or closed volume
  (the inside of the object shows its back faces); the cap is the explanatory content of a cutaway.
- **Explode as per-part animation.** Authors can already animate each node's transform; the construction
  here is one value with a centre, so the whole assembly opens and closes together and the drawing at
  amount 0 is the assembled model exactly.
- **Geodesic separation.** Measuring `separation` along the surface (exponential map) needs geodesics on a
  triangle mesh; the chord is what the viewer sees and what the spatial hash can test.
- **Shock lines from a solver.** Shot 30's shock lines need a compressible flow solver; this draft draws
  lines of a field the author states (expressions or a force field), and the solver is a later milestone.

## Backwards compatibility

Additive: three new optional children of `object3D` and one new Schematron pattern; documents at 1.7 and
earlier are unchanged. The constructs are refused below version 1.8 (V17).

## Open issues

1. **The corner cutaway.** Removing the intersection of two fronts (a quarter cut) is not a sequence of
   half-space cuts. The construction that keeps the result closed: clip every triangle into the cells of the
   two planes' arrangement (back/back, back/front, front/back), keep all three, take the cap on plane 1 from
   the cells behind plane 2 and the cap on plane 2 from the cells behind plane 1, and close both caps' chains
   along the planes' intersection line with segments computed once per triangle from the line. A `mode`
   attribute on a `section` group would select it; the editor should decide whether S5 needs it.
2. **Open meshes.** An imported model that is not closed gets walls and no cap; whether the engine should warn
   (a render-report finding) is open.
3. **Nearest point on a mesh.** Rule 8 uses the nearest triangle's normal; across a crease the normal jumps and
   a flow line may stall at the crease (a stagnation by rule 9). Smooth vertex normals interpolated over the
   triangle would avoid it and are not stated here.
4. **Explode of nested nodes.** A node's part includes its descendants' triangles; a `part` on a child moves
   the child relative to the already moved parent. The draft states the nesting as composition and the
   reference tests flat assemblies only.
5. **Force fields in 3D.** `forceField` of `physics` is 2D (SREP 11); `w` = 0 is stated and a 3D force field
   is not.
