# SREP 0 — Add deep sky: procedural galaxies, N-body mergers and emission-line nebulae

## Abstract

[SREP 12](srep-0012.md) names, as its SP8 building block, "procedural galaxies (density-wave arms, Sérsic
bulge, dust lanes), N-body mergers, nebulae with emission-line palettes, through A2", with the bar "galaxy
profiles follow their exponential and Sérsic laws" (line 288); nothing of it has landed. This SREP adds a
`galaxy` node: a seeded catalogue of stars whose disk follows the exponential law in radius and sech² in
height, whose bulge follows the Sérsic law, whose azimuths follow logarithmic spiral arms, and whose
brightness is dimmed by dust lanes inside the arms. A `dynamics` child turns the catalogue into N-body
particles on the galaxy's rotation curve, stepped by a softened leapfrog with a fixed step count per frame, so
a frame alone equals the frame played; a `merger` child places a second galaxy on approach with the centre of
mass at rest. A `nebula` is an A2 medium (the volumes draft) whose emission is the sum of its emission lines
through a palette: natural colours by wavelength, or the SHO and HOO mappings of astrophotography. The plan's
bar is measured by `conformance/tools/deepsky_reference.py`: the catalogues follow their laws with a
Kolmogorov statistic of 0.0071 over 20000 stars, and b_n solves the half-light condition exactly.

## Motivation

- **Shot 14** (SREP 12 line 602) needs a star field behind a black hole and **shots 17 and 18** (lines 605,
  606) a starry void and a zoom out past stars: a procedural galaxy seen from inside or outside gives the
  three a sky that is a function of a seed, not a texture.
- **The laws are the look.** An exponential disk with a Sérsic bulge, two arms of 15° pitch and dust lanes is
  what a spiral looks like; a uniform disc of points is not. The histogram tests make the look checkable.
- **Mergers are the motion.** The plan's N-body merger is the one deep-sky shot that moves; a softened
  leapfrog with a fixed step count keeps it a function of the frame number ([SREP 14](srep-0014.md) rule 1).
- **Nebulae are palettes.** Hα, [O III] and [S II] are what the telescopes record; the SHO (Hubble) and HOO
  mappings are how the pictures are coloured. Stating both makes two engines agree on a nebula's colour.

### Semantics

The disk lies in the node's xz plane with its normal +y; r is the cylindrical radius, θ the azimuth from +x
toward +z; G = 1 in the dynamics. The names are the functions of `conformance/tools/deepsky_reference.py`.

1. **The exponential disk** (`exponential_profile`, `exponential_enclosed`). Σ(r) = Σ₀ exp(−r/h) with
   h = `scaleLength`; the mass within r is 1 − (1 + r/h) exp(−r/h). The vertical profile is sech²(y/z₀) with
   z₀ = `scaleHeight` (`sech2`).
2. **The Sérsic bulge** (`gamma_p`, `sersic_b`, `sersic_profile`, `sersic_enclosed`). I(r) = I_e exp(−b_n((r/R_e)^{1/n} − 1))
   with n = `sersicIndex`, R_e = `effectiveRadius`; the light within r is P(2n, b_n (r/R_e)^{1/n}), the
   regularised lower incomplete gamma function, and b_n is the root of P(2n, b_n) = ½ so that R_e holds half
   the light: 0.693147 (n = ½), 1.678347 (n = 1), 7.669249 (n = 4). At n = 1 the enclosed light is the
   exponential law with h = R_e / b₁. The bulge's stars take this law as their radial law in three dimensions
   (open issue 1).
3. **Radii** (`sample_disk_radius`, `sample_bulge_radius`). A star's radius is the inverse of its component's
   enclosed law at the uniform draw u, by bisection; the disk is truncated at 20 scale lengths and the bulge at
   100 effective radii (a draw beyond the truncation gives the truncation radius).
4. **Heights** (`sample_height`). y = z₀ atanh(2u − 1), the inverse of the sech² law; bulge stars take a
   uniform direction on the sphere instead.
5. **Arms** (`arm_phase`, `arm_density`, `azimuth_cdf`, `sample_azimuth`). The ridge of m = `count` arms at
   radius r is at azimuth φ(r) = ln(r/r₀)/tan(pitch) with r₀ = `arms/@radius`; the disk's density around the ring
   is modulated by 1 + A cos(m(θ − φ(r))), A = `amplitude`; a star's azimuth is the inverse at the draw u of the
   ring's cumulative fraction (θ + (A/m)(sin(m(θ − φ)) + sin(mφ)))/2π. At A = 0 the azimuth is 2πu.
6. **Dust lanes** (`dust_transmission`). A lane runs `dustOffset` radians inside each ridge; a disk star's
   brightness is multiplied by exp(−τ exp(−d²/2w²)) with τ = `dust`, w = `dustWidth` and d the azimuthal
   distance to the nearest lane: exp(−τ) on the lane, 1 between the arms.
7. **Colours** (`cie_xyz`, `xyz_to_rgb`, `wavelength_colour`, `planck`, `blackbody_colour`). A star's colour is
   the linear sRGB colour of a blackbody at its component's `temperature` times 0.8 + 0.4u, scaled so its
   largest channel is 1: (1, 0.484, 0.154) at 3000 K, (1, 0.88, 0.823) at 5778 K, (0.363, 0.497, 1) at 30000 K;
   the CIE 1931 functions are the multi-lobe fit of Wyman, Sloan and Shirley (2013) and the Planck spectrum is
   summed at 5 nm over 380 to 780 nm.
8. **The catalogue** (`star`, `galaxy_stars`, `profile_error`). Star i draws S(e, p, 0, i) of the random-streams
   draft with e = E(project, id, seed, "stars") and purposes component 0, radius 1, angle 2, height 3, colour 4,
   brightness 5; the component is the bulge when the component draw times (disk share + bulge share) is below the
   bulge share, else the disk; brightness is (0.25 + 1.5u³) times the dust transmission. A star is a function of
   (e, i) and of the galaxy's attributes, so the first n stars of a galaxy of `stars="m"` are those of
   `stars="n"` for n ≤ m, and the catalogue is the same at every frame (the seek check). The Kolmogorov
   statistic between the sorted radii of 20000 stars and their law is 0.007123 for the disk and for the bulge
   (the same draws invert both laws); with arms of amplitude 0.6, 10003 of 20000 disk stars sit where the
   modulation exceeds 1.3 and 3365 where it is below 0.7.
9. **The rotation curve and the bodies** (`rotation_speed`, `galaxy_bodies`). v_c²(r) = M(disk share × disk
   enclosed(r) + bulge share × bulge enclosed(r))/r + v_h² r²/(r² + r_c²), the enclosed mass taken as spherical
   and the halo a cored isothermal sphere with v_h = `halo/@speed`, r_c = `coreRadius`; the galaxy's first
   `bodies` stars become particles of mass M/`bodies` on circular orbits at v_c of their cylindrical radius. With
   the table's galaxy v_c is 1.09 at r = 1 and 1.01 at r = 40 (v_h = 1).
10. **The N-body and mergers** (`accelerations`, `energy`, `momentum`, `nbody_run`, `_centred`, `merger_bodies`).
    Accelerations are the direct sum with Plummer softening ε: Σ G m_j d/(|d|² + ε²)^{3/2}; a step is
    kick–drift–kick leapfrog of `step`; frame f is the run of f × `stepsPerFrame` steps from frame 0, so the
    state is a function of the frame alone (the seek check) and a run of 40 steps equals two runs of 20. A
    two-body circular orbit closes within 1.7 × 10⁻⁴ after 500 steps of one period and 10⁻⁵ after 2000; four
    bodies over 1000 steps drift in energy by 3.8 × 10⁻⁶ and keep their momentum. A merger centres each galaxy
    (centre of mass and total momentum zero), places them `separation` apart along x and `impact` apart along
    z, scaled by the mass ratios so the centre of mass is at the origin, and boosts them by `speed` along x in
    the same ratios so it stays at rest.
11. **Emission lines and palettes** (`LINES`, `PALETTES`, `palette_colour`, `nebula_emission`). Hα 656.28, Hβ
    486.13, [O III] 500.7, [S II] 672.4, [N II] 658.3 nm. `natural` gives each line its wavelength's colour
    ((1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0.323) and (0, 0.536, 1) for Hα, [O III] and Hβ); `sho` maps [S II] to red, Hα and [N II]
    to green, [O III] and Hβ to blue; `hoo` maps Hα, [N II] and [S II] to red and [O III] and Hβ to cyan. The
    nebula's emission colour is Σ strength × palette colour; a negative strength, an unknown species or palette
    or more than 16 lines is an error.
12. **The nebula as a medium** (`nebula_sample`). At a point of density ρ the A2 medium has extinction
    ρ × `extinction` and emission ρ × `emissionScale` × the emission colour; the volumes draft's transport
    integrates both.
13. **Bounds.** 4194304 stars, 4096 bodies, 65536 steps per run, 8 arms, 16 lines, the truncations 20 h and
    100 R_e, and the Sérsic index in 0.5 to 10 are constants of the reference module.

## Rationale

- Inverse-CDF sampling (rules 3 to 5) makes every star a function of its draws, so the catalogue is stable
  under the count and under time without a state; rejection sampling would need an unbounded loop.
- The Sérsic law is stated through P(2n, x), which is also what defines b_n, so the profile, the enclosed
  light and the sampler agree by construction.
- Leapfrog with a fixed step count is symplectic and reversible; its energy drift bounds the merger's
  believability and the fixed count is what SREP 14 needs.
- The palettes name the two mappings astrophotographers use; `natural` is the physical colour for an engine
  that wants it.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A texture of a galaxy** (an image layer): it cannot be flown through or lit, and shots 14 and 18 move.
- **Barnes–Hut as the normative sum**: the direct sum at 4096 bodies is the reference; a tree is an allowed
  implementation when it agrees within a tolerance (open issue 3).
- **Density-wave arms as a potential in the N-body**: the plan's "density-wave arms" are a look, stated here as
  a modulation of the catalogue; a spiral potential is open issue 2.

## Backwards compatibility

- `galaxy` and `medium/lines` are new and gated on 1.8 (GX6, V17); every 1.7 document is valid and renders
  the same.

## Open issues

1. **Deprojection.** The Sérsic law is a projected profile; the draft applies it to the bulge's 3D radii, so
   the projected bulge is close to but not exactly Sérsic. The editor decides whether to deproject (Prugniel
   and Simien 1997) or to state the 3D law as this draft does.
2. **A spiral potential.** The arms modulate the catalogue; an N-body run does not hold them. A rotating
   spiral potential (Lin–Shu density waves) would, at the cost of a second parameter set.
3. **Approximate sums.** The tolerance within which a Barnes–Hut or GPU sum may replace the direct sum.
4. **Halo stars.** The share left over by the disk and bulge is not drawn; a stellar halo could take it.
5. **Gas and dust in mergers.** The bodies are collisionless; tidal tails appear, star formation does not.
