# SREP 0 — Add scattering and vegetation to the terrain grid

## Abstract

SREP 12's G1: three children of `terrain`. **`tree`** prototypes grown by one of three methods: an L-system
(**Lindenmayer**; **Prusinkiewicz** and Lindenmayer 1990) rewritten a bounded number of `iterations` and drawn by a
3D **turtle**; **space colonisation** (**Runions**, Lane and Prusinkiewicz 2007) toward `attractors` drawn in a crown
ellipsoid, with radii by **Murray**'s law; or the parametric model of **Weber** and **Penn** (1995) with its crown
`shape` ratios, `levels`, `downAngle` and `curveRes`. **`scatter`** elements that place a `prototype` over the
terrain by **Bridson**'s (2007) **Poisson-disk** sampling, no two points closer than `radius`, gated by a terrain
`mask` and a `density`, every draw from the random streams, and exchanged as a USD **PointInstancer** or a glTF
node with **EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing** (SREP 26's instancing precedents) written and read by the reference. One
**`ecosystem`** that starts from a scatter and grows it year by year — logistic `growth` to `maxRadius`, death at
`maxAge`, `offspring` from `maturity`, and the **self-thinning** of **Deussen** et al. (1998): where two crowns
overlap the smaller dies. Every instance sways as a **cantilever** under the **drag** of the wind-field draft's
`wind` at its own position. The reference tests are SREP 12's: no two points closer than r; the **Fibonacci**
algae lengths and the **Koch** curve's **5ⁿ** segments. Rules VG1–VG5 and V17; the schema files are unchanged, the
diff is inside the draft. Reference module `conformance/tools/vegetation_reference.py`; engine card SR-110.

## Motivation

- **SREP 12 names the deltas.** Row G1 of the plan: "seeded Poisson-disk scattering over terrain masks (Bridson
  2007), exchanged as USD PointInstancer and glTF EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing, read and written by our own code; trees
  by L-systems, space colonisation and Weber–Penn parameters; sway from the wind field (F4); ecosystem growth with
  self-thinning (Deussen et al. 1998)". This draft is that row, on the terrain grid (SR-100), the wind field
  (SR-101) and the random streams.
- **Nothing declares a tree.** SREP 66's programs can emit an L-system as nodes, and SREP 26 places copies on
  generated points; neither says what a tree is, where a forest stands, or how it grows. A hillside of trees
  that bend in the wind is the most common establishing shot in a science explainer.
- **Masks already exist.** The terrain-grid draft derives slope, altitude, curvature and flow masks; a scatter
  over a mask is the one operation that turns them into a forest line, a valley meadow or a scree slope.
- **Every frame renders alone.** A scatter is a function of its seed; an ecosystem's state at t is the state
  after round(t · `yearsPerSecond`) years replayed from the scatter (SREP 14), bounded by `maxYears`.
- **Exchange is part of the delta.** A forest an author places here must leave as a PointInstancer or a glTF
  instancing node, and one made elsewhere must come in; both formats are open specifications (OpenUSD under
  Apache-2.0, glTF under the Khronos specification licence), and the reference writes and reads them without a
  library.

**Schematron** (`scene-render.sch`):

- **VG1** On a scatter: `radius` > 0; `attempts` and `maxPoints` ≥ 1; `density` in [0, 1]; 0 < `scaleMin` ≤
  `scaleMax`.
- **VG2** A scatter's `mask` names a mask of its terrain and its `wind` a `wind` of `physics`.
- **VG3** A scatter's `prototype` names a `tree` of its terrain.
- **VG4** A tree's `method` is one of the three; `iterations` ≤ 12 and `maxSymbols` ≥ 1; `kill` < `influence`;
  `levels` in 1..4 and `shape` one of the eight; the stem count Π `branches` ≤ 100 000; `stiffness` > 0 and
  `dragArea` ≥ 0; every length, radius and ratio positive.
- **VG5** An ecosystem's `scatter` names a scatter and each `species`'s `prototype` a tree; `growth`, `spread` ≥ 0;
  `maxRadius`, `yearsPerSecond` > 0; `maxAge` ≥ 1; `maturity`, `offspring`, `maxYears` ≥ 0.
- **V17** (extended) `tree`, `scatter` and `ecosystem` require `scene/@version` 1.8 or later.

### Semantics

Lengths are metres on the terrain's grid (i right, j down, `metersPerCell` = dx apart; a point's height is the
terrain's surface at its cell); a prototype's own frame is z up from its root at the origin. e is the element
seed E(project, id, seed, purpose) of the random-streams draft with purpose `scatter`, `tree` or `ecosystem`, S
its stream draw. The reference is `conformance/tools/vegetation_reference.py`; function names in parentheses.

1. **Scattering.** Bridson 2007 over the terrain's rectangle. The first point is the first of at most 1000
   uniform draws (S(e, 1, n, 0) · width, S(e, 1, n, 1) · height) the mask admits; then, while points are active,
   draw n picks an active point by S(e, 1, n, 2) and offers up to `attempts` candidates in the **annulus**
   [r, 2r] around it (angle 2π S(e, 1, n, 0), distance r (1 + S(e, 1, n, 1))), accepting the first that lies on
   the grid, is at least r from every point (a hash of buckets r/√2 wide) and is admitted — S(e, 1, n, 3) <
   `density` · m, m the mask's value at the candidate's cell, 1 without a mask; an active point whose candidates
   all fail retires. The sampling is **maximal** on the admitted region (no empty disk of radius r survives the k
   attempts, so no probe is farther than 2r from a point) and stops at `maxPoints`. A point's scale is `scaleMin`
   + (`scaleMax` − `scaleMin`) S(e, 1, n, 4) and its yaw 360 S(e, 1, n, 5) degrees when `rotate`, else 0.
   (`scatter`, `scatter_on`, `candidate`, `first_point`)
2. **Exchange.** An instance's transform in the **y-up** frame of USD and glTF is the translation (x, height, y),
   the rotation about +y by the yaw and the uniform scale. A scatter is written as a usda `PointInstancer`
   (`positions`, `orientations` as quath with the real part first, `scales`, `protoIndices`, one prototype) or
   as a glTF 2.0 asset with one node carrying `EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing` (TRANSLATION VEC3, ROTATION VEC4, SCALE
   VEC3 float accessors over one embedded buffer), and read back from either; a round trip reproduces every
   point (exactly from usda, to float32 from glTF). (`instance_transform`, `to_point_instancer`,
   `from_point_instancer`, `to_gltf_instancing`, `from_gltf_instancing`)
3. **L-systems.** The `axiom` is rewritten `iterations` times by the `rules`; a symbol with several rules takes
   the one whose cumulative `weight` first exceeds S(e, 1, iteration, position) · Σ weights. The string is refused
   past `maxSymbols`. The turtle starts at the origin heading +z with F (draw) and f (move) a step of `length` ·
   `lengthRatio`^depth and a radius `radius` · `radiusRatio`^depth, + − turning about U, & ^ pitching about L,
   \ / rolling about H, | turning around, [ ] pushing and popping the state (depth + 1 inside). The Fibonacci
   algae (A → AB, B → A) has lengths 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, …; the Koch curve (F → F+F−F−F+F) has 5ⁿ segments.
   (`lsystem_string`, `turtle`, `lsystem_tree`)
4. **Space colonisation.** `attractors` points uniform in the crown ellipsoid (semi-axes `crownRadius`,
   `crownRadius`, `crownHeight`/2, centred `crownBase` + `crownHeight`/2 up the trunk) from S(e, 2, k, 0..2). From
   the root the trunk grows one `segment` straight up per iteration until an attractor is within `influence` of
   a node; then each iteration assigns every live attractor to its nearest node within `influence`, grows from
   each such node one segment toward the mean unit direction of its attractors, and kills the attractors within
   `kill` of a node; it stops when no attractor influences a node, when an iteration adds nothing, or at
   `maxIterations`. Every node but the root has one parent; radii follow Murray's law with exponent 2 (r² = Σ
   children's r²) from `tipRadius` at the leaves. (`colonise`, `attractors`, `attractor_point`)
5. **Weber–Penn.** The trunk has length `scale` · `length`₀ (1 + `lengthVariation`₀ (2 S(e, 3, 0, 0) − 1)) and
   radius length · `ratio`, in `curveRes`₀ segments turning `curve`₀ over the stem. A level-n stem gets
   `branches`ₙ₊₁ children spread evenly along it (from `baseSize` of the trunk's length on the trunk, from the
   base elsewhere), the k-th leaving at `downAngle`ₙ₊₁ (± its variation) from the parent's direction at k ·
   `rotate`ₙ₊₁ of azimuth, with length parent · `length`₁ · shapeRatio(`shape`, (parent − offset)/(parent −
   base)) at level 1 and `length`ₙ₊₁ · (parent − 0.6 offset) deeper, and radius parent radius · (length /
   parent length)^`ratioPower`. The shape ratios are Weber and Penn's table 1 (conical 0.2 + 0.8 r, spherical
   0.2 + 0.8 sin π r, hemispherical 0.2 + 0.8 sin ½π r, cylindrical 1, tapered cylindrical 0.5 + 0.5 r, flame
   r/0.7 or (1 − r)/0.3, inverse conical 1 − 0.8 r, tend flame 0.5 + half the flame). (`weber_penn`,
   `shape_ratio`)
6. **Sway.** An instance of height H (the prototype's height times its scale) in the grid-space wind w of its
   scatter's `wind` at its own position and the frame's time bends as a cantilever under the crown's drag
   F = ½ ρ C_d A |w|² (ρ 1.225 kg/m³, C_d 0.5, A `dragArea` · scale²): tip deflection δ = F H³ / (3 EI), EI the
   `stiffness`, capped at H/2; the lean is atan(δ/H) toward the wind. Calm air, no `wind`: no sway. (`sway`,
   `wind_at_point`, `instances`)
7. **Ecosystem.** The founding plants are the `scatter`'s points as seedlings of radius 0.25 m, each of a
   `species` by S(e, 1, 0, k) when there are several. Year y: every plant ages one year and grows by `growth`
   (1 − r/`maxRadius`); a plant reaching `maxAge` dies; a plant at least `maturity` years old spawns `offspring`
   seedlings at distance r + `spread` S(e, 2, y, 64 id + k) and azimuth 2π S(e, 2, y, 64 id + k + 16), admitted
   when on the grid, admitted by the scatter's mask (S(e, 2, y, 64 id + k + 32) < m) and overlapping no plant;
   then the stand thins: plants are kept largest first (then oldest, then by id) and a plant overlapping a kept
   one dies (Deussen et al. 1998's competition, the smaller losing). The state at time t is after
   min(round(t · `yearsPerSecond`), `maxYears`) years, replayed from the scatter (SREP 14). (`ecosystem`,
   `ecosystem_on`, `years_for`)
8. **Instances.** With an ecosystem the terrain's instances are its plants (scale r/`maxRadius`, yaw 360 S(e,
   3, 0, id), the species' prototype); without one, every scatter's points with the scatter's prototype. Each
   carries its height on the surface and its sway. The run reports the grown prototypes, the instances and the
   digest. (`instances`, `run`)
9. **Validity.** VG1–VG5 and V17 hold or the document is refused; the reference lists the codes.
   (`validate_document`, `bad_document`)

## Rationale

- **Bridson, not jitter or dart throwing.** A jittered grid shows its grid; dart throwing is O(n²) and not
  maximal; Bridson's active list is O(n), maximal, and every draw is indexed by a counter so the streams
  reproduce it. The mask gates candidates rather than warping the radius, which keeps the no-closer-than-r
  guarantee exact and the maximality on the admitted region.
- **The draws are indexed, not sequential.** S(e, 1, n, component) with n the candidate counter means an engine
  can verify any point without replaying the sampler's random state; the first point and the annulus candidate
  are table rows.
- **Three tree methods.** An L-system states a species in a line and is the test SREP 12 names; space
  colonisation gives the crown's shape from a volume and never self-intersects; Weber–Penn is the parameter
  set that tree libraries and Blender's Sapling share, so an author can port a tree. Each is bounded by its
  own attribute (`iterations` and `maxSymbols`, `maxIterations`, Π `branches`).
- **Murray's law for colonised radii.** Runions et al. use r² = Σ children's r² at every branching (the da Vinci
  rule); it needs one number, `tipRadius`, and makes the trunk's radius a property of the leaf count.
- **A cantilever for the sway.** The static lean under drag is the first term of every tree-wind model (Mayhead
  1973 for the drag; the oscillation at the tree's natural frequency is an open issue); the wind draft's gusts
  and turbulence already vary it in time and place.
- **Deussen's circles.** Plants as circles that grow and compete for area are the model Deussen et al. ran for
  their meadows and forests; largest-first thinning is their rule that the weaker dies, and `maturity` is the
  one addition that keeps a stand from exploding in seedlings before the founders have grown.
- **The scale from the radius.** An ecosystem's instance is the prototype at r/`maxRadius`, so a seedling is a
  small tree and a mature one the full prototype; a growth model for the prototype itself is an open issue.
- **Own writer and reader.** SREP 12 asks for exchange "read and written by our own code"; both formats are
  simple enough (a text array; a JSON with one buffer) that a dependency on the USD or glTF libraries would
  replace a hundred lines and bring a toolchain.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A density function instead of a mask.** The masks exist and are testable; a scalar field would want its own
  syntax and the terrain's fields are the masks' quantities already.
- **Whole-forest L-systems.** Prototypes are grown once and instanced; an L-system per instance would make a
  forest's cost the sum of its trees.
- **Dynamic wind response (a damped oscillator per tree).** Needs a time integration, which SREP 14 forbids
  unless stepped; the static lean under the wind draft's gusts reads as sway at scene scale.
- **Deussen's full viability (soil, water, light).** The terrain has no soil or water-table field yet; the
  mask and the competition for area are the parts that exist.
- **Vertex-level foliage.** Leaves, bark and the prototype's mesh are rendering, not the structure this draft
  declares (open issue).

## Backwards compatibility

A 1.7 document has no `terrain`; a 1.8 terrain without `tree`, `scatter` or `ecosystem` is unchanged. The
elements are additive.

instances carry prototype, height, yaw, scale and sway and its digest changes with time; VG1–VG5 and V17 one at
a time; the defaults and constants.

## Open issues

1. **Dynamic sway.** The lean is static; the damped oscillation at the tree's natural frequency (and the
   branches' own) needs a stepped state or a modal sum of the gusts.
2. **Foliage and meshes.** The prototype is a skeleton of segments with radii; leaves, bark and the mesh the
   renderer builds from it are outside this draft.
3. **Prototype growth.** An ecosystem scales the prototype; a sapling is not a scaled oak.
4. **Deussen's viability.** Soil, water and light preferences wait for the terrain's soil and water-table fields
   and for the sun of the sky draft.
5. **Shade tolerance.** Seedlings under a canopy die here by overlap; species that grow under others need a
   light model.
6. **Mask-shaped prototypes.** One prototype per scatter; a species mix by mask value is an ecosystem of one
   generation.
7. **Import of foreign instancers.** The reader takes the reference's own layout; a general PointInstancer
   (several prototypes, time samples) or a glTF with sparse accessors is not read.
