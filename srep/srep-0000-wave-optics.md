# SREP 0 — Add polarisation and wave optics

## Abstract

Two additions for the optics explainers draw beyond rays, SREP 12's milestone 6 (OW3): polarisation on the
ray-optics bench, where a `source` carries a Jones vector (`polarisation` linear, circular or elliptical, or
none for unpolarised light) and `polariser`, `waveplate` and `rotator` elements multiply it by their Jones
matrices while a `detector` records intensity, so that Malus's law and the colour bands of a sugar column
between crossed polarisers are declared facts; and a `diffraction` 2D node that illuminates an `aperture`
(`slit`, `doubleSlit`, `circular`, `grating` or a sampled `mask`) with a plane wave of a stated wavelength and
draws the far-field pattern by the closed forms and by an FFT of the aperture function, or the near field by
Huygens–Fresnel summation, with the complex field's phase shown as hue. The reference tests are the ones SREP
12 names: Malus's law, the Airy first zero at 1.22 λ/D and the fringe spacing λL/d; the draft adds the Stokes
parameters, Biot's law of optical rotation, the single-slit minima and the grating orders.

## Motivation

SREP 12 row OW3 asks for "Jones calculus; Huygens–Fresnel summation; Fraunhofer patterns by FFT; phase shown
as hue", measured by "Malus's law; Airy first zero 1.22 λ/D; fringe spacing λL/d", and makes shot 28 (optical
rotation through a sugar column, with OW2) and shot 15 (the double slit, with OW4) native. scene-render has
no notion of polarisation or of a wave: shot 28's rotating E-vector helix and its colour bands are hand-written
(SREP 12 probe 28) and shot 15's double-slit pattern is a painted gradient (probe 15). An explainer that shows
why crossed polarisers go dark, why sugar turns the plane of polarisation more for blue than for red, or why
two slits make fringes λL/d apart, needs those numbers to come from the stated physics so that the kit can
measure them on the frame.

  `ellipticity` (`xs:double`, degrees, −45 to 45, default 0: the ellipticity angle of `elliptical`; 45 is
  circular).
- The ray-optics draft's `opticalBench` gains four element kinds, each with `x`, `y`, `angle` (the element's
  line, like a thin lens) and `aperture` (its length), traced as flat surfaces that change the ray's Jones
  vector and not its direction:

    `concentration` (`xs:double`, g/mL); with the Biot attributes the rotation follows rule 2 at the ray's
    wavelength (the spectral draft's `wavelength`, else 589 nm).
  - `detector`: a screen that records intensity |J|² per hit instead of a bare hit, and `polarisation` of the
    recorded beam as Stokes parameters.
- `diffraction`, a new member of `nodeChoice` (5359), a 2D node with the common node attributes: the node's
  box is the screen, its horizontal axis the screen coordinate x in metres through `scale` and its vertical
  axis either a second screen axis (`circular`) or the intensity plot.
  - `aperture` (one of `slit`, `doubleSlit`, `circular`, `grating`, `mask`, required), `width`

  - `wavelength` (`positiveDecimal`, nanometres, required), `distance` (`positiveDecimal`, metres, required:
    aperture to screen), `samples` (`xs:positiveInteger`, a power of two up to 65536, default 4096: the
    aperture's sampling for the FFT and the count of Huygens sources), `extent` (`positiveDecimal`, metres,
    default 2 mm: the sampled width of the aperture plane), `regime` (one of `auto`, `fraunhofer`, `huygens`,
    default `auto`: rule 4), `scale` (`positiveDecimal`, pixels per metre of screen, required), `frequency`
    (`xs:double`, Hz of scene time, default 0: the time-harmonic phase of rule 5).
  - `display`, optional child: `phase` (one of `hue`, `none`, default `hue`), `amplitude` (one of `value`,

    `plot` (`xs:boolean`, default true: also draw the intensity curve along the screen).
- Schematron:
  - **WO1** `polarisation="circular"` has a valid `handedness`; `elliptical` has `ellipticity` in −45 to 45;

### Semantics

The reference module (`conformance/tools/wave_optics_reference.py`) names the function of each rule. Jones
vectors are pairs of complex amplitudes (Eₓ, E_y) in the bench's frame; diffraction geometry is in metres;
angles in the document are degrees.

1. **Jones calculus.** A `source` emits `source_jones(src)`: `jones_linear(θ)` = (cos θ, sin θ),
   `jones_circular(h)` = (1, ±i)/√2 (left is +i), `jones_elliptical(θ, χ)` = R(θ)(cos χ, i sin χ), or, for
   `none`, two incoherent orthogonal linear states of half the power each. A `polariser` applies the projector
   `polariser(θ)` = R(θ) diag(1, 0) R(−θ); a `waveplate` applies `waveplate(δ, θ)` = R(θ) diag(1, e^{iδ})
   R(−θ) (`quarter_wave`, `half_wave` for δ = 90°, 180°); a `rotator` applies the rotation `rotator(α)`. The
   ray's power after an element is `intensity` = |Eₓ|² + |E_y|², so that two polarisers at θ apart pass
   `malus(θ)` = cos² θ (Malus's law), a quarter-wave plate at 45° makes linear light circular (|S₃| = 1), a
   half-wave plate at θ turns the plane by 2θ, and unpolarised light passes `unpolarised_through` = 1/2 of any
   polariser. A `detector` records `stokes(J)` = (S₀, S₁, S₂, S₃) and `degree_of_polarisation`. Elements
   compose right to left (`compose`); the Fresnel split of the ray-optics draft keeps the Jones vector of the
   parent on both children (the dielectric Rs and Rp of a split are open issue 1).
2. **Optical rotation.** A Biot `rotator` turns the plane by `biot_rotation([α], l, c, λ)` = [α] l c
   (589/λ)² degrees, the 1/λ² dispersion of rotation (Biot's law), so that blue turns more than red. White
   light through a polariser, a column and an analyser at β transmits `rotation_transmission(α(λ), β)` =
   cos²(α(λ) − β) per wavelength; the column's colour is that spectrum through the CIE matching functions and
   the sRGB matrix, `rotation_colour(l, c, β)`, with the untouched white at (1, 1, 1) — the colour bands of
   shot 28 — and the E vector's helix has pitch `helix_pitch` = 360/α per dm. The spectral draft's
   `wavelength` on the source selects one wavelength; without it the bench draws the integrated colour.
3. **Far-field patterns.** With `regime="fraunhofer"` the screen intensity is the closed form of the kind:
   `single_slit(a, λ, θ)` = sinc²(πa sin θ/λ) with minima at `slit_minimum`, a sin θ = mλ;
   `double_slit(a, d, λ, θ)` = cos²(πd sin θ/λ) sinc²(πa sin θ/λ) with fringes `fringe_spacing` = λL/d apart on
   the screen and missing orders where d/a is an integer; `airy(D, λ, θ)` = (2J₁(x)/x)², x = πD sin θ/λ, with
   `bessel_j1` by quadrature, the first dark ring at `airy_first_zero`, sin θ = `AIRY_FACTOR` λ/D = 1.2197 λ/D
   (the first zero of J₁, 3.8317, over π; 1.22 λ/D in the texts), the first bright ring at 1.75 % and 83.8 %
   of the energy inside the first ring (`airy_encircled`); `grating(N, d, λ, θ)` = (sin Nφ/(N sin φ))²,
   φ = πd sin θ/λ, with principal maxima at `grating_orders`, d sin θ = mλ, resolving power `resolving_power`
   = mN and dispersion `grating_dispersion` = m/(d cos θ). For a `mask`, and as the check of every other kind,
   the pattern is `fraunhofer_fft`: the aperture function `aperture_function` sampled at `samples` points over
   `extent` is transformed by the radix-2 `fft`, and |X_k|² is drawn at x = Lλk/extent, normalised to a unit
   peak; the reference's FFT agrees with the sinc² form to 2 × 10⁻² and finds the first minimum within two
   samples of Lλ/a.
4. **Near field.** With `regime="huygens"`, or `auto` when the Fresnel number `fresnel_number(a, λ, L)` =
   a²/(λL) of the aperture's largest size is above 1, the field at a screen point is the Huygens–Fresnel sum
   `huygens(points, λ, L, x)` = Σ e^{ikr}/√r over `aperture_points` secondary sources spread evenly over the
   open parts (cylindrical waves for the one-dimensional apertures), normalised to a unit peak
   (`huygens_pattern`); in the far field it reproduces the fringes of rule 3 (the bright fringe at λL/d is
   0.992 of the centre with 64 sources).
5. **Phase as hue and time.** A complex field value z is drawn as `complex_rgb(z, A)`: hue `phase_hue` =
   arg z/2π, saturation 1, value |z|/A clipped to 1 (`hsv_to_rgb`), so that phase 0 is red, 2π/3 green and
   4π/3 blue, and zero amplitude is black; `display/@amplitude="alpha"` puts |z|/A in the alpha instead, and
   `none` draws the hue alone. With `frequency` f the field is the time-harmonic `time_harmonic(x, t, λ, f, A)`
   = A e^{i(kx − ωt)} at scene time t, so that the hue scrolls one cycle per period; the field at a frame is
   `frame_field(frame, fps, …)`, a pure function of the frame (SREP 14; `seek_test` renders 49 frames of the
   field, its colour, a Huygens sum and a Jones chain and compares frames 0, 7, 24 and 48 rendered alone with
   the run). OW4's wave simulations use the same display.
6. **Validation.** `validate_polarisation` and `validate_jones_element` are WO1 (and WO3 for the Biot
   rotator), `validate_aperture` WO2 and `validate_display` WO4.

## Rationale

- **Jones, not Mueller, on the bench.** The bench's rays are coherent and fully polarised once they pass a
  polariser; Jones vectors are two complex numbers and compose by 2 × 2 products. Unpolarised light is two
  incoherent states, which is exact for every intensity the bench records; a `detector` reports Stokes
  parameters so that partial polarisation is still a measured number (Collett ch. 4–5).
- **Biot's law for the sugar column.** The 1/λ² dispersion of rotation is the textbook approximation that
  produces the colour bands of shot 28 from one number per substance; a full rotatory dispersion curve is an
  asset problem (open issue 2).
- **Closed forms and the FFT together.** The closed forms are what the kit measures and what captions quote;
  the FFT is what renders an arbitrary mask and what checks the closed forms (Goodman ch. 4). Both are in the
  reference so neither is trusted alone.
- **Cylindrical Huygens sources.** The apertures are one-dimensional (slits, gratings), so the secondary
  sources radiate cylindrical waves (1/√r); this matches the Fraunhofer forms in the far field, which is the
  test.
- **The Airy factor as a computed number.** 1.22 is a rounding; the draft states 3.8317/π and lets the kit's
  tolerance absorb the difference.
- **Phase as hue.** The convention of complex-function plots (domain colouring) is what explainers use for
  wavefunctions and fields; OW4 reuses it.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Vector diffraction and polarisation in the diffraction node.** Scalar diffraction is the explainers'
  regime; polarised diffraction (Born and Wolf ch. 11) is out of scope.
- **2D apertures by 2D FFT.** The circular aperture is served by the Airy form; general 2D masks need a 2D
  FFT and a 2D screen and are a follow-up (open issue 3).
- **Fresnel integrals for the near field.** The Huygens sum is general and the same for every aperture; the
  Fresnel integrals cover edges and slits only.
- **Mueller matrices and depolarisers.** No depolarising element is on the bench.

## Backwards compatibility

Additive. Documents without `polarisation`, the four element kinds or `diffraction` validate and render
unchanged; the new attributes and elements require version 1.8 (V17). The draft depends on the ray-optics
draft for the bench and on the spectral draft for `wavelength` and the colour conversion.

## Open issues

1. **Polarisation at dielectric splits.** The ray-optics draft's Fresnel split uses the unpolarised average;
   with Jones vectors the split should use Rs and Rp per component, which would make Brewster windows
   polarise. A small follow-up once both drafts have numbers.
2. **Rotatory dispersion beyond Biot.** A specific rotation tabulated against wavelength, as an asset, for substances where 1/λ² is poor.
3. **Two-dimensional masks.** A 2D FFT and a 2D screen for arbitrary apertures and the circular aperture's
   image (not only its radial profile).
4. **Partially coherent sources.** The slit patterns assume a coherent plane wave; a source size washes
   fringes out (van Cittert–Zernike), which shot 15's explanation may want.
5. **Units on the node.** The diffraction node mixes metres (the physics) and pixels (the screen) through
   `scale`; whether the plots draft's axes should host the screen instead is for the editor.
