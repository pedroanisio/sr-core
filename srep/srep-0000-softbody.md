# SREP 0 — Add cloth and soft bodies in 3D

## Abstract

[SREP 12](srep-0012.md) names, as its S1 building block, "Vertex Block Descent (Chen et al. 2024) with stable
neo-Hookean material (Smith et al. 2018) and offset contact (Chen et al. 2025); XPBD rods for rope and hair",
with the bars "hanging-sheet rest shape; no energy gain without forces; zero self-intersections in a twisting
test" (line 294); the engine has 2D spring lattices only (`softBody`, schema line 3047). This SREP adds a
`softBody3D` node of three kinds: a **cloth** sheet of springs and bends, a **solid** block of stable
neo-Hookean tetrahedra, and a **rope** of XPBD distance constraints. Cloth and solids are stepped by Vertex
Block Descent: an inertial target, then a fixed count of Gauss–Seidel sweeps of local Newton steps over the
vertices, each with a positive semidefinite Hessian and halved while it raises the vertex's local energy.
Self-contact is an offset penalty on the vertex–triangle and edge–edge pairs closer than the cloth's
thickness. Every count is fixed by the document, so a frame rendered alone equals the frame played (the seek
check of [SREP 14](srep-0014.md)). The plan's bars are measured by `conformance/tools/softbody_reference.py`:
the hanging sheet comes to rest with a residual below 1 % of its weight, a released sheet's energy never
rises, and a strip twisted one and a half turns reports zero self-intersections.

## Motivation

- **Milestone 4, machines and matter** (SREP 12 line 358) opens with S1; the shots it serves are the 3D
  mechanics family (shots 3, 5, 29 and 30, line 627) and every shot that drapes, hangs or ties: flags, curtains,
  cables, nets, capes on skinned characters.
- **The 2D lattice does not extend.** `softBody` (`crates/sr-sim/src/soft.rs`) is a 2D grid of springs with
  pressure for jelly; it has no triangles, no volume, no contact and a 16 × 16 cap, so a 3D sheet, a block or
  a cable cannot be stated.
- **Parity plus** (line 184): Houdini Vellum, Chaos Cloth and Blender cloth all use iterative solvers with
  adaptive termination; a fixed iteration count with a fixed substep count gives a result that is a function
  of the frame, which the plan names as the gain over them.

### Semantics

Units are metres, kilograms and seconds; y is up. `softbody_reference.py` states every rule as a function.

1. **Meshes** (`sheet`, `block`, `rope`, `pins`). A cloth of `rows` × `cols` vertices spans `width` along
   +x and `height` along −z from the node's origin, vertex (r, c) at index r × cols + c; each quad is split
   along one diagonal in a checkerboard (quads with r + c even along the a–e diagonal, the others along b–d),
   so a sheet with an even number of quads per row mirrors left to right. A solid is a block of `width` ×
   `height` × `depth` whose cells are each six tetrahedra of positive volume (the Kuhn split). A rope is
   `segments` points from the origin down −y over `length`. The total `mass` is shared equally. Pins: `top` is
   the first row, `corners` its ends, `ends` the first and last rows (or the rope's end points), `bottom` the
   lowest vertices of a solid; a pinned vertex keeps its rest position and has zero velocity.
2. **Springs and bends** (`spring_energy`, `spring_force`, `spring_hessian`). A cloth has a spring on every
   grid edge at `stiffness` and on each quad's split diagonal at half of it, and a bend between second
   neighbours along each row and column and across each quad's other diagonal at `bending`. A spring of rest
   length r and stiffness k between a and b has the energy k/2 (|b − a| − r)² and the force k (|b − a| − r)
   (b − a)/|b − a| on a. Its Hessian in the solver is k (u uᵀ + max(0, 1 − r/L)(I − u uᵀ)) with u the unit
   direction and L the length: the exact Hessian when stretched and its positive semidefinite part when
   compressed.
3. **Stable neo-Hookean tetrahedra** (`lame`, `snh_energy`, `snh_stress`, `tet_energy`, `tet_gradients`).
   Lamé's μ = E/(2(1 + ν)) and λ = Eν/((1 + ν)(1 − 2ν)) from `youngs` and `poisson`. A tetrahedron's
   deformation gradient is F = Ds Dm⁻¹ with Dm its rest edge matrix. Its energy density is Ψ(F) = μ/2 (I_C − 3)
   + λ/2 (J − α)² − μ/2 ln(I_C + 1) with I_C = tr(FᵀF), J = det F and α = 1 + μ/λ − μ/(4λ) (Smith, de Goes and
   Kim 2018, equation 14): the rest state is stress free, and the stress P = μ (1 − 1/(I_C + 1)) F + λ (J − α)
   ∂J/∂F is finite through inversion. The tetrahedron's energy is its rest volume times Ψ, and its gradient at
   vertices 1 to 3 is the columns of V P Dm⁻ᵀ, at vertex 0 their negated sum.
4. **The inertial target** (`vbd_step`). A step of h under gravity g starts every free vertex at y = x + h v +
   h² g and every pinned one at its rest position; the step minimises G(x) = Σ m/(2h²) |x − y|² + E(x), with
   E the sum of the springs, bends, tetrahedra and contacts. A body without elastic terms therefore falls
   exactly: after n steps its position is x₀ + n(n + 1)/2 · h² g.
5. **Vertex Block Descent** (`vbd_step`, Chen, Liu, Yang and Yuksel 2024). `iterations` Gauss–Seidel sweeps
   over the free vertices in index order. For vertex i the sweep takes the local gradient f = ∇ᵢE + m/h² (xᵢ
   − yᵢ) and the local Hessian H = Σ Hₖ + m/h² I over its springs (rule 2), its tetrahedra (the finite
   difference of rule 3's gradient, symmetrised and projected to its positive semidefinite part) and its
   contacts (rule 8), then moves xᵢ by −H⁻¹f, halving the move while it raises the vertex's local energy, at
   most 8 times (`MAX_HALVINGS`), and leaving the vertex where it was when every halving fails. After the
   sweeps v = (1 − damping)(x_new − x)/h; a pinned vertex's velocity is zero.
6. **Substeps and the seek check** (`run`). A frame is `substeps` steps of h/substeps. The state after frame f
   is the run of f × substeps steps from the rest state, so `run(30)` equals `run(15)` continued by `run(15)`
   and a frame rendered alone equals the frame played (SREP 14 rule 1).
7. **Proximity** (`point_triangle_distance`, `barycentric`, `edge_edge_distance`, `find_contacts`). When
   `selfCollision` is on, each step first lists, from the positions at its start, every vertex–triangle pair
   (the vertex not of the triangle) and every edge–edge pair (sharing no vertex) closer than twice the
   `thickness`; the closest points are Ericson's (2005, sections 5.1.5 and 5.1.9).
8. **Offset contact** (`contact_energy`, `contact_gradient_hessian`). A listed pair with gap d below the
   `thickness` t adds the energy k/2 (t − d)² with k the `contactStiffness`; its gradient at a vertex of weight
   w along the gap's direction n is −k (t − d) w n, and its Hessian in the vertex solve is the Gauss–Newton
   term k w² n nᵀ; a pair at or beyond t adds nothing. A vertex thrown at a pinned sheet stops above the offset
   and rebounds.
9. **The twisting test** (`triangles_intersect`, `segment_triangle_intersects`, `self_intersections`,
   `twist_test`). A strip pinned at both ends has its end rows turned about the strip's long axis in opposite
   senses, `turns` turns in all over the test's frames, stepped by rule 5 with contact; after every frame the
   pairs of triangles that share no vertex and intersect (an edge of either crosses the other, Möller and
   Trumbore 1997) are counted. The bar is zero over the run.
10. **XPBD rods** (`xpbd_distance`, `xpbd_step`, `run_rope`, `rod_curvature`). A rope's constraints are the
    distances between neighbours with compliance 1/stiffness and between second neighbours with compliance
    1/bending (none when `bending` is 0). A step predicts the positions as in rule 4, then runs `iterations`
    sweeps of the constraints in order: for a constraint C = |a − b| − r with inverse masses wₐ, w_b,
    accumulated multiplier λ and α̃ = compliance/h², Δλ = (−C − α̃λ)/(wₐ + w_b + α̃), a moves by −wₐ Δλ n and
    b by +w_b Δλ n (Macklin, Müller and Chentanez 2016); the multipliers reset at each substep. Velocities and
    damping follow rule 5. A rod's curvature is the sum of its turning angles.
11. **Energy and rest** (`total_energy`, `energy_gain`, `residual`, `weight`). The total energy is the kinetic
    energy plus the springs, bends, contacts and the tetrahedra's energy relative to the rest state. A sheet
    released from a deformed shape without gravity or damping never gains energy between frames (the largest
    rise is at most 0). The residual of a state is the largest |∇ᵢE − m g| over the free vertices; the hanging
    sheet of the kit comes to rest with a top speed below 10⁻³ m/s and a residual below 1 % of its weight.
12. **Bounds.** 65536 vertices per body, 256 rows or cols, 256 iterations, 64 substeps, 65536 frames per run
    and 8 halvings: every loop is owned by the document and the solver, none by convergence.

## Rationale

- **Vertex Block Descent over XPBD for cloth and solids.** VBD minimises the implicit Euler objective one
  vertex at a time with a fixed sweep count; its result is a function of the sweep count, needs no global
  solve, and takes any energy with a per-vertex Hessian, so the same loop serves springs, tetrahedra and
  contact. XPBD serves rods, where a chain of distance constraints is its natural form.
- **Stable neo-Hookean.** The only hyperelastic model among the standard ones that is stress free at rest,
  stays finite through inversion and keeps volume at ν near 0.5 (the block of the kit keeps 99.8 % of its
  volume under gravity); a corotated model needs a polar decomposition and still inverts.
- **The positive semidefinite Hessian.** A compressed spring's exact Hessian has a negative direction;
  Newton steps along it move away from the minimum. Projecting each term keeps every step a descent step, and
  the halving guards the rest.
- **Contact as an energy.** A penalty below an offset enters the same minimisation as the elastic terms;
  the alternative, impulses after the solve, breaks the energy argument of rule 11.
- **The checkerboard split.** A single diagonal direction makes a hanging sheet shear sideways by a
  millimetre per metre; alternating the split removes the bias.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Extending `softBody` with a `z` extent.** The 2D element's pressure model and 16 × 16 cap do not carry
  into 3D; a new node with the 2D element unchanged keeps SREP 11's documents valid.
- **Adaptive iteration counts.** The convergence test would decide the iteration count from the state and the
  seek check would fail.
- **Continuous collision detection.** Exact in principle, but its root finding takes a variable count of
  steps; the offset contact with a per-step pair list is bounded and the twisting bar is met with it.
- **Mass-spring solids.** Lattices of springs have no volume term; a block of springs collapses under its
  own weight at the Poisson's ratios the shots need.

## Backwards compatibility

A new node at schema 1.8 (V17); documents at 1.7.1 are unchanged and `softBody` keeps its meaning.

## Open issues

1. **Collision with the scene.** This draft handles self-contact; contact with `rigidBody` shapes and the
   ground plane is left to the editor, with the same penalty form as the likely answer.
2. **Attachment.** Pinning to a `joint` or a skinned mesh (capes) needs a pin that follows a transform; the
   `pin` set here is static.
3. **Tetrahedral meshes from glTF.** Solids are blocks; an imported tetrahedral mesh needs a format decision.
4. **Hair.** Rods serve a rope; thousands of strands need an instanced form and a wider contact model.
5. **Friction.** The contact of rule 8 is frictionless.
