# SREP 0 — Add ray-optics diagrams

## Abstract

A 2D `opticalBench` node for the ray diagrams that optics explainers draw, SREP 12's milestone 6 (OW1): light
`source`s (point fans and parallel beams) and optical elements (thin and thick `lens`es, flat and spherical
`mirror`s, `prism`s, refracting `block`s, `grin` slabs and `screen`s) placed in the bench's pixel space, with
rays traced through them by Snell's law, total internal reflection and, optionally, Fresnel splitting into
reflected and transmitted rays, either non-sequentially (the nearest element wins at every step) or
sequentially (the element order of the document); an `object` child that draws the image construction of a
thin lens with its three principal rays; a bounce bound and a power floor owned by the document, so that a
bench of two facing mirrors terminates. The reference tests are the ones SREP 12 names: Snell's law, total
internal reflection and the lensmaker equation; the draft adds Brewster's angle, the prism's minimum deviation
and the sinusoidal ray of a parabolic GRIN slab, each measurable in pixels.

## Motivation

SREP 12 row OW1 asks for "sequential and non-sequential 2D tracing through thin and thick lenses, mirrors,
prisms, GRIN media; Snell and Fresnel; image construction", measured by "Snell; total internal reflection;
lensmaker equation". The peer survey names ray-optics (ricktu288) as the tool explainers use for these diagrams
and notes that it has no video export; the family's lead is "verified ray and wave optics that renders to film
quality". scene-render today has a path tracer with `ior`, `transmission` and `dispersion` on materials (xsd
2468–2507), which renders refraction through 3D glass but draws no ray, no lens outline and no image
construction; a lens diagram is drawn by hand from `path` elements with the angles computed elsewhere. The
bench makes the diagram a declared fact: an author states the elements and the sources, the engine traces,
and the kit measures the traced angles against the reference's numbers.

  - `block`, zero or more: `points` (`pointListType`, three or more vertices of a simple polygon), `n`

  `mirror/@angle`, `prism/@angle`, `grin/@alpha` and `object/@x` accept `animate` and `expression` children
  (rule 7).
- Schematron:
  - **RO1** every `n` and `n0` is at least 1; a `grin` has `alpha` × `height` / 2 below 1, so that the profile
    stays positive across the slab.
  - **RO2** a `thin` lens has a non-zero `focal` and no `r1`, `r2`, `thickness` or `n`; a `thick` lens has all
    four, both radii non-zero, and `aperture` at most twice the smaller |radius|; a `spherical` mirror has a
    non-zero `radius`; a `prism` has `apex` strictly between 0 and 180; a `block` has three or more points.
  - **RO3** `mode` is one of the two values, `maxBounces` lies in 1 to 1024, `minPower` lies strictly between
    0 and 1, and a bench has at least one element.
  - **RO4** a `source` has `rays` at least 1; a `point` source has `spread` in 0 to 360 and no `width`; a
    `parallel` source has a positive `width` and no `spread`; an `object` refers to a `thin` lens of the same
    bench.
  - **V17** `opticalBench` and its children need `scene/@version="1.8"` or later.

### Semantics

The reference module (`conformance/tools/ray_optics_reference.py`) names the function of each rule. All
geometry is in the bench's pixel space; angles in the reference are radians unless a function takes degrees
from the document.

1. **Snell, reflection and total internal reflection.** At a hit with unit normal n and incident unit direction
   d, a mirror reflects to `reflect(d, n)` = d − 2 (d · n) n. A dielectric interface from index n₁ to n₂
   refracts to `refract(d, n, n1, n2)`, the vector form of `snell(n1, n2, θ)`: n₁ sin θᵢ = n₂ sin θₜ, with the
   normal flipped to face the incoming ray; when n₁ sin θᵢ exceeds n₂ (`total_internal_reflection`, beyond
   `critical_angle(n1, n2)` = asin(n₂/n₁)) the ray reflects instead. Each element knows its own index; the
   medium between elements is air at index 1, and a ray leaving a block, prism or thick lens returns to it.
2. **Fresnel.** With `fresnel="true"` every dielectric hit splits the ray: the reflected ray carries
   `fresnel_unpolarised(n1, n2, θ)` = (Rs + Rp) / 2 of the power, with `fresnel(n1, n2, θ)` = (Rs, Rp) the
   Fresnel reflectances of a dielectric interface (Rs = ((n₁ cos θᵢ − n₂ cos θₜ) / (n₁ cos θᵢ + n₂ cos θₜ))²,
   Rp = ((n₁ cos θₜ − n₂ cos θᵢ) / (n₁ cos θₜ + n₂ cos θᵢ))², both 1 beyond the critical angle), and the
   transmitted ray `transmittance` = 1 − R; Rp vanishes at `brewster(n1, n2)` = atan(n₂/n₁). A split ray whose
   power falls below `minPower` is dropped and its power is counted as dropped; power is conserved among the
   drawn, escaped, absorbed, truncated and dropped rays (the reference's `trace_bench` reports the four sinks).
   Without `fresnel`, the transmitted ray keeps the full power and no reflected ray is spawned.
3. **Lenses, mirrors and image construction.** A `thin` lens is a segment across its axis that deflects a ray
   at height h by `deflect_thin`: slope_out = slope_in − h / `focal` in the lens frame, so a parallel beam meets
   the axis at `focal` and an object at s₀ images at `thin_lens_image(so, f)` = s₀ f / (s₀ − f) (1/s₀ + 1/sᵢ =
   1/f) with `magnification` = −sᵢ/s₀. A `thick` lens is two spherical surfaces (`spherical_surface`, vertex
   at the stated point and centre `r` along the axis) of index `n` separated by `thickness`, traced by rule 1;
   its paraxial focal length is the `lensmaker` equation 1/f = (n − 1) (1/R₁ − 1/R₂ + (n − 1) d / (n R₁ R₂)),
   its `principal_planes` lie at h₁ = −f (n − 1) d / (n R₂) and h₂ = −f (n − 1) d / (n R₁) from the vertices,
   and the traced focus of a ray at small height (`paraxial_focus`) equals the second vertex plus the
   `back_focal_distance` f + h₂; marginal rays focus shorter (spherical aberration, which the bench draws and
   the table shows). A `flat` mirror reflects by rule 1; a `spherical` mirror is a circular arc of
   `mirror_focal_length` = R / 2. An `object` with a `thin` lens draws the arrow, the three principal rays
   (parallel then through the focus, through the centre, through the front focus then parallel) and the image
   arrow at `image_construction(so, height, f)`; a virtual image (sᵢ < 0) is drawn with `imageStroke` and the
   rays extended backward dashed.
4. **Prisms, blocks and GRIN slabs.** A `prism` is the isosceles triangle of apex angle `apex` and base `size`
   with its centroid at (`x`, `y`); a ray entering a face at incidence i is deviated by
   `prism_deviation(n, apex, i)` = i + asin(n sin(A − asin(sin i / n))) − A, least at
   `minimum_deviation(n, A)` = 2 asin(n sin(A/2)) − A where the ray inside is parallel to the base
   (`trace_prism` traces it and the table checks the two agree). A `block` is a polygon of index `n` traced by
   rule 1 face by face. A `grin` slab integrates the ray equation d/ds (n dr/ds) = ∇n through the profile
   `grin_index(y, n0, alpha)` = n₀ (1 − α² y² / 2) by classical RK4 in the path parameter (`grin_trace`, step
   0.05 px), so that paraxial rays are sinusoids of `grin_period` = 2π/α (a ray entering at height y₀ parallel
   to the axis crosses the axis a quarter period later) and the drawn polyline is the integrator's path.
5. **Tracing modes and bounds.** Each source emits `source_rays`: a `point` source fans `rays` directions
   evenly across `spread` about `angle`, a `parallel` source spaces `rays` parallel origins across `width`. In
   `nonSequential` mode every step hits the nearest element along the ray (`trace_ray`); in `sequential` mode
   the ray is tested only against elements later in document order than the one it last met, so that a
   lens train is traced in order and a ray that misses an element skips it. A ray stops when it escapes the
   bench's elements, hits a `screen`, or has interacted `maxBounces` times (its last segment is drawn to the
   hit and the remaining power counted as truncated); the bound and `minPower` belong to the document and the
   trace terminates for every bench (the two-mirror row of the table has exactly `maxBounces` + 1 segments).
6. **Drawing.** Rays are polylines from the source through every hit, in `rayStroke` with the alpha
   multiplied by `ray_alpha(power)` = √power so that a 4 % Fresnel reflection is still visible at 0.2; a
   GRIN path is its sampled polyline; element outlines are drawn in their `strokeStyle` (a thin lens as a
   double-arrowed segment, a thick lens as its two arcs and edges, a spherical mirror as its arc with a hatched
   back); a `screen` records each hit's point and power, and a hit marker is drawn there.
7. **Animation and the seek check.** Every animated attribute is keyed by `animate` and `expression` as SREP
   14 defines; the trace at a frame is a pure function of the attributes at that frame (`frame_angle`,
   `frame_trace`; `seek_test` renders 97 frames of a swept source and compares frames 0, 17, 48 and 96 rendered
   alone with the same frames from the run). No state carries between frames.
8. **Validation.** `validate_element` is RO1 and RO2, `validate_bench` RO3 and `validate_source` RO4.

## Rationale

- **A bench node, not elements in `object3D`.** Ray diagrams are planar and in pixels; the path tracer's glass
  already covers 3D refraction. A 2D node keeps the trace exact (no sampling noise), lets the kit measure
  angles in pixels, and composes with every 2D node through the ordinary transform.
- **Two modes.** Non-sequential tracing is what ray-optics (ricktu288) does and is what an author expects when
  elements overlap or rays reflect back; sequential tracing is the lens designer's convention (Hecht ch. 6,
  Pedrotti ch. 18) and makes a lens train's image predictable. Both are one function with an order argument.
- **Power, not colour, for the Fresnel split.** A split ray's visibility follows its power through the alpha,
  so an author sees the 4 % reflection off a lens surface and the near-total reflection at grazing incidence
  without choosing colours; the unpolarised average is what unpolarised sources carry (Born and Wolf §1.5).
- **The bound and the floor belong to the document.** Two facing mirrors or a Fresnel cascade would otherwise
  run forever; `maxBounces` and `minPower` are explicit attributes with explicit sinks so that the engine never
  decides when to stop, which is SREP 12's principle for every simulation the schema declares.
- **The parabolic GRIN profile.** Marchand's parabolic profile has the closed-form sinusoidal paraxial solution
  that the table can check against the integrator; a general profile would be a sampled asset problem.
- **Image construction as a child, not a mode.** The three principal rays are a convention for thin lenses
  only; an `object` names its lens and the trace stays general.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Wave effects (diffraction, interference) on the bench.** OW3 and OW4 of SREP 12 cover them; the bench is
  geometric optics only.
- **Dispersion per ray.** A prism's rainbow needs a wavelength per ray and a Cauchy or Sellmeier index; that is
  OW2 (spectral rendering), which may later add a `wavelength` to `source` and make `n` a function of it.
- **3D optical benches.** Lens design in 3D (skew rays, astigmatism) is a different tool; the planar bench is
  what explainers draw.
- **A `polarisation` on sources.** Rs and Rp are both in the reference; the drawn split uses their average.
  Polarised sources, and the Brewster window demonstration, are a follow-up once a `polarisation` attribute has
  a consumer (OW3's polarisers).

## Backwards compatibility

Additive. Documents without `opticalBench` validate and render unchanged; the node and its children require
version 1.8 (V17). The draft depends on SREP 14 for keyed attributes and on nothing else; the materials'
`ior`, `transmission` and `dispersion` are untouched.

## Open issues

1. **Wavelength on sources.** OW2 will want a `wavelength` per source and dispersive `n`; the attribute's home
   (the source or the bench) is for the OW2 draft.
2. **Aspheric and cylindrical surfaces.** Only spherical surfaces are specified; a conic constant on `thick`
   lenses is a candidate once the kit measures spherical aberration.
3. **Coatings.** The Fresnel split assumes bare dielectrics; an anti-reflection coating attribute (a fixed
   reflectance) is a small follow-up.
4. **Non-paraxial GRIN exit.** The slab's ends are flat and refract by Snell at the local index; whether the
   slab's side walls reflect or absorb is open (the reference stops a ray that leaves through a side wall).
5. **Sequential mode and screens.** In sequential mode a screen earlier in document order than a lens is never
   hit after the lens; the editor may prefer screens to be tested in every mode regardless of order.
