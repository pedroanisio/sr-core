# SREP 0 — Add relativity diagrams

## Abstract

Four elements for the diagrams of special and general relativity that science explainers draw, SREP 12's
milestone 6 (SP6): a `spacetime` child of the plots draft's `axes` that turns the box into a Minkowski diagram
in units of c, with a `boost` (animated in rapidity, so that boosts compose by addition), the grid of the boosted
frame, `event`s with light cones, inertial, accelerated and parametric `worldline`s and `lightRay`s, and a
`compact` mode that draws the Penrose diamond of Minkowski space or the Penrose diagram of the Schwarzschild
solution through Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates; an `embedding` primitive of `object3D` that draws Flamm's
paraboloid z = 2√(r_s(r − r_s)) or the Newtonian potential sheet as rings and spokes; and a `strain` modifier
that displaces any outline by the plus and cross polarisations of a gravitational wave, at a fixed frequency or
along the chirp of a given chirp mass. The reference tests are the ones SREP 12 names: a boost preserves the
interval and Flamm's paraboloid is z = 2√(r_s(r − r_s)); the draft adds that light cones stay at 45° under any
boost and that radial arc length on the sheet equals the proper radial distance.

## Motivation

SREP 12 row SP6 asks for "Minkowski diagrams with boosts, light cones, Penrose diagrams, embedding and curvature
grids, gravitational-wave strain", measured by "boost preserves the interval; Flamm's paraboloid
z = 2√(r_s(r − r_s))". It makes shot 4 (worldlines on a curved spacetime sheet) and, with SP7, shot 7 (a Penrose
diagram of the extended black hole) native; shot 13 (light cones on a spacetime diagram) is native today as
hand-placed paths, and shot 16 (Minkowski space squeezed into a Penrose diamond) is a custom expression per grid
line. At 67a661a nothing boosts a diagram, draws a cone from an event, compactifies a plane, embeds a metric or
strains a ring; the plots draft gives the `axes` box and its data-to-pixel mapping, the space-transforms draft
gives `map` modifiers over expressions, SREP 70 gives `parametricSurface` and SREP 76 gives `blackHole` with
r_s = 2 `mass` in units G = c = 1. This draft composes them so that the author writes the physics (a speed, a
mass, a strain) and not its algebra.

### Semantics

The reference module (`conformance/tools/relativity_reference.py`) names the function of each rule. Diagram
coordinates are the box's data units with c = 1; embedding coordinates are scene units with G = c = 1 as SREP
76 uses them; strain acts in the modifier's pixel space.

1. **Minkowski diagrams.** An event (x, t) in the box's rest frame is drawn at `boost(x, t, β)` =
   (γ(x − βt), γ(t − βx)), γ = `gamma(β)`; `interval(x, t)` = t² − x² is preserved by every boost (the
   reference checks it at β up to 0.999), `classify` names a separation timelike, lightlike or spacelike by
   its sign, `proper_time` and `proper_length` are its square roots. Boosts compose by `compose_beta` =
   (β₁ + β₂)/(1 + β₁β₂), which is addition of `rapidity` = atanh β. An `event` with `lightCone` fills the
   triangles `light_cone(event, extent)` whose edges have `cone_slopes` (1, 1) in every frame. A `worldline`
   is `worldline_inertial(x0, t0, β, t)`, the Rindler hyperbola `worldline_accelerated(a, τ)` =
   ((cosh aτ − 1)/a, sinh aτ/a) from rest at (`x0`, `t0`) whose speed is `accelerated_beta` = tanh aτ and
   whose proper time is τ, or `worldline_parametric(x, t, τ)` whose tangent must be timelike and future
   directed (an error otherwise). A `lightRay` is the segment from its event along (`direction`, 1). The lines
   x' = k and t' = k of a frame moving at β, in the coordinates of the frame it moves through, are
   `grid_x_line` (x = k/γ + βt) and `grid_t_line`, collected by `boosted_grid(β, n, extent)`. The axes map
   every child's point as the plots draft's rule 9 does (`diagram_pixel(x, t, box, β, compact)`), after the
   boost and the compactification. The grid the diagram draws is `diagram_grid(β, n, extent)`: the box frame's
   lines x = k and t = k where the boost carries them, which, since from the drawn frame the box frame moves
   at −β, are `boosted_grid(−β, n, extent)` in the drawn frame's own coordinates (x = k is x' = k/γ − βt'
   there), mapped by the axes with no further boost and compactified like everything else; at β = 0 they
   are the axes' own grid, and under an animated boost they shear while the light cones keep their 45°.
   (`boosted_grid(β, n, extent)` is the same picture from the other side: boosting its points by β makes them
   axis-aligned, so a diagram must not send them through the boost.)
2. **Compactification.** `compact="minkowski"` draws every point at `penrose(x, t)`: U = atan(t − x),
   V = atan(t + x), X = (V − U)/2, T = (U + V)/2, the diamond |X| + |T| < π/2 whose corners are
   `penrose_infinity` (spacelike i⁰ at (π/2, 0), future and past timelike infinity at (0, ±π/2)); null rays keep
   slope ±1 (`penrose_slope`), and `penrose_inverse` recovers the event. `compact="schwarzschild"` reads the
   child coordinates as Schwarzschild (r, t) with r_s = 2 `mass` of the `blackHole`, maps them to
   Kruskal–Szekeres `kruskal(r, t, rs)` = (T, X) with X² − T² = (r/r_s − 1) e^(r/r_s) outside the horizon and
   T² − X² = (1 − r/r_s) e^(r/r_s) inside, and then applies `penrose(X, T)` (`penrose_schwarzschild`): the
   horizon is the pair of diagonals, the singularity `kruskal_singularity(X)` = √(1 + X²) is the horizontal line
   T = π/4 of the diagram, and `kruskal_radius(T, X, rs)` recovers r. The diagram shows the region of the box;
   the extended (Kruskal) regions III and IV are drawn when the box's `xMin` is negative.
3. **Embeddings.** `kind="flamm"` draws the surface of revolution z = `flamm(r, rs)` = 2√(r_s(r − r_s)) for
   r from `rMin` ≥ r_s to `rMax`, sagging downward in scene y (`embedding_point` = (r cos φ, −z, r sin φ)), as
   `rings` circles and `spokes` radial lines of `samples` vertices each (`embedding_grid`). Its slope is
   `flamm_slope` = √(r_s/(r − r_s)), infinite at the horizon; the arc length of a radial line on the sheet
   (`flamm_arc_length`, Simpson's rule in s = √(r − r_s)) equals the proper radial distance
   `proper_radial_distance` = [√(r(r − r_s)) + r_s ln(√r + √(r − r_s))] between the radii, which is the sheet's
   reason to exist; its Gaussian curvature is `flamm_curvature` = −r_s/(2r³). `kind="potential"` draws
   z = `potential(r, k)` = −k/r from `rMin` > 0, the rubber-sheet picture of Newtonian gravity, labelled as such
   in the Rationale. With `surface="true"` the engine also fills the sheet as the `parametricSurface` of SREP
   70 with u = r and v = φ.
4. **Strain.** A `strain` modifier moves every outline point (x, y) about the centre by
   `strain_displacement(x, y, h₊, hₓ)` = ((h₊x + hₓy)/2, (hₓx − h₊y)/2) times cos(2πft + `phase`)
   (`strain_point`): a plane wave along the viewing axis, which turns a unit ring into an ellipse of semi-axes
   `ring_axes` = 1 ± h/2, h = √(h₊² + hₓ²), the plus ellipse along the axes and the cross ellipse along the
   diagonals (`strained_ring`). With `chirpMass` M_c (`chirp_mass(m1, m2)` = (m₁m₂)^(3/5)/(m₁ + m₂)^(1/5))
   the frequency τ = `merger` − t seconds before coalescence is the quadrupole-formula chirp
   `gw_frequency(τ, M_c)` = (1/π)(5/(256τ))^(3/8)(GM_c/c³)^(−5/8), with `time_to_merger` its inverse, the phase
   is 2π `chirp_cycles(τ₀, τ, M_c)` = 2π k (8/5)(τ₀^(5/8) − τ^(5/8)) accumulated from the modifier's start, and
   `gw_amplitude(d, M_c, f)` = (4/d)(GM_c/c²)^(5/3)(πf/c)^(2/3) is the physical strain at distance d that the
   author scales `hPlus` and `hCross` from (order 10⁻²¹ for 28 M☉ at 410 Mpc, which is why the attributes are
   the drawn amplitudes and not the physical ones). After `merger` the modifier applies no displacement.
5. **Animation and the seek check.** A keyed `boost` interpolates in rapidity: the value at a frame is
   `frame_beta(frame, fps, β₀, β₁, t₀, t₁)` = tanh of the linear rapidity, so that the per-frame increments
   compose to the keyed value and the diagram at a frame is a pure function of the frame (SREP 14; `seek_test`
   plays 240 frames by composition and compares every frame with the direct value to 1e-12 while checking the
   interval). Embeddings and strain are pure functions of their attributes and of scene time.
6. **Validation.** `validate_spacetime` is RD1, `validate_worldline` RD2, `validate_embedding` RD3 (after
   `embedding_radii` resolves `rs` and `rMin`) and `validate_strain` RD4. A `blackHole` that a `spacetime` or
   an `embedding` names is drawn by the diagram or the sheet, so SREP 76's undrawn-hole warning does not report
   it; an `accretionDisk` of such a hole is still reported, since neither draws the disk.

## Rationale

- **A child of `axes`, not a new box.** The plots draft already maps data to pixels, draws ticks and clips; a
  spacetime diagram is a cartesian box with a boost applied before the mapping, so `spacetime` adds the physics
  and reuses the box. The alternative, a `spacetimeDiagram` node, would duplicate every axes attribute.
- **Rapidity for the keyed boost.** β is bounded and its composition is not linear; rapidity is unbounded and
  additive, so a key from 0 to 0.8 passes 0.5 at the middle frame (the table shows it) and two half-boosts
  compose to the whole. Keying β linearly would make the diagram's grid accelerate toward the end of the key.
- **Kruskal, then the same compactification.** The Schwarzschild Penrose diagram is the Minkowski diamond's
  map applied to Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates, in which the metric is conformally flat in (T, X); one
  `penrose` function serves both modes and the singularity lands on a straight line, which the kit can measure.
- **Flamm's paraboloid as the measured test.** The sheet is defined by one property, that radial arc length on
  it is proper radial distance; the reference checks that property by quadrature and not only the formula, and
  states the curvature so that a "curvature grid" has a number behind it. The potential sheet is kept because
  shot 4's source draws it, with the caveat that it pictures a potential, not spacetime; the draft names it
  `potential` so no document can call it curvature.
- **Drawn amplitudes, physical chirp.** No video shows a strain of 10⁻²¹; the attributes are the amplitudes to
  draw, and the chirp mass gives the frequency evolution that is the recognisable signature. `gw_amplitude` is
  in the reference so that a caption can quote the real number.
- **Composition over new primitives.** The embedding is a `parametricSurface` with a known formula and the
  strain is a `deform` modifier; both reuse the subdivision and meshing of SREPs 70 and the space-transforms
  draft.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A 3D spacetime diagram (two space axes and time).** Light cones become cones and boosts rotate the grid in
  3D; worth a later draft once `axes3D` of the plots draft lands.
- **Kerr diagrams and the Einstein–Rosen bridge as a 3D surface.** The bridge is Flamm's paraboloid reflected
  through the throat; a `mirror` attribute may come with SP7's wormhole camera. Kerr's Penrose diagram has
  infinitely many regions and is out of scope.
- **Geodesics on the embedding (shot 4's falling apple).** Straight paths on the sheet are the geodesics of
  the (t, r) slice, a different embedding from Flamm's spatial one; open issue 1.
- **A `strain` with a waveform asset.** A sampled LIGO waveform is a `data` asset problem; the chirp formula
  covers the inspiral that explainers draw.

## Backwards compatibility

Additive. Documents without `spacetime`, `embedding` or `type="strain"` validate and render unchanged; the new
`primitive` and modifier `type` values and the new attributes require version 1.8 (V17). The draft depends on
the plots and space-transforms drafts for `axes` and modifier subdivision and on SREP 76 for `blackHole`.

## Open issues

1. **Worldlines on the embedding.** Shot 4 draws a falling body's worldline as a straight path on a curved
   (t, r) sheet; that is a different embedding (of the time–radius plane) from Flamm's spatial slice. A
   `kind="worldline"` sheet with its geodesics is a candidate for a follow-up.
2. **Kruskal regions III and IV.** Rule 2 draws them when the box reaches negative X; whether a document
   should name them (the white hole and the second exterior) is for the editor.
3. **Tick labels under compactification.** The axes' ticks are in data units; in the diamond they are
   meaningless. The draft hides them when `compact` is not `none`; a conformal tick scheme is open.
4. **The strain's physical amplitude.** `gw_amplitude` is in the reference for captions only; whether a
   `physical="true"` mode that scales by it with an `exaggeration` attribute is wanted is open.
5. **Doppler colour of a moving source.** `doppler` is in the reference; a `lightRay` colour shift in the
   boosted frame is not specified.
