# SREP 11 — Add rigid bodies to object3D

## Abstract

A `<rigidBody>` child of `object3D` makes the object a body in a 3D physics world, simulated beside the 2D
one with the same start, step, gravity and bounds. The simulated pose replaces the object's transform. Collision
shapes follow the primitive or are chosen explicitly, including a convex hull, an exact triangle mesh and a
convex decomposition. Constraints between 3D bodies gain a depth coordinate, an axis and a ball joint;
force fields and gravity gain a component along z.

## Motivation

Physics in the baseline moves 2D nodes only. 3D motion graphics (crates tumbling, objects dropping into
place, pendulums, product shots that settle) need bodies that turn in depth and collide as volumes, and
today an author has to bake that motion in a separate tool and import it as an animation clip.

### Semantics

In this section Δt is `physics@fixedStep` (default 1/120 s), k is `physics@solverIterations` (default 8),
h = Δt/k, ppm is `physics@pixelsPerMeter` (default 100), m is a body's `mass` and s its start scale.

**World.** The 3D world is separate from the 2D world: 3D bodies meet only 3D bodies. It uses the 2D world's
`physics@start`, `@fixedStep`, `@solverIterations`, `@pixelsPerMeter` and `@bounds`. Its axes are metres with y
up and z toward the camera: a scene point (x, y, z) in pixels is (x, −y, −z) / ppm, and a rotation quaternion
(qx, qy, qz, qw) in scene axes is (qx, −qy, −qz, qw). Gravity g is (`gravityX`, `gravityY`, `gravityZ`) m/s² in
those axes (default (0, −9.80665, 0)).

**Substeps.** For the 3D world `solverIterations` is normative: it is the number k of substeps into which each
step is divided. This overrides, for the 3D world only, the reading of `solverIterations` as a quality hint in
`conformance/DEFINITIONS.md` (D3 item 1 and D9, Physics); the 2D world keeps it as a hint.

**One step.** Step n advances the world from t = `physics@start` + n·Δt to t + Δt, in this order:

1. *Activation.* A dynamic body held on its animation (see Motion) whose `activateAt` ≤ t becomes dynamic, with
   the velocities of its `velocityX/Y/Z` and `angularVelocityX/Y/Z`.
2. *Animated bodies.* Each kinematic body, and each dynamic body still held, is given as its target the
   object's animated world pose at t + Δt (the Start pose rule evaluated at that time); it moves there during
   the step, with the velocity that motion implies, and pushes dynamic bodies it meets.
3. *Forces.* For each dynamic body, force fields are evaluated once, at time t, from the body's position and
   velocity at the start of the step; the force F = m·a (a in m/s², see Force fields) is held constant for
   all k substeps.
4. *Substeps.* k times, with v the linear and ω the angular velocity:
   1. v ← v + (g + F/m)·h (angular velocity gains the gyroscopic correction of Rapier 0.36);
   2. contacts and joints are solved, with position correction (one solver iteration);
   3. |v| is capped at 400 m/s and |ω| at π/(4·Δt) rad/s; then x ← x + v·h and the orientation turns by ω·h;
   4. contacts and joints are solved again without position correction (one iteration).
5. *Damping,* once per step, after the substeps: v ← v / (1 + c·Δt) and ω ← ω / (1 + c_a·Δt), with c =
   `linearDamping` and c_a = `angularDamping` in 1/s. Positions already integrated in the step are not changed.
6. *Breaking.* Joints are tested against `breakForce` (see Constraints).

For a free body starting at rest with no damping, velocity grows by g·h each substep and position by the new
velocity times h, so after n steps (N = n·k substeps) it has fallen

    d = g·h²·N(N+1)/2

metres. With damping c > 0, let r = 1/(1 + c·Δt). The velocity at the start of step n is v_n = (g/c)·(1 − rⁿ),
each step moves the body Δt·v_n + g·Δt²·(k+1)/(2k), and after n steps it has fallen

    d = n·g·Δt²·(k+1)/(2k) + (g·Δt/c)·(n − (1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r)).

A body starting at rest with velocity u (no gravity, no damping) moves u·n·Δt; with damping c its step
displacements are u·Δt·rᵐ (m = 0 … n−1).

Contact and joint solving (soft contacts, warm starting of contacts, contact prediction distance, the order in
which constraints are solved, continuous collision detection) is Rapier 0.36's (rapier3d-f64, enhanced
determinism) with `IntegrationParameters::default()` except for `dt` = Δt and `num_solver_iterations` = k. An
independent engine need only match it within the conformance tolerances.

**Time.** At a time t ≥ `physics@start` a frame shows the state after floor((t − start)/Δt + 10⁻⁹) steps.
Seeking gives the same state as playing up to t. Before `physics@start` the object is placed by its own
transform and animation, not by the simulation.

**Start pose.** At `physics@start` a body's pose is the object's world transform, split into translation,
rotation and scale. The world transform is the object's own M (conventions rule 2.5) premultiplied by its
parent's:

- with `@parent` naming an object3D, that object's world transform (its simulated pose if it is a body);
  naming a 2D node, that node's 2D world transform embedded in 3D (z unchanged);
- otherwise, with a child `transformConstraint type="parent"`, its target's transform as above, weighted by the
  constraint's `influence` w (clamped to [0, 1]): the parent's scale S_p, rotation R_p and translation T_p
  become lerp(1, S_p, w), slerp(identity, R_p, w) and w·T_p;
- otherwise, the 2D container's world transform, embedded as above.

Translation is the matrix's last column. The scale sᵢ is the length of column i, with s_x negated when the
matrix's determinant is negative. The rotation is taken from the columns divided by their scales; shear (from a
rotated parent with non-uniform scale) is not represented and is dropped. The scale sizes the collision shape
and is kept: from the start on, the object's world transform is T(simulated position) · R(simulated rotation)
· S(start scale), replacing its own transform, parent and parent constraint. Objects parented to a body follow
it. A static body keeps its start pose; later animation of a static body is ignored.

**Shapes.** Sizes are in the object's units times its start scale |s| and centred on the object's origin. With
the object's attributes: r = `radius` (default 50); w = `width`, default 2r; H = `height`, default 2r; D =
`depth` (default 10); and for a capsule the total length L = max(`height` or 4r, 2r).

| `shape` | collider |
|---|---|
| `auto`, primitive `box` or `text` | a box w·\|s_x\| × H·\|s_y\| × D·\|s_z\| |
| `auto`, primitive `plane` | a box w·\|s_x\| × H·\|s_y\| × 1·\|s_z\| |
| `auto`, primitive `sphere` or `globe` (SREP 10) | a sphere of radius r·\|s_x\| when \|s_x\| = \|s_y\| = \|s_z\|; otherwise the convex hull of its tessellation |
| `auto`, primitive `cylinder` or `cone` | that shape along y, half height H/2·\|s_y\|, radius r·\|s_x\|, when \|s_x\| = \|s_z\|; otherwise the convex hull of its tessellation |
| `auto`, primitive `capsule` | a capsule along y, radius r·\|s_x\|, straight half length (L/2 − r)·\|s_y\|, when \|s_x\| = \|s_z\|; otherwise the convex hull of its tessellation |
| `auto`, primitive `torus`, `extrude` or `mesh` | the convex hull of its triangles |
| `auto`, any other primitive (`clay`, `map`) | a sphere of radius r·max\|s\|, with a warning |
| `box` | on `box`, `text`, `extrude`: as `auto` on a box; on `plane`: as `auto` on a plane; on any other primitive a box 2r·\|s_x\| × H·\|s_y\| × 2r·\|s_z\| |
| `sphere` | a sphere of radius r·max(\|s_x\|, \|s_y\|, \|s_z\|) |
| `capsule` | a capsule along y, radius r·max(\|s_x\|, \|s_z\|), straight half length (L/2 − r)·\|s_y\| |
| `cylinder`, `cone` | that shape along y, half height H/2·\|s_y\|, radius r·max(\|s_x\|, \|s_z\|) |
| `convex-hull` | the convex hull of the object's triangles |
| `trimesh` | the object's triangles (static and kinematic bodies only, C48) |
| `decomposition` | convex parts of the object's triangles (below) |

A cone's apex points toward scene −y (up on screen). Each collider dimension is at least 10⁻⁴ m. Where the
object has no triangles (a primitive other than those with a tessellation below, an `extrude` without a usable
`@path`, a mesh that cannot be loaded or holds splats), `convex-hull`, `trimesh` and `decomposition` fall back
to a sphere of radius r·max|s|, with a warning. A hull or mesh the physics engine cannot build (degenerate
points) becomes a sphere of 0.01 m.

*Triangles.* The triangles of a primitive are its tessellation at s = `segments` (default 32, clamped to
[3, 512]), so a convex hull and a decomposition depend on `segments`; for collision the tessellation is
normative, whatever D3 item 1 allows for drawing. With vertices on the surface: `sphere` and `globe`, s
meridians by max(⌊s/2⌋, 2) bands of latitude; `cylinder`, s points on each rim; `cone`, s points on the base rim
and the apex; `capsule`, s meridians and max(⌊s/2⌋, 2) bands on each hemisphere; `torus`, tube radius
min(H/2, r) (0.35r without `height`), s segments round the ring by max(⌊s/2⌋, 3) round the tube; `box` and
`plane`, the eight corners; `extrude`, the path flattened with a tolerance of 0.25 px, extruded by D with
`bevel`. A mesh asset contributes its triangles in its rest pose, after the import convention of rule 2.6.

*Decomposition.* `decomposition` is Rapier's convex decomposition (parry3d-f64 0.31 V-HACD, with
`VHACDParameters::default()`): resolution 64, concavity 0.01, plane downsampling 4, convex hull downsampling 4,
alpha 0.05, beta 0.05, convex hull approximation on, at most 1024 convex hulls, fill mode flood fill without
cavity detection. The input is the triangles in metres in physics axes. Beyond these parameters the parts are
engine-defined, and no conformance case covers them.

**Mass.** The collider's mass is the body's `mass` in kilograms. Its centre of mass and inertia are those of the
collider shape (in metres) at a uniform density that gives that mass; a body turns about that centre of mass,
and its reported pose is the object's origin. `fixedRotation` locks all rotation.

**Motion.** `velocityX/Y/Z` are pixels per second and `angularVelocityX/Y/Z` degrees per second, in scene axes;
a dynamic body takes them at `physics@start`, or at activation. A dynamic body whose `activateAt` is later than
`physics@start` is held on its animation until activation (step 1): it moves as a kinematic body does, and does
not respond to gravity, forces or contacts. `sensor` bodies detect overlaps but exert no contact forces.
`bullet` enables continuous collision detection for the body.

**Collision filtering.** A body belongs to group bit min(`collisionGroup`, 31). `collidesWith` is `all` (every
bit) or a list of non-negative integers separated by spaces or commas, each giving bit min(n, 31); tokens that
are not integers are ignored. Two bodies collide only when each one's group bit is in the other's
`collidesWith`.

**Materials.** Friction and restitution between two bodies are the averages of the two bodies' values. The
bounds take each body's own `friction` and `restitution`: their colliders have coefficient 0 with the
maximum as combine rule, so a contact with a wall uses max(body's value, 0), as D9 (Physics) states for 2D
walls.

**Bounds.** `physics@bounds="floor"` adds a static ground whose top is scene y = H_f (the frame height),
5 m thick and 2·10⁴ m wide in x and z. `"frame"` adds six static slabs, 5 m thick, whose inner faces enclose
0 ≤ x ≤ W_f, 0 ≤ y ≤ H_f, −D_f/2 ≤ z ≤ D_f/2, with W_f × H_f the frame size and D_f = max(W_f, H_f).

**Constraints.** A constraint is 3D when `@a` names a 3D body. `@b` then names another 3D body; a `@b` that names
a node without a 3D body, and a constraint whose `@a` is a 2D body and whose `@b` is a 3D body, are reported and
ignored. Without `@b` (always so for `pin`, C9) the other end is a fixed body whose origin is the scene origin.
Joined bodies still collide with each other. Let m_a be the mass of body a.

- The anchor is (`x`, `y`, `z`) in scene pixels at the start when both `x` and `y` are given, `z` defaulting to
  0; otherwise b's centre at the start, or a's without `@b`. C10 requires `x` and `y` on a pin, so a pin's anchor
  is always given. The anchor is fixed in each body's frame at the start.
- The axis is (`axisX`, `axisY`, `axisZ`) in scene axes; an absent component is 0 when any is given; with none
  given it is (0, 0, 1) for `hinge` and `motor` and (1, 0, 0) for `slider`; a zero vector means (1, 0, 0). The axis
  is fixed in each body's frame at the start.
- `spring`: a force F = stiffness·(ℓ − ℓ₀) + damping·dℓ/dt along the line between the two bodies' centres
  (anchors are not used), with ℓ₀ = `restLength` (default the centres' distance at the start), stiffness in N/m
  default 10·m_a, damping in N·s/m default 0.5·m_a.
- `distance`: as `spring` with defaults stiffness 10⁴·m_a and damping 10²·m_a.
- `rope`: the centres' distance may not exceed `restLength` (default their distance at the start); anchors are
  not used.
- `pin`: without `stiffness`, a ball joint between a and the world at the anchor; with `stiffness`, a spring of
  rest length 0 between the anchor point on a and the world anchor, damping default 0.5·m_a.
- `ball`: the anchor points on the two bodies stay together; rotation is free.
- `hinge`: the anchor points stay together and the only relative rotation is about the axis. When both
  `minAngle` and `maxAngle` are given, the angle from the start pose, in degrees, positive right-handed about the
  axis in scene axes (for the default axis, clockwise on screen, as a positive 2D rotation), stays between them;
  with only one given there is no limit.
- `motor`: a hinge whose relative angular velocity is driven toward `motorSpeed` degrees per second by Rapier's
  velocity motor (acceleration-based, damping factor 10³·m_a), its torque capped by `maxForce` in N·m when given.
- `slider`: the only relative motion is translation along the axis; when both `minAngle` and `maxAngle` are
  given, the travel from the start, in pixels, stays between them.
- `weld`: b keeps its pose relative to a at the start; the anchor is not used.

`breakForce` (newtons): after each step, the joint's force is |λ| / h, where λ is the impulse the joint applied
on its locked translational axes in the step's last substep (Rapier's `ImpulseJoint::impulses`, linear part). When
the force exceeds `breakForce` the joint is removed; it has acted for the whole of that step and acts in no
later step, and the frame reports it broken. The joints that lock a translation are `pin` without stiffness,
`ball`, `hinge`, `motor`, `slider` (across its axis) and `weld`; `spring`, `distance`, `rope` and a `pin` with
stiffness lock none, so `breakForce` never removes them.

The 2D world tests the same Rapier impulse but divides it by the whole step Δt. Both quotients are in newtons,
but they measure different things: |λ|/h is the mean force during the last substep, while the 2D |λ|/Δt spreads
that substep's impulse over a whole step and is 1/k of it. For the same load and the same `solverIterations`, a
2D joint therefore withstands k times the force a 3D joint with the same `breakForce` does.

**Force fields** act on dynamic 3D bodies in 3D, in scene pixels and scene axes, converted to m/s² and applied as
m·a (sampled as in step 3): `radial` pulls toward (or pushes from) (`x`, `y`, `z`) along the 3D distance;
`directional` and `wind` push along (`forceX`, `forceY`, `forceZ`) with +z toward the camera, the z part of `wind`
gusting with the same noise as its x and y parts; `drag` opposes the velocity in 3D; `vortex` turns about the
line through (`x`, `y`) parallel to z; `turbulence` and `attractor-path` act in the xy plane, from the body's x
and y.

### Defaults and the neutral case

The element is new, and the new attributes default to zero; documents without 3D bodies render as before.

## Rationale

- **A separate 3D world** keeps the baseline's 2D behaviour bit-identical.
- **Scene axes in the document, y-up metres inside** follow the conventions (rule 2.1, 2.6) and the 2D
  physics baseline (metres, y up, `pixelsPerMeter`).
- **Substeps as the integration rule.** A 3D fall of any length is only reproducible when the substep count is
  fixed; making `solverIterations` that count reuses the existing attribute and the value the 2D world already
  passes to the same solver.
- **Damping once per step** is what Rapier does; describing it any other way would make the reference engine
  non-conforming.
- **Shapes from the primitive** mirror the 2D baseline, which derives a body's box from its node.
- **Walls take each body's material,** as D9 already requires in 2D.
- **C48:** a dynamic triangle mesh has no well-defined volume or inertia; every mainstream engine (Rapier,
  PhysX, Bullet) restricts it to static or kinematic use.

## Rejected alternatives

- **One world for 2D and 3D bodies.** 2D nodes have no depth or volume; mixing them changes 2D results.
- **A new `rigidBody3D` element name.** The context (`object3D`) already says it is 3D, and authors
  expect the 2D name.
- **Per-substep damping,** v ← (v + g·h)/(1 + c·h) each substep, as the first draft stated. It is not what the
  solver does, and the two differ measurably (for the damped case below, 109.6 px instead of 113.7 px).
- **Dividing the break impulse by Δt as in 2D.** It understates the force the joint carries by the factor k.

## Backwards compatibility

Every 1.1 document is valid and renders the same. `object3D/rigidBody` requires `version="1.2"` (V5). The
new constraint, field and physics attributes are accepted in every version and do nothing without 3D bodies.

## Conformance

| Case | Checks | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| `conformance/cases/srep-0011-rigid3d-fall.xml` | a sphere at rest, `linearDamping="0"`, falls for 1 s with Δt = 0.1 s, k = 4, ppm = 20: N = 40 substeps give d = g·h²·N(N+1)/2 = 5.0259 m = 100.52 px (10 whole steps would give 107.87 px and the exact fall 98.07 px) | 2 px |
| `conformance/cases/srep-0011-rigid3d-damped.xml` | the same fall with `linearDamping="1"` (`linearDamping` is at most 1) at 30 px per metre: the damped formula with n = 10, r = 1/1.1 gives 3.7912 m = 113.74 px (per-substep damping would give 109.57 px) | 2 px |
| `conformance/cases/srep-0011-rigid3d-velocity.xml` | a body with `velocityX` = u px/s, gravity 0 and `linearDamping="0"` moves u·t px along x in t s | 2 px |
| `conformance/cases/srep-0011-rigid3d-rest.xml` | a sphere of radius 10 dropped onto a static box whose top is at y = 250 rests with its centre at y = 240 | 2 px |

The fall and damped cases cover the substep and damping rules and the frame-time rule; the velocity case covers
the units of `velocityX` and the axis mapping; the rest case covers contacts and `shape="auto"` for spheres and
boxes. Decomposition, the tessellation of hulls, joints and `breakForce` are not yet covered.

## Open issues

- The contents of `physics@cache` are engine-specific; whether a 3D cache format should be standardised.
- Whether `turbulence` and `attractor-path` should act along z.
- `breakForce` never removes a `spring`, `distance`, `rope` or stiff `pin`, in 2D or 3D, because they lock no
  translation; whether it should test their spring or limit impulse.
- A non-pin constraint without `@b` ties a to a fixed body at the scene origin, so a `spring`, `distance` or
  `rope` then pulls toward the scene origin rather than toward the anchor; whether it should use the anchor.
- Whether the 2D `breakForce` should also divide by the substep duration.
