# SREP 0 — Add scale: powers of ten, depth resolution and a floating origin

## Abstract

Four rules for scenes that span twenty decades of distance, SREP 12's milestone 6 (SP5): a depth-resolution
requirement on the camera, stated as a property the engine's reversed binary32 depth already has, with a
logarithmic mapping for buffers that lack it; a `geometric` key interpolation so that a camera distance keyed
from 10 to 10²⁰ scene pixels pulls back one decade per equal interval (the powers of ten camera); a `scaleBar`
child of `camera` that draws the largest 1-2-5 × 10ᵏ bar that fits a width in pixels at the measured depth and
labels it in metres, au, ly or pc; and a floating origin inside the engine, where positions are rebased about
the eye in binary64 before any binary32 arithmetic, so that the document keeps one space (SREP 14, rule 4) and
objects near the camera do not jitter when the scene is 10²⁰ units across. The reference tests are the ones
SREP 12 names: no depth fighting across 10²⁰ in scale.

## Motivation

SREP 12 row SP5 asks for a "powers-of-ten camera and scale bars in scene pixels; logarithmic depth; double
precision and a floating origin inside the engine only, never a second space in the document", measured by "no
depth fighting across 10²⁰ in scale". At 67a661a `camera.rs` clamps `near` to 1e-4 and `far` to `near` + 1e-3
and builds the projection in binary32 with the reversed mapping (`three.rs` clears depth to 0 and tests
`GreaterEqual` on a `Depth32Float` buffer); nothing says what ratio of `far` to `near` the engine must resolve,
`curveType` (xsd 232) has no interpolation that moves a value in its logarithm, no element draws a scale bar,
and world positions reach the GPU as binary32 after a binary32 model matrix. The planets, orbits and time-frames
drafts place bodies at 10¹¹ m and star fields at 10¹⁷ m in the same scene; without this draft the author cannot
state the distances, see the scale or trust the depth order.

## Specification

### Syntax

A diff for schema 1.8 against `schema/scene-render.xsd` at 1.7.1 (line numbers of that file); the schema
files are not changed by this draft.

- `cameraType` (4423): a new attribute `depth`, one of `reversed` (default) and `logarithmic`: the depth
  mapping of rule 1. `near` (4468) and `far` (4471) keep their types; SC1 bounds their ratio.
- `curveType` (232): a new enumeration value `geometric` (rule 2); `keyType` (421) is unchanged, the value
  pair of a `geometric` key is checked by SC2. `geometric` is a key curve: on the `curveType` consumers that
  ease a progress from 0 to 1 (`motionPath/@interpolation`, `flyTo/@easing`, `transition/@curve`, `sizeCurve`
  and `colorCurve` of `particleEmitter` and `particles3D`) the implicit pair 0 → 1 fails SC2 and the value is
  refused, never mapped to `linear` (a document would then mean something other than what it wrote).
- `scaleBar`, a new optional child element of `cameraType` (the choice at 4430), at most one:
  - `maxWidth` (`xs:positiveInteger`, pixels, default 200: the bar never exceeds it), `units` (one of `auto`
    and `si`, default `auto`: rule 3's unit ladder or metres only), `distance` (`positiveDecimal`, scene
    pixels, optional: the depth the bar measures; absent, the distance to `target`, else to the nearest
    surface under the frame centre, else `far`), `x`, `y` (`xs:double`, pixels from the frame's bottom left,
    default 24 and 24), `color` (`colorType`, default white), `thickness` (`positiveDecimal`, pixels, default
    2), `label` (`xs:boolean`, default true).
- Schematron:
  - **SC1** `near` is positive, `far` exceeds `near`, and `camera/@far` divided by `camera/@near` is at most
    1e20 (`depth_ratio_valid`).
  - **SC2** a `geometric` key and the key after it have non-zero values of one sign (a key's `interpolation`
    is the curve of the segment it leaves, as `keyType` reads every per-key curve attribute; a key with no
    curve of its own under `defaultInterpolation="geometric"` is checked too, `timeRemap` keys included; a
    last key that is `geometric` governs no segment and is not checked). The static check applies when both
    values are plain numbers (XPath `number()` finite); a pair whose value is an expression or carries a unit
    is the engine's to check with the same rule on the magnitudes at build time, a build error of the engine
    as the reactive-values draft's E1 and E2 are, and two lengths of one unit interpolate geometrically; the
    `curveType` consumers that ease a progress (the `curveType` entry above) refuse `geometric` outright.
  - **SC3** a camera has at most one `scaleBar`, `scaleBar/@maxWidth` (default 200) is at most
    `project/@width`, and `units` is `auto` or `si`.
  - **SC4** `scaleBar/@distance`, when present, lies between the camera's `near` and `far` inclusive (their
    defaults 0.1 and 10 000 when absent).
  - **V17** `camera/@depth`, `geometric`, `scaleBar` and its attributes need `scene/@version="1.8"` or later.

### Semantics

The reference module (`conformance/tools/scale_reference.py`) names the function of each rule. Distances are
scene pixels (SREP 4: `physics/@pixelsPerMeter` pixels per metre, default 100) unless a rule says metres; a
"binary32" value is an IEEE 754 single, a "binary64" value a double.

1. **Depth resolution.** The depth buffer value of a surface at distance z between `near` n and `far` f is
   `depth(z, n, f, mode)`: `reversed` is d = n (f − z) / ((f − n) z), 1 at n and 0 at f, the engine's clip
   mapping at 67a661a (z_clip = n f / (f − n) − n z / (f − n) with w = z); `logarithmic` is
   d = ln(z / n) / ln(f / n), 0 at n and 1 at f. The gap the buffer resolves at z is
   `resolvable_gap(z, n, f, mode, kind)` = ulp(d) / |dd/dz| where ulp(d) is `depth_ulp(d, kind)`: the binary32
   spacing at d for a `float` buffer (`f32_ulp`), 2⁻²⁴ for a 24-bit `fixed` buffer; `relative_resolution` is
   the gap over z. A camera **resolves** when `worst_resolution(n, f, mode, kind)`, the largest relative
   resolution over n × 10ᵏ for every decade up to f, is at most `RESOLUTION` = 1e-5 (`resolves`). The engine
   must render every camera that satisfies SC1 so that two surfaces whose distances differ by more than
   `resolvable_gap` at that distance never swap order (no depth fighting), and must not clamp `near` or `far`
   inside the document's values when SC1 holds. Under `reversed` with a binary32 buffer the relative resolution
   is 2⁻²³ at every decade for any ratio, so the default resolves 10²⁰; under `logarithmic` it is
   ulp(d) ln(f / n), 2.7e-6 with 24 bits at 10²⁰; `reversed` with a fixed 24-bit buffer gains nothing over
   the standard mapping, its relative resolution at the far plane is about 2⁻²⁴ f / n, so it holds 10⁻⁵ only up to
   f / n = 168 (`resolves(1, 168, 'reversed', 'fixed')` is true, 169 false). An engine whose buffer is
   fixed-point must use `logarithmic` whatever the document says and report it. An engine may store 1 − d in
   either mode (the reverse-Z convention: a greater-or-equal compare, a clear to 0); the gap the buffer resolves
   then reads ulp(1 − d) in place of ulp(d), the resolution profile is mirrored over the range and its worst
   case is unchanged (under `logarithmic` with a binary32 buffer, 2⁻²⁴ ln(f / n) = 2.74 × 10⁻⁶ at f / n =
   10²⁰, at the far decades for d and at the near ones for 1 − d).
2. **Geometric keys.** Between two keys of values v₀ and v₁, the first with `interpolation="geometric"`
   (the curve of the segment it leaves), the value at fraction u of the key interval is `geometric(v0, v1, u)` = v₀ (v₁ / v₀)ᵘ (`interpolate(v0, v1, u, mode)`
   dispatches `linear` and `geometric`). The powers of ten camera is a camera distance keyed geometrically:
   `powers_of_ten(t, t0, t1, d0, d1)` holds d₀ before t₀ and d₁ after t₁, and `time_at(d, t0, t1, d0, d1)` is
   its inverse, so a key pair from 10 to 10²⁰ over 38 s passes one decade every 2 s (`decades(v0, v1)` =
   log₁₀(v₁ / v₀)). The value at a frame is `frame_distance(frame, fps, t0, t1, d0, d1)`, a pure function of the
   frame (the seek check of SREP 14; `seek_test` plays 960 frames as a running product and compares every
   frame with the direct value to 1e-9).
3. **The scale bar.** The screen pixels per metre at the measured depth are `pixels_per_metre(depth, focal,
   ppm)` = `pixelsPerMeter` × focal / depth, where focal is `focal_pixels(fov, width)` = (width / 2) /
   tan(fov / 2) for the camera's horizontal `fov` (or the value `focalLength` and `sensorWidth` give), and
   `pixels_per_metre_ortho(orthoHeight, height, ppm)` = `pixelsPerMeter` × height / `orthoHeight` for an
   orthographic camera. The bar is `scale_bar(px_per_m, maxWidth, units)`: its unit is `bar_unit`, the largest
   of m, au (149 597 870 700 m, IAU 2012), ly (9 460 730 472 580 800 m, a Julian year of light) and pc
   (648 000 / π au) not above `maxWidth` / px_per_m, or m when `units="si"`; its value is `nice_length`, the
   largest 1, 2 or 5 × 10ᵏ of that unit that fits; its label is `scale_label(value, unit)`, with SI prefixes
   from p to G on metres and parsecs and plain values on au and ly. The bar is therefore between 40 % and
   100 % of `maxWidth`, is at least one picometre (an error below), and is recomputed every frame
   (`scale_bar_at`, `scale_bar_frame`) as the measured depth changes.
4. **The floating origin.** Before any binary32 arithmetic the engine subtracts the eye position from every
   world position in binary64 and converts the differences (`floating_origin(positions, eye)`); it never
   converts world positions to binary32 first (`world_positions`, the error it avoids). The error of a gap g
   between a point and the eye is `world_error(p, eye)` in world coordinates and `rebased_error(p, eye)` after
   rebasing: the first is up to the binary32 spacing at |p|, 2⁴³ ≈ 8.8e12 units at 10²⁰, the second is at most
   half the spacing at g. `rebase_needed(p, eye)` is true when the world error exceeds `RESOLUTION` × g, and
   `world_extent(gap)` = 2^(24 + ⌊log₂ gap⌋) is the magnitude below which binary32 world coordinates still
   resolve the gap (16 777 216 units for a gap of 1). The document keeps one coordinate space in scene pixels
   (SREP 14, rule 4): no element declares an origin, a second unit or a precision; authors write 10²⁰ where
   they mean 10²⁰ and the engine rebases. Model, view and projection matrices are composed in binary64 and the
   view-relative result is sent to the GPU in binary32; the depth test then sees camera-relative distances and
   rule 1 applies as stated.
5. **Validation.** `validate_camera(near, far, mode)` is SC1 and the mode enumeration; `validate_keys(v0, v1,
   mode)` is SC2; `validate_scale_bar(maxWidth, units, distance, near, far, width)` is SC3 and SC4.

## Rationale

- **A requirement, not a buffer format.** Rule 1 states what the engine must resolve and gives the mapping
  that reaches it on each buffer kind; it does not mandate `Depth32Float` or reversed-z, which are the
  reference engine's choices at 67a661a and already resolve 10²⁰ (the table shows 2⁻²³ at every decade). The
  `logarithmic` attribute exists for engines with fixed-point buffers and for authors who want the gap to be
  a fixed fraction of the distance; on the reference engine both modes resolve and the attribute changes
  nothing visible.
- **A geometric curve rather than a camera feature.** The powers of ten camera is one key pair; making it a
  curve value lets any positive attribute (a light's intensity, a particle rate, a focal length) move in its
  logarithm with the same two keys and keeps the keyed value a pure function of time.
- **1-2-5 and a unit ladder.** Cartographers' and oscilloscopes' 1-2-5 series keeps the bar between 40 % and
  100 % of its limit; the ladder m, au, ly, pc follows what astronomers write at each scale ("50 000 au" for
  the Oort cloud, "2 kpc" for a galactic arm), and `units="si"` is there for the physics classroom.
- **Rebasing in the engine, not the document.** SREP 14 rule 4 refuses a second space; a `precision` or
  `origin` attribute would be one. The floating origin is a property of the renderer that the author cannot
  observe except by its absence (jitter at 10²⁰), so the rule is stated as an error bound the kit can measure.
- **1e-5.** One part in 10⁵ is a metre at 100 km and 10 km at 1 au, enough for the SREP 12 shots, and it is
  what a 24-bit logarithmic buffer gives across 10²⁰ with a factor of 3.6 to spare; 1e-6 would exclude that
  buffer for no shot's benefit.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Scene-level `unit` or `origin` attributes (a second space).** Refused by SREP 14 rule 4; every element
  would then carry a frame and the kit would measure two spaces.
- **Binary64 on the GPU.** Not available on the software adapters the kit runs on and four times the bandwidth;
  rebasing in binary64 on the CPU gives the same result for everything within binary32 of the eye.
- **Cascaded depth ranges (several near/far slabs).** Correct but costly, and unnecessary when a binary32
  reversed buffer already resolves 10²⁰; left to the engine as an implementation choice, not a rule.
- **A scale bar as a 2D `text` with a keyed width.** The author would have to compute the depth and the
  1-2-5 choice per frame; the bar is a camera property because its length is a function of the camera.
- **A logarithmic distance attribute on the camera (`distanceLog`).** The geometric curve covers it and more.

## Backwards compatibility

Additive. The default `depth="reversed"` is the mapping the engine uses at 67a661a; scenes without
`geometric` keys or a `scaleBar` validate and render unchanged. A scene whose `far` / `near` exceeds 1e20
is invalid under SC1 where it validated before: at 67a661a the engine clamps `near` to 1e-4 and `far` to
10 000 by default, so no kit scene reaches the bound. The new attributes and element require version 1.8
(V17).

## Open issues

1. **The measured depth without a target.** Rule 3 falls back to the nearest surface under the frame centre;
   an empty centre gives `far`, which may be a surprising bar. A `distance` key may be the better default.
2. **The bar's position in a stereo or dome output.** `x` and `y` are frame pixels; a dome projection has no
   bottom left. Left to the output drafts.
3. **Shadow maps at 10²⁰.** Rule 1 covers the main depth buffer; the cascaded shadow maps keep their own
   near/far per cascade and are not bounded here, and an AO/SSR prepass that outputs view depth from its own
   reverse-Z buffer is unchanged by the mode.
4. **Geometric keys through zero.** SC2 refuses a sign change; a two-key work-around (geometric to a small
   value, linear through zero) is possible and may deserve a note in the keys SREP.
5. **A `units="imperial"` ladder.** Not proposed; the schema editor may want it for a US classroom.
