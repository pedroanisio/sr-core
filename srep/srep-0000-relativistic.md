# SREP 0 — Add Kerr geodesics, ray bundles, beaming and a relativistic camera

## Abstract

[SREP 12](srep-0012.md) names, as its SP7 building block, "Schwarzschild and Kerr geodesics with ray bundles
(James et al. 2015); accretion disks with g⁴ beaming; relativistic camera (aberration, Doppler, searchlight)",
and records what landed in [SREP 76](srep-0076.md): the Schwarzschild hole, its disk and its sky; "Kerr, ray
bundles, beaming and the relativistic camera are open" (line 287). This SREP is the open part. A `blackHole`
gains a `spin`, and a geodesic camera then traces Kerr geodesics in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates from the
Hamiltonian, with the energy, the axial angular momentum and Carter's constant conserved along every ray as the
check. The disk's matter follows the Kerr Keplerian orbits and is beamed by g⁴. A camera gains a `motion`:
static (today), on the circular orbit at its radius, or in radial free fall; the frame is then seen through
aberration, the Doppler factor and the searchlight D⁴. A camera gains `bundle`: the sky is filtered over the
ellipse into which the hole maps each pixel's bundle of rays, so stars and the sky texture neither alias nor
drop out near the shadow. `conformance/tools/relativistic_reference.py` states the rules as pure functions and
measures the plan's bars: the photon sphere at 3M, the shadow at sqrt(27) M (traced and exact), the ISCO at 6M
and the weak-field deflection 4M/b, with the Kerr closed forms beside them.

## Motivation

- **Shot 7** (SREP 12 line 595, a black hole seen from a ship that falls toward it) and **shot 14** (line 602,
  the lensed disk of a spinning hole) are the plan's two shots of SP7; SREP 76 renders a hole that does not spin,
  seen by a camera that does not move, and the plan's row names both as open.
- **Spin is visible.** The shadow of a hole at `a = 0.95M` is lopsided: the prograde edge sits at 2.58 M and the
  retrograde edge at 6.92 M (both measured below), against sqrt(27) M = 5.20 M on both sides without spin. The
  ISCO moves from 6M to 2.32 M, so the disk reaches further in and its approaching side is brighter by g⁴.
- **A moving camera is the shot.** At the orbit of 10M the camera moves at 0.354 of light and the frame ahead
  of it is brighter by D⁴ = 3.3 and bluer by D = 1.35; a camera falling freely sees the sky behind it redden by
  1/(1 + sqrt(2M/r)). Without a defined camera motion two engines cannot agree on shot 7.
- **Stars alias.** Near the shadow one pixel's rays spread over a long thin ellipse of sky (axes 1.12 and 1.11
  at 10M looking away from the hole, below; larger and thinner near the ring), and point stars sampled at the
  pixel centre flicker from frame to frame. James et al. (2015) filter over that ellipse; SREP 76 supersamples.
- **The seek check.** SREP 14 rule 1 holds for every element here: the frame at time t depends on the hole,
  the disk, the camera's motion and the pixel, not on earlier frames.

### Semantics

Units: G = c = 1; lengths are in the unit of `mass`, written M; `a` is `spin`. Boyer–Lindquist coordinates
(t, r, θ, φ) with θ measured from `+y`; Σ = r² + a² cos²θ, Δ = r² − 2Mr + a². The names are the functions of
`conformance/tools/relativistic_reference.py`.

1. **The metric** (`metric`). g_tt = −(1 − 2Mr/Σ), g_tφ = −2Mar sin²θ/Σ, g_rr = Σ/Δ, g_θθ = Σ,
   g_φφ = (r² + a² + 2Ma²r sin²θ/Σ) sin²θ. At a = 0 it is SREP 76's Schwarzschild metric; the engine's frame at
   `spin="0"` is bit-identical to SREP 76's.
2. **Closed forms** (`horizons`, `ergosphere`, `photon_orbit`, `isco`, `omega_zamo`, `omega_kepler`). The
   horizons r± = M ± sqrt(M² − a²); the ergosphere r = M + sqrt(M² − a² cos²θ); the equatorial photon orbit
   r_ph = 2M(1 + cos(⅔ arccos(∓a/M))) (upper sign prograde); the ISCO of Bardeen, Press and Teukolsky with
   Z₁ = 1 + (1 − χ²)^⅓((1 + χ)^⅓ + (1 − χ)^⅓), Z₂ = sqrt(3χ² + Z₁²), r_isco = M(3 + Z₂ ∓ sqrt((3 − Z₁)(3 + Z₁ + 2Z₂))),
   χ = |a|/M; the frame-dragging rate ω = 2Mar/((r² + a²)² − a²Δ sin²θ); the Keplerian rate
   Ω = ±sqrt(M)/(r^{3/2} ± a sqrt(M)), negative when retrograde. At a = 0: r+ = 2M, r_ph = 3M, r_isco = 6M.
3. **Spin bounds.** |a| > M is an error (BH9); a camera inside the ergosphere, where no static observer exists,
   is an error (BH7 and BH12 keep it outside for the shipped bounds).
4. **The static observer** (`static_momentum`). The camera at (r, θ) is static; its frame is e_t = ∂_t/sqrt(−g_tt),
   e_r = ∂_r/sqrt(g_rr), e_θ = ∂_θ/sqrt(g_θθ), e_φ = (∂_φ − (g_tφ/g_tt) ∂_t)/N with N² = g_φφ − g_tφ²/g_tt. A
   pixel's ray leaves along the unit local direction d = (d_r, d_θ, d_φ) with unit local energy: k = e_t + d·e,
   and the conserved E = −p_t, L = p_φ with p_r = sqrt(g_rr) d_r, p_θ = sqrt(g_θθ) d_θ. The impact parameter is
   |L/E|; looking straight in (d = (−1, 0, 0)) gives L = 0 and E = sqrt(−g_tt).
5. **The trace** (`hamiltonian`, `carter`, `trace`). The ray follows Hamilton's equations of
   H = (Δ p_r² + p_θ² + (L − aE sin²θ)²/sin²θ − ((r² + a²)E − aL)²/Δ)/(2Σ) in the affine parameter, integrated
   with the classical Runge–Kutta step h = 0.005 r. E and L are exact; H = 0 and Carter's constant
   Q = p_θ² + cos²θ (L²/sin²θ − a²E²) are the checks: |H| < 10⁻⁶ and |Q − Q₀| < 10⁻⁵ at the end of every ray
   (measured below). At a = 0 the ray is SREP 76's.
6. **Outcomes.** A ray below 1.02 r+ is **captured** (black); one past the far radius is **escaped** (the sky,
   rule 12); one that crosses θ = π/2 between r_in and r_out, with the crossing interpolated linearly in the
   step, hits the **disk**; one that reaches 65536 steps is unresolved and is an error.
7. **The disk's redshift** (`disk_redshift`). Matter at r moves on the circular orbit of rule 2 in the sense of
   `orbit`; u^t = 1/sqrt(−(g_tt + 2Ω g_tφ + Ω² g_φφ)) and g = 1/(u^t (1 − Ω L/E)). At a = 0 and L = 0 it is
   SREP 76's sqrt(1 − 3M/r); approaching matter (ΩL > 0) has g > 1 over the face-on value, receding matter less.
8. **The Schwarzschild checks** (`critical_impact`, `critical_impact_exact`, `schwarzschild_deflection`,
   `traced_deflection`, `shadow_angle`). The capture boundary found by bisection on traced equatorial rays is
   sqrt(27) M at a = 0 and equals the exact b_c = sqrt(r_ph/M)(r_ph + 3M)/2 within 10⁻⁴ for every spin: 2M and 7M
   at a = M. The deflection of a ray of impact parameter b, by quadrature of the orbit equation after the
   substitution u = u₀ − s², is 4M/b in the weak field (within 0.74 % at b = 400M, 0.18 % at 1600M), 0.590396 at
   b = 10M, and the traced ray reproduces it; prograde rays bend less than retrograde ones (0.676, 0.859, 1.253 at
   b = 8M for a = 0.9M, 0 and −0.9M). The shadow angle of a static observer at d is SREP 76's
   asin(sqrt(27) M sqrt(1 − 2M/d)/d).
9. **The camera's velocity** (`orbit_speed`, `infall_speed`, `camera_velocity`). In the static frame, `orbit`
   moves along e_φ at v = sqrt(M/(r − 2M)) (0.5 at r = 6M) and `infall` along −e_r at v = sqrt(2M/r) (0.5 at
   r = 8M); `static` is at rest. The hole is Schwarzschild for these speeds; with spin the engine uses the
   same closed forms (open issue 2).
10. **Aberration and Doppler** (`aberration`, `doppler`, `infall_redshift`). For a view direction d of the
    static frame and the camera's velocity β, the moving camera looks along
    d' = (d + β̂((γ − 1) β̂·d + γβ))/(γ(1 + β·d)) and receives the light with D = γ(1 + β·d): light from ahead is
    bluer (D = γ · 1.5 at β = 0.5) and from behind redder (γ · 0.5); the map inverts under −β. The infalling
    camera sees the sky behind it with g = 1/(1 + sqrt(2M/r)): ⅔ at r = 8M, ½ at the horizon.
11. **Beaming** (`searchlight`, `beamed_radiance`). Radiance scales as the fourth power: the disk's rest-frame
    radiance times g⁴, and the whole frame times D⁴ for a moving camera; SREP 76's blackbody temperature is
    multiplied by g and D as before.
12. **Ray bundles** (`sky_direction`, `bundle_jacobian`, `magnification`, `ellipse_axes`, `bundle_filter`).
    The sky map takes the camera's look angles (x sideways, y up) to the azimuth and elevation of the escaped
    ray's travel direction at the far radius; J is its central-difference Jacobian at the pixel with the
    pixel's half-width as the step; |det J| is the magnification and the singular values of J are the semi-axes
    of the ellipse into which the pixel's bundle opens. With `bundle="true"` the sky and the stars are the mean
    over `bundleSamples` points of that ellipse; at 10M looking away from the hole the axes are 1.12 and 1.11
    and the magnification 1.25 (measured below), and they tend to 1 far away.
13. **Bounds.** 65536 steps per ray, 4096 bundle samples, the step fraction 0.005 and the capture radius
    1.02 r+ are constants of the reference module; `samples` keeps SREP 76's 1 to 4096.

## Rationale

- The Hamiltonian form in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates is the standard one for Kerr (Carter 1968); it
  reduces to SREP 76's Binet form at a = 0 and gives two conserved checks for free, where SREP 76 has one.
- The static observer is the only camera frame that exists at every point outside the ergosphere and
  coincides with SREP 76's camera; the moving cameras are stated as boosts of it (rule 10), so aberration and
  Doppler are one special-relativistic transform and not a second integrator.
- D⁴ and g⁴ are the exact intensity transforms of a Lorentz-invariant I/ν³, given here as separate functions
  because SREP 76 applies the factor through the blackbody temperature only.
- Bundles by central differences of the sky map cost four traces per pixel, which is what the engine already
  spends on antialiasing; the geodesic deviation equation (James et al.) is allowed as an implementation of the
  same Jacobian (open issue 3).

## Rejected alternatives

- **Kerr–Schild coordinates**, which are regular at the horizon: the camera, the disk and SREP 76 are stated
  in Boyer–Lindquist, and no ray needs to cross the horizon.
- **A free camera velocity** (`vx vy vz`): the two named motions are the plan's shots; a free boost needs an
  acceleration history to be a seekable function of time (SREP 14).
- **Beaming through the temperature only** (SREP 76): it is exact for a blackbody but not for `emission` colours
  or the sky; g⁴ on radiance holds for both.

## Backwards compatibility

- Every 1.7 document is valid in 1.8 and renders the same: `spin` defaults to 0 (rule 1), `orbit` to prograde,
  `motion` to static, `bundle` to false. BH10 at zero spin is BH4.
- The new attributes are gated on 1.8 (V17), as [SREP 0](srep-0000.md) requires.

## Open issues

1. **Polar rays.** The Boyer–Lindquist equations divide by sin θ; a ray through the axis needs a regularised
   step, which the reference module does not provide (no test ray passes within 10⁻³ of the axis).
2. **Moving cameras with spin.** Rule 9 uses the Schwarzschild orbit and infall speeds; for a spinning hole
   the circular orbit has Ω of rule 2 and a different local speed. The editor decides whether `orbit` takes the
   Kerr speed or `motion` is refused with spin.
3. **Geodesic deviation.** Whether an engine may compute the bundle Jacobian from the deviation equation
   instead of four traces, with the two agreeing within a stated tolerance.
4. **Kerr deflection in closed form.** Rule 8 checks the Kerr capture boundary against the exact value but the
   Kerr deflection only by ordering; a closed form (Iyer and Hansen 2009) could be a further row.
5. **Lens flare and depth of field** stay unused with geodesics (SREP 76).
