# SREP 0 — Add a style layer

## Abstract

[SREP 12](srep-0012.md) names, as its CL1 building block, "the same scene rendered flat, hand-drawn (marker
strokes with seeded jitter), toon-shaded with outlines, or photoreal", with the bar "toon bands fall at their
declared thresholds" (line 263), and its Looks row sets the lead as "one scene rendered flat, hand-drawn,
toon-shaded or photoreal" (line 200). This SREP is that block. A `look` asset names one of four looks,
`flat`, `sketch`, `toon` or `photoreal`, with its bands, outline and stroke parameters, and a `look`
attribute on `composition`, `group`, `object3D` and `shape` applies it to a subtree, the nearest ancestor
winning. The looks are defined as functions of quantities the engine already has: `n · l` of the 3D pass,
the depth and normal buffers, the flattened paths of the vector pass, and D24's gradient noise. A document
without `look` renders exactly as today: 3D nodes photoreal, vector nodes flat.

## Motivation

- **Shot 24** (SREP 12 line 612, minutephysics' spacetime diagrams) is "hand-drawn worldlines on a
  whiteboard", and the probe says "the hand-drawn look is a stroke font and hand-wobbled paths". Stroke fonts
  exist ([SREP 56](srep-0056.md)); nothing wobbles a path. Shot 25 (line 613) wants "hand-drawn charges with
  electric field lines", which is the fields draft (MF5) under this look.
- **The Looks row** (line 200) compares scene-render with After Effects for Kurzgesagt's flat look and Blender
  for Primer's toon look. The schema renders PBR through `renderer="raster"` or `"pathtrace"`
  (`schema/scene-render.xsd:4537`) and has no cel shading, no outlines and no sketch strokes; the effects of
  `effectType` (`posterize`, `threshold`, `halftone`, `schema/scene-render.xsd:5554`) act on the finished
  frame and cannot read `n · l`, so bands made with them move with the lighting of the whole image, not with
  the declared thresholds. The engine has no toon, sketch or outline code (a grep of `crates/` at 67a661a).
- **The bar** needs a quantised shading whose band boundaries are numbers the kit can measure: on a sphere lit
  by a directional light, the band of threshold `t` begins at the angle `acos(t)` from the light.
- **Determinism** ([SREP 14](srep-0014.md) rules 1 to 3): a hand-drawn jitter that is a stream of the frame
  number, not of the wall clock, so that the frame alone equals the frame played, and a boil that steps at a
  declared rate.

Attributes of a kind that does not use them are inert and a validator SHOULD report them ([SREP 18](srep-0018.md)):
`photoreal` uses none; `flat` uses the outline attributes only; `toon` uses shading and outline; `sketch` uses
all. The `band` elements are listed in increasing `threshold`; `darkest` is the level of the band below the
first threshold, so a look with `n` bands has `n + 1` levels.

### Semantics

The reference implementation is `conformance/tools/style_layer_reference.py`; the names in parentheses are
its functions.

1. **Resolution** (`resolve_look`). The look of a node is the `look` of the nearest ancestor-or-self that has
   one, the `composition` included. Without one, an `object3D` (and the other 3D nodes: `model`, `particles`,
   `volume`) is `photoreal` and every other node is `flat`. A `photoreal` look changes nothing: the node
   renders as schema 1.7 renders it. A `flat` look on a vector node changes nothing but adds the outline of
   rule 5 when `outline` is above 0.
2. **Bands** (`band_index`, `validate_bands`). For a surface point with unit normal `n` and unit direction to
   the light `l`, let `d = max(n · l, 0)` (`lambert`). The band of `d` is the number of thresholds `t_k` with
   `d ≥ t_k`: a value exactly at a threshold is in the upper band. On a sphere lit by a directional light the
   band of threshold `t` therefore begins at `acos(t)` from the light (`band_angle`; 60° for 0.5). The level
   of band `k` is `level_k`, with `level_0 = darkest`. At most 16 thresholds; thresholds strictly increasing in
   (0, 1]; levels nondecreasing in [0, 1].
3. **Toon shading** (`toon_shade`, `toon_specular`, `rim`). For each light of the 3D pass, the diffuse term
   is `albedo × level(band(d)) × light` with `light` the light's colour times its attenuation as the raster
   pass computes it (`crates/sr-gpu/src/three.wgsl` `shade_light`), summed over the lights, plus
   `albedo × ambient`, clamped to [0, 1] per channel. The specular band adds `light × 1` where
   `(n · h)^shininess ≥ specular` on the lit side (`h` the unit half vector of `l` and the view direction
   `v`), 0 elsewhere; the rim band adds the brightest level's colour where `1 − max(n · v, 0) ≥ rim`. The
   metallic, roughness, sheen, clearcoat and subsurface terms of the material are not evaluated; its
   `unevenness` ([SREP 39](srep-0039.md)) still perturbs `n`, so a clay knight stays clay in a toon shot.
4. **Flat shading** (`flat_shade`). A 3D node under `flat` is its albedo (base colour times texture) with no
   lights and ambient 1; a vector node is what it is today.
5. **Outlines** (`edge_mask`, `dilate`, `outline_radius`). After the 3D pass, a pixel is an edge pixel when a
   4-neighbour's depth exceeds its own by more than `depthThreshold` (so the near side of a silhouette is
   outlined, not the far side), or when `1 − n · n'` with a 4-neighbour's normal exceeds `normalThreshold`
   (both sides). The edge mask is dilated by the Chebyshev radius `ceil((outline − 1) / 2)` pixels and painted
   `outlineColor` over the shaded frame; `outline` 0 paints nothing. Pixels outside the frame are not
   neighbours.
6. **Mesh edges** (`face_normal`, `silhouette_edges`, `crease_edges`). The `sketch` look also draws, as
   strokes of rule 7, the silhouette edges of each mesh (a boundary edge, or an edge shared by a face with
   `n · view < 0` and one without, `view` the unit direction from the camera into the scene for an orthographic
   camera and the direction to the edge's midpoint for a perspective one) and its crease edges (shared edges
   whose face normals differ by at least `creaseAngle`). Face normals are the unit cross products of
   counter-clockwise triangles; a degenerate triangle is a build error.
7. **Sketch strokes** (`wobble`, `vertex_normal`, `closed_wavelength`). Every flattened path of a node under
   `sketch` (shape outlines and fills' edges, stroke-text strokes of SREP 56, chart and plot lines, the edges
   of rule 6 projected to the frame) is a polyline of points `p_i` with arc lengths `s_i` from the first point
   and total length `L`. Each point moves along its left normal `n_i` (the left normal of the mean of the unit
   directions of its two segments; one segment at an open end) by `a · N(σ, c, s_i / λ) · taper_i`, where `a`
   is `amplitude`, `N` is D24's gradient noise, `σ` is the path's seed of rule 9, `c` the channel of rule 8,
   and `λ` is `wavelength`. For an open path `taper_i = min(1, s_i / λ, (L − s_i) / λ)`, so the endpoints are
   fixed and the stroke leaves them smoothly; for a closed path there is no taper and
   `λ' = L / max(1, round(L / λ))`, so the noise closes on itself after whole turns. A path of fewer than two
   points (three closed) or of zero length is left alone. `amplitude` 0 moves nothing.
8. **Overdraw and boil** (`overdraw`, `boil_channel`). The path is drawn `passes` times, pass `k` on channel
   `k + passes · floor(time · boil + 10⁻⁹)`, each pass with the node's stroke paint at the node's opacity,
   blended source-over in pass order, so overlapping passes darken as marker strokes do. `boil` 0 holds one drawing for the whole composition; `boil` 12 redraws twelve
   times a second. The step is of `time`, never of the wall clock (SREP 14 rule 1).
9. **Seeds** (`path_seed`). The seed of a path is `E(project, id, seed, "sketch")` with `E` of D24 section 4,
   `id` the node's `id` and `seed` the look's `seed` (empty when absent). A node with several paths (a
   stroke-text, a chart) seeds path `index` (counting from 0) as `E(project, id + "/" + index, seed, "sketch")`;
   a node without `id` takes the `id` of its nearest ancestor with one, with its own index among that
   ancestor's paths. The frame alone equals the frame played.
10. **Marker width** (`marker_width`). The stroke width along a sketch pass is
    `width · (1 − taper + taper · sin(π s / L))`: the full width at the middle, `width · (1 − taper)` at both
    ends. The reference computes `sin(π · min(t, 1 − t))`, `t = s / L`, the same value and exact at the ends.
11. **Hatching** (`hatch_segments`, `hatch_layers`, `hatch_angle`, `sketch_surface`). A 3D surface under
    `sketch` takes the bands of rule 2; a surface pixel in band `k` of `n + 1` levels is hatched with `n − k`
    layers (the darkest band gets the most, the brightest none), layer `j` being parallel lines at
    `hatchAngle + 60° · j` (modulo 180°) spaced `hatchSpacing` pixels apart in screen space, each line
    `p · n = m · spacing` for the integers `m` and `n` the left normal of the direction, clipped to the
    pixel's band region and drawn as a stroke of rule 7. The reference clips to an axis-aligned box.
12. **Determinism and bounds.** Every function here is a pure function of the document and of `time`. A path
    has at most 10⁶ points after flattening; a look has at most 16 passes and 16 bands; a hatch region
    produces at most 10⁶ lines per layer. Exceeding a bound is a build error, not a truncation.

## Rationale

- **A look is an asset, applied by reference.** The alternative, attributes on every node, repeats a dozen
  numbers and cannot say "this shot is toon" in one place. An asset mirrors `material` and `strokeFont`, and
  the nearest-ancestor rule mirrors how `material` overrides resolve ([SREP 38](srep-0038.md)).
- **Bands by threshold, the band at the threshold being the upper one.** The bar of SREP 12 measures where the
  bands fall; `d ≥ t` makes the boundary angle exactly `acos(t)`, which the kit can check on a sphere without a
  half-pixel convention.
- **Outlines from depth and normals, not from mesh edges alone.** Saito and Takahashi (1990) draw outlines
  from the G-buffer, which outlines imported meshes, particles and volumes alike with no topology; mesh edges
  (rule 6) are kept for the sketch look, where the strokes must be paths to be wobbled.
- **The wobble is D24's gradient noise along arc length.** It is smooth, zero at the integers (so a path whose
  points sit on whole wavelengths is left alone, the first table row of the sketch group) and already a
  conformance quantity (SREP 26). The endpoint taper keeps joins closed; the closed-path rounding keeps a
  circle's wobble continuous at its seam.
- **Overdraw with per-pass channels and a boil step** is Lake et al. (2000)'s multiple-stroke pencil look
  made deterministic: the channel, not a random generator, changes per pass and per step.
- **Hatching by band** follows the same thresholds as the toon look, so a scene can switch between the two
  without re-authoring its lighting.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Toon as a post effect** (`posterize`, `threshold` on the frame): the bands then depend on the image's
  final luminance, including textures and fog, and cannot fall at `acos(t)`; rejected by the bar.
- **A `shading` attribute on `material`**: a scene with many imported materials would need every one edited,
  and the flat look on a vector subtree would have no material to carry it.
- **Per-frame random jitter** (a new draw each frame): fails SREP 14 rule 1 and makes every frame boil at the
  frame rate with no way to hold a drawing.
- **Outlines as inverted-hull geometry**: needs a mesh and a scale per object; the G-buffer edge is uniform in
  pixels and covers non-mesh nodes.

## Backwards compatibility

New asset, new optional attributes with neutral defaults, accepted in every version by the V17 gate. A
document without `look` renders as before: `photoreal` for 3D nodes and `flat` for vector nodes are the names
of today's behaviour, and `flat` with `outline` 0 adds nothing. `unevenness` and stroke fonts are unchanged.

## Open issues

- **Shadow bands.** Shadows of the raster pass (`castShadow`) under a toon look should be a band of their own
  (the darkest level) rather than a soft multiplier; this draft leaves the shadow term as the raster pass
  computes it, multiplied into `light`.
- **Paper and pencil texture.** Lake et al. (2000) multiply a paper texture into the sketch look; a `paper`
  image attribute on the look is one addition away, with the `film-grain` effect as the interim.
- **Fills under sketch.** A filled shape under `sketch` keeps its fill and wobbles its outline; a hatched fill
  (rule 11 applied to 2D fills by their luminance) is a possible `hatchFills` attribute.
- **Path tracing.** The `pathtrace` renderer ignores `toon` and `sketch`; the editor may rule that a toon look
  forces the raster path or that the path tracer bands its direct lighting.
