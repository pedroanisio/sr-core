# SREP 0 — Add the wind field

## Abstract

SREP 12's F4: one `wind` element under `physics`, a velocity field w(p, t) in metres per second over the
physics space, read by everything that moves in air. It has a mean (`speed` toward `direction`, `speedZ`
along the scene's depth), a stochastic gust factor (`gustiness`, `gustPeriod`, `gustOctaves`: an octave sum
of the random-streams draft's value noise over time), declared `gust` children (`at`, `duration`, `peak`,
`shape` one of `cosine`, `iec`, an optional `direction`) and a curl-noise `turbulence` (`turbulenceScale`,
`turbulenceOctaves`, `turbulenceTime`): the analytic curl of a vector potential of three D24 value-noise
fields on the lattice index pack(i, j, k), so the turbulence is divergence-free by construction (Bridson,
Hourihane and Marschner 2007). The turbulence rides the mean wind (Taylor's frozen turbulence) and slides in
time. Particles and rigid bodies that list the wind in their `forceFields` relax toward it with a `response`
time: a = (w − v) / `response`; the later SREPs of milestone 8 (clouds, ocean, snow, wildfire, vegetation)
sample w directly. The baseline's `forceField type="wind"` is unchanged. Rules WD1–WD5 and V17; the schema
files are unchanged, the diff is inside the draft. Reference module `conformance/tools/wind_reference.py`.

## Motivation

- **SREP 12 names one wind for six systems.** Row F4 of the plan: "one animatable wind field with declared
  gusts and curl-noise turbulence, read by particles, vegetation, clouds, water, snow drift and fire spread",
  needed within milestone 8 by A3 clouds, T3 snow, T5 wildfire and G1 vegetation. Its quality-bar row names
  earth.nullschool and Windy as the look to reach for wind.
- **The baseline's wind is an acceleration, not a velocity.** `forceField type="wind"` pushes along
  (`forceX`, `forceY`, `forceZ`) in m/s², modulated by 1 + ½ noise(seed, 0.7 t, 0.1 y / `scale`)
  (`fields.rs:64`). A cloud layer, a water spectrum or a fire model needs the wind *speed* at a point, and a
  particle's steady speed under a constant acceleration is unbounded; nothing in the baseline can be shared.
- **Its noise is in the engine's own space.** The gust reads the pixel y and a legacy hash; it is not seekable
  through the random-streams draft and is not reproducible by a second engine from the document alone.
- **Turbulence must not pile up matter.** A noise velocity with divergence gathers tracers into clumps and
  empties regions; the curl of a potential does not (Bridson, Hourihane and Marschner 2007), and that is what
  the plan's "curl-noise turbulence" asks for.

**`gustType`**, zero or more `gust` children of `wind`:

| Attribute | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `at` | `nonNegativeDecimal` | 0 | the second the gust starts (WD2) |
| `duration` | `positiveDecimal` | 1 | its length in seconds (WD2) |
| `peak` | `xs:double` | 0 | the speed it adds at its peak, m/s; negative is a lull |
| `shape` | `cosine`, `iec` | `cosine` | the raised cosine, or the IEC 61400-1 extreme operating gust (WD2) |
| `direction` | `xs:double` | — | degrees as on `wind`; absent, the wind's `direction` at that time |

**Readers.** The `forceFields` IDREFS of `particleEmitter`, the 3D particle systems, `pyro`, the agents and
the rigid-body world (`scene-render.xsd:2375`, `:3030`, `:3099`, `:4765`, `:5057`) MAY name a `wind`; an
entry names a `forceField` or a `wind` of the document (WD3). `useForceFields="false"` switches winds off
with the fields.

**Validation.** WD1: the numeric attributes are in the ranges above, animated values at the wind's `start`.
WD2: each `gust` has `at` ≥ 0, `duration` > 0 and a `shape` from the list. WD3: every `forceFields` entry of
a reader names a `forceField` or a `wind`. WD4: `end`, when present, is after `start`. WD5: `affects` is one
of its three values. V17: a `wind` in a document whose `version` is below 1.8 is refused.

### Semantics

The reference module is `conformance/tools/wind_reference.py`; the function named in each rule is its
definition. It loads the random-streams reference (F1) for E, U, V and pack. Points p are in the physics space
(metres, +y up, the scene's depth as the third coordinate); the engine converts to pixels per second by
`pixelsPerMeter` with y flipped (`to_pixels`) and along the depth as it converts `forceZ`.

1. **The mean wind.** m(t) = `speed`(t) · (cos `direction`(t), sin `direction`(t), 0) + (0, 0, `speedZ`(t)),
   the attributes at their animated values of SREP 14. `mean_velocity`.
2. **The element seed.** e = E(project, `id`, `seed`, "wind") (random-streams draft section 3). Every draw of
   this SREP is U(e, c, i) on a channel c of this element: the gust octave k reads channel 1024 + k; the
   potential component a (0, 1, 2) at octave k reads channel (2 + a) · 1024 + k. `element_seed`.
3. **The gust factor.** gust_noise(e, x, n) = Σₖ ½ᵏ V(e, 1024 + k, x · 2ᵏ) / Σₖ ½ᵏ over k < n, V the value
   noise of the random-streams draft (section 2.6); g(t) = 1 + `gustiness`(t) · gust_noise(e, t /
   `gustPeriod`, `gustOctaves`), in [1 − `gustiness`, 1 + `gustiness`]. Value noise rather than gradient
   noise, because gradient noise is zero at every lattice time and the wind would fall to its mean every
   `gustPeriod`. `gust_noise`, `gust_factor`.
4. **The declared gusts.** For each `gust`, τ = (t − `at`) / `duration`; the bump is 0 outside [0, 1],
   (1 − cos 2πτ) / 2 for `cosine`, and −0.37 sin(3πτ)(1 − cos 2πτ) divided by its peak 0.74 for `iec`, so
   both peak at 1; the gust adds `peak` · bump along its `direction` (the wind's when absent). Gusts sum.
   `bump`, `IEC_PEAK`, `declared_gusts`.
5. **The potential.** On channel c the lattice value at (i, j, k) is 2 U(e, c, pack(i, j, k)) − 1
   (random-streams draft section 2.3); value3 blends the eight surrounding lattice values with the quintic
   fade 6f⁵ − 15f⁴ + 10f³ on each axis (Perlin 2002), so the field is C² and its gradient, computed
   analytically, is continuous and zero at lattice points. The vector potential is Ψₐ(q) = Σₖ ½ᵏ value3(e,
   (2 + a) · 1024 + k, q · 2ᵏ) / Σₖ ½ᵏ for a = 0, 1, 2 over k < `turbulenceOctaves`, with the gradient taken
   with respect to q (octave k contributes 2ᵏ times its own gradient). `lattice_value`, `value3`,
   `potential`, `potential_gradient`.
6. **Time and the frozen turbulence.** D(t) is the integral of m from 0 to t by the trapezoid rule over the
   document's frame times (k / fps up to t, then the partial step), exact for constant and linearly animated
   means and computable for any frame alone (SREP 14 rule 5). The lattice coordinate of p at t is
   q = (p − D(t)) / `turbulenceScale` + (0, 0, t / `turbulenceTime`): the pattern is carried by the mean wind
   (Taylor 1938) and slides one cell per `turbulenceTime` along its third coordinate, so a calm wind still
   stirs. `displacement`, `lattice_point`.
7. **The turbulence.** w_turb(p, t) = `turbulence`(t) · ∇_q × Ψ(q), with the curl (∂Ψ₂/∂q_y − ∂Ψ₁/∂q_z,
   ∂Ψ₀/∂q_z − ∂Ψ₂/∂q_x, ∂Ψ₁/∂q_x − ∂Ψ₀/∂q_y): divergence-free by construction, within DIVERGENCE_TOLERANCE
   (10⁻⁶) when measured by central differences of 10⁻⁴ m. A reader in the plane (a 2D particle system, the
   agents, the 2D rigid-body world) reads the 2D form `turbulence`(t) · (∂Ψ₂/∂q_y, −∂Ψ₂/∂q_x) at p_z = 0,
   which is divergence-free in the plane; the planar part of the 3D curl is not. `turbulence` is an
   amplitude, not the RMS: with the weights of rule 5 every octave adds about the same speed, and the unit
   pattern's RMS over four cells is 1.76 at one octave and 1.48 at three (the table). `turbulence_at`,
   `turbulence2_at`, `divergence`, `divergence2`, `turbulence_rms`.
8. **The wind.** w(p, t) = g(t) · m(t) + Σ gusts(t) + w_turb(p, t) for `start` ≤ t ≤ `end`; outside the
   window the wind is absent and readers see nothing from it. A 2D point reads the 2D turbulence form.
   `wind_at`, `to_pixels`.
9. **Readers.** A particle or body moving at v that lists the wind (and that `affects` admits) gets the
   acceleration a = (w(p, t) − v) / `response`; a body's force is its mass times that. From rest in a steady
   wind the speed is w (1 − e^(−t / response)): 63.2 % after one `response`. The 1.7 force fields it lists act
   as before, in sum. `relaxation`, `settle_fraction`, `settled_speed`.
10. **Later readers.** The SREPs of milestone 8 state how they sample w: clouds (A3) advect their weather map
    by w at the layer's height; the ocean (A4) reads |w| and its direction as the spectrum's wind; snow (T3)
    drifts with w; wildfire (T5) takes |w| as Rothermel's midflame wind; vegetation (G1) bends with w. None of
    them defines a wind of its own (SREP 12, "the foundations").
11. **Determinism.** Every draw is a function of (e, channel, lattice index) and time enters only through
    continuous coordinates and D(t); the state at frame n is the same whether or not earlier frames were
    rendered (SREP 14 rules 1 and 5). A document with the same `project/@seed`, `id` and `seed` reproduces
    its gusts and turbulence exactly on every conforming engine.
12. **Validation.** `validate_document` returns the sorted codes of WD1–WD5 and V17 a document breaks.

## Rationale

- **A velocity field, not an acceleration.** Six systems with six couplings (drag on a particle, advection of
  a cloud, a spectrum's input, a rate of spread) can share a velocity; they cannot share a force. The
  relaxation a = (w − v) / `response` gives particles a bounded steady speed equal to the wind, which the
  baseline's push cannot.
- **Curl of a potential.** Divergence-free by construction, at the cost of three noise fields and their
  gradients; the gradients are analytic, so the engine needs no finite differences and the field is exact at
  every scale (the baseline's `curl2` differentiates with a 0.01 step, `rng.rs:46`).
- **Quintic fade and value noise.** Value noise is what D24's lattice gives without gradients per axis; the
  quintic fade makes the potential C² so the velocity is C¹ and a tracer's acceleration does not jump at
  lattice planes (the cubic smoothstep of the baseline's `value_noise2` would).
- **Frozen turbulence.** Taylor's hypothesis is how gusts are measured from a single anemometer: a pattern
  carried past the point at the mean speed. Carrying the lattice with D(t) makes a steady wind look like
  weather blowing through rather than a pattern boiling in place; the slide along the third coordinate keeps a
  calm wind alive.
- **Two gust mechanisms.** The stochastic factor is the texture of real wind; a declared gust is the
  authored beat (the tree bends at 0:12). The IEC shape is the one standard gust waveform with a citation;
  the raised cosine is the shape an author expects.
- **U with channels rather than S with steps.** Section 5 of the random-streams draft addresses stepped
  simulations; the wind is a function of continuous time with no step, and its frame-independence comes from
  the address (rule 11), as the terrain draft does for its layers.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Extending `forceField type="wind"` in place.** Its gust is a 2D noise of (t, pixel y) on a legacy hash and
  its output is an acceleration; keeping its contract and adding the field's would make one element mean two
  things. It stays as it is for 1.7 documents (Backwards compatibility).
- **A gridded wind.** M4 of SREP 12 (vector-field layers) brings U/V grids from GRIB2 and NetCDF; this SREP
  is the procedural field those layers can replace point by point. The two meet in M4.
- **Spectral synthesis (Mann 1998, Kaimal spectra).** A turbulence box with the measured atmospheric spectrum
  is the engineering standard, but it needs an FFT over a volume and a specified spectrum per site; the
  plan's explainers need a believable, cheap, seekable field. The spectrum is open issue 2.
- **4D noise for time.** pack(i, j, k) has three coordinates; a fourth would need a second index space. The
  slide along q_z with `turbulenceTime` gives time evolution within three.
- **Finite-difference curl.** What the baseline does; the step size then enters the result and two engines
  with different steps disagree. The analytic gradient of value noise is a few lines.

## Backwards compatibility

- Documents of version 1.0 to 1.7 contain no `wind` element; `forceField type="wind"` renders exactly as
  before (`fields.rs:64`, `:161`), with its legacy draws (random-streams draft, Appendix A).
- A 1.8 document may use both; a reader's `forceFields` sums them.
- V17 refuses a `wind` in a document below 1.8, as the version gate of the random-streams draft refuses its
  streams.

## Open issues

1. **The vertical profile.** Real wind grows with height (the log law, Stull 1988); a `wind` here is uniform
   in height. The editor may admit `referenceHeight` and `roughness` once the terrain grid (F2) gives a
   ground to measure from.
2. **The gust spectrum.** The gust factor is fractal value noise, not the Kaimal or von Kármán spectrum of
   IEC 61400-1's normal turbulence model; a `spectrum` attribute could select a measured one.
3. **Normalising the turbulence.** `turbulence` is an amplitude (RMS 1.48 at three octaves); the editor may
   prefer the attribute to be the RMS itself, dividing by the measured factor.
4. **Obstacles.** Bridson's curl noise ramps the potential to zero at solid boundaries so the flow goes
   around; this SREP has no colliders. T1–T3 on the terrain and the rigid bodies may want them.
5. **The depth axis.** `speedZ` follows `forceZ`'s convention ("toward the camera"); the engine's 3D camera
   looks along +z (`camera.rs`), and the sign is the engine's to pin in the kit case.
6. **Arrows and streamlines.** The fields draft (MF5) could read a wind as a source for its glyphs and
   streamlines; this draft does not add the reference, to keep the two independent.
