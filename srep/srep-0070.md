# SREP 70 — Add parametric paths, parametric surfaces and heightfields

## Abstract

This SREP adds three kinds of geometry that are defined by expressions in the existing expression language:

- **`parametricPath`**: a curve (x(t), y(t)) sampled at a stated number of points. It becomes the outline of a
  shape with `shape="parametric"`.
- **`parametricSurface`**: a mesh (x, y, z)(u, v) sampled on a grid. It is drawn by an `object3D` with
  `primitive="parametric"`.
- **`heightfield`**: a mesh y = −height(x, z) over a rectangle. It is drawn by an `object3D` with
  `primitive="heightfield"`.

The sampling, the triangulation, the normals and the face orientation are given exactly. These need no sandbox and
no loops, so they can be built while SREP 66 (`program`) is pending. They cover the 3D graph and the Klein
bottle cells of the owner's test, and closed-form curves.

## Motivation

**The owner's 72-cell atlas test (2026-10-09, not published).** 3 cells needed procedural meshes or materials
(SREP 71 covers the materials). The test notes name "a parametric surface or a heightfield defined by expression
(for the Klein bottle and the 3D graph)". They also name, among the missing features, "at least a parametric curve
and an L-system node". Paths today must be written out literally.

**Today** (schema 1.5.0 and `rs-scene-render` commit `c09b58a`):

- **Shapes.** `shape` takes `rect`, `ellipse`, `rounded-rect`, `polygon`, `star`, `line`, `path` (literal
  path data in `@path`) and `stroke-text`.
- **3D primitives.** `object3D/@primitive` takes `sphere`, `box`, `plane`, `mesh` (an imported asset), `cylinder`,
  `cone`, `torus`, `capsule`, `text`, `extrude`, `clay`, `map`, `globe` and `volume`. None of them is defined by a
  function.
- **Expressions.** These evaluate in 64-bit floats (`crates/sr-eval/src/expr/vm.rs`), with `Math.*` functions and
  the `NaN` literal (`crates/sr-eval/src/expr/parse.rs`), and without loops.

### Semantics

**1. Expressions.** The attributes typed `expressionString` use the expression language of `expressionType`, with
every built-in it lists. A curve also gets the variable `t`, a surface `u` and `v`, and a heightfield `x` and `z`.
They are evaluated in 64-bit floats. They are evaluated at each frame, so an expression that reads `time`, `prop`
or `param` animates the geometry. An expression that reads none of them yields the same geometry at every frame, and
an engine MAY compute it once.

**2. `parametricPath`.** Let N = `samples` and Δ = (t1 − t0)/(N − 1) for an open curve, or (t1 − t0)/N with
`closed="true"`. The points are p_i = (x(t_i), y(t_i)) with t_i = t0 + i·Δ, i = 0 … N − 1. They are in the shape's
local pixel space, like the coordinates of `@path`.

- **The outline.** The shape's outline is the path `M p₀ L p₁ … L p_{N−1}`, followed by `Z` when the curve is
  closed.
- **Equivalence.** A `shape="parametric"` draws exactly as a `shape="path"` with that path data, the same `fill`,
  `stroke`, stroke style, trim, markers (SREP 15) and `fillRule` would draw.
- **Non-finite points.** A point with a NaN or infinite coordinate is dropped and breaks the path: the next finite
  point starts a new subpath with `M`. A closed curve that has a break is not closed.
- **Shape attributes.** `width` and `height` are not read. The shape's box is the bounding box of the finite
  points.

**3. `parametricSurface`.**

- **Grid.** Let U = `uSamples` and V = `vSamples`. Then u_i = u0 + i·(u1 − u0)/(U − 1) for i = 0 … U − 1, or
  (u1 − u0)/U with `closedU`. v_j is defined the same way.
- **Vertices.** P_{i,j} = (x, y, z)(u_i, v_j) in the object's local space, in scene units (pixels), before the
  `object3D` transform (CONVENTIONS 2.5).
- **Triangles.** Each grid cell (i, j) to (i+1, j+1) gives the triangles (P_{i,j}, P_{i+1,j}, P_{i+1,j+1}) and
  (P_{i,j}, P_{i+1,j+1}, P_{i,j+1}). The last row or column joins the first when the direction is closed, with
  indices taken modulo U or V. Otherwise it has no cells.
- **Normals.** The vertex normal is the unit vector of `D_v × D_u`, where `D_u = P_{i+1,j} − P_{i−1,j}` and
  `D_v = P_{i,j+1} − P_{i,j−1}`. At an open border the missing neighbour is replaced by the vertex itself (a
  one-sided difference); a closed direction wraps.
  - If the cross product has zero length, the normal is the normalised sum of the face normals of the triangles that
    share the vertex. The face normal of a triangle (a, b, c), with its vertices in the order of the triangle list
    above, is `(c − a) × (b − a)`, unnormalised, so larger faces weigh more. For the first triangle of a cell this
    is `(D_u + D_v) × D_u = D_v × D_u`, the orientation of the vertex normals above.
  - If that sum is also zero, the normal is (0, 0, −1).
- **Front face.** A triangle is front-facing when its vertex normals point toward the camera. With x = u, y = v
  and z = 0, the normal is −z, which faces the implicit camera as a `plane` does (CONVENTIONS 2.5). Back faces are
  culled unless the material is `doubleSided`.
- **Texture coordinates.** ((u − u0)/(u1 − u0), (v − v0)/(v1 − v0)).
- **Non-finite vertices.** Triangles with a non-finite vertex are dropped.

**4. `heightfield`.** A heightfield is the `parametricSurface` with:

- u = z ranging over [−depth/2, depth/2] with `zSamples` samples;
- v = x ranging over [−width/2, width/2] with `xSamples` samples;
- vertices (x, −height(x, z), z).

"Up" is −y in scene space (CONVENTIONS 2.1). With the normal rule of 3, the normal of a flat heightfield is
(0, −1, 0), so its top face is the front face. Turning the object by `rotationX="90"` shows that top face to the
implicit camera.

**5. Limits.** U × V is at most 4096², and a curve has at most 1,000,000 samples. These are the XSD facets.

### Defaults and the neutral case

These are new values and elements, so existing documents are unaffected.

## Rationale

- **Reuse the expression language.** It already has a numeric definition (f64), seeding and built-ins. A curve
  or surface needs only a function of one or two variables, with no loops.
- **Equivalence to a path.** Every existing stroke, fill, trim and marker rule applies without being restated, and
  the kit can compare against a literal path.
- **Uniform sampling.** It is exact and simple. Adaptive sampling would make the vertex positions depend on an
  error tolerance, which is harder to specify identically across engines.
- **Normal orientation.** The rule is chosen so that the identity mapping (x = u, y = v) faces the camera like the
  `plane` primitive, and a flat heightfield faces up.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A native `lsystem` node.** SREP 66 covers L-systems, dragon and Gosper curves and every other loop-based
  geometry. Per the plan, a native `lsystem` node is proposed only if SREP 66 is rejected or late.
- **Curves as `@path` with expressions inside the path data**: it would need a second parser and still have no loop
  over samples.
- **Implicit surfaces (f(x, y, z) = 0, marching cubes)**: a different algorithm with its own resolution and
  ambiguity rules. It is out of scope here.

## Backwards compatibility

The change is MINOR: new enumeration values and elements under the version gate V14.
