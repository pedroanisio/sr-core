# SREP 0 — Stroke geoLayer outlines with round caps and joins

## Abstract

A `geoLayer` strokes the rings of its polygons and its lines with round caps and round joins, at `strokeWidth`.
When `progress` is below 1 the traced part of each ring or line (SREP 36) ends in the same round caps. The schema
has no cap or join attribute on `geoLayerType`, so no document changes meaning; the sentence states what the
reference implementation draws and lets the two compatibility-kit cases of SREP 36 that measure a partial trace
stop being pending.

## Motivation

SREP 36 traces each polygon ring of a `geoLayer` by `progress` "through the same trim as shapes" and says nothing
about the ends of the trace. `geoLayerType` declares `stroke` and `strokeWidth` only; `shapeType`'s `strokeCap` and
`strokeJoin` do not exist on it. The kit cases `srep-0036-geo-outline-p125` and `srep-0036-geo-outline-p875` have
been pending since 2026-10-12 because their expected boxes assumed butt ends, while the reference draws caps that
extend the trace by half the stroke width at each end:

| Case | Expected with butt ends | Measured on the reference (0.5.0 at 12ede1e, 0.7.4 at e586b57 and 67a661a) |
|---|---|---|
| `srep-0036-geo-outline-p125` | green cx 186.67, cy 221.67, w 504, h 100 | green cx 190.89, cy 221.46, w 504, h 104 |
| `srep-0036-geo-outline-p875` | green cx 275.96, cy 167.99, w 604, h 207 | green cx 270.65, cy 167.8, w 604, h 208 |

The height 104 of a 100 px trace of a 4 px stroke is the mark of a cap that adds half the width (2 px) at each end;
butt ends give 100. An unspecified cap lets two conforming engines draw different outlines, and a kit case cannot
assert a number the specification does not fix. SREP 0 ("How a proposal is argued") settles a point the schema is
silent on by the reference's behaviour; this SREP writes that behaviour down.

## Specification

### Syntax

### Semantics

1. **Caps and joins.** A `geoLayer` MUST stroke every polygon ring and every line of its geo asset with round
   joins at every vertex and round caps at every end, with the stroke centred on the path and `strokeWidth` wide
   in map pixels. A round cap is the half-disc of radius `strokeWidth / 2` centred on the end point; a round join is
   the disc of that radius centred on the vertex. There is no miter limit to apply.
2. **A closed ring at `progress` 1** has no ends: its stroke is the closed outline with round joins at every
   vertex, including the first.
3. **A traced ring or line (`progress` below 1, SREP 36)** is an open path from its first vertex to the point at
   `progress` of its length. Both ends carry the round cap of rule 1: the stroke extends `strokeWidth / 2` beyond
   the first vertex and beyond the trace's end, along the path's direction there. At `progress` 0 nothing of the
   outline is drawn (SREP 36 rule 1), so no cap is drawn either.
4. **Point outlines** (`stroke` on a point feature) are closed circles and have no cap.
5. The fill of a polygon is not affected: it stays whole at every `progress` (SREP 36).

### Defaults and the neutral case

No attribute is added or changed, so every document renders as the reference renders it today. A document with a
`geoLayer` without `stroke` draws no outline and is unaffected. A document that sets `progress` below 1 renders the
trace the reference already draws, now with its ends specified.

## Rationale

- **The reference decides where the schema is silent (SREP 0).** The Rust reference strokes every geo feature
  through one painter, `Painter::stroke` in `crates/sr-gpu/src/text_map.rs`, which builds its stroke style with
  `Cap::Round` and `Join::Round` and a miter limit that round joins never read. The measurements in Motivation are
  that style's: a 100 px trace of a 4 px stroke measures 104 px tall.
- **Round ends suit a trace that moves.** The head of a draw-on animation sits on a vertex only at the moments the
  trace passes one; a round cap reads the same whichever direction the path turns next, where a butt end changes
  shape at every corner and a square cap overshoots a corner by half the width in the wrong direction.
- **Round joins need no limit.** A miter join on the sharp angles of coastlines and administrative borders would
  need `miterLimit`, which `geoLayerType` does not have; bevel joins notch every corner at the widths maps use.
- **Precedent.** SVG 2 (section 13.5, `stroke-linecap` and `stroke-linejoin`) defines the three caps and joins
  this SREP chooses from; its default is butt and miter for authored shapes, which `shapeType` follows through
  `strokeCap` and `strokeJoin`. Cartographic renderers draw administrative boundaries with round joins and caps
  by default (Mapbox GL Style Specification, `line-cap` and `line-join`, default `butt` and `miter` on authored
  lines but round in its basemap styles), which is the convention a map layer reads as.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Butt caps, as the kit's first expectation assumed.** They would make the trace's visible length equal the
  traced length, which is tidy for a derivation, but the reference does not draw them, and changing the reference
  would change the pixels of every existing geo scene at every `progress` below 1 and at every open line end.
- **Square caps.** Equal extents to round at axis-aligned ends, so the kit cannot tell them apart from round at
  2 px tolerance, but they overshoot corners diagonally and are not what the reference draws.
- **`strokeCap` and `strokeJoin` attributes on `geoLayerType`.** A new feature, needing a MINOR schema release and
  engine work; nothing in the catalogue asks for it. It stays open for a later Standards SREP, whose defaults would
  be the values this SREP fixes.

## Backwards compatibility

Every document valid under 1.7.1 stays valid; no rendering changes, since the reference already draws what this
SREP states. No version gate.
