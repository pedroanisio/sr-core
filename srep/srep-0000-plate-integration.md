# SREP 0 — Add plate integration

## Abstract

SREP 12's milestone 7, row LA3: 3D objects anchored in plates, shadow catchers on tracked surfaces and
lens-distortion matching. A `camera` gains `track`, an IDREF to a `trackData` of kind `camera` (computed by
the computed-tracking draft or imported), and `trackScale`, the scene units per track unit, default the
scene's `pixelsPerMeter`; the camera's `x`, `y`, `z`, `yaw`, `pitch`, `roll` and `fov` at a frame are then
the camera row held at that frame, written into the engine's conventions (y down, z forward, horizontal
`fov`). An `object3D` gains `anchor`, an IDREF to a `trackData` of kind `planar`, and `anchorName`; its pose
is the tracked plane's pose carried into the world through the tracked camera, so a `shadowCatcher` object
anchored there darkens the plate by SREP 41's rule, scaled by its `opacity`. The camera `solve` of the
computed-tracking draft gains `lens="plumb"`: the plumb-line method estimates the `k1` of the engine's radial
model (`three_post.wgsl`) from the straight lines of the plate, the solve undistorts the tracked points
before the pose fit, writes `k1` into the track cache, and a tracked camera without `lensDistortion` takes it
from there. The reference test is the one SREP 12 names: a solved camera re-projects scene points onto their
plate positions within 0.5 px (0.327 px measured over eight frames of the synthetic camera, 0 px after
undistortion of a lens with `k1 = 0.15`, 0.696 px without it). Rules PI1–PI4 and V17; schema files unchanged,
the diff is inside the draft.

## Motivation

SREP 12 row LA3 asks for "3D objects anchored in plates, shadow catchers, lens-distortion matching", measured
by "a solved camera re-projects scene points onto their plate positions". At 67a661a the pieces exist apart:
`cameraType` has `x`, `y`, `z`, `yaw`, `pitch`, `roll`, `fov` and `lensDistortion` (`render_three.rs:559`
reads it into the post pass), `object3DType` has `shadowCatcher` (SREP 41, schema 1.2), `trackData` of kind
`camera` is parsed (`track.rs`: rows of `tx ty tz rx ry rz fov`) but drives nothing, since "only point, planar,
mask and face may drive a transformConstraint or a layer's stabilize". Putting a rendered object into footage
therefore means typing a camera per frame by hand, which no document does. The computed-tracking draft
(LA1) produces the camera rows; this draft connects them to the camera and the objects, states the exact
conventions the connection relies on, and adds the lens match without which the rendered object drifts
against a real plate by the lens's bend (up to 0.7 px on a 96 px plate at `k1 = 0.15`, more on a full frame).

## Specification

### Syntax

The diff against `scene-render.xsd` 1.7.1 and `scene-render.sch`; the schema files are unchanged in this
draft, the editor applies the diff on acceptance.

**`cameraType`** (`scene-render.xsd:4423`) gains two attributes:

| Attribute | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `track` | `xs:IDREF` | — | a `trackData` of `kind="camera"` that drives this camera |
| `trackScale` | `positiveDecimal` | the scene's `pixelsPerMeter` | scene units per track unit (a track in metres at `pixelsPerMeter="100"` has `trackScale="100"`) |

`lensDistortion` (`scene-render.xsd:4520`, `xs:double`, default 0, positive is barrel) is unchanged; when the
camera has `track` and no `lensDistortion`, the value comes from the track cache (rule 4).

**`object3DType`** (`scene-render.xsd:4112`) gains two attributes:

| Attribute | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `anchor` | `xs:IDREF` | — | a `trackData` of `kind="planar"` the object sits on |
| `anchorName` | `xs:string` | the first plane of the cache | the plane's name in the track cache |

**`solve`** (the child of a computed `trackData`, computed-tracking draft) gains, for `kind="camera"`:

| Attribute | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lens` | `none` \| `plumb` | `none` | `plumb` estimates the radial `k1` from the plate's straight lines and undistorts the tracked points before the pose fit |

The track cache (the engine's JSON, `track.rs:101`) gains an optional top-level `"lens": {"k1": <number>}`
written by a `plumb` solve, rounded to four decimals like every other value.

**Schematron** (`scene-render.sch`):

- **PI1** A `camera/@track` references a `trackData` whose `kind` is `camera`. A camera with `track` and any
  of `x`, `y`, `z`, `yaw`, `pitch`, `roll`, `fov`, `target`, `focalLength` reports a warning naming the
  ignored attribute (the track wins).
- **PI2** `camera/@trackScale` is positive.
- **PI3** An `object3D/@anchor` references a `trackData` whose `kind` is `planar`.
- **PI4** `solve/@lens` is `none` or `plumb` and appears on `kind="camera"` solves only;
  `camera/@lensDistortion`, written or taken from a cache, lies within ±0.5.
- **V17** (extended) `track`, `trackScale`, `anchor`, `anchorName` and `lens` require `scene/@version` 1.8
  or later.

### Semantics

The reference module is `conformance/tools/plate_integration_reference.py`; the function named in each rule
is its definition. It loads the computed-tracking reference for the rotation conventions, the pinhole and
the pose fit.

1. **The engine's camera.** The scene axes are x right, y down, z forward: the camera looks along +z and its
   pose is T(x, y, z) · Ry(`yaw`) · Rx(`pitch`) · Rz(`roll`) (`render_three.rs` `pose3`, `camera.rs`
   `orientation`; positive yaw turns +z towards +x, positive pitch towards −y, which is up). The focal length
   in pixels is zoom = (w / 2) / tan(`fov` / 2) with `fov` horizontal, and a scene point at camera
   coordinates (x, y, z) lands at (w / 2 + zoom · x / z, h / 2 + zoom · y / z); the default camera at
   (w / 2, h / 2, −zoom) draws the z = 0 plane at pixel scale. `engine_orientation`, `engine_zoom`,
   `engine_project`.
2. **A tracked camera.** The camera row held at the frame (the last row at or before it, the first row before
   the first; `camera_at`) has (R, t) with x_cam = R x_world + t and R = Rz(rz) Ry(ry) Rx(rx) (computed-tracking
   draft rule 6). The engine camera is M = R^T, eye = −`trackScale` · R^T t, (`yaw`, `pitch`, `roll`) the
   Y-X-Z decomposition of M (`engine_euler`; roll is 0 at the gimbal lock), and `fov` the row's horizontal
   fov. `camera_from_row`. Scene points anchored on the track's world frame are the world coordinates times
   `trackScale`. The engine projection of these points equals the tracking draft's pinhole to within round-off
   (`pinhole_agreement`), and the solved camera re-projects the target's corners onto their plate positions
   within 0.5 px (`reprojection_run`).
3. **An anchored object.** The plane pose of `anchorName` at the frame is (R_p, t_p), the plane's frame in
   camera coordinates (the tracking draft's `planar_pose` refined by `refine_pose`, or the camera pose itself
   when the camera was solved from that plane). The object's world pose is (M R_p, eye + `trackScale` · M t_p),
   written as the object's own transform order T(x, y, z) · Rz(`rotation`) · Ry(`rotationY`) · Rx(`rotationX`)
   (`render_three.rs` `local3`); the object's written `x`…`rotationX` compose after it. `anchor_from_plane`,
   `object_from_pose`. Over the solved synthetic track the anchored plane sits within 0.19 scene units
   (1.9 mm) and 1.8 degrees of the world origin where the truth puts it (`anchor_drift`).
4. **The lens.** The engine's post warp (`three_post.wgsl`): with p = (uv − 0.5) · (w / h, 1), the output
   pixel at p samples the source at p (1 + `k1` |p|²); positive `k1` pulls off-centre content towards the
   centre, barrel. `normalised`, `source_of`; the inverse `output_of` solves r (1 + k1 r²) = r_s by Newton
   and refuses a source point beyond the fold of a negative k1. A scene point rendered by a camera with
   `lensDistortion` appears at `distort_pixel` of its pinhole position; a plate pixel's pinhole position is
   `undistort_pixel`. A tracked camera without `lensDistortion` takes `k1` from the cache's `"lens"`, 0 when
   absent.
5. **The plumb-line solve.** `lens="plumb"` takes the plate's straight lines (at least two, at least five
   points each; the resolve step finds them by edge following, the reference takes them as given) and
   returns the `k1` in [−0.5, 0.5] that minimises the sum of squared total-least-squares residuals of the
   undistorted lines, sampled on a grid of 41 values and refined by golden-section search. `line_residual`,
   `plumb_line_k1`. The solve then undistorts every tracked point with that `k1` before the homography and
   the pose fit, and writes `"lens": {"k1": …}`. On synthetic grids of `k1` 0.08, −0.05, 0 and 0.2 the
   estimate is exact to 0.001; with undistortion the pose fit of a 0.8 × 0.6 m target under `k1 = 0.15`
   re-projects within round-off through a camera with `lensDistortion="0.15"`, without it 0.696 px
   (`lens_run`).
6. **The shadow catcher.** An anchored object with `shadowCatcher="true"` draws, as `three.wgsl` does, black
   with alpha = `opacity` · (1 − lit_sh / lit_un), where lit_un is the luma (0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722) of the
   light that arrives at the point without casters and lit_sh with them; without light the alpha is 0.
   `catcher_alpha`, `composite_catcher`, `luma`. For a directional light the light arrives at the cosine of
   its angle to the plane's normal, and a caster blocks it when the ray back towards the light hits it
   (`sphere_shadow`, `shadow_alpha_at`): one light blocked gives `opacity`, two equal lights of which the
   vertical one is blocked give 1 / (1 + cos 45°) = 0.5858. Through the frame-0 camera a 0.1 m sphere 0.2 m
   over the tracked plane darkens a disc of radius 10.03 px on the plate (`catcher_plate`,
   `footprint_radius`).
7. **Holding and interpolation.** Camera rows and plane poses hold (rule 2); a document that needs smoothing
   applies it in the solve, never in the camera. Frames before the first row use the first row, frames after
   the last use the last.
8. **Validation.** `validate_document` raises PI1–PI4 and V17 with the rule name in the message and returns
   the PI1 warnings; a 1.7 document without the new attributes stays valid and unchanged in meaning.

## Rationale

- **The track drives the camera directly** rather than a resolve step writing per-frame `x`…`fov` keyframes:
  the cache is the pinned artefact (SREP 9), a re-solve changes one file and not the document, and the
  conventions in rule 2 are stated once instead of being baked into thousands of keyframes.
- **The conventions are spelled out** because they are where plate integration fails in practice: the
  engine's y-down, z-forward frame and Y-X-Z camera order against the tracking draft's Z-Y-X rows, the
  horizontal fov against the track reader's "vertical fov degrees" comment (`track.rs:18`), and the lens
  model's sign. The numeric table pins each one.
- **Anchors go through the camera** because a plane pose is only defined relative to the camera that saw
  it; carrying it through the tracked camera puts it where the camera track's world frame says, and a scene
  whose camera was solved from that same plane gets the plane at the world origin, which the drift row
  checks.
- **`k1` only.** Devernay and Faugeras show one radial term straightens consumer and cinema lenses to a
  fraction of a pixel at these resolutions; the engine's shader has exactly that term. Higher orders and
  decentring (Brown) would need a shader change and are an open issue.
- **The plumb-line method** needs no target and no camera pose, so it runs before the solve and improves it;
  the solve's own residual could fit `k1` too, but then a planar target's pose ambiguity and the lens trade
  against each other.

## Rejected alternatives

- A `cameraTrack` element of its own: a camera is a camera; one IDREF keeps every other camera attribute
  (`near`, `far`, depth of field, exposure, the renderer) in force.
- Writing the track's fov as `focalLength` on the 36 mm sensor: the row is a horizontal angle and
  `cameraType/@fov` is a horizontal angle; the conversion would add a rounding step and a sensor assumption.
- Interpolating rows between frames: a camera track is one row per plate frame; interpolation belongs to
  the solve's frame rate, not to the camera.
- A full Brown model (`k1`, `k2`, `p1`, `p2`): the shader has `k1` only and the measurable gain on the kit's
  plates is below the 0.5 px floor.
- Anchoring 2D layers to planar tracks: a `transformConstraint` already drives a 2D layer from a planar
  track; this draft is about 3D objects.

## Backwards compatibility

A 1.7 document is valid and unchanged in meaning: `track`, `trackScale`, `anchor`, `anchorName` and `lens` are
new and optional, and a camera without `track` reads as today. Documents using them require version 1.8
(V17). The draft depends on the computed-tracking draft for the rows and the plane poses, on SREP 41 for the
catcher and on SREP 9 for the pinned cache.

## Open issues

1. **The row's fov.** `track.rs:18` documents the camera row's last value as "vertical fov degrees" while the
   computed-tracking draft writes and this draft reads a horizontal angle (`cameraType/@fov` is horizontal).
   One of the two changes with SR-97; the draft assumes the reader changes, since every other fov in the
   schema is horizontal.
2. **Small targets tilt.** The anchored plane's orientation is only as good as the solve: a 48 px target
   leaves a 1.8 degree tilt that a 0.19 scene unit shift compensates (the planar-pose ambiguity). Anchors
   for shadow catchers want a target that fills the frame or survey points; whether the solve should report
   its pose covariance is for the editor.
3. **One radial term.** `k1` alone; `k2` and decentring would need a shader change (`three_post.wgsl` and
   the 2D `lens-distortion` effect, `effects.wgsl` case 7, whose comment reads "barrel < 0 < pincushion"
   although its formula is the same sampling warp and `cameraType/@lensDistortion` documents positive as
   barrel: the comment is wrong, the formulas agree).
4. **Finding the lines.** The reference takes the plumb lines as given; the resolve step must find them
   (Canny edges and connected segments, Devernay and Faugeras' procedure) and reject short or curved ones.
5. **Ambient and environment light on the catcher.** `three.wgsl` scales the ambient term by screen-space
   occlusion for the catcher's lit_un; a plate's own ambient occlusion is already in the footage, so a
   catcher under an environment light double-darkens contact regions. A `catcherLights` list or an ambient
   exclusion is for the editor.
6. **Jitter.** Rows hold per frame (rule 7), so a noisy solve shows as jitter on the anchored object; a
   smoothing option belongs to the solve, with its window as part of the pinned cache.
