# SREP 10 — Add draped map ground and globes to object3D

## Abstract

`object3D` gains two primitives. `primitive="map"` lays a map asset down as ground in 3D, draped with the map
as the 2D renderer draws it, optionally raised by elevation tiles (`@terrain`) and with the basemap's buildings
extruded (`@buildings`). `primitive="globe"` wraps a sphere in the map's content drawn over the whole world.
Authors can fly a 3D camera over terrain, cities and a globe drawn from the same `<map>` they use in 2D.

## Motivation

A tilted 2D map layer is a flat card: it has no relief, no buildings and no horizon, and a globe drawn in an
orthographic projection cannot be lit or orbited by the 3D camera. Map scenes in documentaries and news
graphics routinely fly a camera over terrain and cities; doing it in scene-render today needs pre-rendered
video.

## Specification

### Syntax

```xml
<!-- object3D/@primitive gains two values -->
<xs:enumeration value="map"/><xs:enumeration value="globe"/>

<!-- new object3D attributes -->
<xs:attribute name="map" type="xs:IDREF">
  <xs:annotation><xs:documentation>
    primitive="map": the map asset as ground in the object's xy plane, one map pixel per scene
    unit, draped with the map as drawn; @terrain (tiles of elevation, Terrarium or Mapbox
    Terrain-RGB per @terrainEncoding) raises it toward the camera (−z) by true height times
    @exaggeration on a grid of @resolution cells, and @buildings extrudes the footprints of its
    first basemap's vector tiles to their heights. Terrain and buildings need a Web Mercator
    map. primitive="globe": a sphere of @radius and @segments (poles on the y axis, 0° longitude
    facing the camera) draped with the map's content drawn over the whole world. @material
    dresses the ground, the globe and the buildings. @textureSize is the drape's resolution in
    pixels along a map's longer side, and a globe drape's height (its width is twice that); it
    does not change what the drape shows.
  </xs:documentation></xs:annotation>
</xs:attribute>
<xs:attribute name="terrain" type="xs:IDREF"/>
<xs:attribute name="terrainEncoding" default="terrarium">
  <xs:simpleType><xs:restriction base="xs:string">
    <xs:enumeration value="terrarium"/><xs:enumeration value="mapbox"/>
  </xs:restriction></xs:simpleType>
</xs:attribute>
<xs:attribute name="exaggeration" type="nonNegativeDecimal" default="1"/>
<xs:attribute name="buildings" type="xs:boolean" default="false"/>
<xs:attribute name="textureSize" default="2048">
  <xs:simpleType><xs:restriction base="xs:positiveInteger"><xs:minInclusive value="64"/><xs:maxInclusive value="8192"/></xs:restriction></xs:simpleType>
</xs:attribute>
```

The existing `@resolution` (8–256, default 64) sets the terrain grid; the existing `@radius` (default 50),
`@segments` (default 32), `@material`, `@opacity`, `@castShadow` and `@receiveShadow` apply as described below.

```xml
<!-- in the object3D rule of pattern p26 -->
<sch:assert id="C47" test="not(@primitive='map' or @primitive='globe') or @map">object3D primitive="map" or "globe" requires @map.</sch:assert>
<sch:assert id="R28" test="not(@map) or /scene/assets/map[@id=current()/@map]">object3D/@map must name a map asset.</sch:assert>
<sch:assert id="R29" test="not(@terrain) or /scene/assets/tiles[@id=current()/@terrain]">object3D/@terrain must name a tiles asset.</sch:assert>

<!-- the version gate V5 (added by SREP 9): its test gains the term -->
.//object3D[@primitive='map' or @primitive='globe']
```

The two primitives are new constructs, as SREP 8's 1.1 primitives were (V7): V5's test gains the term
`.//object3D[@primitive='map' or @primitive='globe']`, so a document that uses them MUST declare
`version="1.2"`. The new attributes are accepted in every version, as new attributes on existing elements are,
and have no effect on other primitives.

### Semantics

In this section W × H is the map asset's size in pixels, k its projection scale in pixels per radian at the
current time, `map_zoom = log2(2π·k / 512)` (SREP 9: at zoom 0 the world is 512 pixels wide), `e` =
`@exaggeration` and R = 6378137 m.

**Drape.** The drape is the picture the map asset draws as a 2D layer of size W × H at the current time: its
background, every child (`basemap`, `geoLayer`, `graticule`, `route`, `pin`, with their labels) under its
camera and fly-tos, and the attribution plate in its bottom-right corner, in the order and at the sizes the 2D
layer uses. The drape is laid out in map pixels and then scaled uniformly to its texture, so line widths,
label sizes and the attribution plate do not depend on `@textureSize`:

- for `map`, the texture is round(W·f) × round(H·f) pixels with f = `@textureSize` / max(W, H);
- for `globe`, the map's content is laid out in the equirectangular projection centred on 0° longitude and 0°
  latitude, filling a frame 2H wide and H tall (north at the top), and the texture is 2T × T pixels with
  T = min(`@textureSize`, 4096).

**Ground (`map`).** The ground is a rectangle W × H scene units centred on the object's origin in its xy plane,
map pixel (px, py) at scene point (px − W/2, py − H/2, 0), so the map's up is toward −y and its surface faces
the camera (normal −z). The drape covers it with texture coordinates (px/W, py/H). The object's transform
(rule 2.5 of the conventions) then applies.

**Scale of heights.** Heights in metres become scene units by

    s = k / (R · cos φ₀)

where φ₀ is the latitude at the map's centre (W/2, H/2): one metre of height is as many scene units as one
metre of ground there.

**Terrain grid.** The ground is a grid of nx × ny cells:

    W ≥ H:  nx = resolution,                       ny = max(1, round(resolution · H / W))
    W < H:  nx = max(1, round(resolution · W / H)),  ny = resolution

Vertex (i, j), 0 ≤ i ≤ nx, 0 ≤ j ≤ ny, lies over map pixel (W·i/nx, H·j/ny). Without `@terrain` every vertex
has z = 0. Each cell is two triangles, (i, j), (i, j+1), (i+1, j) and (i+1, j), (i, j+1), (i+1, j+1). The
vertex normal is normalize(gx, gy, −1), where gx = (z(i+1, j) − z(i−1, j)) / (2·W/nx) and
gy = (z(i, j+1) − z(i, j−1)) / (2·H/ny), with indices outside the grid clamped to its edge.

**Terrain.** With `@terrain`, each vertex's longitude and latitude are the inverse of the map's Web Mercator
view at its map pixel, and its height h in metres is sampled from the elevation tiles at zoom

    zd = round(map_zoom + 1 − log2(cell)),   cell = max(W, H) / resolution,

clamped to the archive's minimum and maximum zoom (one 256-pixel-tile elevation pixel per grid cell). The
sample reads one tile: the tile (x, y) = (floor(tx), floor(ty)), each clamped to 0 … 2^zd − 1, where (tx, ty)
are the point's fractional tile coordinates at zd. With w the tile image's actual width and hₜ its height,
the sample position in the tile is at pixel centres,

    fx = clamp((tx − x)·w − 0.5, 0, w − 1),   fy = clamp((ty − y)·hₜ − 0.5, 0, hₜ − 1),

and h is the bilinear interpolation of the pixels floor(fx), floor(fx)+1 and floor(fy), floor(fy)+1, the
second of each clamped to the tile's last column or row. Samples are not interpolated across tiles. Each
pixel's elevation is decoded from its R, G, B (0–255 integers; alpha is ignored) as

    terrarium: h = R·256 + G + B/256 − 32768
    mapbox:    h = −10000 + (R·65536 + G·256 + B)·0.1

A tile missing from the archive, or one that does not decode, gives h = 0. The vertex's z is

    z = −(h − h₀) · e · s

where h₀ is the height sampled at the map's centre: the centre stays on the object's plane and higher ground
moves toward the camera.

**Buildings.** With `@buildings`, buildings come from the map's first `basemap` child. A map with no basemap
is an error, reported, and draws no buildings; a first basemap of raster tiles draws no buildings. The tile
zoom is

    zt = min(clamp(floor(map_zoom + detail), 0, 24), 22)

with `detail` that basemap's `@detail`. For each tile of the visible tile set at zt (SREP 9), the source tile
is that tile, or, when zt exceeds the archive's maximum zoom, its ancestor at the maximum zoom with only the
tile's part of it used. A source tile missing from the archive is skipped. In a source tile, the first layer
named `buildings` or `building` gives the buildings; its polygon features are the footprints. For each
feature:

- top = `height`, else `render_height`, else 8; bottom = `min_height`, else `render_min_height`, else 0 (metres);
  a feature whose top is not above its bottom is skipped;
- each polygon is clipped to its tile's square (the tile's buffer is discarded) and to the map's frame, and
  placed in map pixels as the basemap places it; a building that crosses a tile boundary becomes one prism
  per tile, and the pieces are not merged;
- g is the terrain z (above) at the mean of the vertices of the clipped polygon's first ring;
- the polygon, with its holes, is extruded into a prism between z = g − bottom · s · e (its base) and
  z = g − top · s · e (its roof, toward the camera), with a depth of at least 0.001 scene units.

**Web Mercator only.** Terrain and buildings need a map in Web Mercator (SREP 9). On any other map, a `map`
object with `@terrain` or `@buildings` is drawn flat, without buildings, and the renderer reports it.

**Globe.** A sphere of radius `@radius` centred on the object's origin, tessellated into `@segments` columns
(clamped to 24 … 512) and max(2, floor(segments/2)) rows. The point at longitude λ and latitude β is

    r · (cos β · sin λ,  −sin β,  −cos β · cos λ)

so 0° longitude on the equator faces the implicit camera (−z), 90° east is at +x and north is toward −y
(up). The drape's texture coordinate at that point is ((λ + 180°)/360°, (90° − β)/180°). The object's
transform then applies.

**Material.** The ground and the globe take from `@material` its base colour, which multiplies the drape,
and its roughness, metallic and unlit properties. Buildings take `@material` whole. Without `@material`, the
ground and globe have base colour white (the drape unchanged), roughness 0.9 and metallic 0, and buildings
have the linear base colour (0.86, 0.85, 0.83), roughness 0.8 and metallic 0. `@opacity`, `@castShadow` and
`@receiveShadow` apply to the ground, the globe and the buildings.

**Resolve.** For a tiles asset with `@url`, the resolve step of SREP 9 also fetches: for a tiles asset named
by `@terrain`, the tiles at zoom zd of the visible tile set of each frame's map view; and for a globe, the
tiles of each of its map's basemaps over the whole world at the zoom the globe drape draws them. Rendering
never fetches tiles.

### Defaults and the neutral case

Both primitives are new; other primitives ignore the new attributes. A `map` object without `@terrain` and
`@buildings` is a flat drape.

## Rationale

- **One map pixel per scene unit** lets a 2D map and its 3D ground line up exactly, so a scene can cut from one
  to the other.
- **Heights at the map's scale, not `pixelsPerMeter`.** The ground's horizontal scale is fixed by the map: one
  metre of ground at the centre is s = k / (R cos φ₀) map pixels. Heights use the same s so relief keeps its
  true proportion to the ground under it, whatever the map's zoom. `physics/@pixelsPerMeter` is the physics
  world's scale and has no relation to a map's scale; using it would make a mountain's height depend on a
  physics setting and not on the map. This keeps SREP 0's one-space rule: there is still one space, heights
  are scene pixels, and metres enter it only through the map's own scale (see Backwards compatibility).
- **The drape at the map's own size.** Laying the drape out at W × H and scaling it makes the 3D ground show
  exactly what the 2D layer shows, and makes `@textureSize` a resolution setting only.
- **Terrarium and Mapbox Terrain-RGB** are the two public elevation encodings (AWS Terrain Tiles, MapTiler,
  Mapbox), and the two values of `encoding` for `raster-dem` sources in the MapLibre style specification
  (Sources, raster-dem).
- **True-scale height with an exaggeration factor** is MapLibre's terrain model (MapLibre style
  specification, Terrain: `exaggeration`, default 1).
- **The attribution plate in the drape.** The credit belongs to the map's data, and the drape is the map as
  drawn; keeping it in the drape means a 3D scene shows the same credit as the 2D map without a second,
  screen-space overlay rule.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A separate `<terrain>` element.** Terrain only has meaning under a map; attributes on the object keep it
  one element.
- **Heights through `physics/@pixelsPerMeter`.** See Rationale: it ties relief to an unrelated setting.
- **The drape laid out at the texture's size.** Line widths and labels would shrink as `@textureSize` grows.
- **The attribution plate in screen space.** It would need its own placement rule for 3D views, and a globe
  or ground seen at an angle would carry a credit that does not move with it.
- **Drawing the drape per frame on the GPU as a render target.** An engine choice, not a format question; the
  specification only fixes what the drape contains.

## Backwards compatibility

Every 1.1 document is valid and renders the same. The new primitives require `version="1.2"` (V5). The new
attributes are accepted in every version and do nothing on other primitives.

One space (SREP 0): heights are converted from metres by the map's scale s, not by `physics/@pixelsPerMeter`.
This is not a second coordinate system: the ground, its heights and its buildings are all in scene pixels,
and the metres of the elevation and building data enter at the scale the map already uses for its ground.
