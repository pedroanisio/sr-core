# SREP 0 — Add wildfire to the terrain grid

## Abstract

SREP 12's T5: a **`wildfire`** child of `terrain` lights a surface fire at a point and a time and spreads it over
the grid with **Rothermel**'s surface fire spread model (Rothermel 1972, with **Albini**'s 1976 adjustments, as
**Andrews** 2018 lays it out and **BehavePlus** computes it) on one of the forty standard fire behaviour fuel
models of **Scott** and **Burgan** (2005). The midflame wind is the wind of the wind-field draft at the cell, times
`windAdjustment`; wind and slope combine as vectors into an effective wind that sets the head's direction and rate;
the fire's footprint is **Anderson**'s (1983) ellipse with the **length-to-breadth** ratio of the effective wind,
so the rate toward any direction is the head's times (1 − e) / (1 − e cos θ) (**Finney** 1998, FARSITE's
**Huygens** wavelets reduced to a graph); every cell's arrival time is the **minimum travel time** from the ignition
over the eight-neighbour graph (Finney 2002, **Dijkstra**'s search), the rate out of a cell read with the wind at
the moment the fire leaves it. A frame reads the arrival map: a cell burns from its arrival over the time the
front takes to cross it plus Anderson's **residence** time 384 / σ, the terrain's **`burn`** layer is that fraction,
its **`fuel`** layer scales the model's loads and goes with the burn (zero is a firebreak), the **flame length** is
**Byram**'s. The model runs in its own units (ft, lb, min, Btu; wind in **ft/min**, 88 per **mi/h**) and the document
in SI; the reference converts at the boundary and reproduces Rothermel's table 17 (**81.6** ft/min for two tons
per acre of fine fuel at 5 mi/h) and BehavePlus's **GR2** curve (**24.1** ft/min at 90 % live herbaceous moisture).
Rules WF1–WF5 and V17; the schema files are unchanged, the diff is inside the draft. Reference module
`conformance/tools/wildfire_reference.py`; engine card SR-109.

## Motivation

- **SREP 12 names the deltas.** Row T5 of the plan: "Rothermel rate of spread with wind (F4) and slope factors on
  the grid (Rothermel 1972; Finney 1998); reference tests: BehavePlus rates for the standard fuel models". This
  draft is that row, on the terrain grid (SR-100) and the wind field (SR-101).
- **The terrain grid reserved the layers.** The terrain-grid draft lists `fuel` (kg/m²) and `burn` ([0, 1]) among
  its fields with nothing yet writing them; erosion, snow and lava are the processes that exist. A grass fire
  running before the wind in a climate explainer is the fourth.
- **Fire spread is a published, tested model.** Rothermel's model has driven the US fire behaviour tools for fifty
  years (BehavePlus, FARSITE, FlamMap); its inputs are a fuel model, moistures, a midflame wind and a slope, all
  of which an author can state, and its outputs are tabulated to the digit, which conformance can check.
- **Every frame renders alone.** The arrival map is a function of the document and the wind field, not of the
  frames before; a frame at t reads it. Seeking to any time gives the same picture (SREP 14).
- **The terms.** SREP 12 left the sources' terms open. Rothermel 1972, Albini 1976, Anderson 1983, Scott and
  Burgan 2005 and Andrews 2018 are USDA Forest Service research papers, works of the United States government in
  the **public domain** (17 U.S.C. § 105); the equations and the fuel-model table are transcribed from them, and no
  code of BehavePlus or FARSITE is copied.

**Schematron** (`scene-render.sch`):

- **WF1** The five moistures are in [0, 2]; `windAdjustment` is in (0, 1]; `curing`, when given, is in [0, 1];
  `ignitionTime` is ≥ 0; `windLimit` is a boolean.
- **WF2** `fuelModel` is one of GR1–GR9, GS1–GS4, SH1–SH9, TU1–TU5, TL1–TL9, SB1–SB4.
- **WF3** The terrain has a `burn` layer.
- **WF4** The ignition lies on the grid: 0 ≤ `ignitionX` < width and 0 ≤ `ignitionY` < height of the terrain.
- **WF5** `wind`, when given, is the id of a `wind` of `physics`.
- **V17** (extended) `wildfire` requires `scene/@version` 1.8 or later.

### Semantics

Lengths in the document are metres on the terrain's grid (i right, j down, `metersPerCell` = dx apart), loads
kg/m², speeds m/s, time seconds of scene time. The model's equations are in Rothermel's units: surface-area-to-
volume ratios σ in 1/ft, loads w₀ in lb/ft² (a ton per acre is 2000 / 43560 lb/ft²), depth δ in ft, heat content
h in Btu/lb, the midflame wind U in ft/min (a m/s is 196.85 ft/min, a mi/h 88 ft/min), rates in ft/min (a ft/min
is 0.00508 m/s), intensities in Btu/ft²/min; a kg/m² is 0.20482 lb/ft². The reference is
`conformance/tools/wildfire_reference.py`; function names in parentheses.

1. **The fuel models.** Each of the forty models (Scott and Burgan 2005, table 7) is a load for the dead 1-hour,
   10-hour and 100-hour classes and the live herbaceous and woody classes, the σ of the 1-hour, herbaceous and
   woody classes (10-hour 109, 100-hour 30), the bed depth, the dead moisture of **extinction** M_x, the heat
   content and whether it is **dynamic**. In a dynamic model the herbaceous load is split by the cured fraction
   c = clamp((1.20 − M_herb) / 0.90, 0, 1) (or `curing`): c of it becomes a dead class with the herbaceous σ at
   the 1-hour moisture, the rest stays live. Where the terrain has a `fuel` layer, a cell's loads are the
   model's times the layer's value over the model's total load; a cell at zero has no fuel. (`FUEL_MODELS`,
   `curing`, `standard_bed`, `standard_load`, `cell_bed`)
2. **Rothermel's rate.** With the weighting of Rothermel 1972 and Albini 1976 (Andrews 2018, sections 3.2.2 to
   3.2.5): the surface-area fractions f_ij and f_i, the net loads by σ subclass g_ij, the mean σ, the bulk density
   ρ_b = Σ w₀ / δ, the **packing ratio** β = ρ_b / ρ_p (ρ_p 32 lb/ft³), the optimum β_op = 3.348 σ^−0.8189, the
   **reaction intensity** I_R = Γ′ Σ_i w_n,i h_i η_M,i η_s,i with Γ′ = Γ′_max (β / β_op)^A exp(A (1 − β / β_op)),
   Γ′_max = σ^1.5 / (495 + 0.0594 σ^1.5), A = 133 σ^−0.7913, the moisture damping η_M = 1 − 2.59 r + 5.11 r² −
   3.52 r³ (r = M_f / M_x, 0 at and past the extinction) with the live extinction moisture of Albini, the mineral
   damping η_s = 0.174 S_e^−0.19 (S_e 0.010, S_T 0.0555), the **propagating flux** ratio ξ = exp((0.792 + 0.681
   √σ)(β + 0.1)) / (192 + 0.2595 σ), the heat sink ρ_b ε Q_ig = ρ_b Σ_i f_i Σ_j f_ij exp(−138 / σ_ij)(250 + 1116
   M_ij), the wind factor Φ_w = C U^B (β / β_op)^−E (C = 7.47 exp(−0.133 σ^0.55), B = 0.02526 σ^0.54, E = 0.715
   exp(−3.59·10⁻⁴ σ)) and the slope factor Φ_s = 5.275 β^−0.3 tan²φ (0 downslope):
   R = I_R ξ (1 + Φ_w + Φ_s) / (ρ_b ε Q_ig) ft/min. Anderson's residence time is t_r = 384 / σ min, Byram's
   fireline intensity I_B = I_R t_r R / 60 Btu/ft/s and flame length L = 0.45 I_B^0.46 ft. The reference reproduces
   Rothermel's table 17 and BehavePlus's curves within 2 % (the numeric tests). (`characteristics`, `fire`,
   `moisture_damping`, `mineral_damping`, `wind_factor`, `slope_factor`)
3. **The midflame wind and the effective wind.** At time t the cell's wind is the wind-field draft's `wind` at
   the physics point of the cell centre, ((i + ½) dx, −(j + ½) dx), as a grid vector (w_x, −w_y), times
   `windAdjustment`, in ft/min; calm without a `wind`. The slope is the gradient of the terrain's surface (the
   sum of its solid layers, `bedrock` and above) at the cell; its equivalent wind U_s = (Φ_s / (C (β / β_op)^−E))^(1/B)
   points up the slope (Andrews 2018, section 4.1). The effective wind is the vector sum of the two; its
   direction is the head's, its magnitude the U of the wind factor, so that a fire on a slope in calm air heads
   upslope at the slope factor's rate. With `windLimit` the magnitude is capped at 0.9 I_R. (`wind_at_cell`,
   `effective_wind`, `local_fire`)
4. **The ellipse.** The length-to-breadth ratio is Anderson's (1983) in the effective wind in mi/h,
   LB = 0.936 e^(0.2566 U) + 0.461 e^(−0.1548 U) − 0.397, at most 8; e = √(LB² − 1) / LB; the rate at the angle θ
   from the head is R(θ) = R_head (1 − e) / (1 − e cos θ) (Finney 1998). (`length_to_breadth`, `spread_toward`)
5. **The arrival map.** The ignition cell is (⌊`ignitionX` / dx⌋, ⌊`ignitionY` / dx⌋), settled at `ignitionTime`.
   The arrival time of every other cell is the minimum travel time over the eight-neighbour graph: a cell settled
   at T with a head rate R_head and direction d (rules 2 to 4 with the wind read at T) offers each neighbour at
   the distance ℓ (dx, or dx √2 on a diagonal) the time T + ℓ / R(θ), θ the angle between d and the step; the
   smallest offer settles next (Dijkstra). A cell without fuel is never offered: a zero in the `fuel` layer is a
   firebreak, and a cell behind one is reached around it or never. The map is finite where the fire arrives
   and infinite elsewhere; it is a function of the document and the wind field alone. (`arrival`,
   `arrival_from_layers`, `ignition_cell`)
6. **Frames.** At time t the `burn` layer of a cell with arrival T and head rate R (m/s) is clamp((t − T) /
   (dx / R + t_r), 0, 1), t_r its residence time in seconds; the `fuel` layer, where present, is the generated
   fuel times (1 − burn); a cell with 0 < burn < 1 is burning with Byram's flame length (metres) from its head
   rate; the run reports the burning and burnt counts and the digest of the `burn`, `fuel` and flame grids at
   three decimals. (`run`, `burn_fraction`, `digest`)
7. **Every frame alone.** Nothing in rules 5 and 6 depends on an earlier frame; the wind is read as a function
   of time from the wind field, so a time-varying wind changes the map, not the frame's reading of it
   (SREP 14). (`run`)
8. **Validity.** WF1–WF5 and V17 hold or the document is refused; the reference lists the codes.
   (`validate_document`, `bad_document`)

## Rationale

- **Rothermel, not a cellular rule of thumb.** Probabilistic cellular automata (Alexandridis et al. 2008) spread
  by tuned neighbour probabilities; Rothermel's model turns a fuel model, moistures, a wind and a slope into a
  rate with published tables an author can read and conformance can check.
- **Scott and Burgan's forty models.** The thirteen of Anderson 1982 have no dynamic herbaceous curing and no
  high-moisture grass; the forty are the set BehavePlus, FARSITE and LANDFIRE use, so an author's `fuelModel`
  means what the fire community means by it.
- **A travel-time map, not a propagated front.** FARSITE propagates Huygens wavelets along a polygon; on a grid
  the minimum travel time over the eight neighbours (Finney 2002, FlamMap's MTT) is the same ellipse arithmetic
  with a deterministic shortest-path search, and it makes every frame a lookup (SREP 14). Its cost is the
  eight-direction quantisation, an open issue.
- **The effective wind as a vector.** Adding the slope factor to the wind factor loses the direction; the
  equivalent-wind vector (Andrews 2018, section 4.1, as BehavePlus and FARSITE) keeps the head's direction
  between the wind's and the upslope. The test checks that a slope alone reproduces the slope factor to 10⁻⁹.
- **The wind at departure.** The rate out of a cell is read at the cell's arrival time, as Finney 2002: a
  time-varying wind is honoured step by step without a time-stepped simulation.
- **No wind limit by default.** Rothermel's cap U ≤ 0.9 I_R was meant for his data's range; Andrews, Cruz and
  Rothermel (2013) show it underpredicts grass fires and recommend removing it. It stays as an option because
  BehavePlus still offers it.
- **The fuel layer as a scale.** One `fuelModel` per terrain and the `fuel` layer as its multiplier keeps the
  terrain's field a mass (kg/m²) as the terrain-grid draft declared it, lets a mask paint a firebreak, and lets
  the burn consume it; a per-cell fuel model is an open issue.
- **The burn duration.** A cell is alight from the front's arrival until the front has crossed it and the
  residence time has passed; the crossing is at the head rate, a lower bound of the time a flank takes.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A probabilistic cellular automaton.** Tunable, not testable; nothing in it is a measured rate.
- **A level-set front (Mallet et al. 2009).** Exact on the ellipse, but it is a stepped PDE solve whose result
  depends on the step; the travel-time graph is deterministic and seekable.
- **Crown fire and spotting (Van Wagner 1977, Albini 1979).** Surface fire only in this draft; crown transition
  and spotting are the next deltas, listed as open issues.
- **Anderson's thirteen fuel models.** Superseded by the forty for the reasons above.
- **Burning the terrain's `bedrock` or `loose` layers.** Fire consumes the `fuel` layer only; the ground is
  unchanged, and the lava draft's `temperature` layer is not written (open issue).

## Backwards compatibility

A 1.7 document has no `terrain`; a 1.8 terrain without `wildfire` is unchanged. The element is additive.

## Open issues

1. **Eight directions.** The travel-time graph quantises the ellipse to eight bearings; FlamMap refines the
   lattice with longer edges (Finney 2002). A sixteen-neighbour graph is the candidate.
2. **Crown fire and spotting.** Surface fire only; Van Wagner's crown transition and Albini's spotting distance
   are the next deltas of T5.
3. **Per-cell fuel models.** One model per terrain, scaled by the `fuel` layer; a fuel-model layer (LANDFIRE's
   raster) would need a typed layer.
4. **Heat and smoke.** The fire writes neither the lava draft's `temperature` layer nor an emitter; the pyro
   draft's smoke from a burning cell is a coupling for the weather or pyro drafts.
5. **Moisture from the weather.** The five moistures are attributes; the weather draft's humidity and rain could
   drive them (Nelson's dead-fuel moisture model).
6. **The crossing time.** A cell's burn duration uses its head rate for every direction; the flank's slower
   crossing lengthens the burn in reality.
7. **The wind adjustment.** A constant 0.4; the sheltered and unsheltered factors of Andrews 2012 depend on the
   fuel bed depth and the canopy.
