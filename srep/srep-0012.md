# SREP 12 — Plan the format's coverage of science-explainer video

## Abstract

The format's goal is to produce almost any shot of a science-explainer video (Veritasium, PBS Space Time,
Kurzgesagt, 3Blue1Brown, minutephysics, Steve Mould, Real Engineering, Primer, Sebastian Lague) from a
document, with every frame exact under seeking and every physical claim tested. This SREP measures the format
against a published catalogue of 36 reference shots from those channels (Appendix A) and against its usual
peers (Lottie, Rive, SVG 2, Remotion, Motion Canvas, After Effects text animators, HyperFrames, Shotstack,
Creatomate, JSON2Video, Editly, MLT, OTIO, FCPXML, OpenUSD with UsdLux and UsdGeomCamera, glTF, OCIO, plus
Manim, OpenSpace, ParaView and ray-optics for the new families), with one measurable exit criterion per peer.
It takes the specialist tools that lead each area as the quality bar and orders the Standards and Semantics
SREPs that close the gaps into nine milestones by share of screen time and size of gap, dependencies first.
The design rules the planned SREPs share are normative in [SREP 14](srep-0014.md). This SREP proposes no
schema change.

## Motivation

Explainer videos are built from a small number of shot families. A survey of the channels above gives a rough
share of screen time for each (a judgement, not a measurement). The catalogue of Appendix A samples each
family; its status column is an estimate until milestone 0 measures it. "Screen time not native" is the
family's share times the fraction of its catalogue shots that are not native today, which is the measure the
milestones are ordered by.

| Shot family | Share | Catalogue shots | Native today (estimated) | Screen time not native | Today |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Live action and compositing | ≈22% | 3 | 3 | 0% | keys, imported point, planar, camera and face tracks, stabilisation |
| Flat motion graphics and typography | ≈16% | 3 | 1 | ≈10.7% | shapes, text animators, transitions; no grid morphs, no hand-drawn look |

| Scientific visualisation (fields, waves) | ≈10% | 6 | 1 | ≈8.3% | particles in force fields; no field elements |
| 3D mechanics and physics | ≈9% | 4 | 0 | ≈9.0% | 3D rigid bodies ([SREP 11](srep-0011.md), in review, reference implementation on a branch) |
| Data, charts and maps | ≈8% | 3 | 2 | ≈2.7% | charts; maps with points, lines and choropleths; basemaps and 3D terrain from elevation tiles ([SREPs 9](srep-0009.md) and [10](srep-0010.md), in review); no relief, heatmap or time-series layers |

| All | 100% | 36 | 15 | ≈48.5% | |

Of the 36 catalogue shots, an estimated 15 render natively today, 6 need custom work (shaders, hand-built
geometry, motion computed outside the document) and 15 hit a gap. None of the peers declares the missing
families: the motion-graphics formats and JSON video APIs have none of them, the code frameworks reach them only
through embedded three.js, Python or scripts that the author must make deterministic by hand, and the
interchange formats describe results (volumes, rigid bodies, instances) without simulating anything. The
specialist tools that do simulate or render these subjects (Houdini, OpenSpace, ParaView, ray-optics) are not
driven by a declarative document, do not render to finished video under a single timeline, and are rarely
validated. That is the opening.

Explainers are usually published as a long video plus several short vertical cut-downs. The peers that
template video (the JSON video APIs, Remotion) render each cut-down as a separate job. [SREP 13](srep-0013.md)
closes that gap in this format: one document declares the long video and its Shorts as outputs with
segments. The milestones below assume it.

## Specification

This section is the plan. It is informative: each item becomes normative only through its own Standards or
Semantics SREP, and the design rules through SREP 14. Once accepted, this SREP is Active (SREP 0) and is
revised in place, with a dated line under **History**, as the planned SREPs land and the catalogue is
re-scored.

Three things follow. No peer simulates any of these processes in the document: where they appear at all, the
author writes the simulation in embedded code, or imports a result baked elsewhere. Embedded simulations are
deterministic only if the author re-runs them from the start at a fixed step on every seek, which none of the
frameworks does for them. And the interchange formats (UsdVol, UsdPhysics, PointInstancer, glTF instancing and
3D Tiles) are the right way to exchange results, so the plan reads and writes them.

The criteria on which the format must beat every peer:

1. **Declared, not programmed.** Each capability is elements and attributes in the document, validated by the
   schema; no script is needed.
2. **Simulated on the timeline.** Processes run along the composition's time and seek exactly, by the rules of
   SREP 14.
3. **Checked against physics.** Each capability passes reference solutions (analytic cases, published data,
   GDAL for relief), where the peers offer none.
4. **Interchange.** Volumes, instances, rigid bodies and 3D tiles are read from and written to OpenUSD and glTF
   forms, so results move to and from the peers' ecosystems.

### The measure: a benchmark catalogue

Progress is measured on the catalogue of 36 reference shots of 5 to 10 seconds in Appendix A. Each shot records
its source video, what it shows, the track it needs, its status today (native, custom, gap) and the milestone
expected to make it native. The statuses are estimates. Milestone 0 checks each description against its video,
records a timestamp, adds a probe scene per shot and a scoring script to the compatibility kit, and replaces
the estimates with measurements.

A shot counts as native when its probe scene validates under `--strict`, meets its pixel checks, and passes the
seek check of SREP 14. The format leads when at least 90% of the catalogue is native and every peer's exit
criterion above holds.

What no leading tool offers, and every SREP below must keep: a render is a pure function of the document,
its pinned inputs and the frame time, and seeking to any frame gives exactly what playing up to it gives.

### Design rules

The planned SREPs follow the rules of SREP 14, which is the normative source: simulations advance in fixed
steps from their own start or use a closed form in time; every random value comes from the D24 hash through
one set of derived draws; results do not depend on evaluation order; lengths are scene pixels and external
frames (geographic, ICRF, body-fixed) are inputs mapped into them, with any rebasing kept inside engines;
engines agree within each case's stated tolerance and each engine passes a seek check; every capability has
reference solutions; external data is resolved into pinned caches; and reference implementations depend only
on public-domain or permissively licensed code and data.

### Shared building blocks

Four definitions are reused across the plan. Each building block's SREP comes before the first SREP that uses
it (see **Order of work**):

- **Random streams (F1).** The derived draws on the D24 hash that every stochastic SREP uses.
- **Medium (F3).** One description of participating media (absorption, scattering, emission, phase function)
  used by the atmosphere, fog, clouds, volumes, water and smoke, so the raster renderer and the path tracer
  agree.
- **Terrain grid (F2).** One element holding a regular grid of layers over a rectangle: bedrock, loose
  material, water depth and flow, suspended sediment, lava depth and temperature, snow (by type), ice, fuel and
  burn state, hardness and wetness. The ground surface is the sum of the solid layers. Processes read and write
  layers; display turns them into a 3D surface (like `object3D primitive="map"` of SREP 10, in review) or a 2D
  picture (like `<erosion>` in the baseline).
- **Wind (F4).** The baseline's `wind` force field (a push along `forceX`, `forceY`, gusting with noise)
  extended into one animatable wind field with declared gusts and curl-noise turbulence, read by particles,
  vegetation, clouds, water, snow drift and fire spread.

### Order of work

Milestones are ordered by the screen time their shots carry once native: each catalogue shot weighs its
family's share divided by the family's number of catalogue shots, so a milestone's weight is share of screen
time times size of gap. A milestone's prerequisites come before it, and each building block comes before its
first use. Milestones that make no catalogue shot native come last, ordered by their family's share. Shot
numbers refer to Appendix A.

|---|---|---|---|---|

Accepted. Milestone 0's exit criterion is that the catalogue's probe scenes and scoring script are in the
compatibility kit and the count of native shots is measured. Milestone 5 needs only F3 from the building
blocks, which milestone 0 provides; milestone 8's clouds (A3), snow (T3), wildfire (T5) and vegetation (G1)
follow F4 within it. Tracks may be worked on in parallel; the order is the order of review.

## Rationale

- **One roadmap before many SREPs** lets implementers see the shared pieces (random streams, media, terrain
  grid, wind) and the order, so no SREP defines its own version of them.
- **Two comparisons.** The format is positioned against its peers, because those are what authors choose
  between; none of them offers these capabilities natively, and each has an exit criterion. Quality, though,
  is measured against the specialist tools that lead each area, because the peers set no bar there. No single
  product covers all the shot families.
- **Order by screen time times gap.** Milestones 1, 2 and 4 (math and fields, characters and looks, machines)
  carry most of the screen time not yet native (≈35.0 of the ≈48.5 points). Live action carries ≈22% of screen time, but its catalogue
  shots already render with imported tracks, so footage work gains no shot and comes late. Landscapes are
  about 2%.
- **Dependencies first.** Light and volumes carry little screen time of their own but are needed for smoke,
  fire and plasma in milestone 4; the media model is needed by the atmosphere of milestone 5 and so moves to
  milestone 0.
- **A catalogue as the measure.** Progress is counted in reference shots made native, with the seek check, so
  claims of coverage become measurements once milestone 0 lands.
- **Algorithms were chosen** for being gather-form or closed-form in time (so they can be exact and seekable),
  having published reference solutions, and being current practice in production tools.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Environments first.** Terrain, water and weather were the first plan, but they fill about 2% of explainer
  screen time and no catalogue shot needs them; they move to the last milestone.
- **Footage early, by share of screen time alone.** Its catalogue shots are native with imported tracks, so it
  would move no shot.
- **One SREP for everything.** It would take years to reach Accepted and could not be reviewed in pieces.
- **Adopting an external engine's model wholesale** (for example Houdini's heightfield layers or Unreal's
  water). Their definitions are not public specifications and are not deterministic.
- **Particle droplet erosion as the main terrain method.** Its scattered deposits need exact accumulation to
  be order-independent, and it has no analytic test; it stays available as a detail pass.
- **A new generator for F1 (Philox or PCG).** D24 already defines the normative hash (SREP 14).
