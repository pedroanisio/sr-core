# SREP 75 — Add fractal assets with deep-zoom precision

## Abstract

A new asset, `<fractal>`, renders the Mandelbrot or a Julia set at any depth of zoom. Its centre is given as
decimal strings of any length. Its zoom is an animatable power of ten. The SREP fixes what each pixel must show, and
not how to compute it: the escape count of the pixel centre in exact arithmetic, allowing only for points within
2⁻²⁰ of a pixel. An engine may meet this with double-float emulation, native f64 or perturbation theory. This SREP
compares the three. f32 shaders, which are all the format has today, break down at about 10⁻⁷.

## Motivation

**The owner's 72-cell atlas test (2026-10-09, not published).** "Double precision: without it, deep zooms in
fractals do not work." The plan (B10): fractal zooms past about 1e-7 need more than f32. It asks this SREP to compare
double-float emulation (df64) with perturbation theory (one high-precision reference orbit, low-precision deltas).

**Today** (checked 2026-10-09):

- **The format.** A fractal can only be drawn by a `shader` effect: GLSL compiled through the engine's pipeline,
  which computes in f32. The 10 `generator` kinds include no fractal.
- **WGSL.** The core specification defines `f32` and, as an extension, `f16`. It has no `f64` (W3C WGSL, read
  2026-10-09).
- **wgpu.** The engine's wgpu, 30.0.1 (`Cargo.lock`), has `Features::SHADER_F64`, a native-only feature
  documented as "frequently between 16 and 64 times slower" than f32 (`wgpu-types-30.0.1/src/features.rs`). On
  Vulkan, wgpu-hal maps it to the device feature `shaderFloat64` (`wgpu-hal-30.0.1/src/vulkan/adapter.rs:360` and
  `:762`). The engine requests only timestamp features today (`crates/sr-gpu/src/gpu.rs:468-478`).
- **llvmpipe.** On this host, Mesa 25.0.7 with LLVM 19.1.7, `vulkaninfo` reports `shaderFloat64 = true` for
  "llvmpipe (LLVM 19.1.7, 256 bits)". So wgpu would expose `SHADER_F64` there.
- **Not checked:**
  - the render VMs' Mesa versions;
  - whether naga 30 accepts f64 in WGSL or GLSL shaders with the feature on;
  - the accuracy of llvmpipe's f64 arithmetic.

**Where f32 and f64 stop.** The view width at zoom z is 4·10⁻ᶻ. Near the kit's centre, c ≈ −0.744 + 0.132i, a
pixel of a 640-px-wide view spans p = 4·10⁻ᶻ/640.

- **f32:** 24 significant bits, a spacing of about 6·10⁻⁸ near 0.74. It cannot address pixels once p falls below
  that spacing, at z ≈ 5.
- **f64:** 53 bits, a spacing of about 1.1·10⁻¹⁶. It stops at z ≈ 13.

The kit's cases sit at z = 6, 12 and 20: just past f32, within f64, and past f64.

### Semantics

**1. The plane.** `centerX` and `centerY` are read as exact decimal numbers from their lexical form, at full
length. An engine MUST NOT round them through a binary float. Let W and H be the asset's `width` and `height`, and
let `p = span · 10^(−zoom) / W` be the pixel size in the complex plane, where `zoom` is the animated value at the
frame's time. The centre of pixel (i, j), counted from the top-left with +y down, is:

    c(i, j) = (centerX, centerY) + R(rotation) · ((i + 0.5 − W/2) · p, −(j + 0.5 − H/2) · p)

The imaginary axis points up on screen. R turns clockwise on screen by `rotation` degrees.

**2. The iteration.**

- **Mandelbrot:** `z₀ = 0`, `z_{n+1} = z_n² + c(i, j)`.
- **Julia:** `z₀ = c(i, j)`, `z_{n+1} = z_n² + (juliaX + juliaY·i)`.

The escape count n is the smallest n ≥ 1 with |z_n| > `bailout`. A pixel with no such n ≤ `maxIterations` is
*inside*.

**3. The precision requirement (normative).** For every pixel, the count an engine uses MUST be the exact count, in
exact arithmetic, of some point c′ with `|Re(c′ − c)| ≤ 2⁻²⁰·p` and `|Im(c′ − c)| ≤ 2⁻²⁰·p`. Here c is the pixel
centre for a Mandelbrot asset, and the pixel's z₀ for a Julia asset. "Inside" counts as a count.

At any zoom, the picture is therefore the true fractal, up to an error far smaller than a pixel. Pixels whose count
changes within that tiny square, such as those right on the set's boundary, may differ between engines.

**4. Colour.**

- **Inside.** An inside pixel is `insideColor`.
- **Palette.** The palette is the list of colours in `palette`, of length L.
- **`bands`.** The colour is `palette[n mod L]`.
- **`smooth`.** Let `ν = n + 1 − log₂(ln|z_n| / ln bailout)` and `u = ν · paletteScale + paletteOffset`. The colour
  is interpolated linearly between `palette[⌊u⌋ mod L]` and `palette[(⌊u⌋ + 1) mod L]` at `u − ⌊u⌋`, in
  display-encoded values.
- **Tolerance.** `smooth` involves float logarithms, so its colours are exact within 1 code value. `bands` is exact.

**5. As an asset.** A fractal is an image source like a `generator`. A `layer` shows it at width × height, and it
can feed a material map through SREP 71. Its animated attributes are evaluated at the time of the frame being
drawn. There is no anti-aliasing within the asset. An author who wants it uses `project/@supersample` (SREP 72).

## Rationale

### Why a result requirement, not a method

The three methods below all meet Semantics 3 where they apply. Fixing one in the format would bind every engine to
it, and would forbid better ones later.

| Method | How | Reach (near \|c\| ≈ 0.74) | Cost | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Native f64** (`SHADER_F64`) | the iteration in f64 on the GPU, or on the CPU | to z ≈ 13 | 16–64× slower than f32 on many GPUs (wgpu docs); unknown on llvmpipe | the feature is optional: a device without it needs a fallback. Not in WGSL core. |
| **df64** (double-float: an unevaluated sum of two f32, using Dekker's and Knuth's error-free transformations) | each operation becomes about 10–20 f32 operations | about 48 bits of precision, to z ≈ 12; the f32 exponent range (≈10⁻³⁸) still limits | about 10–20× f32 | correct only if the compiler keeps the exact order of operations: no reassociation, no FMA contraction except where intended. **Not checked on naga, llvmpipe or any target GPU.** |
| **Perturbation** (K. I. Martin, 2013) | one reference orbit Z_n at arbitrary precision on the CPU; each pixel iterates its difference δ_{n+1} = 2Z_nδ_n + δ_n² + Δc in low precision | no limit from the deltas' precision. With an extended exponent (a float with a separate integer exponent), zooms of 10⁻¹⁰⁰⁰ and deeper are routine in fractal software. | one arbitrary-precision orbit per frame, plus about the f32 or f64 cost per pixel | **glitches**: pixels whose orbit runs close to the reference lose precision. These must be detected (for example when \|Z_n + δ_n\| ≪ \|Z_n\|) and recomputed with a new reference or by rebasing. Without that, a result can violate Semantics 3. |

**Recommendation for the reference implementation:**

- **Perturbation** with f64 deltas (an extended exponent beyond 10⁻³⁰⁰), glitch detection and rebasing, and the
  reference orbit in arbitrary precision on the CPU.
- **Direct f64** (CPU, or GPU with `SHADER_F64`) for zooms where p is above 2⁻⁴⁰ of |c|. This path is simpler
  and glitch-free.

df64 adds complexity, reaches no further than f64, and depends on compiler behaviour that has not been checked. It is
therefore not recommended as the primary method.

### Other choices

- **Decimal strings for the centre.** A deep zoom's centre has as many significant digits as its depth: a zoom of
  10⁻⁵⁰ needs about 55 digits. `xs:decimal` carries them exactly, and `xs:double` cannot.
- **Zoom as a power of ten**, so a linear animation of `zoom` is a constant-rate zoom.
- **The 2⁻²⁰ allowance.** It is the smallest tolerance that lets a method with limited precision conform without
  making the picture visibly different. Without one, no finite-precision method could conform at pixels on the
  boundary.

## Rejected alternatives

- **f64 in the shader language only** (`precision="f64"` on shader effects): it reaches z ≈ 13, not deep zoom. It
  depends on an optional device feature, and authors would still write the iteration themselves.
- **Requiring a specific method** (for example perturbation): see Rationale.
- **A `generator` kind `mandelbrot`**: generators take `xs:double` parameters and have no place for an exact
  centre.

## Backwards compatibility

The change is MINOR: a new asset kind under the version gate V16.

## Conformance

The cases are in `conformance/srep_cases/srep-0075.json`, written by `conformance/tools/srep75_reference.py`.

- **How the expected counts were computed.** In decimal arithmetic at 60 and at 90 significant digits, at each
  sample pixel's centre and at the four points 2⁻²⁰·p away along the axes. A pixel is kept only when all ten counts
  agree, so every conforming engine must show that count.
- **The palette.** It alternates red and blue (`bands`): the colour gives the parity of the count, and black means
  inside.
- **Counts at zoom 6:** 223, inside, 1005, 275, 1308, 486, inside, 307, inside.
- **Counts at zoom 12:** 1972 to 4047.
- **Counts at zoom 20:** 8485 to 10082.

All counts are in the cases' `rule` text. All cases are pending.

| Case | Checks | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| `srep-0075-mandelbrot-zoom-6` | nine pixel parities and inside pixels, just past f32's reach (1–4) | 3 code values |
| `srep-0075-mandelbrot-zoom-12` | the same within f64's reach | 3 |
| `srep-0075-mandelbrot-zoom-20` | the same past f64's reach (only perturbation or arbitrary precision passes) | 3 |
| `srep-0075-version-gate`, `-julia-needs-c` | V16, FRC1 | exact |

The ten-point check is necessary but not sufficient for Semantics 3: it samples the allowance square without
covering it. A kit pixel could in principle change its count elsewhere in the square. The check makes this
unlikely, but does not exclude it.
