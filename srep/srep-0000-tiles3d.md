# SREP 0 — Add 3D Tiles to the map

## Abstract

SREP 12's M5: **OGC 3D Tiles 1.1** city models on a map. A **`tileset3d`** asset names a tileset (its root
`tileset.json`, OGC 22-025r4) and, after the **resolve step**, one **`cache`** file — a deterministic **ZIP**
archive holding the root tileset, every external tileset and **subtree** file the traversal loaded and every
**glb** or **b3dm** content any frame's view selects — whose SHA-256 the document pins in **`cacheSha256`**;
the renderer reads only the archive and never fetches. A **`tiles3d`** map child draws the tileset where SREP
10 draws buildings: each frame the renderer walks the tileset from its root, skips tiles outside the view or
whose `viewerRequestVolume` excludes the viewer, computes each tile's **screen-space error** from the camera
alone (geometric error × pixels per metre for a 2D map, geometric error × scale × focal length / distance for
a perspective camera), refines a tile whose error exceeds **`maxError`** (**REPLACE** drops its content,
**ADD** keeps it), always descends into an external tileset, reads **implicit tiling** through its subtrees in
**Morton** order, and places every content through the tile's composed transform, the glTF **y-up** to z-up
rotation and SREP 10's ground frame. Traversals are bounded by `maxTiles` and 32 levels. Validation rules
TL1–TL4, warnings W-TL1–W-TL3, the schema gate V17. Reference `conformance/tools/tiles3d_reference.py`
(**numpy**; it also carries the subtree, glb and b3dm writers the tests use), tests `tests/test_tiles3d.py`.
Engine card [SR-116](https://faz-ai.atlassian.net/browse/SR-116).

## Motivation

A city explainer flies over the real roofs of Helsinki, not over extruded footprints. SREP 10 extrudes a
basemap's building polygons to flat-topped prisms; a photogrammetric or CityGML-derived mesh carries the
roofs, the towers and the terrain. The **City of Helsinki** publishes its 3D city model (mesh and semantic
models) under **CC BY 4.0** and serves it through a Cesium viewer, and OGC 3D Tiles 1.1 is the open exchange
format for such models (SREP 12, M5, which excludes proprietary tile services). Today an author renders the
model in a browser and screen-records it, which is neither reproducible nor seekable, and what the browser
loads depends on the network's timing. This draft pins the tiles a composition needs in one archive, states
the tile choice as a function of the camera and the document alone, and defines the picture a 2D map draws
from them.

## Specification

### Syntax

A new asset kind, `tileset3d`:

| Attribute | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | xs:ID | required | The asset's identifier. |
| `src` | xs:string | required | The tileset's root JSON, a file or URL; its directory is the base of the tileset's relative uris. |
| `cache` | xs:string | required | The archive the resolve step writes and the renderer reads (rule 9). |
| `cacheSha256` | xs:string | required | The SHA-256 of the archive, 64 hex digits; the renderer refuses an archive that differs (TL1). |
| `maxError` | positiveDecimal | `16` | The maximum screen-space error in pixels; a tile above it is refined (rule 7). |
| `maxTiles` | xs:positiveInteger | `4096` | The most tiles one view's traversal visits, 1 to 1 000 000 (TL2, W-TL1). |
| `credit` | xs:string | absent | The model's attribution (CC BY 4.0 requires it), drawn with the map's credits. |

A new map child, `tiles3d`, drawn in the map's child order:

| Attribute | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | xs:ID | required | The child's identifier. |
| `tileset` | xs:IDREF | required | A `tileset3d` asset (TL3). |
| `heightOffset` | xs:decimal | `0` | Metres added to every tile's height before placement (rule 10). |
| `opacity` | xs:decimal | `1` | Multiplies the painted colour, 0 to 1 (TL2). |

### Validation

An engine reports every rule it can check:

- **TL1** (error): a `tileset3d` without a 64-digit hexadecimal `cacheSha256`, or whose archive's SHA-256
  differs from it. The renderer draws nothing for the asset.
- **TL2** (error): a `tileset3d` whose `maxError` is not positive or whose `maxTiles` is not an integer in
  1..1 000 000; a `tiles3d` whose `opacity` is outside [0, 1], whose `heightOffset` is not a number, or whose
  `color` is not a colour.
- **TL3** (error): a `tiles3d` whose `tileset` does not name a `tileset3d` asset.
- **TL4** (error): a malformed tileset: the archive is not a ZIP or holds no `tileset.json` at its root; the
  tileset is not a JSON object with `asset.version` `"1.0"` or `"1.1"`, a non-negative `geometricError` and a
  `root`; a tile without exactly one of `box`, `region` or `sphere` in its `boundingVolume`, a `box` that is
  not 12 numbers, a `region` that is not six numbers with west ≤ east, south ≤ north and minimum ≤ maximum
  height, a `sphere` that is not four numbers with a non-negative radius, a `geometricError` that is not a
  non-negative number, a `refine` that is not `ADD` or `REPLACE` or a root without one, a `transform` that is
  not 16 numbers, a `content` without a `uri`, a `contents` or `children` that is not a list, an
  `implicitTiling` whose `subdivisionScheme` is not `QUADTREE` or `OCTREE`, whose `subtreeLevels` is not a
  positive integer, whose `availableLevels` is below it or whose `subtrees.uri` is not a template with
  `{level}`, an implicit tile with children of its own, or an implicit tile on a `sphere`. The reference
  reports the first such error per asset; an engine may report more.
- **W-TL1** (warning): a view's traversal reached `maxTiles`; the tiles visited so far are drawn and the
  warning is reported once per child.
- **W-TL2** (warning): a selected content is a format the renderer does not draw (`i3dm`, `pnts`, `cmpt`,
  `.gltf` or an unknown extension); the content is skipped and the resolve step does not fetch it; reported
  once per child.
- **W-TL3** (warning): a selected content is absent from the archive (the document's views changed after the
  resolve step); the frame is drawn without it and the warning is reported once per child. The resolve step
  itself fails on an absent content.
- **V17** (error): a `tileset3d` or `tiles3d` in a document before version 1.8, or an attribute the schema
  does not declare on them.

### Semantics

1. **Tileset reading.** `parse_tileset`: a tileset is OGC 22-025r4's JSON; `asset.version` is `"1.0"` or
   `"1.1"`. Each tile is normalised to its bounding volume (`box`: a centre and three half-axes, 12 numbers;
   `region`: west, south, east, north in radians and two heights in metres on **EPSG:4979**; `sphere`: a centre
   and radius), its `geometricError`, its `refine` (inherited from the nearest ancestor that states one; the
   root MUST state one), its composed transform (the parent's matrix times the tile's `transform`, 16 numbers
   in column-major order; a tile without one inherits the parent's), its contents (`content` then `contents`,
   each uri resolved against the base of the file that declared it), its `viewerRequestVolume`, its children
   and its `implicitTiling`. Transforms apply to boxes, spheres, contents and request volumes; a region is
   never transformed. Anything else in the file is ignored (metadata, extensions, statistics: open issue 5).
2. **Content kinds.** `content_kind`: a uri ending `.glb` is a binary glTF, `.b3dm` a legacy Batched 3D
   Model, `.json` an external tileset; every other uri (`.i3dm`, `.pnts`, `.cmpt`, `.gltf`, …) is unsupported
   (W-TL2). The 1.1 specification deprecates the legacy formats; `b3dm` is kept because the Helsinki model and
   the 1.0 samples ship it.
3. **External tilesets.** `_children`: a tile whose content is a `.json` is a reference to another tileset:
   it carries that content alone and no children (TL4), and its subtree is the external tileset's root with
   the referring tile's composed matrix as its parent matrix, one level deeper, its uris relative to the
   external file's directory. Such a tile is **always refined** when visited and visible, whatever its
   screen-space error, as CesiumJS does: the external root's own error decides from there.
4. **Implicit tiling.** `_implicit_children`, `implicit_bounds`, `implicit_error`: an implicit tile at
   coordinate (level, x, y[, z]) has 2ⁿ children at (level + 1, 2x + dx, 2y + dy[, 2z + dz]) in **Morton**
   order (`morton`: x's bits least significant, then y's, then z's: `morton(0b1010, 0b0011)` = 78); its
   bounding volume is the root's box halved along its first two (three) axes or the root's region halved in
   longitude and latitude (and height) `level` times, the (x, y[, z]) cell taken; its geometric error is the
   root's over 2^level; `refine`, the transform and the content templates are the root's; it has no explicit
   children; a child exists when the subtree says so and `level + 1` is below `availableLevels`. Contents are
   the root's uris with `{level}`, `{x}`, `{y}` and `{z}` substituted (`template`), each present when the
   subtree's content availability of that index says so.
5. **Subtrees.** `subtree_read`, `subtree_write`: the subtree holding a coordinate has the root at the
   greatest multiple of `subtreeLevels` at or above it (`subtree_root((5, 13, 6), 3)` = (3, 3, 1)), its file is
   `subtrees.uri` with the root's coordinate substituted, and the coordinate is local to it (`local_coord`).
   A subtree file is the magic `subt`, version 1, the JSON and binary chunk lengths as uint64, the JSON padded
   with spaces and the binary with zeros to 8 bytes. `tileAvailability`, each `contentAvailability[k]` and
   `childSubtreeAvailability` is `{"constant": 0 | 1}` or a `bitstream` naming a buffer view of the internal
   buffer (external buffers: open issue 5), with an `availableCount` the reference writes and does not trust.
   Bits are packed least significant first (bit i is bit i mod 8 of byte ⌊i / 8⌋); a tile's bit is
   `level_offset(level) + morton(x, y[, z])` with `level_offset` = (4^level − 1) / 3 for a quadtree and
   (8^level − 1) / 7 for an octree; a child subtree's bit is the Morton index of its coordinate at level
   `subtreeLevels` (4^subtreeLevels or 8^subtreeLevels bits). The `SparseImplicitQuadtree` sample's root
   subtree packs its seven tiles as the bytes **`0d 32 01`**, and the reference reproduces that file at 352
   bytes (numeric table). A content available on an unavailable tile is an error.
6. **Content geometry.** `glb_read`, `b3dm_glb`, `content_meshes`: a binary glTF's triangle primitives (mode
   4, dense accessors on the internal buffer) are reached from the default scene (or every root node when there
   is none), each vertex transformed by the node hierarchy (matrix, or translation · rotation · scale), then by
   the **y-up** to z-up rotation about x by +90° ((x, y, z) → (x, −z, y)), then by the tile's composed matrix
   into ECEF; a b3dm's 28-byte header (`b3dm`, version 1, byte length, the feature and batch table lengths,
   little-endian uint32), its feature table's `RTC_CENTER` added to every position after the rotation and
   before the tile matrix, and its glb at the 8-byte boundary after the tables. Other primitive modes,
   materials, textures and normals are ignored (open issue 4); a non-tree node hierarchy, a non-VEC3
   `POSITION`, an index count not a multiple of 3 or an index outside the positions is an error.
7. **Screen-space error.** `sse_orthographic`, `sse_perspective`, `pixels_per_metre`, `focal_pixels`: for a
   2D map view the error of a tile is geometricError · s(φ) with s(φ) = 256 · 2^zoom / (2π R cos φ) the Web
   Mercator pixels per metre at the latitude φ of the tile's volume centre (SREP 9's tile size and R =
   6378137 m); for a perspective camera it is geometricError · s₀ · e · f / d with s₀ the pixels per metre at
   the map's centre, e SREP 10's exaggeration, f = (W / 2) / tan(fov / 2) the focal length in pixels (the
   schema's `fov` is horizontal) and d the distance from the camera to the tile's axis-aligned bounding box in
   the ground frame (infinite error inside the box). This is OGC 22-025r4's rule (section 6.1: an error in
   pixels compared with a maximum) with the ground frame's scale made explicit.
8. **Traversal.** `select`: depth first from the root, children in declared (or Morton) order, a stack. For a
   visited tile: its volume's eight corners (a region's from its geodetic extent, a sphere's bounding cube) are
   placed in the ground frame (rule 10) and their axis-aligned box tested against the view (`visible`: in a map
   view the box overlaps the W × H window; in a perspective view not every corner lies in front of one of the
   near, left, right, top or bottom planes' outsides); a tile outside the view is skipped with its subtree.
   With a `viewerRequestVolume`, the viewer (the camera position, or the window's centre at height 0 for a 2D
   map) MUST lie in that volume's box, else the tile is skipped with its subtree. The tile is **refined**
   when it has children, lies above level 32 and either its content is an external tileset (rule 3) or its
   screen-space error exceeds `maxError`; a refined `REPLACE` tile contributes no content and a refined `ADD`
   tile contributes its contents before its children; an unrefined tile contributes its contents. At most
   `maxTiles` tiles are visited per view (W-TL1 and stop). The result lists each content's uri, kind, depth,
   error and placement, in traversal order; `.json` contents are never listed. Frame k's selection is a pure
   function of the document, the archive and k.
9. **Resolve step and archive.** `resolve`, `archive`: before rendering, the resolve step (SREP 9) walks the
   tileset from `src` for the map view of every frame (and every camera that looks at the child, open issue
   3), fetching tileset, subtree and content files as the traversal asks for them, and writes the archive: a
   ZIP file in which the root `tileset.json`, every external tileset and subtree loaded and every `glb` and
   `b3dm` selected are **stored** uncompressed at their uris relative to the root, in sorted order, each
   with the timestamp 1980-01-01 00:00:00 and mode 0600, so the same input gives the same bytes and the same
   `cacheSha256`. A selected content that cannot be fetched fails the resolve step; unsupported contents are
   not fetched (W-TL2). **Rendering MUST NOT access the network**: the renderer reads the archive alone, refuses
   one whose SHA-256 differs from `cacheSha256` (TL1), and warns on a selected content it lacks (W-TL3).
10. **Placement.** `MapPlacement`: SREP 10's ground frame, x east and y south in map pixels, z into the
    ground. An ECEF point is converted to geodetic longitude, latitude and height (WGS 84: a = 6378137 m,
    f = 1 / 298.257223563), the longitude and latitude to the Web Mercator pixel (px, py) of the map's window
    centred on (`centerLon`, `centerLat`) at the frame's `zoom`, and lands at (px − W / 2, py − H / 2,
    −(h + `heightOffset`) · s₀ · e), with s₀ the pixels per metre at the window's centre and e the
    exaggeration (1 on a 2D map). A tile's volume corners, request volume corners and content vertices all
    take this path, so a tile and its mesh agree.
11. **Painting on a 2D map.** `raster_top`, `shade`: a 2D map draws the selected contents from above. Each
    triangle's vertices are placed by rule 10; a face whose normal points down (its (b − a) × (c − a) has
    non-negative z) is skipped; a pixel is covered when its centre lies in the triangle, and the surface with
    the greatest height wins; the pixel takes the material colour (`color`, or the default) times the shade
    0.4 + 0.6 · max(0, n · l) with l = (−1, −1, −2) / √6 (a light from the north-west, above), times `opacity`,
    alpha `opacity`. The child paints where it sits in the map's child order; SREP 10's `map` object in 3D
    draws the same meshes with its material and lights (open issue 4).
12. **Seek check.** `run`, `trace`: a frame reads nothing from a previous frame; `run(doc, k)` reports the
    frame's zoom, the selected uris, the contents drawn, the covered pixels, the tallest height and the
    buffer's digest, and `trace(doc, k)[j]` equals `run(doc, j)`'s digest (SREP 14).

### Defaults

`maxError` 16 px and `maxTiles` 4096 (CesiumJS's `maximumScreenSpaceError` 16; the tile bound is the draft's);
`heightOffset` 0, `opacity` 1, `color` absent (linear (0.86, 0.85, 0.83), SREP 10's buildings); a depth limit
of 32 levels and a hard `maxTiles` ceiling of 1 000 000 (TL2).

## Rationale

- **One pinned archive.** A tileset is thousands of files behind a server; a composition needs the few hundred
  its frames select. The resolve step (SREP 9's mechanism) collects exactly those, the archive's bytes are a
  function of the inputs, and `cacheSha256` makes the render reproducible on any machine with no network.
- **The camera alone chooses tiles.** CesiumJS refines by screen-space error too, but with a frame budget,
  a loading queue and a skip-level heuristic that depend on time. The draft keeps the error rule and drops the
  rest, so two engines select the same tiles for the same frame, and the seek check holds.
- **The 2D map's error from pixels per metre.** A 2D map has no camera distance; its screen-space error is the
  geometric error in map pixels at the tile's latitude, which is what a Mercator map shows.
- **Always descend into external tilesets.** The referring tile's error is often zero or arbitrary; CesiumJS
  treats the reference as transparent and so does the draft. The external root's own error then applies.
- **Stored ZIP, sorted, 1980 timestamps.** Every language has a ZIP reader; glb and b3dm are already compact
  (their textures are PNG or JPEG), and stored entries keep the archive seekable and its bytes predictable.
- **y-up to z-up after the node hierarchy.** The 1.1 specification's order (glTF node transforms, then the
  rotation, then the tile transform) is what makes a b3dm's `RTC_CENTER` and a glb's node matrix in ECEF
  terms work; the reference's `_placed_glb` shows the matrix a y-up node needs for an ECEF frame.
- **Top-down painting for the 2D map, SREP 10's material in 3D.** The 2D map is a drape; a flat shade from
  the north-west is enough to read roofs, and the full 3D case belongs to the `map` object's lights.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Streaming tiles at render time.** Rejected: the picture would depend on the network, and SREP 14's seek
  check could not hold; the resolve step makes the archive once.
- **Cesium ion or Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles.** Rejected by SREP 12 (M5 excludes proprietary tile
  services); the draft reads any 1.0 or 1.1 tileset from a file or an open server.
- **Implementing CesiumJS's selection (skip levels, priorities, a time budget).** Rejected: the budgets are
  time-dependent heuristics; `maxTiles` is the only budget and it is deterministic.
- **Drawing i3dm, pnts and cmpt.** Rejected for M5: 1.1 deprecates them in favour of glTF with instancing and
  point extensions; they are warned, not drawn (open issue 4).
- **A per-frame archive.** Rejected: one archive per asset keeps `cacheSha256` one attribute and lets frames
  share tiles.
- **Compressed archive entries.** Rejected: deflate output differs between implementations and levels, which
  would make `cacheSha256` tool-dependent.

## Backwards compatibility

Additive. No existing element changes. Schema version 1.8 (V17). SREP 10's `map` object draws a map with a
`tiles3d` child as it draws one with buildings; a renderer before this draft ignores the child under V17.

Properties that hold for every input: a frame's selection and picture are pure functions of the document, the
archive and the frame index; `geodetic(ecef(p))` returns p to 1e-6 m; a subtree written and read back gives the
same availability; a glb written and read back gives the same positions rotated to z-up; the selection never
lists a `.json` content, never visits more than `maxTiles` tiles and never descends below level 32; the
archive's bytes depend on its files' names and bytes alone; a tampered archive is refused (TL1); a removed
content warns (W-TL3) and the rest is drawn.

### Kit cases

To be measured on the engine (SREP 14's seek check applies to each):

1. `tiles3d-samples-load`: the CesiumGS 3d-tiles-samples `TilesetWithDiscreteLOD`, `TilesetWithRequestVolume`,
   `TilesetWithTreeBillboards`, 1.1 `MultipleContents`, `SparseImplicitQuadtree` and `SparseImplicitOctree`
   parse without TL4 (test input only; their repository states no licence, open issue 2).
2. `tiles3d-samples-select`: for the draft's fixed cameras, the engine's selection over each sample equals the
   reference's list of uris in order, including the request-volume sample's city tiles appearing only when the
   viewer is inside the volume.
3. `tiles3d-implicit`: the `SparseImplicitQuadtree` root subtree reads back the tile bits `0d 32 01` and the
   selection at `CAMERA(-10)` over `IMPLICIT` is the table's five coordinates.
4. `tiles3d-frame-48`: the example at frame 48; the buffer equals the reference's pixel for pixel (1/255),
   eight contents selected, W-TL2 reported, the tallest building 40 m.
5. `tiles3d-seek`: frames 0, 4, 24 and 48 rendered alone equal the played frames pixel for pixel.
6. `tiles3d-archive`: the resolve of the example gives an archive whose SHA-256 equals the reference's; the
   archive with one bit flipped is refused with TL1 and the archive without `b3.b3dm` renders frame 0 with
   W-TL3 and three buildings.
7. `tiles3d-errors`: the bad document; the engine reports TL1–TL4 and V17 and renders nothing for the invalid
   child.

## Open issues

1. The City of Helsinki states CC BY 4.0 for its 3D city model and serves it in a Cesium viewer; whether the
   3D Tiles form of the model is itself published under those terms (and at which URL) is to be confirmed from
   the city's data pages before a kit case fetches it.
2. The CesiumGS 3d-tiles-samples repository carries no licence file (checked 2026-10-24 and 2026-10-25); the
   samples stay test input the kit downloads and never redistributes until their terms are stated. The
   draft's own fixtures (the example tileset, `IMPLICIT`, `LOD`, `ADDITIVE`) are synthetic.
3. Which cameras the resolve step traverses for a `map` object in 3D (SREP 10): the draft states the map
   view of every frame; the 3D case needs every camera that sees the object at every frame, or an author-stated
   bound.
4. `.gltf` with external buffers, `i3dm`, `pnts` and `cmpt` contents, glTF materials, textures, normals and
   `EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing`, and the `map` object's lit rendering of the meshes.
5. 3D Tiles metadata (`3DTILES_metadata`, property tables), external subtree buffers, `extensionsRequired`
   (the draft ignores extensions; a required one it cannot honour should be TL4) and S2 bounding volumes.
6. The distance in rule 7 is to the tile's axis-aligned box in the ground frame; a geodesic or ECEF distance
   would differ for tiles far from the map centre at low zooms.
