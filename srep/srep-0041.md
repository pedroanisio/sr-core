# SREP 41 — Add a shadow catcher to 3D objects

## Abstract

An `object3D` gains the boolean attribute `shadowCatcher`. A surface marked as a catcher draws no colour of its own: it
darkens whatever is behind the 3D layer by exactly the share of light that the shadow casters in the scene take away at
that point, and is transparent where it is fully lit. A flat catcher under a 3D character therefore grounds the
character on a photograph, a video plate or a 2D scene below the layer. `opacity` scales the darkening. A document that
sets nothing renders as before.

## Motivation

The NOLN music video places 3D cut-outs and a 3D rig on 2D plates. Without a ground that receives shadows the figures
float; with an ordinary plane the plane covers the plate with its own colour. The only ways to get the shadow with the
baseline are to paint a fake shadow by hand in 2D (it does not follow the animation or the light) or to render a ground
in a matching colour (it never matches the plate). The baseline has `castShadow` and `receiveShadow` on `object3D`, shadow
maps for lights, and a 3D layer composited with alpha over the nodes below, but no way to keep the shadow and drop the
surface. Measured on the Rust reference before the change (renderer-level test, a sphere and a ground lit by a shadow-casting
sun and ambient light): the ground drawn as an ordinary plane has alpha 1.0 everywhere, including a lit pixel of colour
(0.656, 0.656, 0.656), so nothing of the plate shows through; with the catcher flag set but ignored the shadow pixel
also has alpha 1.0, where the ratio of the lights gives 0.396.

## Specification

### Syntax

```xml
<!-- object3DType gains -->
<xs:attribute name="shadowCatcher" type="xs:boolean" default="false">
  <xs:annotation><xs:documentation>The surface draws no colour of its own. It is transparent where no light is
  blocked and black, with alpha up to @opacity, where the shadow casters block the light that would reach it, so
  that it darkens what is behind the 3D layer by the share of light that is taken away (ambient light counted). It
  does not cast shadows.
  </xs:documentation></xs:annotation>
</xs:attribute>
```

```xml
None.
```

New attribute with a neutral default, accepted in every version (SREP 0, Versioning).

### Semantics

**1. Applicability.** `shadowCatcher` applies to an `object3D` of any `primitive` that draws surfaces. On a
`particles3D`, `ocean`, `volume`-type object or other non-surface object it has no effect and a validator SHOULD report
it as an inert attribute (SREP 18).

**2. Quantities.** For one point P of a catcher surface seen by the camera, let the scene's lights be indexed by `i`.
Let `Lᵢ` be the linear RGB radiance that light `i` contributes at P with its occlusion ignored (the same value the
shading would add without shadows, including the surface's own diffuse and specular response), and `vᵢ ∈ [0,1]` the
visibility of light `i` from P after shadow maps, shadow softness, and contact shadows (1 for lights that cast no
shadow). Let `A` be the radiance from ambient and image-based lighting at P after ambient occlusion. Let `Y(c) = 0.2126 R
+ 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B` of a linear colour. Then

```
E_un = Y(A) + Σᵢ Y(Lᵢ)              (all light, no shadows)
E_sh = Y(A) + Σᵢ vᵢ · Y(Lᵢ)         (light that arrives)
```

**3. Output.** The catcher fragment's colour is black and its alpha is

```
α = opacity · (1 − E_sh / E_un)   if E_un > 0,   otherwise 0
```

where `opacity` is the object's opacity (animated values as at the frame). The fragment is composited like any other
partly transparent surface of the 3D layer: premultiplied colour `(0,0,0)` and alpha α, depth tested against the rest of
the layer, then the layer is composited over what is behind it as usual. Nothing of the surface's material colour, maps,
emission or reflection appears.

**4. What it does to the plate.** For a point behind the layer with colour `C`, `C′ = C · (1 − α) + 0 · α =
C · (1 − opacity · (1 − E_sh/E_un))`. With `opacity` 1, `C′ = C · E_sh/E_un`: the plate is multiplied by the ratio of
the light that arrives to the light that would arrive without casters, which is how much darker that point of a real
surface lit by the same lights would be.

**5. Casting and receiving.** A catcher does not cast shadows (it is not drawn into any shadow map). It receives them
whatever `receiveShadow` says: `receiveShadow="false"` on a catcher has no effect. A catcher is not a shadow caster for
other catchers.

**6. Lights without shadows.** Lights with `castShadow="false"` have `vᵢ = 1`. A scene whose lights cast no shadow gives
α = 0 on every catcher.

**7. Other renderers.** A renderer that cannot evaluate `E_sh/E_un` for a surface (for example a path tracer that does
not separate shadows) MUST report that the catcher is not applied and MUST NOT draw the catcher's material instead.

### Defaults and the neutral case

`shadowCatcher` absent or `false`: every object renders as before.

## Rationale

- **Ratio of lights, not "the shadow".** A shadow is not a colour but a multiplication of the light. The identity in
  Semantics 4 holds for any plate, and is the first-principles result: a diffuse surface lit by `E_un` shows `ρ · E_un`;
  with casters it shows `ρ · E_sh`; the ratio does not depend on `ρ`, so the same black-with-alpha catcher is correct for
  any plate.
- **Black with alpha** composites in the existing 3D layer unchanged and works over any backdrop (stills, video, 2D
  scenes), which a multiply blend mode on a separate node would not (the 3D layer's blend is per layer, not per
  surface).
- **Ambient light counted.** Without `A`, a shadow would always be fully black; real shadows keep the ambient fill.
- **`opacity` as strength.** The attribute already exists on `object3D` and means "how much of the object shows"; here
  that is how much shadow shows. No second attribute is needed.
- **Precedent.** Several 3D tools offer a "shadow catcher" surface for compositing; this draft's definition is
  derived above and not copied from any of them (their documentation was not read for this draft).

## Rejected alternatives

- **A separate `<shadowCatcher>` element.** An object already has position, scale and extent; the property belongs on it.
- **A shadow colour or a separate strength attribute.** `opacity` covers strength; coloured shadows are an effect on the
  layer below.
- **Rendering the shadow as a second layer in multiply mode.** The layer-level blend cannot differ per surface and
  would darken every transparent pixel of the layer.
- **Counting only direct light in the ratio.** Gives black shadows under ambient light.

## Backwards compatibility

Class: Added, MINOR in effect, accepted in every version. No valid document changes validity or rendering.

## Open issues

- How to evaluate the ratio in a path tracer (shadow rays with and without occluders, or a denoised ratio).
- Whether a catcher should also receive reflections of casters (a mirror-floor look) as a separate option.
