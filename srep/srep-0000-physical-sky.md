# SREP 0 — Add the physical sun, moon and sky to dome lights

## Abstract

A third value of the dome light's `sky`, `physical`, for the space and daylight shots of SREP 12's milestone 6
(A1): the sun is placed from a `date` and the observer's `latitude`, `longitude`, `altitude`, `pressure`,
`temperature` and `deltaT` by the Solar Position Algorithm of Reda and Andreas, written from the report's
equations and tables; the moon is placed by the same observer from a low-precision lunar theory; and the sky
is the atmosphere of Bruneton and Neyret as parameterised by Hillaire, with Rayleigh, Mie and ozone layers,
transmittance, single and multiple scattering, the sun and moon discs, aerial perspective on surfaces and a
height fog. Every value is a pure function of the frame's `date` and the document's constants, so the
seek check (SREP 14) holds by construction. The reference module `conformance/tools/physical_sky_reference.py`
states each rule as code and prints the numeric table of the Conformance section.

## Motivation

- **The catalogue has the shot.** SREP 12 line 271 lists A1 as "solar position by NREL SPA (Reda and
  Andreas 2004), implemented from the paper ...; atmosphere after Hillaire 2020 and Bruneton and Neyret 2008
  with multiple scattering, through the media model (F3); aerial perspective; height fog", and shot 36 (line
  624, Sebastian Lague's camera sweep over a planet's limb at sunset) is scored a gap: "the dome sky is a
  ground-level gradient and the planet has no scattering shell". SREP 71's `sky="gradient"` has three colours
  and a sun placed by `sunAzimuth` and `sunElevation` by hand (xsd 5962–5991); it has no date, no observer,
  no moon, no atmosphere and no fog.
- **An author places the sun by hand today.** A shot at a real place and time needs the sun's azimuth and
  elevation looked up elsewhere and typed in, and a time-lapse needs them keyframed; a sunset's colour is
  painted into `skyHorizon`; haze over distance is a fog material on every far object; the moon is a sprite.
- **The constructions have published checks.** The report of Reda and Andreas prints every intermediate of
  one example (2003-10-17 12:30:30, UTC−7, Golden, Colorado): topocentric zenith 50.11162°, azimuth
  194.34024°, incidence 25.18700° on a tilted surface. Meeus prints the moon of example 47.a. The
  atmosphere's transmittance has a closed form when the ozone layer is removed, the phase functions
  integrate to one, and the sky delivers less than the sun's own irradiance. These are the numeric tests
  below.

## Specification

### Syntax

A diff for schema 1.8 against `schema/scene-render.xsd` at 1.7.1 (line numbers of that file); the schema
files are not changed by this draft.

- `sky` on `lightType` (5962, read for dome lights) gains the value `physical` beside `none` and `gradient`.
- `sunIntensity` on `lightType` (5990) loses its XSD `default="0"`; its documentation states the defaults
  instead: an absent value reads as 0 under `none` and `gradient` (SREP 71's sunless sky, which renderers
  already apply as their fallback) and as 1 under `physical`. A schema default is filled before any
  consumer of the validated infoset sees the document, so with it absence and an explicit 0 are the same
  value and a physical sky without the attribute would be lit by a sun of 0.
- New dome light attributes, read when `sky="physical"` (PSKY1: each is ignored, with a warning, under the
  other two values):
  - `date` (`xs:string`, required with `physical`, animatable through the fields of SREP 4's values: an ISO
    8601 date and time with seconds and either `Z` or a `+hh:mm` offset, for example
    `2003-10-17T12:30:30-07:00`; the year is −2000 to 6000, PSKY2), `latitude` (`xs:double`, degrees north,
    −90 to 90, default 0), `longitude` (`xs:double`, degrees east, −180 to 180, default 0), `altitude`
    (`xs:double`, metres above the ellipsoid, default 0), `pressure` (`nonNegativeDecimal`, hPa, default
    1013.25), `temperature` (`xs:double`, °C, above −273, default 10), `deltaT` (`xs:double`, seconds of
    TT − UT, −8000 to 8000; default 69 for dates in 1900 to 2100 and required outside them, PSKY2).
  - `moon` (`xs:boolean`, default true: the moon's disc and its light are drawn), `moonIntensity`
    (`nonNegativeDecimal`, default 1: a multiplier on the moon's physical radiance).
  - `atmosphere` (one of `earth`, `none`; default `earth`), `aerosol` (`nonNegativeDecimal`, default 1: a
    multiplier on the Mie layer), `ozone` (`nonNegativeDecimal`, default 1: a multiplier on the ozone
    layer), `planetRadius` (`positiveDecimal`, metres, default 6360000), `atmosphereHeight`
    (`positiveDecimal`, metres, default 100000), `groundAlbedo` (`xs:double`, 0 to 1, default 0.3),
    `sunIrradiance` (`colorType`, default white; the sun's irradiance at the top of the atmosphere in the
    scene's linear units). The sun's effective irradiance is `sunIrradiance` times SREP 71's `sunIntensity`,
    which under `physical` reads as 1 when absent (`effective_sun_irradiance`; the schema carries no default
    for it, see Syntax): SREP 71's default 0 removes
    the gradient sky's decorative sun, but a physical sky is lit by the sun (rule 9) and a date never asks for
    a black one, and `colorType` channels are in [0, 1], so `sunIntensity` is the document's only scalar
    above white. An explicit `sunIntensity="0"` under `physical` removes the sun's light and disc (a black sky
    and a dark moon); `sunSize="0"` removes the disc alone, as SREP 71 says.
  - `aerialPerspective` (`xs:boolean`, default true: surfaces are seen through the atmosphere by rule 10),
    `fogDensity` (`nonNegativeDecimal`, per metre at sea level, default 0), `fogHeight` (`positiveDecimal`,
    metres, the fog's scale height, default 100).
- Warning (a native check, not a Schematron assert, as the star-fields draft's SF4):
  - **PSKY1** `date`, `latitude`, `longitude`, `altitude`, `pressure`, `temperature`, `deltaT`, `moon`,
    `moonIntensity`, `atmosphere`, `aerosol`, `ozone`, `planetRadius`, `atmosphereHeight`, `groundAlbedo`,
    `sunIrradiance`, `aerialPerspective`, `fogDensity` and `fogHeight` are read only with `sky="physical"`;
    written under `none` or `gradient`, each is ignored with a warning.
- Schematron (pattern `p-srep-physical-sky`; the ids are prefixed because SREP 71's pattern already owns
  `SKY1` and `SKY2`, and assert ids are unique across the schema):
  - **PSKY2** `date` parses as rule 1 says; its year is −2000 to 6000; `deltaT` is present when the year is
    outside 1900 to 2100.
  - **PSKY3** `latitude` is −90 to 90, `longitude` −180 to 180, `pressure` at least 0, `temperature` above
    −273, `deltaT` −8000 to 8000, `groundAlbedo` 0 to 1, `atmosphereHeight` and `fogHeight` positive.
  - **PSKY4** `sunAzimuth` and `sunElevation` are not written with `sky="physical"`: the sun comes from the
    date.
  - **V17** `sky="physical"` needs `scene/@version="1.8"` or later.

### Semantics

The reference module names the function of each rule. Angles are degrees unless a rule says radians.

1. **Date.** `parse_datetime` splits `date` into year, month, day, hour, minute, second and the offset in
   hours; `julian_day` is the report's equation 4 with the Gregorian correction for dates after
   1582-10-15; `time_scales` adds `deltaT` for the ephemeris day and forms the Julian century and
   millennium (report 3.1). The example's Julian day is 2452930.312847.
2. **Earth.** `heliocentric_longitude`, `heliocentric_latitude` and `earth_radius_vector` sum the report's
   periodic terms (its Table A4.2) in the ephemeris millennium (report 3.2); the geocentric longitude is the
   heliocentric plus 180° and the latitude its negative (3.3).
3. **Nutation and obliquity.** `nutation` sums the 63 terms of the report's Table A4.3 for the nutation in
   longitude and in obliquity (3.4); `true_obliquity` is the mean obliquity polynomial plus the nutation in
   obliquity (3.5); the aberration is −20.4898″ over the radius vector (3.6); the apparent longitude is the
   geocentric longitude plus the nutation in longitude plus the aberration (3.7).
4. **Equatorial and topocentric sun.** `sidereal_time` is the apparent sidereal time at Greenwich (3.8);
   right ascension and declination follow from the apparent longitude, latitude and obliquity (3.9, 3.10);
   the local hour angle is the sidereal time plus `longitude` minus the right ascension (3.11);
   `_topocentric` shifts right ascension, declination and hour angle for the observer's parallax with the
   flattened Earth of `latitude` and `altitude` and the sun's equatorial horizontal parallax 8.794″ over
   the radius vector (3.12).
5. **Zenith, azimuth, refraction.** The topocentric elevation follows from the topocentric declination and
   hour angle; `refraction_correction` adds the report's refraction (3.14.1) scaled by `pressure` over 1010
   hPa and 283 K over the air temperature, applied while the sun's elevation is above −(0.26667° + 0.5667°)
   and zero below it or at zero pressure; the zenith is 90° minus the corrected elevation; the azimuth is
   measured from north eastward (3.15); `sun_position` returns every intermediate. For the report's example
   the module gives zenith 50.11162°, azimuth 194.34024° and incidence 25.18700° on its 30° surface, each
   within 0.001° of the printed values, and every printed intermediate to its printed digits except the
   topocentric hour angle, whose printed 11.10629 differs from the report's own printed H − Δα by 2 × 10⁻⁵.
6. **The sun in the scene.** `sun_direction` turns zenith z and azimuth a into the unit vector
   (sin z sin a, −cos z, sin z cos a) in scene axes, the frame of SREP 71 (up is −y, azimuth 0 is +z,
   azimuth 90° is +x); `sun_from_document` applies PSKY2 and PSKY3 and returns the direction. SREP 71's
   `sunSize` keeps its meaning and `sunColor` tints the disc alone, not the sky or the lighting (the light's
   colour is `sunIrradiance`); the disc's radiance is `sun_disc_radiance`: `sunColor` times rule 9's
   `sun_radiance` (the effective irradiance `sunIrradiance` × `sunIntensity`, scaled once, through the
   transmittance) over the disc's solid angle `disc_solid_angle(sunSize)` = 2π(1 − cos(`sunSize`/2)), so
   the disc's power over its solid angle is the transmitted irradiance whatever `sunSize` says, and
   `sunSize="0"` draws no disc.
7. **The moon.** `moon_position` is the low-precision lunar theory of Montenbruck and Pfleger (geocentric
   ecliptic longitude and latitude of the date, better than 0.1° over the 20th and 21st centuries) with the
   Almanac's four-term horizontal parallax for the distance; `moon_from_document` adds the nutation in
   longitude, converts with rule 4's obliquity and sidereal time, applies the topocentric shift with the
   moon's own parallax, and returns zenith, azimuth, the angular diameter from a 1737.4 km radius and the
   illuminated fraction from the phase angle (Meeus 48.2 and 48.3). The moon's disc is drawn uniform at
   `moon_radiance`: `moonIntensity` times the geometric albedo `MOON_ALBEDO` 0.12 times the Lambert sphere's
   phase law `lambert_phase` at the phase angle, (sin α + (π − α) cos α)/π, times the sun's effective
   irradiance over π, through rule 9's transmittance along the moon's direction (zero below the horizon);
   the full moon is 0.12/π of the transmitted irradiance, quadrature 1/π of that, the new moon nothing. The
   disc's shape is not phased (open issue 6). With `moon="false"` nothing is drawn.
8. **The atmosphere.** `atmosphere()` is Hillaire's Earth: planet radius 6360 km, top 100 km above it,
   Rayleigh scattering (5.802, 13.558, 33.1) × 10⁻⁶ m⁻¹ with scale height 8 km, Mie scattering 3.996 × 10⁻⁶
   and absorption 4.4 × 10⁻⁶ m⁻¹ with scale height 1.2 km and Henyey–Greenstein anisotropy 0.8, ozone
   absorption (0.650, 1.881, 0.085) × 10⁻⁶ m⁻¹ in a tent of half-width 15 km about 25 km. `aerosol` and
   `ozone` scale their layers; `atmosphere="none"` removes all three (the sky is black, the sun's
   transmittance one). Density profiles are `rayleigh_density`, `mie_density` and `ozone_density`, zero
   above the top.
9. **Transmittance and radiance.** `transmittance(a, p, d)` is exp of minus the optical depth along the ray
   to the top of the atmosphere, by composite four-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature over segments
   clustered toward the observer; zero when the ray meets the ground. `sky_radiance(a, obs, d, sun)` is the
   single-scattering integral (Rayleigh phase 3(1 + μ²)/16π, Henyey–Greenstein for Mie) plus the
   multiple-scattering term: Hillaire's isotropic estimate, `_ms_cell`, the second-order radiance over one
   minus the fraction scattered again, both integrated over a Fibonacci sphere of directions and including
   a Lambertian ground of `groundAlbedo`, tabulated over height and sun cosine and interpolated. The sky's
   irradiance on the ground is below the sun's irradiance (`energy_test`). `sun_radiance` is the sun's
   irradiance through the transmittance.
10. **Aerial perspective.** With `aerialPerspective="true"`, a surface point at distance s along d with
    outgoing colour c is seen as c times the transmittance over s plus the in-scattered radiance over s
    (`aerial_perspective`); at s = 0 it is c exactly and as s grows it tends to the sky's radiance in d.
    From above the atmosphere the planet's limb and its scattering shell follow from the same integrals
    (`limb_direction` gives the grazing direction; `sky_radiance` from orbit is zero away from the planet).
    Lengths are scene pixels and a metre is `physics/@pixelsPerMeter` pixels (SREP 14 rule 4; the
    attribute's default 100 when the scene has no `physics` element): the scene's origin is rule 9's observer
    at `altitude`, scene up (−y) is the atmosphere's +z and the scene's x and z span the tangent plane, so a
    scene point p is at `atmosphere_position(a, p, altitude, pixelsPerMeter)` = (x, z, R + `altitude` − y) /
    `pixelsPerMeter` (the planet's radius R added to the third), the camera included, and s is the segment's
    length in pixels over `pixelsPerMeter`. Rule 10 walks the straight ray through the real sphere between
    the two mapped points; the drop of the sphere below the tangent plane is not applied to the points
    themselves (196 m at 50 km).
11. **Height fog.** With `fogDensity` > 0, a fog of extinction σ_f(h) = `fogDensity` × e^(−h / `fogHeight`)
    joins rule 10's march over every surface segment: at each sample the extinction is the air's plus σ_f(h),
    h the sphere's altitude there, and the source is the air's in-scatter plus σ_f(h) × L_h, both attenuated
    by the combined transmittance to the eye (`aerial_perspective` with `fog` and `horizon`; one march, so
    no order of the two media arises). The fog's source is isotropic and its albedo 1; L_h is rule 9's sky
    radiance at elevation 0 in the view direction's azimuth at the observer (`horizon_radiance`; a vertical
    ray takes azimuth +x), so fog at sunset is warm toward the sun. The media draft's `fog` phase is for
    declared media, not this fog. With `aerialPerspective="false"`, or `atmosphere="none"` (L_h is then 0),
    a segment is the closed form c × T_f + (1 − T_f) × L_h (`fogged`), T_f =
    `fog_transmittance(fog, h0, h1, length)` = e^(−density × length × H / (h1 − h0) × (e^(−h0/H) −
    e^(−h1/H))), and e^(−density × length × e^(−h0/H)) when h0 = h1, with the segment ends' altitudes
    `scene_altitude(y, altitude, pixelsPerMeter)` = `altitude` − y / `pixelsPerMeter` (rule 10's scale and
    origin; the tangent-plane altitude, 8 cm off the sphere's at 1 km) and the length in metres by the same
    scale. In an atmosphere without air the march reproduces the closed form within 2 × 10⁻⁴ at 1 km, the
    sphere's curvature against the tangent plane. The fog is not applied to the sky itself, only to surface
    segments.
12. **Frames and seek.** The dome light at a frame is evaluated from that frame's `date` (after SREP 4's
    animation of the string) and the document's constants, with no state carried between frames
    (`sky_frame`); the multiple-scattering table depends only on the atmosphere's parameters, so a frame
    rendered alone equals the frame reached by playing (`seek_test`).

## Rationale

- **The report, not NREL's code.** NREL's C implementation of the SPA is under a licence that forbids
  redistribution; the report itself is a public document with every equation and table. The module is
  written from the report and its tables were cross-checked against pvlib's BSD-3-Clause `spa.py`, which
  prints the same tables; pvlib's code is not used. The report's example pins every intermediate.
- **Why Hillaire's parameterisation.** Bruneton and Neyret's model is the reference physics; Hillaire's
  2020 version adds the ozone layer, the isotropic multiple-scattering estimate and a form that fits a
  real-time renderer (three small lookup tables). The reference keeps the integrals explicit and gives the
  engine's tables something to be compared with.
- **Why a date on the dome light and not a clock element.** SREP 71 placed the sun on the dome light; the
  date is its missing input. A scene clock is SP1's (SREP 12) and when it lands `date` can be fed from it
  through SREP 4's value fields without a change here.
- **Why the moon is low precision.** Montenbruck and Pfleger's series is a page long and good to 0.1°, five
  moon diameters below what a frame resolves; the full theory (ELP) is thousands of terms for no visible
  gain.
- **Why the refraction limit.** The report applies refraction while the sun's top limb is above the
  horizon; below that the correction would place an invisible sun above it. The same limit applies to the
  moon.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A scale of the draft's own for rules 10 and 11.** One scene unit per metre under `physical`, or a new
  `metresPerUnit` on the dome light, were both put forward by SR-86. SREP 14 rule 4 already maps physical
  lengths through `physics/@pixelsPerMeter`, the time-frames draft follows it, and two scales in one scene
  would disagree; the draft reuses it, with the scene's origin at rule 9's observer.
- **Applying the fog at one end of a surface segment.** Fog applied after rule 10's march (at the surface end)
  or before it (at the eye end) is a product of two transmittances with the fog's source either unattenuated
  by the air or attenuating all of the air's in-scatter; both are off by the fog's optical depth over the
  segment, and SR-86 asked which. One march with the combined extinction and source is the exact answer
  and costs nothing beyond a fog term per sample.
- **Keeping `sunIntensity`'s schema default.** A default of 1 for `physical` alone is not expressible (an
  XSD default is per attribute, not per sibling value), and reading an explicit 0 as 1 under `physical`
  would remove the only scalar that switches the sun's light off while keeping the atmosphere. The default
  goes, and the two readings are stated in the documentation (SR-86 found the contradiction: its typed
  model fills every schema default, and a physical sky without the attribute rendered black).
- **Hosek–Wilkie or Prague sky models.** Both are analytic fits for a ground observer under a clear sky
  with turbidity as the knob; neither gives the atmosphere from altitude or orbit (shot 36), the
  transmittance along a surface ray, or a moon. They remain comparison baselines (Open issue 2).
- **The sun's position by a low-precision formula** (Meeus' chapter 25, 0.01°): the report's algorithm is
  0.0003° and its example gives a hard test; the cost is a table of terms, not run time.
- **Spectral rendering of the sky.** The three-channel coefficients are Hillaire's; a spectral Rayleigh
  coefficient is the media draft's open issue 2 and would change the sky's colour by less than the
  display's quantisation.
- **A separate `atmosphere` element.** One planet, one observer, one dome light: the attributes belong to
  the light until a scene has two atmospheres.

## Backwards compatibility

Every document at 1.7.1 keeps its meaning: `sky` defaults to `none`, `gradient` is untouched, and the new
attributes are read only with `physical`. The value is refused below version 1.8 (V17). The one change to
an existing attribute is `sunIntensity` losing its schema default: a 1.7.1 document with `gradient` and no
`sunIntensity` keeps its sunless sky, since renderers fall back to 0 and `sr_core.scene`'s generated model
leaves an absent optional attribute `None`; a consumer that relied on the schema supplying the 0 applies it
itself.

## Open issues

1. **The sun's irradiance in scene units.** `sunIrradiance` defaults to white (1, 1, 1) so that the sky is
   in the scene's linear units; a physical default (the solar spectrum binned to RGB, about 1.5 × 10³ W m⁻²
   over the three bands) needs the exposure model of the camera, which the schema does not have.
2. **Comparison baselines.** The catalogue asks for the sky radiance against Bruneton's precomputed
   atmospheric scattering (BSD-3-Clause), Hosek–Wilkie (BSD-3-Clause) and Prague (Apache-2.0). None of
   them is a dependency of sr-core and none runs in the conformance kit; the comparison is an engine-side
   measurement for SR-86 (a ground observer, sun at 30°, turbidity 2 ↔ `aerosol="1"`), reported in the kit's
   evidence, not asserted here.
3. **The limb from orbit at low sun.** The multiple-scattering estimate is isotropic; at the limb with the
   sun below the planet's horizon the single-scattering term dominates and the estimate's error is not
   measured. Shot 36 is the measurement.
4. **Terrain shadows on the sky.** Rule 9's ground is a sphere of `groundAlbedo`; mountains that shadow the
   air (the "Earth shadow" band) are not modelled.
5. **Dates before 1582.** The Julian calendar switch is the report's; documents with proleptic Gregorian
   dates before 1582-10-15 would be off by the calendar difference. The schema could state which calendar
   `date` is in.
6. **The moon's disc.** Rule 7 draws a uniform disc whose radiance follows the Lambert phase law; the
   crescent's shape, the real moon's opposition surge and its darker quadrature (about a tenth of full, not
   a third) are not modelled.
