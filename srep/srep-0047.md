# SREP 47 — Pose and aim a joint of an imported model

## Abstract

An imported model's joints move only through its clips. A new child element of `object3D`,
`<joint name="head" rotation="12" lookAt="camera1" lookAxis="z" influence="0.8" maxAngle="35"/>`, poses one joint from the
document: rotation offsets (animatable) on top of the clip's pose, and an optional look-at that turns the joint so that
one of its axes points at another element, with a weight and an angle limit. An eye that follows a target, a head that
turns toward the camera and a jaw opened by an animated angle no longer need a baked clip. A document that uses no
`joint` element renders as before.

## Motivation

The NOLN face-model assessment (§C and §D, item 3, "per-joint pose override and look-at for imported joints"): jaw,
neck and eye joints cannot be driven from the document, and "eye gaze at a target is the single most alive cue; today this
needs a pre-baked clip". With the Rust reference an imported model's joints move only through `animationClip`
(and its blend, SREP 42).

## Specification

### Syntax

```xml

<xs:complexType name="jointType">
```

### Semantics

**1. Order.** The pose an object draws is, node by node: the clip's local transforms (blended as SREP 42 says), then
for each `joint` in document order its rotation offset, then the look-ats, those of joints nearer the model's root first
(equal depths in document order). The result replaces the clip's pose for skinning, `node` selection and the draw.

**2. Rotation offset.** Let `R = Rz(rotation) · Ry(rotationY) · Rx(rotationX)` (angles in degrees, positive as for an
object's own `rotationX`, `rotationY` and `rotation`). The joint's local rotation `q` becomes `q · R`: the offset is in the joint's
own axes. Several `joint` elements of one name compose in document order.

**3. Look-at.** Let `P` be the joint's position and `a` the unit `lookAxis` in the joint's frame, in the model's own space
at the pose of 1 up to this joint; let `f` be `a` turned by the joint's world rotation, and `T` the target's position in
the model's own space (the target's scene position taken through the inverse of the object's world matrix, the model's
axis conversion and the `node` selection). The turn `q*` is the shortest rotation taking `f` to `normalize(T − P)`
(none if `T = P` or `f` is already on it), `q' = slerp(identity, q*, influence)`, and, with `θ` its angle, the angle is
limited to `maxAngle`. The turn is applied in the world about the joint and expressed in the joint's local
rotation: `q_local' = Rp⁻¹ · q' · Rp · q_local`, with `Rp` the world rotation of the joint's parent (identity at a root),
so the joint's position and its parents' transforms do not change. The target is a node, light or camera: its position as
`look-at` constraints read it.

**4. Joint sockets.** An element attached to the joint (SREP 43) follows the rotation offsets. It does not
follow a look-at, which needs the 3D scene's positions that the evaluator does not have (Open issues).

**5. Errors.** A `name` that no node of the model has MUST be reported, listing the node names, and has no effect.

### Defaults and the neutral case

No `joint` element: the pose is the clip's, as before. A `joint` with no rotation and no `lookAt` changes nothing.

## Rationale

- **Rotation offsets on the clip, not replacement.** The joint keeps what the clip gives it (a walking head bob) and
  the document adds a turn; replacing it would need every joint of a limb.
- **Look-at with a weight and a limit** because eyes and heads must not turn past what a body can do, and a gaze blends
  in and out (`influence` animates the way the rest does).
- **In the world about the joint, expressed locally** so that a look-at of an eye does not tilt the head it is in.
- **A child element** like `morph` and `blob`: its attributes animate through the document's animation system.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A `lookAt` attribute on `object3D`.** One object has many joints.
- **Inverse kinematics.** A different feature: a chain of joints reaching a point, with a bend direction.
- **Replacing the clip's rotation.** See Rationale.

## Backwards compatibility

Class: Added element, MINOR, gated by version 1.2 (V5). No existing document is affected.

## Conformance

| Case | Checks | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|

| `srep-NNNN-joint-rotation-keyed` | the rotation keyed 0 to 90° over a second: half way is 45° | 1.5 px |
| `srep-NNNN-joint-look-at` | a joint whose x axis looks at a target straight above it turns a quarter turn, and its mesh with it | 2 px |
| `srep-NNNN-joint-look-at-limits` | `influence="0.5"` turns 45°; `maxAngle="30"` turns 30°; `influence="0"` turns nothing | 2 px |
| `srep-NNNN-joint-unknown` | an unknown joint name is reported with the model's node names; `joint` under version 1.1 fails V5 | exact |

## Open issues

- Making a socketed element follow a look-at: the evaluator would need the 3D positions of the scene's elements.
- Translation offsets, scale offsets and a pole (up) target for the look-at.
- Whether a look-at should apply to several joints at once (both eyes) from one element.
