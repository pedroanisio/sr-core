# SREP 0 — Add fields and flow

## Abstract

[SREP 12](srep-0012.md) names, as its MF5 building block, "one field element (analytic, grid, simulated;
vector, scalar, complex); arrow glyphs; evenly spaced streamlines (Jobard and Lefer 1997); line integral
convolution (Cabral and Leedom 1993); phase portraits (Wegert 2012); charge and current field lines", with the
bar "dipole lines r = C sin²θ; winding number of zeros and poles; streamline separation" (line 256). This SREP
is that block. A `field` node, placed in the data frame of an `axes` (the plots SREP, MF1) or with its own
ranges, is a vector, scalar or complex function of the plane given by expressions, a named complex function
(MF2), a grid of a data source, a `forceField` of the physics section, or charges, currents and dipoles. Its
children say how it is drawn: `arrows` on a grid, `streamlines` evenly spaced by the Jobard–Lefer queue,
`lic` of a seeded noise raster, a `portrait` by phase and modulus, `lines` launched from charges, and
`contours` by the marching squares of MF1. Every presentation is a pure function of the field and its
attributes with stated step and count bounds. Everything a 1.7.1 document can say renders as before.

## Motivation

- **The plan depends on it.** SREP 12 counts shots 2 (Poynting vectors around a wire loop) and 12 (a vector
  field with sources and curl points) under MF5, shot 25 with CL1, and S5 and M4 read it (lines 298, 341).
  The SRC-17 measurement (2026-10-24) scored 2 as a gap ("no field element draws E, B and Poynting vectors
  from a source current") and 12 as custom ("the field is a vortex preset and the arrows are hand-placed").
- **The format has forces, not fields.** `forceField` (xsd:5999) pushes bodies, particles and flocks
  (`crates/sr-eval/src/sim.rs`, `FieldSrc`, seven kinds in metres and +y up) and draws nothing. An author who
  wants to *show* a field today places arrows by hand or writes one `parametricPath` per streamline.
- **The algorithms are published and testable.** Evenly spaced streamlines, line integral convolution and
  phase portraits each have one accepted construction with closed-form checks: a uniform field's streamlines
  lie exactly d_sep apart; the LIC of a field along a row keeps that row's noise; the winding number of
  (z − 1)(z + 1)/z is 1 around radius 2 and −1 around radius ½; a point dipole's field lines keep r / sin²θ.
  Those are the bars SREP 12 states, and this draft turns them into table rows.
- **Bounds.** Streamline integration is a loop driven by the field; a closed orbit never leaves the box. Every
  loop here has a document-stated step and a schema bound (SREP 14; the fold bounds of the reactive-values
  draft are the same shape).

### Semantics

Let the **data frame** be the `axes` mapping of the plots draft (rule 1 there): data (x, y) with +y up to the
box's pixels. Every rule below is stated in data units; `separation`, `length`, `maxLength` and `step` are in
pixels and convert through the box's pixels-per-unit on each axis (the mean of the two when they differ).
Outside an `axes` the box is `width` × `height` pixels at the node's origin (FLD5). A stroked presentation
(`arrows`, `streamlines`, `lines`, `contours`) draws in its own `stroke` and `strokeWidth`, else the field's
(white, 2 px by default).

1. **Kinds.** A `vector` field is F(x, y) = (u, v); a `scalar` field is s(x, y); a `complex` field is
   w(z) = f(x + iy), drawn as the vector (Re w, Im w) by `arrows`, `streamlines` and `lic`, and as a portrait
   by `portrait`. A `scalar` field draws only `contours` and `lic` of its gradient. The source fits the kind
   (FLD1): a `vector` field comes from `u` and `v`, a `grid` of rows `x y u v`, a `forceField` or the source
   children; a `scalar` field from `u` alone or a `grid` of rows `x y s`; a `complex` field from `function`
   (with `coefficients` for `mobius`, as the space-transforms draft states) or `w`.
2. **Sources** (`grid_sampler`, `complex_sampler`, `charge_field`, `wire_field`, `dipole_field`). A `grid`
   is bilinear between its lattice nodes and (0, 0) outside its lattice box. A `forceField` is the physics
   field in pixel space of `crates/sr-eval/src/sim.rs` (`to_pixels`), divided by `pixelsPerMeter` and with
   +y flipped, at composition time. Charges, currents and dipoles sum (the constant of each law is 1):
   E = Σ q r / |r|³ (Coulomb, in the plane of the charges), B = i / (2π |r|) counterclockwise (a straight
   current out of the plane), D = (3 (m·r̂) r̂ − m) / |r|³ (a point dipole in the plane of its axis); the
   field is (0, 0) at a source. A `wire` from a to b carrying the charge q per unit length and the current i
   (`line_field`) adds to F the in-plane field of the finite line charge, E = q (φ n̂ + (1/√(s² + d²) −
   1/√((L − s)² + d²)) t̂) with t̂ the direction a → b, n̂ its left normal, s the position along the wire, d
   the signed distance from its line (positive on the left) and φ = ((L − s)/√((L − s)² + d²) +
   s/√(s² + d²)) / d (Griffiths example 2.1, constant 1); its magnetic field is out of the plane,
   B_z = i φ / (4π) (Biot–Savart for a segment, Griffiths example 5.5, constant 1; a long wire gives i / (2π d),
   the point current's magnitude); both are 0 on the wire's line. The **Poynting** vector of an in-plane E
   and an out-of-plane B is (E_y B, −E_x B) (`poynting`); `arrows show="poynting"` draws it with E the sum of
   the charges, dipoles and charged wires and B_z the sum of the wires' (`poynting_at`), shot 2's picture: a
   loop of two wires of opposite charge carrying the current round the loop, where S runs along the loop
   from the source toward the load (`SHOT2_WIRES`).
3. **Operators** (`divergence`, `curl`). ∂u/∂x + ∂v/∂y and ∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y by central differences of step
   10⁻⁴ data units; the kit reports them at named points.
4. **Streamlines** (`rk4_step`, `streamline`). A streamline through p₀ is the polyline of Runge–Kutta 4 steps
   of length h along the **unit direction** F / |F| (so h is arc length), forward and backward, while the
   point is inside the box, the field is finite and non-zero at every stage, and fewer than `maxSteps` steps
   were taken each way. A step whose point meets a stop condition (rules 6 and 10) ends the line.
5. **Arrows** (`arrow`, `arrow_grid`). At each node of the `cols` × `rows` grid spanning the box (edges
   included, row by row from yMin), the glyph is a shaft from the node of length min(|F| · `scale`,
   `maxLength`) along F, or along the Poynting vector S of rule 2 when `show="poynting"`, and a head (tip, left, right) of length `headLength` × shaft and half-width
   `headWidth` × that, in the presentation's stroke; a zero vector draws nothing.
6. **Evenly spaced streamlines** (`evenly_spaced_streamlines`, `min_separation`), after Jobard and Lefer
   1997. Let d = `separation`, d_t = `test`, h = `step`. The first streamline starts at the seed. A queue holds
   the accepted streamlines in order; for each, the candidate seeds are the points at distance d on both sides
   of each of its points (perpendicular to the local direction), in order along the line, each side in turn;
   a candidate is accepted when it is inside the box, the field is non-zero there, and it is at least d from
   every point of every accepted streamline. When the queue is empty, the nodes of the grid of spacing d from
   (xMin, yMin), in row-major order, are tried under the same test, so regions the seed's streamline does not
   reach are covered. A streamline is traced by rule 4 and a step ends it when its point is within d_t of a
   point of another streamline, or of its own line at least ⌈d / h⌉ + 1 steps away along the arc (a closed
   orbit). The result is bounded by `maxLines` streamlines. Consequently every pair of points on distinct
   streamlines is at least d_t apart, and a uniform field's streamlines lie exactly d apart.
7. **Line integral convolution** (`lic`), after Cabral and Leedom 1993. The noise raster has `resolution`
   pixels across the box, each the unit draw U(seed, row, column) of D24 (convention 5.19; the project's seed
   when `seed` is absent). The output pixel is the mean of the noise at the nearest raster pixel of the points
   at arc length 0, ±h, ±2h, … up to `length` along rule 4's streamline through the pixel's centre, samples
   outside the raster skipped; a zero field gives the noise itself. The raster is drawn over the box at
   `opacity`.
8. **Phase portraits** (`phase_hue`, `modulus_shade`), after Wegert 2012. Each pixel's colour has hue
   arg w / 2π (mod 1; 0 for w = 0 or non-finite), full saturation, and lightness 0.5 lowered by
   0.25 × frac(log_base |w|) when `modulus` is true (0 at powers of the base), so modulus contours appear
   at powers of `base` and phase turns once around each zero and pole.
9. **Winding number** (`winding_number`). The number of turns of arg f along the circle of radius r about c,
   counterclockwise, is the sum of the wrapped differences of arg at 256 equally spaced samples divided by
   2π, rounded; it equals zeros minus poles inside, with multiplicity. A zero or non-finite value on a sample
   is an error. The kit reports it for the portraits it measures.
10. **Charge lines** (`charge_lines`, `line_ends_on`, `dipole_line_constant`). From each positive charge,
    `perCharge` lines start at `radius` r₀ at angles 2πk / `perCharge` and follow E by rule 4, forward only,
    until the point is within r₀ of a negative charge (that point is the line's last), leaves the box, or
    `maxSteps` is reached; no line leaves a negative charge. Along a field line of a point dipole r / sin²θ is
    constant (θ the angle from the axis); the kit measures its largest relative change.
11. **Determinism.** Every rule is a pure function of the field, the attributes and the D24 draws; the orders
    of the queue (rule 6), the grid scan and the samples are stated; the bounds are attributes with schema
    maxima (`cols`, `rows` and `perCharge` at most 1000, `resolution` at most 4096, `maxSteps` and `maxLines`
    at most 1,000,000), so the glyph, line and sample counts of a frame are bounded by the document alone
    (SREP 14's deterministic-bound rule). The seek check of SREP 14 holds because nothing depends on earlier
    frames.

## Rationale

- **A node in the data frame, not an asset.** Arrows, streamlines and lines are strokes a camera and a style
  layer see; LIC and portraits are rasters over the same box. One node in the axes frame composes with every
  plot of MF1, and the ranges default to the axes.
- **Unit-direction integration.** Streamlines show direction; integrating F itself makes the step vary with
  |F|, which breaks the separation guarantee near a source. Arc-length steps make `separation` and `test`
  mean what they say.
- **A queue with a grid fallback.** Jobard and Lefer's queue alone leaves a region empty when no streamline
  reaches it (two separated basins, or a seed on a zero); the grid scan after the queue is deterministic and
  costs nothing when the queue covered the box.
- **Closure by arc distance.** Stopping a streamline when it meets its own earlier points, except the last
  few along the arc, closes an orbit after one turn without stopping it at its own start.
- **Hue by phase and shade by log modulus.** Wegert's portraits are the standard the catalogue's complex
  shots use; the base-2 contours make |w| readable without a legend.
- **The sources as laws with unit constants.** The kit measures shapes (rays, circles, r / sin²θ), which are
  independent of ε₀ and μ₀; an author scales with `scale`.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Per-streamline `parametricPath`s.** That is the workaround of probe 16's compactification; it cannot
  place streamlines evenly.
- **Integrating F rather than its direction.** See the rationale; it also makes `maxSteps` a time bound
  rather than a length bound.
- **Random seeds for the streamlines.** The queue is deterministic and gives even spacing; seeding by draws
  would need the D24 hash and give no better coverage.
- **LIC by a Hanning kernel.** A box kernel keeps the row-noise identity that the table checks; a smoother
  kernel is a `kernel` attribute the editor may add.
- **A 3D field element.** S5's flow lines over parts need streamlines on surfaces; the plots draft's 3D axes
  and tubes are the route, left open.

## Backwards compatibility

- Every 1.7.1 document validates and renders unchanged: `field` is a new node and `forceField` is unchanged.
- A 1.8 document that uses it is refused by a 1.7 validator (unknown element), as V17 states.

## Open issues

- **Scalar fields as heat maps.** `contours` covers level curves; a colour ramp raster of s(x, y) is a
  `heat` child the editor may add, with MF1's ticks for the legend.
- **3D fields and surface flow lines.** S5's flow over parts and shot 30's shock lines need streamlines in
  3D and on surfaces; the plots draft's `axes3D` and tubes are the route.
- **LIC kernels and animation.** A box kernel is stated; animated LIC (a phase shift of the kernel) is one
  attribute away.
- **Time-varying grids.** A `grid` is one lattice; M4's U/V rasters per time step need a sequence, which the
  pinned-data rules of SREP 14 (rule 7) govern.
