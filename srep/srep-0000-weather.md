# SREP 0 — Add a weather state

## Abstract

SREP 12's A5: **`weather`** elements of `physics`, beside `wind`, hold one animatable state — `temperature`,
`humidity`, `precipitation`, `cloudiness`, `visibility`, the `lightning` rate — and a `wind`. Every element that
names a weather is **driven** by it instead of its own attributes: `cloudLayer` (coverage and precipitation),
`snowpack` (snowfall and temperature), `shallowWater` and hydraulic `erode` (rain), rain and snow `emitter`s
(their rate from the drop and flake fluxes), `medium` (extinction from the visibility) and `material`s (wetness and
snow cover). The precipitation `rainMax` · `precipitation` splits into rain and snow by the temperature (all snow at
1 °C, all rain at 4 °C; Auer 1974); the drop spectrum is **Marshall and Palmer**'s with the fall speed of **Atlas**,
Srivastava and Sekhon, the flake spectrum **Gunn and Marshall**'s; the extinction is **Koschmieder**'s 3.912 /
visibility. Surface **wetness** and snow depth follow a stepped recurrence from the scene's start (`wetTime`,
`dryTime`, `snowScale`, the **degree-day** `meltRate`; SREP 14); a wet dielectric darkens by the water film's
geometric series after **Lekner** and **Dorf** (1988) — **monotonically** in the wetness and never below zero — and
its roughness goes toward the film's, never up; snow cover blends upward faces toward snow. **Lightning** strikes
are a **Poisson** process by the **time-change** of the integrated rate with unit-exponential gaps drawn from the
random streams (S(e, 1, k, 0)); each bolt is a **dielectric breakdown** channel (**Niemeyer**, Pietronero and
Wiesmann 1984; **Kim and Lin** 2004, η = 2) grown on a `lightningGrid` with the fast potentials of Kim et al.
(2007), its growth draws S(e, 2, k, n), so a bolt is a function of the element seed and its index. Rules WX1–WX4
and V17; the schema files are unchanged, the diff is inside the draft; the clouds draft's map attribute is renamed
`weatherMap`. Reference module `conformance/tools/weather_reference.py`; engine card SR-107.

## Motivation

- **SREP 12 names the deltas.** Row A5 of the plan: "one weather state for clouds, fog, rain and snow, wet and
  snowy surfaces (after Lagarde 2012–2013), lightning (dielectric breakdown, Kim and Lin 2004); reference tests:
  wetness darkens albedo and lowers roughness monotonically; lightning is a function of its seed". This draft is
  that row.
- **Five drafts each hold a piece of the sky.** The clouds draft has a precipitation per layer, the snow draft a
  snowfall and a temperature per terrain, the surface-water and erosion drafts a rain each, the emitter presets a
  rate each, the media a density. A storm that is one thing in the story is six numbers to keep consistent in
  the document. One state, named by its readers, is the join.
- **The engine's weather is three presets.** Rain and snow are `emitter` presets (`crates/sr-eval/src/sim.rs`),
  the gradient sky has no fog, no material is ever wet. Nothing in the engine knows the temperature.
- **Every frame renders alone.** Wetness and snow on surfaces are integrals of the rain; a frame cannot ask the
  frame before it how wet the road was. The stepped recurrence from the scene's start with `maxSteps`, as the
  stepped processes of the terrain drafts, is the replay SREP 14 allows; the lightning's time-change makes the
  k-th strike's time a function of the rate's integral, not of the frames rendered before.
- **Conformance needs a reference, not a look.** Testable to the digit: the wet albedo is monotone in the wetness
  and in the dry albedo and never exceeds it; the wet roughness never rises; the recurrence matches the analytic
  saturation; the Marshall–Palmer spectrum carries the rate it was built from (within 20 %); a bolt is a function
  of its seed; strikes count the integrated rate.

**`weather`**, a new `xs:IDREF` attribute on the readers: `cloudLayer` (clouds draft), `snowpack` (snow draft),
`shallowWater` (surface-water draft), `erode` (erosion draft, read by the hydraulic kind), `emitter`
(`particleEmitterType`, read with the `rain` and `snow` presets), `medium` (`mediumType`) and `material`
(`materialType`). The clouds draft's weather-map attribute is renamed **`weatherMap`** (its History records it).

**Schematron** (`scene-render.sch`):

- **WX1** `temperature`, `visibility`, `wetTime`, `dryTime`, `snowScale`, `step` and `flashDuration` are > 0;
  `humidity`, `precipitation` and `cloudiness` are in [0, 1]; `lightning`, `rainMax`, `meltRate` and
  `lightningEta` are ≥ 0; `maxSteps` and `maxStrikes` are positive integers; `lightningGrid` is an integer from
  8 to 256. Animated attributes satisfy the bounds at every key.
- **WX2** `wind`, when present, is the id of a `wind` of `physics`.
- **WX3** A reader's `weather` is the id of a `weather` of `physics`.
- **WX4** A reader that names a weather leaves the attributes the weather drives unset: `coverage` and
  `precipitation` on `cloudLayer`, `snowfall` and `temperature` on `snowpack`, `rain` on `shallowWater` and
  `erode`, `rate` on `emitter`, `extinction` on `medium`.
- **V17** (extended) `weather` requires `scene/@version` 1.8 or later.

### Semantics

Rates are mm/h of water, depths metres, temperatures kelvin, time seconds of scene time; e = E(project, id, seed,
"weather") is the element seed and S the stream draw of the random-streams draft; an animated attribute is read
at the time named. The reference is `conformance/tools/weather_reference.py`; function names in parentheses.

1. **Rain and snow.** The precipitation rate at t is R = `rainMax` · `precipitation`(t). The snow fraction of the
   temperature T is clamp((277.15 − T) / 3, 0, 1): all snow at or under 274.15 K, all rain at or over 277.15 K,
   half at 275.65 K (2.5 °C, Auer 1974). Rain is R (1 − f), snow R f, in water. (`rain_rate`, `snow_fraction`,
   `split_rate`)
2. **Drops.** The drop spectrum of a rain rate R is Marshall and Palmer's N(D) = N₀ e^(−Λ D) drops per m³ per mm,
   N₀ = 8000, Λ = 4.1 R^(−0.21) mm⁻¹; the fall speed v(D) = max(0, 9.65 − 10.3 e^(−0.6 D)) m/s (Atlas, Srivastava
   and Sekhon 1973). The drop flux is ∫ N v dD over D from 0 to 7 mm (the trapezoid at 0.01 mm), drops per m² per
   second; the spectrum's own rate 3.6·10⁶ · ∫ (π/6) D³ N v dD in SI is within 20 % of R (numeric table).
   (`marshall_palmer`, `drop_density`, `rain_speed`, `drop_flux`, `implied_rain_rate`)
3. **Flakes.** The flake spectrum of a snow rate R (water) in melted diameters is Gunn and Marshall's, N₀ = 3800
   R^(−0.87), Λ = 2.55 R^(−0.48); flakes fall at 1 m/s. The flake flux is ∫ N dD · 1 m/s; it falls as R rises
   (fewer, larger flakes) while the water the spectrum carries follows R within 25 %. (`gunn_marshall`,
   `flake_flux`, `implied_snow_rate`, `particle_fluxes`)
4. **Visibility.** The extinction is 3.912 / `visibility` per metre (Koschmieder 1924, contrast threshold 0.02).
   (`extinction`, `extinction_at`)
5. **Surface recurrence.** The wetness w and the snow depth d of surfaces at t are the values after
   min(round(t / `step`), `maxSteps`) steps from w = d = 0, step n at τ = n Δt reading the state at τ:
   w += Δt (rain / 10 / `wetTime` · (1 − w) − (1 − `humidity`) / `dryTime` · w), clipped to [0, 1];
   d += Δt (snow / 3.6·10⁶ / 0.1 − `meltRate` · max(0, T − 273.15)), clipped at 0 (fresh snow at a tenth of
   water's density; the degree-day law, Hock 2003). Under constant rain w saturates as a/(a + b) (1 − e^(−(a+b)t)),
   a = rain / 10 / `wetTime`, b = (1 − `humidity`) / `dryTime`, which the recurrence matches within 1 % at the
   default step. The snow cover is 1 − e^(−d / `snowScale`). (`surface_state`, `steps_for`, `snow_cover`)
6. **Wet look.** With R_e the hemispherical Fresnel reflectance of diffuse light at an air–water interface (n =
   1.333; 0.0664, integrated numerically) and r_i = 1 − (1 − R_e) / n² the reflectance from inside the film
   (0.4746), a dielectric of diffuse albedo a under a film has the albedo a_film = (1 − R_e)(1 − r_i) a / (1 − a
   r_i): the geometric series of the light bouncing between the substrate and the film's surface (Lekner and
   Dorf 1988). The material's albedo at wetness w is lerp(a, a_film, w (1 − `metallic`)) per channel — monotone
   in w and in a, never above a, metals untouched — and its roughness is lerp(r, min(r, 0.05), w): the film is a
   smooth dielectric and never rougher than the surface. The specular reflection of the film itself is the
   renderer's water layer (F₀ 0.02), outside this draft. (`diffuse_fresnel`, `internal_reflectance`,
   `wet_albedo`, `wet_roughness`)
7. **Snowy look.** With c the snow cover and u = max(0, n · up) the normal's upward component, the albedo is
   lerp(a, 0.85, c u) per channel and the roughness lerp(r, 0.7, c u): walls and ceilings stay bare. The wet
   look applies first, the snow on top. (`snowy_albedo`, `snowy_roughness`, `surface_look`)
8. **Strikes.** With λ(t) = `lightning`(t) and L(t) its integral by the trapezoid over the steps of rule 5, the
   k-th strike (k from 1) is at the first time where L reaches Θ_k = Σ_{i ≤ k} −ln(1 − S(e, 1, i, 0)), placed
   linearly inside its step — the time-change of a unit Poisson process, so the count over any window has the
   mean of λ's integral — at the position x_k = S(e, 1, k, 1) across the sky (0 to 1); never more than
   `maxStrikes`. The strikes up to t are the same whatever frame asks. (`strikes`)
9. **Bolt.** Bolt k grows on the `lightningGrid` × `lightningGrid` grid from the top cell nearest x_k to the
   ground row by dielectric breakdown: the candidates are the eight-neighbours of the channel inside the grid;
   the potential of a candidate p is the mean over the channel cells c of (1 − R / max(R, |p − c|)) plus the mean
   over the ground cells g of R / max(R, |p − g|), R = ½ (the channel's grounded spheres at 0, the far field at 1,
   the ground's pull; Kim et al. 2007); the growth weight is (φ − min φ)^η normalised (uniform when all equal),
   and the growth site of step n is the candidate whose cumulative weight first exceeds S(e, 2, k, n). Growth
   stops at the first cell on the ground row (the hit) and is refused past 4 · grid² cells. The main channel is the
   parent chain from the hit to the start, brightness 1; every other cell's brightness is 0.3 per branch
   generation off the main channel. A bolt is a function of e, k and x_k. (`bolt`, `potential`,
   `growth_weights`, `pick`, `max_growth`)
10. **Flash.** The flash at t is Σ over the strikes at or before t of e^(−(t − t_k) / `flashDuration`). (`flash`)
11. **Readers.** An element naming a weather takes, at t: `cloudLayer` coverage = `cloudiness`(t) and
    precipitation = `precipitation`(t); `snowpack` snowfall = snow / 3.6·10⁶ / 0.1 metres of snow per second and
    temperature = `temperature`(t); `shallowWater` and hydraulic `erode` rain = rain / 3.6·10⁶ metres per second;
    an `emitter` with the `rain` preset rate = drop flux × its width in metres × 1 m of depth per second, with the
    `snow` preset the flake flux likewise; a `medium` extinction = rule 4; a `material` the looks of rules 6–7 from
    the surface state of rule 5; every reader that takes a wind takes the weather's `wind`. The reference's
    `state` returns these for one weather and the digest of them. (`state`, `digest`)
12. **Validity.** WX1–WX4 and V17 hold or the document is refused; the reference lists the codes.
    (`validate_document`, `bad_document`)

## Rationale

- **One element, named by its readers.** The alternative — every reader keeping its own numbers — is what the
  five drafts have today; WX4 forbids a reader from carrying both a weather and the value the weather drives, so
  a document cannot disagree with itself.
- **The water film, not a darkening constant.** Lagarde's wet shading darkens the diffuse albedo and raises the
  gloss by hand-tuned factors; Lekner and Dorf explain why things are darker when wet — light that leaves the
  substrate is reflected back by the film's surface and absorbed again — and the geometric series gives the
  factor from the albedo itself: a bright surface darkens less (0.8 → 0.65), a dark one more in proportion (0.3
  → 0.17). The series is monotone and bounded, which is what the SREP 12 test asks.
- **Marshall–Palmer and Gunn–Marshall.** They are the exponential spectra every radar meteorologist starts from,
  and a renderer needs exactly what they give: how many drops of what size cross a square metre. The
  self-consistency row (the spectrum's own rate) is the check that the constants were copied right.
- **Auer's 2.5 °C.** The rain–snow threshold is a surface-temperature fact with a spread; a linear ramp of three
  kelvin centred at 2.5 °C is the simplest rule with the right midpoint.
- **The time-change for strikes.** Drawing the next gap at each strike from the rate then reads the rate at
  the previous strike's time and never starts a storm whose rate was zero at the last strike; integrating the
  rate and crossing fixed thresholds is exact for any rate schedule and makes the k-th strike's time a function
  of the integral alone (SREP 14).
- **Fast potentials.** Solving Laplace's equation at every growth step (Kim and Lin 2004) is a conjugate-gradient
  solve per cell; the superposition of grounded spheres (Kim et al. 2007) is an O(channel) sum per candidate
  and reproducible to the digit across engines. The ground row's pull replaces the far electrode.
- **Purposes.** Strike gaps and positions on purpose 1 at step k, the growth draws on purpose 2 at step k and
  index n: the random-streams draft's S makes each bolt independent of the strikes before it, so an engine may
  compute bolts in parallel.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A weather map instead of a state.** The clouds draft's `weatherMap` already paints the sky; it is a picture
  of coverage, not a temperature or a rain rate, and nothing on the ground can read it.
- **An energy balance for melt and drying.** Both need radiation and wind fields the scene has only partly; the
  degree-day melt and the humidity-scaled drying are first-order laws with one parameter each (open issue).
- **Advected drops.** Rain as a transported particle field is the emitter's job; the weather gives it the flux.
- **Lightning as a texture or an L-system.** A drawn bolt is a look, not a function of a seed; an L-system is a
  function of a seed but not of the sky's electrostatics. The breakdown model is both, and Kim and Lin's η = 2
  is the lightning-like value.
- **Solving Laplace on the grid.** Exact, slow, and not reproducible across float orders; the fast potential
  is the stated approximation.

## Backwards compatibility

A 1.7 document has no `weather`; the emitter presets, the media and the materials behave as before. A 1.8
reader without `weather` behaves as its own draft states. The clouds draft is amended (`weather` → `weatherMap`)
before either is accepted, so no accepted schema changes. The element is additive.

## Open issues

1. **Rain and visibility.** Rain lowers the visibility; here `visibility` is the author's. A rate–visibility
   relation would make it a derived quantity.
2. **Drying by wind and sun.** The drying rate scales by humidity only; wind speed and insolation matter as much
   in the field.
3. **Porosity.** Lagarde darkens porous materials more than sealed ones; the film series treats every dielectric
   as a diffuse substrate under water. A `porosity` attribute scaling the film's weight is the candidate.
4. **Puddles and ripples.** Wetness is a scalar per surface; puddles in the hollows and the rings of drops are
   a height field and a normal map the renderer would animate.
5. **Surfaces versus the terrain's pack.** The terrain's snow is the snow draft's layer; the weather's surface
   snow is a depth per material with no exchange between them.
6. **Thunder.** The sound of a strike delayed by the distance belongs to the audio tracks, not here.
7. **Several weathers.** Nothing blends two weathers over space; a front is one weather animated in time.
8. **Intra-cloud lightning and the cloud's base.** Bolts run from the grid's top to its ground; the cloud base's
   height and strikes that never reach the ground are open.
9. **The flash's light.** The flash is a scalar; which light it drives (the clouds draft's sky, the dome light)
   is a rendering decision.
