# SREP 74 — Add `viewport3D`: a 3D scene with its own camera, as a node

## Abstract

A new node, `viewport3D`, renders its own 3D scene into a width × height picture and draws that picture like a
layer. The scene is made of the `object3D` and `camera` children of the viewport, with the lights it selects. It is
isolated from the document's main 3D scene in both directions:

- the document's active camera does not see the viewport's objects;
- the viewport's cameras never become the document's active camera;
- the viewport's camera sees only the viewport's objects.

A 3D cell can then sit inside a 2D group of an atlas, beside other cells with other cameras.

## Motivation

**The owner's 72-cell atlas test (2026-10-09, not published).** "One 3D scene per cell: per the README, the
composition uses a single active camera, and all 3D objects under one parent become one layer. I could not place the
3D scene inside a group of the atlas. What worked was rendering the cell as a separate document."

**Today** (schema 1.5.0, `rs-scene-render` commit `c09b58a`):

- **One camera.** The `cameraType` annotation reads: "Several cameras may exist; the active one at time t is the last
  active camera whose [start, end) contains t". There is one active camera for the whole document.
- **One layer per parent.** The engine README reads: "All 3D objects under one parent render as one layer. They share
  one depth buffer, one set of lights and one set of shadow maps…". Objects in different groups render as separate
  layers, but all of them go through the same active camera.
- **`viewportCamera` is not a per-node viewport.** It is an attribute of `scene360` (`scene360Type`). The reference
  uses it to choose the camera from which the six cube faces of a 360° frame are rendered
  (`crates/sr-gpu/src/render_three.rs:2425-2429`: "the viewport camera"; if it names no camera, the report says
  "scene360: viewport camera … is not a camera"). DEFINITIONS D5.4 uses it for `project/@mode="viewport"`: the
  rectilinear view of the 360° canvas.

### Semantics

**1. The viewport's space.** A viewport has its own 3D space, defined by CONVENTIONS §2 with the viewport's frame
in place of the project's frame:

- the origin is at the top-left corner of the width × height picture, on the plane z = 0;
- +x points right, +y down and +z away;
- one unit is one pixel of the picture.

Its children's coordinates are in this space. `physics/@pixelsPerMeter` applies as in the document.

**2. Its camera.**

- If `camera` is set, it names a camera child of the viewport, and that camera is the view.
- Otherwise the view is the last active camera child whose [start, end) contains t, by the usual rule applied to
  the viewport's own cameras.
- If there is no such camera, the view is the implicit camera of CONVENTIONS 2.2 for a width × height frame: at
  (W/2, H/2, −(W/2)/tan(30°)), with a 60° horizontal field of view, where W and H are the viewport's size. The plane
  z = 0 then maps onto the picture pixel for pixel.

Cameras inside a viewport are never candidates for the document's active camera, and the document's cameras never
view a viewport's objects.

**3. Lights.** The lights that act in the viewport are those named by `lights`, or every light of the document when
`lights` is absent. Their positions and orientations are read in the viewport's space (1). A dome light gives the
viewport its environment, and `environmentVisible` shows it in the viewport's picture.

**4. The picture.** The viewport renders its scene, with the shading, shadows and effects the document's 3D scene
would have, into a width × height picture.

- The picture starts filled with `background`, transparent by default.
- Nothing outside the picture is drawn: content beyond the frame is clipped.
- The node draws the picture with its top-left corner at its local origin, one pixel per unit. The node's transform,
  timing, opacity, masks, matte, effects and `blend` apply as for a `layer` showing that picture.
- `project/@antialias3d` and `project/@quality` apply.

**5. Isolation.** A viewport's `object3D` and `particles3D` children are not part of the document's 3D scene. They
do not appear in the active camera's view, cast no shadows there, and receive none from it. The document's 3D
objects are not part of the viewport's scene.

**6. 360 and viewport modes.** With `scene360` or `project/@mode="equirectangular"` or `"viewport"`, a viewport3D is a
2D node like a layer showing its picture (DEFINITIONS D5.2–D5.4). Its own camera is unaffected by
`scene360/@viewportCamera`.

### Defaults and the neutral case

This is a new element, so existing documents are unaffected.

## Rationale

- **A node, not a camera option.** The owner's need is a cell that sits in a 2D layout with its own view. A node
  carries layout, timing and effects already. A per-camera "render target" would still need a node to draw the
  result.
- **Children rather than references.** Objects inside the viewport belong to it by containment, like the children of
  a group. This avoids a second id-based membership that could disagree with the tree.
- **The viewport's own pixel space**, so a cell authored as a separate document of size W × H can be pasted in
  unchanged. That was the owner's workaround.
- **Lights by reference.** Lights live in the document's `<lights>` section today. Moving them under the viewport
  would change their schema location. Naming them keeps one place.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Using `scene360/@viewportCamera`**: it selects the camera of a 360° frame and of `mode="viewport"`, which is a
  different job.
- **Per-group active cameras** (a `camera` attribute on `group`): 3D objects would still share the document's depth
  buffer and lights across groups. Isolation would have to be defined anyway.
- **Physics inside viewports now**: a second physics world needs its own stepping and caching rules. VP3 rejects
  bodies until a later SREP defines this.

## Backwards compatibility

The change is MINOR: a new element under the version gate V15.

## Open issues

- Physics inside viewports (VP3).
- Whether `lights` should default to none rather than all. Defaulting to all copies the document's look into each
  cell, which matches the owner's workaround of a separate document with the same lights.
- Text and 2.5D layers (`threeD="true"`) inside a viewport, placed in its camera space.
