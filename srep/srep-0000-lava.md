# SREP 0 — Add lava to the terrain grid

## Abstract

SREP 12's T4: a **`lavaFlow`** child of `terrain` erupts lava from a vent into the terrain's **`lava`** layer and
moves it over the terrain's surface as a **Bingham** fluid in a **cellular** automaton after **MAGFLOW** (**Vicari**
et al. 2007). Each step, in this order: the vent adds `effusion` cubic metres per second (animatable, varied
through the random streams at purpose 2³¹ + 2¹⁷); every cell sends each lower eight-neighbour the steady flux of
**Dragoni**, Bonafede and Boschi (1986) for a Bingham sheet of its thickness on the slope of the surface between
them, which is zero under the **critical thickness** h_cr = τ / (ρ g sin θ) and never takes the cell under its
**plug**; inflow mixes the temperatures by volume; every warm cell radiates to the sky (**Stefan**–**Boltzmann**)
through the thermal mass of its lava or, once it has **solidified**, of its `crust`; lava colder than the `solidus`
becomes **`bedrock`**. The `viscosity` and the `yieldStrength` are log-linear in the temperature from their values
at the `eruptionTemperature` (**Ishihara** et al. 1990, the form MAGFLOW uses, which **Giordano** and Dingwell 2003
fit for Etna); the terrain's **`temperature`** layer is the temperature of the surface. The lava is conserved to
the digit: what erupted equals what lies molten plus what solidified; a deposit on an incline comes to rest with
every cell under its critical thickness (the SREP 12 test). Rules LV1–LV4 and V17; the schema files are unchanged,
the diff is inside the draft. Reference module `conformance/tools/lava_reference.py`; engine card SR-108.

## Motivation

- **SREP 12 names the deltas.** Row T4 of the plan: "Bingham cellular flow with temperature-dependent yield and
  viscosity, radiative cooling, solidification into bedrock (Vicari et al. 2007); reference test: flow halts at
  h = τ/(ρ g sin θ) on an incline". This draft is that row.
- **The terrain grid reserved the layer.** The terrain-grid draft lists `lava` among its solid layers and
  `temperature` among its fields with nothing yet writing them; the erosion and snow drafts are the two processes
  that exist. A volcano in a science explainer is the third.
- **Lava is a yield-stress fluid, not water.** The shallow-water solver of the surface-water draft spreads until
  flat; lava stops on a slope when it is thinner than its critical thickness and stands as a levee. That is the
  one fact every lava picture must get right, and the Bingham rheology states it in one number.
- **Every frame renders alone.** The flow is a stepped process from the scene's start with `step` and
  `maxSteps`, as the other terrain processes; any frame replays the same steps and gets the same digest
  (SREP 14), and the effusion's variation draws from the random streams by step, not from the frames before.
- **Conformance needs a reference, not a look.** Testable to the digit: Dragoni's flux from the velocity profile;
  the **Nusselt** film when the yield strength is zero; the halt on the incline; **mass** conservation through
  flow, cooling and solidification; the cooling rate from the Stefan–Boltzmann law; the numeric table.

### Semantics

Lengths are metres on the terrain's grid (i right, j down, `metersPerCell` = dx apart), thicknesses metres of
lava per cell, volumes cubic metres, temperatures kelvin, time seconds of scene time; g = 9.81 m/s², σ the
Stefan–Boltzmann constant 5.670374419·10⁻⁸ W/(m² K⁴); e = E(project, id, seed, "terrain") is the terrain's element
seed and S the stream draw of the random-streams draft. The reference is `conformance/tools/lava_reference.py`;
function names in parentheses.

1. **Steps.** The layers at time t are the generated layers after min(round(t / `step`), `maxSteps`) steps, step
   n at τ = n · `step` doing, in order, rules 2, 4, 6 and 7. (`run`, `steps_for`, `step`)
2. **The vent.** The vent cell is (⌊`ventX` / dx⌋, ⌊`ventY` / dx⌋). Step n adds V = `effusion`(τ) · `step` ·
   (1 + `effusionVariation` · (2 S(e, 2³¹ + 2¹⁷, n, 0) − 1)) cubic metres there — no draw when the variation is
   zero — as V / dx² metres of lava at the eruption temperature, mixing by volume with the lava already in the
   cell: T = (h T_old + ΔV/dx² · T_e) / (h + ΔV/dx²), T_e when the cell was empty. (`vent_cell`,
   `effusion_volume`, `erupt`)
3. **Rheology.** At temperature T the lava's viscosity is η(T) = `viscosity` · 10^(`viscosityRate` (T_e − T)) and
   its yield strength τ(T) = `yieldStrength` · 10^(`yieldRate` (T_e − T)), T_e the eruption temperature: log-linear
   laws, rising as the lava cools. On a slope of sine s the critical thickness is h_cr = τ / (ρ g s), infinite on
   the flat: a Bingham sheet thinner than h_cr is a plug at rest. (`viscosity`, `yield_strength`,
   `critical_thickness`, `slope_sine`)
4. **Flow.** Let z be the terrain's surface (the sum of its solid layers). Every cell (i, j) with lava h > 0,
   rheology at its temperature, sends each of its eight neighbours k whose surface is lower by d_k > 0 over the
   horizontal distance ℓ_k (dx, or dx √2 on a diagonal), with s_k = d_k / √(d_k² + ℓ_k²) and a_k = h / h_cr,k, the
   steady Bingham flux per unit width of Dragoni, Bonafede and Boschi (1986),

   q_k = ρ g s_k h_cr,k³ / (3 η) · (a_k³ − 3 a_k² / 2 + 1 / 2) for a_k > 1 + 10⁻³, else 0

   (the sheared layer under a plug of thickness h_cr; it vanishes as (a − 1)², so a sheet within a thousandth of
   its critical thickness is at rest; the Nusselt film ρ g s h³ / (3 η) when the yield strength is zero), as the
   volume v_k = min(q_k dx `step`, d_k dx² / 2): across an edge of width dx for one step, never more than brings
   the two surfaces level. The transfers together never take the cell under the smallest critical thickness of
   its receivers: they are scaled by min(1, (h − min_k h_cr,k) dx² / Σ v_k). All cells send at once from the
   surfaces at the step's start; a receiver's temperature becomes the volume-weighted mean of what it kept and
   what arrived, the inflow's temperature when it was empty. Edges are closed. Lava is conserved. (`flow_step`,
   `bingham_flux`)
5. **The temperature layer.** The terrain's `temperature` layer is the temperature of the surface: of the lava
   where lava lies, of the crust where it solidified, `ambient` elsewhere; the snow draft reads it as the
   temperature its snow sees. (`cool_step`)
6. **Cooling.** Every cell warmer than `ambient` loses, over one step, ΔT = `emissivity` σ (T⁴ − T_a⁴) `step` /
   (ρ c max(h, `crust`)), never below `ambient`: radiation from the surface through the thermal mass of the
   lava, or of the `crust` where the lava is thinner or gone (Harris and Rowland 2001 for the budget; conduction
   into the ground, convection and rain are open issues). (`cool`)
7. **Solidification.** Lava colder than the `solidus` moves, whole, from the `lava` layer to the `bedrock` layer
   of its cell; the surface does not change, the temperature keeps cooling by rule 6. The run reports the metres
   solidified. (`solidify`)
8. **Conservation.** After any number of steps, the erupted volume equals the molten lava plus the solidified
   lava times dx² (the run reports `erupted` and `solidified`). (`run`)
9. **Validity.** LV1–LV4 and V17 hold or the document is refused; the reference lists the codes.
   (`validate_document`, `bad_document`)

## Rationale

- **MAGFLOW's cellular rule.** Vicari et al. (2007) and Del Negro et al. (2008) run Etna's flows with exactly this
  transition: Dragoni's steady Bingham flux between neighbours, viscosity and yield strength from the temperature,
  radiative loss, mixing on inflow, solidification. It is the published model that is both physical and cheap
  enough for a grid in a renderer.
- **Dragoni's flux, derived.** Shear stress ρ g s (h − z); below the plug (z < h − h_cr) the shear rate is
  (τ(z) − τ_y) / η; integrating the velocity and adding the plug's gives ρ g s (h − h_cr)² (2 h + h_cr) / (6 η) =
  ρ g s h_cr³ / (3 η) (a³ − 3 a² / 2 + 1 / 2). The test re-derives it from the profile, and the Nusselt limit
  checks the constant.
- **The plug cap.** Applying a steady-sheet flux to a cell and letting it drain in one step overshoots the rest
  state (a runny front ran to the foot of the incline in the first red test, leaving a thin trail). The sheet's
  own physics says the plug under h_cr never moves; capping a cell's outflow at h − h_cr,min restores the rest
  state whatever the author's step, and the half-drop cap keeps two cells from trading lava back and forth.
- **A rest tolerance.** The flux vanishes as (a − 1)², so a sheet approaches its critical thickness
  asymptotically and a halt test would never see it; 10⁻³ of h_cr is below anything a picture or a digest at
  three decimals resolves.
- **Log-linear rheology.** Giordano and Dingwell's fit is a function of composition and water; MAGFLOW uses
  log-linear laws in T for a given lava (Ishihara et al. 1990). Two numbers at the eruption temperature and two
  slopes let an author state basalt or andesite without a composition.
- **The crust's thermal mass.** A cell's lava thins to nothing as it drains or solidifies; with the cooling rate
  ∝ 1 / h a film would cool in a step and a solid cell never. The `crust` thickness is the smallest mass that
  radiates: the crust that keeps glowing after the flow stops.
- **One temperature layer.** The terrain has one `temperature` field; making it the surface's temperature lets
  the snow draft melt snow where lava lies without a second field, and a renderer read the glow from it.
- **Purpose 2³¹ + 2¹⁷.** The erosion draft's children draw at 2³¹ + k, the snowpack at 2³¹ + 2¹⁶; the vent's draws
  are clear of both.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Shallow water with a viscosity.** It spreads until flat; no levees, no halt on a slope. The yield stress is
  the lava.
- **A full Bingham Navier–Stokes solve.** Exact and far too slow for a scene grid; MAGFLOW is the published
  reduction.
- **Random neighbour order (SCIARA, Crisci et al. 2004).** A minimisation of the height differences over the
  neighbours with a random order is not a function of the seed in an obvious way; Dragoni's flux is
  deterministic and local.
- **An energy equation for the crust and the core.** FLOWGO's two-component thermal budget (Harris and
  Rowland 2001) needs the channel's width and velocity; here the cell's lava radiates as one body (open issue).
- **Solidified lava as its own layer.** The terrain-grid draft's `lava` is the molten layer; a solid lava layer
  would want its own erodibility and colour, which `bedrock` already has.

## Backwards compatibility

A 1.7 document has no `terrain`; a 1.8 terrain without `lavaFlow` is unchanged. The element is additive.

## Open issues

1. **Conduction and convection.** Only radiation cools the lava; conduction into the ground and forced convection
   by the wind of the wind-field draft are second-order terms Harris and Rowland (2001) carry.
2. **Partial solidification.** A cell solidifies whole at the solidus; a crust over a molten core (the tube
   regime) is the two-component budget this draft rejected for now.
3. **The step.** The cellular flow is explicit at the author's step; the caps keep it bounded and conservative,
   not accurate when a cell's Bingham velocity crosses more than a cell per step. An adaptive substep as the snow
   draft's ice is the candidate.
4. **Lava over water, snow and ice.** Nothing boils the water layer or melts the snow layer; the snow draft's melt
   sees the temperature layer and nothing more.
5. **Several vents and fissures.** One vent per `lavaFlow`; a fissure is a line of vents.
6. **Composition.** The log-linear rheology stands in for Giordano and Dingwell's composition-and-water model.
7. **Rendering.** The glow from the temperature (blackbody), the crust's darkening and the surface's roughness
   are rendering decisions for the terrain's display, outside this draft.
