# SREP 0 — Add star fields from the Bright Star Catalogue

## Abstract

A `starField` child of a dome light for the night and space shots of SREP 12's milestone 6 (SP2): the stars
of a `catalogue` asset, the Yale Bright Star Catalogue compacted to the columns a renderer needs and pinned by
its sha256 digest, drawn as points whose flux follows the magnitude scale (five magnitudes are a factor of one
hundred), whose colour is the blackbody of the Ballesteros temperature of the star's B−V through the CIE 1931
observer into linear sRGB, whose directions move by proper motion from J2000 to the frame's epoch, and whose
constellations are named by Roman's table of Delporte's boundaries. Every value is a pure function of the
catalogue, the document's constants and the frame's epoch, so the seek check (SREP 14) holds by construction.
The reference module `conformance/tools/star_fields_reference.py` states each rule as code, rebuilds the
fixture `conformance/assets/bsc5-compact.csv.gz` from the distributed catalogue, and prints the numeric table
of the Conformance section.

## Motivation

- **The catalogue has the shot.** SREP 12 line 282 lists SP2 as "the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, 5th edition
  (public domain); magnitude to flux; B–V to temperature (Ballesteros 2012) to colour through CIE 1931; proper
  motion; constellations", with the reference tests "Δm = 5 gives flux ratio 100; Sun colour at about
  5,800 K". SREP 71's dome light (xsd 5855) has a gradient sky and a sun; the night sky is a texture the
  author paints or downloads, with no stars at their places, no magnitudes and no constellations.
- **An author fakes the stars today.** A time-lapse of the night sky, a planetarium shot or the view from
  orbit (SREP 12 shots 36 and 37) needs a star map rendered elsewhere, and the map's stars have no brightness
  scale a lighting effect can meter against, no epoch and no names.
- **The constructions have published checks.** The magnitude scale is a definition; Ballesteros' formula is
  one line; the CIE table is public; Roman's paper prints eight positions with their constellations
  (equinox 1950) and her program; the catalogue's own Bayer and Flamsteed names carry the constellation of
  3,143 stars, and the lookup agrees with all of them. These are the numeric tests below.

## Specification

### Syntax

A diff for schema 1.8 against `schema/scene-render.xsd` at 1.7.1 (line numbers of that file); the schema
files are not changed by this draft.

- `catalogueType`, a new child of `assetsType` (2387), zero or more: `id` (`xs:ID`), `src` (`xs:anyURI`, a
  gzipped CSV with the header `HR,Name,RA,Dec,V,B-V,pmRA,pmDE,SpType`, SF1), `digest` (`xs:string`, required:
  `sha256:` and the 64 hex digits of the file's sha256; SF1). The reference fixture is
  `conformance/assets/bsc5-compact.csv.gz`, digest `cd18b1c9b8c009d922edfed020ba718aa14a577416fb625bf01ddd9cbdaab617`,
  the catalogue's 9,096 stars with a J2000 position (14 of the 9,110 records are novae or non-stellar
  objects without one).
- `starField`, a new optional child element of `lightType` (the choice at 5856), at most one, read for
  `type="dome"` (SF2):
  - `catalogue` (`xs:IDREF`, required: a `catalogue` asset), `limitMagnitude` (`xs:double`, default 6.5: stars
    with V above it are not drawn), `magnitudeZero` (`positiveDecimal`, default 1: the radiance, in the
    scene's linear units, of a star of magnitude 0; SF3), `pointSize` (`positiveDecimal`, pixels, default 1.5:
    the diameter of the disc a star's flux is spread over, SF3), `properMotion` (`xs:boolean`, default true),
    `epoch` (`xs:double`, Julian years, default 2000.0: the epoch of the field when the scene has no `space`
    element; ignored, with a warning, when it has one, SF4), `constellations` (one of `none`, `labels`,
    `boundaries`; default `none`).
- Schematron:
  - **SF1** a `catalogue` asset's file is a gzipped CSV with exactly the nine columns above, HR numbers
    strictly increasing, right ascension in [0, 360), declination in [−90, 90], V in [−5, 30], and whose
    sha256 digest equals `digest`; a mismatch is an error, not a warning.
  - **SF2** `starField` is written only on a dome light.
  - **SF3** `magnitudeZero` and `pointSize` are positive; `limitMagnitude` is finite.
  - **SF4** `epoch` is within 10,000 years of 2000.0; it is not written when the scene has a `space` element
    (the time-frames draft), whose clock gives the epoch.
  - **SF5** `constellations` other than `none` needs the catalogue to be the Bright Star Catalogue fixture
    or another catalogue in J2000 (Roman's table is precessed from J2000 by rule 5).
  - **V17** `catalogue` and `starField` need `scene/@version="1.8"` or later.

### Semantics

The reference module names the function of each rule. Angles are degrees unless a rule says radians.

1. **The catalogue.** `load_catalogue(path, digest)` verifies the sha256 (SF1), decompresses, and parses the
   nine columns into stars (HR, name, J2000 right ascension and declination in degrees, V, B−V or none,
   proper motion in right ascension times cos δ and in declination in arcseconds per Julian year, spectral
   type); any failure is a `StarError`. `compact_catalogue(src, dst)` writes the fixture from the catalogue
   as CDS distributes it (`parse_record` reads the byte columns of its ReadMe: RA at 76–83, Dec at 84–90, V at
   103–107, B−V at 110–114, type at 128–147, proper motion at 149–160), with a gzip member that carries no
   name and a zero time so the bytes depend on the records alone.
2. **Flux.** `flux(m, zero)` is `magnitudeZero` × 10^(−0.4 m): five magnitudes are a factor of exactly one
   hundred and one magnitude is 100^(1/5) = 2.512; `magnitude` inverts it. Sirius (V −1.46) is 1,528 times
   the flux of a star at the default limit 6.5. A star's flux is spread over a disc of `pointSize` pixels;
   the disc's radiance integrates to the flux, so the metered brightness is independent of the size.
3. **Colour.** `temperature(bv)` is Ballesteros (2012), T = 4600 (1 / (0.92 (B−V) + 1.7) + 1 / (0.92 (B−V)
   + 0.62)), with B−V clamped to [−0.4, 2.0] (the catalogue has one value of 5.74); B−V 0.65 gives 5,778 K,
   the Sun. `colour(t)` integrates `planck` (Planck's law in W sr⁻¹ m⁻³) against the CIE 1931 2° colour-matching
   functions at 5 nm from 360 to 830 nm (`CMF`), converts with the IEC 61966-2-1 matrix to linear sRGB and
   scales so that Y = 1; over the clamped range the locus stays inside the sRGB gamut, so nothing is clipped.
   A star without B−V takes the B−V of its spectral class (`spectral_class`, the first of O B A F G K M in
   the type; `CLASS_BV`: O −0.3, B −0.2, A 0.0, F 0.4, G 0.65, K 1.0, M 1.5) and a type naming none takes the
   Sun's 0.65. The dome light's `color`, `intensity` and `exposure` multiply the field as they do the sky.
4. **Directions and proper motion.** `direction(ra, dec)` is the ICRF unit vector (cos δ cos α, cos δ sin α,
   sin δ). With `properMotion="true"`, `moved(star, years)` displaces the J2000 direction along the local east
   and north unit vectors by pmRA × years and pmDE × years (arcseconds to radians) and renormalises, which is
   the great-circle motion to second order in the displacement; the catalogue's largest motion (HR 4550,
   Groombridge 1830, 7.06″ per year) moves 0.196° in a century. The years are the frame's epoch minus
   2000.0: with a `space` element, `years_since_j2000` of its ephemeris time (the time-frames draft, rule 2;
   a Julian year is 31,557,600 s); without one, `epoch` − 2000.0. The scene direction is `scene_direction`, the
   axis mapping of the time-frames draft (scene (x, y, z) = ICRF (x, −z, y), SREP 14 rule 4 through
   `pixelsPerMeter`), followed by the space's frame matrix when a `space` is present and the identity
   otherwise; the field is at infinity, so the origin plays no part.
5. **Constellations.** `constellation(ra, dec, epoch)` precesses the position to equinox 1875 by Herget's
   formula (`precess`, the routine of Roman's program) and returns the first row of Roman's 357-row table
   (`BOUNDARIES`: Delporte's southern boundaries sorted by declination then by the eastern end of each arc)
   whose declination is at or below the position's and whose right-ascension arc holds it; Octans closes the
   table at −90°, so every position has a constellation, and Serpens' two parts share `Ser`. Roman's eight
   printed examples at equinox 1950 reproduce. With `constellations="labels"` the engine draws each
   abbreviation at the centroid of the catalogue stars it holds; with `boundaries` it draws the table's arcs
   (each row is an arc of constant declination from `RA_low` to `RA_up`, and the vertical edges follow from
   consecutive rows) precessed from 1875 to the frame's epoch.
6. **The field.** `star_field(catalogue, limit, years, zero)` is the list, in HR order, of the stars with
   V ≤ `limitMagnitude`, each with its direction (rule 4), flux (rule 2), colour (rule 3) and constellation
   (rule 5); at most `MAX_STARS` (16,384) stars are drawn (SF1 bounds the catalogue). The engine draws the
   field behind every surface and the physical sky of the physical-sky draft in front of it, attenuating each
   star by that draft's transmittance toward it (rule 9 there) when `sky="physical"`.
7. **Frames and seek.** The field at a frame is a function of the catalogue, the attributes and that
   frame's epoch, with no state carried between frames; a frame rendered alone equals the frame reached by
   playing (`seek_test`: 600 frames at one Julian year per second).

## Rationale

- **Why the Bright Star Catalogue.** Its 9,110 entries are the stars to V 6.5, the naked-eye sky, in a file
  of 200 KB compressed; it is the catalogue SREP 12 names, its CDS and HEASARC pages state no terms, and its
  columns carry what the rules need (position, V, B−V, proper motion, spectral type, Bayer and Flamsteed
  names). Hipparcos and Gaia go deeper and are the subject of Open issue 2.
- **Why a compacted CSV and not the 197-byte records.** The distributed file is 1.7 MB of fixed columns that
  every engine would parse with the ReadMe at hand; the compact form has nine named columns, parses with any
  CSV reader, and is rebuilt byte for byte by the reference from the distributed file, so the pin is
  auditable.
- **Why Ballesteros.** The formula is a one-line fit of the blackbody to the B−V index (better than 5 % over
  the main sequence), has a stated domain, and gives the Sun 5,778 K at B−V 0.65; a spectral-type table
  would need the luminosity class the catalogue does not always give.
- **Why the CIE integral and not a colour-temperature table.** The dome light already has `colorTemperature`
  (xsd, `kelvinType`); the engine's table for it and this rule should agree, and the integral is the
  definition both are approximating. The 5 nm table keeps the integral exact to the table.
- **Why Roman's table.** Delporte's boundaries are arcs of constant right ascension and declination at
  equinox 1875; Roman rearranged them into a lookup that is a linear scan of 357 rows and published the
  precession routine to use with it, so the lookup is a fixed, testable procedure rather than a point-in-polygon
  over 88 figures with their own precession conventions.
- **Why the epoch comes from `space` when present.** The time-frames draft owns the scene's clock and
  frames; a second epoch on the star field would let them disagree. The attribute exists for documents
  without a `space`, where the default 2000.0 reproduces the catalogue as published.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A procedural star field** (random stars with a magnitude distribution): it has no Sirius, no Orion and
  nothing to measure; the catalogue is smaller than a texture.
- **A rendered star texture as a dome image.** A texture has a fixed resolution, a fixed epoch and a fixed
  exposure; the point field is resolution independent, moves with proper motion and meters by magnitude.
- **Spectral rendering of each star.** Stars are points; their colour is one triple each, and the Planck
  integral gives it exactly to the CIE table.
- **Constellation lines (the figures).** There is no single authoritative figure list with confirmed terms;
  labels and boundaries are defined by Roman's table. Lines are Open issue 3.

## Backwards compatibility

Every document at 1.7.1 keeps its meaning: a dome light without `starField` draws no stars, and the asset
kind is new. The element is refused below version 1.8 (V17).

## Open issues

1. **The catalogue's terms.** SREP 12 line 438 lists the Bright Star Catalogue among the sources whose licence
   is still to be confirmed before the SREP enters review: the HEASARC and CDS pages state no terms and the
   ReadMe calls the file a preliminary version prepared for the ADC CD-ROM. The schema editor confirms before
   acceptance; until then the fixture is test input.
2. **Fainter stars.** Hipparcos (ESA) and Gaia (ESA, CC-BY 4.0 for DR3) go to V 12 and beyond; SREP 12 line
   427 admits them only under public domain, CC0 or CC-BY terms. A second `catalogue` asset in the same
   nine columns needs no rule change; the attribution a CC-BY catalogue requires has no place in the schema
   yet.
3. **Constellation lines.** The stick figures are conventions (H. A. Rey's, the IAU/Sky & Telescope set);
   none has terms confirmed. `constellations="lines"` waits for a figure list the editor accepts.
4. **Stars through the atmosphere.** Rule 6 attenuates stars by the physical sky's transmittance but adds no
   seeing, scintillation or airglow; the point-spread function is `pointSize`, not the camera's optics.
5. **Epochs far from J2000.** Rule 4 is linear proper motion within 10,000 years; radial velocity and
   perspective acceleration are ignored, and precession of the equinox is not applied to the field itself
   (the field is in the ICRF; only the constellation lookup precesses).
