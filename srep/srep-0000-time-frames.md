# SREP 0 — Add time scales, celestial frames and JPL ephemerides

## Abstract

A `space` element that gives a scene a clock in UTC, TT or TDB and a celestial frame (the ICRF, the
ecliptic of J2000 or a body-fixed frame of the IAU rotation elements) about an origin body, an `ephemeris`
asset that is a JPL SPK file read by our own code and pinned by its sha256 digest, and `object3D`
attributes that place an object on a solar-system body at the frame's time. Positions from the ephemeris
are mapped into scene pixels through `physics/@pixelsPerMeter`, as SREP 14 rule 4 requires. The approximate
Keplerian elements of Standish and Williams are the fallback when no file is given. Every placement is a
pure function of the frame number, the document and the file, so the seek check (SREP 14) holds by
construction. The reference module `conformance/tools/time_frames_reference.py` states each rule as code,
reads the kit's excerpt `conformance/assets/de440s-excerpt.bsp` and prints the numeric table of the
Conformance section.

## Motivation

- **The catalogue has the shot.** SREP 12 line 281 lists SP1 as "UTC and TDB; ICRF and body-fixed frames
  as inputs mapped into scene pixels (SREP 14, rule 4); JPL DE440 ephemerides (public domain) read by our
  own code and pinned by digest; JPL's approximate Keplerian elements (Standish, public domain) as
  fallback", with the reference test "JPL Horizons positions within 1 km"; milestone 5 (line 359) hangs
  SP2 to SP6 and A1 on it, and line 437 asks that the licence terms of DE440 and the Standish elements be
  confirmed before review (Rationale).
- **A document has no clock in a time scale today.** Frames count from zero at a rate the document does not
  name in seconds; the physical sky draft takes a `date` per dome light. A scene of the solar system needs
  one epoch, one rate, and bodies whose positions follow from them.
- **The constructions have published checks.** JPL Horizons prints the ICRF state of every body for any
  epoch; DE440 reproduces it to metres. The leap-second table and the TDB formula are NAIF's own file. The
  IAU elements give the Earth's prime meridian within a tenth of a degree of the sidereal time the physical
  sky draft computes independently. These are the numeric tests below.

## Specification

### Syntax

A diff for schema 1.8 against `schema/scene-render.xsd` at 1.7.1 (line numbers of that file); the schema
files are not changed by this draft.

- `ephemerisType`, a new child of `assetsType` (2387), zero or more: `id` (`xs:ID`), `src` (`xs:anyURI`, an
  SPK file in the DAF format with type 2 segments, TF1), `digest` (`xs:string`, required: `sha256:` and the
  64 hex digits of the file's sha256; TF1).
- `spaceType`, a new optional child of `sceneType` after `physics` (7497), at most one:
  - `epoch` (`xs:string`, required; with `timeScale="utc"` an ISO 8601 date and time with seconds and `Z` or
    a `+hh:mm` offset of at most 23:59, from 1972-01-01 UTC on; with `tt` or `tdb` a Julian day as a decimal
    number, a sign, digits and at most one point, no exponent, `nan` or `inf`; TF2),
    `timeScale` (one of `utc`, `tt`, `tdb`; default `utc`), `rate` (`nonNegativeDecimal`, seconds of
    ephemeris time per second of scene time, default 1; 0 freezes the clock).
  - `origin` (`xs:int`, a NAIF body id, default 0 the solar system barycentre), `frame` (one of `icrf`,
    `eclipticJ2000`, `bodyFixed`; default `icrf`), `frameBody` (`xs:int`, required with `bodyFixed`, TF3).
  - `ephemeris` (`xs:string`: the `id` of an `ephemeris` asset, or the keyword `approximate` for rule 5's
    elements; default `approximate`; TF5).
- On `object3DType` (4112): `body` (`xs:int`, a NAIF id), `positionFrom` (one of `document`, `ephemeris`;
  default `document`), `orientation` (one of `document`, `bodyFixed`; default `document`). TF4.
- Schematron:
  - **TF1** an `ephemeris` asset's file is a DAF/SPK file whose segments are all type 2 in frame 1 and whose
    sha256 digest equals `digest`; a mismatch is an error, not a warning.
  - **TF2** `epoch` parses as rule 1 says: a UTC epoch's fields are in range, its offset is at most 23:59 and
    its instant, after the offset, is not before 1972-01-01 UTC; a TT or TDB epoch is a decimal number; with
    `approximate` the epoch's Julian day is 625332.5 or more and under 2817152.5 (the years −3000 to 3000 of
    rule 5's tables).
  - **TF3** `frame="bodyFixed"` needs `frameBody` with rotation elements (rule 6 lists the bodies).
  - **TF4** `positionFrom="ephemeris"` or `orientation="bodyFixed"` on an `object3D` needs `body` and a
    `space`; the body is in the ephemeris file (or in the approximate tables: 1 to 8), and `orientation`
    needs rotation elements for it.
  - **TF5** `rate` is finite and non-negative; `ephemeris` is `approximate` or the `id` of an `ephemeris`
    asset; `origin` and `frameBody`, when the ephemeris is a file, are in that file or are the barycentre 0;
    with `approximate`, `origin` is 0, 10 or a body 1 to 8 (the elements are heliocentric: the barycentre 0
    is read as the Sun 10).
  - **V17** `space`, `ephemeris` and the three `object3D` attributes need `scene/@version="1.8"` or later.

### Semantics

The reference module names the function of each rule. Positions are kilometres in the ICRF axes until
rule 6 maps them to pixels; ephemeris time is seconds of TDB past J2000 (2000-01-01T12:00:00 TDB).

1. **Calendar and time scales.** `julian_day` and `calendar` are the proleptic Gregorian calendar (Meeus,
   chapter 7). `parse_utc` reads the epoch text, bounds its offset to 23:59 and applies it; `parse_julian_day`
   reads a TT or TDB epoch as a decimal number (a sign, digits and at most one point: no exponent, `nan` or
   `inf`, the lexical of `xs:decimal`). `leap_seconds` is the DELTA_AT table of NAIF's `naif0012.tls`: 10 s on
   1972-01-01, 37 s from 2017-01-01, 28 entries; a UTC instant that, after the offset, is before 1972-01-01
   UTC is an error. TAI = UTC + leap seconds; TT = TAI + 32.184 s; TDB = TT + K sin(E) with E = M + EB sin M,
   M = 6.239996 + 1.99096871 × 10⁻⁷ t, t seconds of TT past J2000, K = 1.657 × 10⁻³ s and EB = 1.671 × 10⁻²
   (the same file). `et_from_utc` carries seconds, not Julian days, so an epoch is exact to a microsecond.
2. **The frame clock.** `space()` turns the epoch into ephemeris time; `frame_time(space, frame, fps)` is
   epoch + frame / fps × `rate`. With `rate="0"` every frame is the epoch.
3. **Reading the ephemeris.** `read_daf` parses the file record (`DAF/SPK`, ND 2, NI 6, LTL-IEEE or
   BIG-IEEE), follows the chain of summary records, and keeps each segment's target, centre, frame, type and
   addresses; a foreign identifier, a truncated file, a segment of another type or frame, or addresses
   outside the file are errors. A type 2 segment holds Chebyshev coefficients of position per record. `open_ephemeris(path, digest)` adds the digest check of TF1. A type 2
   segment's trailer gives INIT, INTLEN, RSIZE and N; the record covering t is index ⌊(t − INIT) / INTLEN⌋,
   its MID and RADIUS give τ = (t − MID) / RADIUS, and each coordinate is Σ cₖ Tₖ(τ) by Clenshaw's recurrence
   (`chebyshev`); the velocity is the derivative over RADIUS (`chebyshev_derivative`).
4. **Positions.** `position(spk, target, center, t)` follows each body's centre to the barycentre and
   subtracts (`state` also returns the velocity); a time outside every segment of the body, or a body
   absent from the file, is an error. `apparent_position(spk, target, observer, t)` iterates the one-way
   light time at most `MAX_LIGHT_TIME_ITERATIONS` times. Against the Horizons vectors of
   `conformance/assets/horizons-vectors.json` (14 bodies, 4 epochs from 2000 to 2040) the excerpt's worst
   distance is 3.2 m; the light time from the Earth to the Sun at J2000 is 490.685 s.
5. **The approximate elements.** `approximate_position(body, t, table=None)` is Standish and Williams's
   algorithm with their Table 1 (1800 to 2050) or Tables 2a and 2b (3000 BC to 3000 AD); `element_table(t)`
   chooses Table 1 when the year of t is 1800 to 2050 and the long tables otherwise, to the years −3000 to
   3000 (Julian days 625332.5 to 2817152.5): elements at the
   epoch's century, ω = ϖ − Ω, M = L − ϖ (+ bT² + c cos fT + s sin fT for Jupiter to Neptune in the long
   table), Kepler's equation by Newton's iterations from E₀ = M + e* sin M bounded by
   `MAX_KEPLER_ITERATIONS`, the orbital plane, and the obliquity 23.43928° to the ICRF. Years outside the
   tables and bodies other than 1 to 8 are errors. `approximate_relative(body, origin, t)` is the position
   about `origin`: the elements are heliocentric, so the Sun 10 and the barycentre 0 are their origin
   (0, 0, 0), and a body 1 to 8 subtracts its own position (TF5). Against the excerpt the heliocentric direction of the
   Earth-Moon barycentre at J2000 is off by 2.8″, Mars in 2025 by 12.9″ and Jupiter from the long table by
   486″, inside the page's stated errors.
6. **Frames and pixels.** `rotation_elements(body, t)` are the IAU 2009 values of `pck00010.tpc` (Sun,
   Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Moon; the Moon with its 13-term
   nutation-precession series, Mercury's and Jupiter's small series omitted); `body_fixed_from_icrf` is
   Rz(W) Rx(90° − δ₀) Rz(90° + α₀); `ecliptic_from_icrf` is Rx(23.43928°). The Earth's expression is the
   report's approximation: its sidereal day is 86164.0962 s and its prime meridian is 0.087° from the
   apparent sidereal time of the physical sky draft at the SPA example. `scene_from_icrf(space, p, origin,
   pixelsPerMeter)` takes p − origin through the space's frame matrix and maps ICRF (x, y, z) km to scene
   (x, −z, y) × 1000 × `pixelsPerMeter`: scene x is the ICRF x, up (−y) is the frame's pole, scene z the
   frame's y, a proper rotation (determinant 1).
7. **Bodies in the scene.** `place_body` evaluates rule 2's time, rule 4's position about `origin` (rule 5's
   `approximate_relative` with `ephemeris="approximate"`) and rule 6's mapping; with `orientation="bodyFixed"` the object's axes are the body's fixed axes in the
   space's frame. With `positionFrom="ephemeris"` the object's own `x`, `y`, `z` are ignored with a warning.
   The physical sky draft's `date` stays a separate input (Open issue 1). A frame rendered alone equals the
   frame reached by playing (`seek_test`).

## Rationale

- **Our own reader.** An SPK file is a documented binary format (NAIF's DAF and SPK required reading); a
  reader for type 2 segments is two hundred lines and removes a dependency on SPICE or jplephem. The excerpt
  writer exists so the kit's fixture is small (28 KB) and so the reader is tested on both byte orders and on
  summary chains longer than one record.
- **Pinned by digest.** The document names the file and its digest; an engine with another file, or a
  corrupted one, refuses the document rather than drawing the wrong sky. The reference file is JPL's
  `de440s.bsp` (1849 to 2150), sha256
  `c1c7feeab882263fc493a9d5a5b2ddd71b54826cdf65d8d17a76126b260a49f2`.
- **Licences.** NAIF's generic kernels (`de440s.bsp`, `naif0012.tls`, `pck00010.tpc`) and the Horizons
  output are works of the US government (JPL/Caltech under NASA contract) and are in the public domain;
  JPL's "Keplerian elements for approximate positions of the major planets" page publishes Standish and
  Williams's tables with the authors' permission. This answers SREP 12 line 437 for this draft; the
  reviewer confirms it.
- **Seconds, not days.** A Julian day in a double resolves 40 µs near today; the clock keeps seconds past
  J2000 so a frame's epoch is exact to a microsecond, which 60 frames per second at `rate="3600"` needs.
- **The IAU Earth.** The report's Earth expression is an approximation (it says so); ITRS needs
  precession-nutation, polar motion and UT1 − UTC, which the document does not carry. The tenth of a degree
  is a kilometre on the ground and under a pixel in every shot of the catalogue; Open issue 2.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A `date` on every element** (as the physical sky draft does for one light): a scene has one clock; the
  sky draft's `date` is kept for compatibility and tied to the clock by Open issue 1.
- **Reading the ASCII ephemeris** (JPL's `ascp` files): the SPK form is the one NAIF maintains and the one
  with a digest in the Horizons documentation; the ASCII form needs a converter.
- **VSOP87 or Meeus's series instead of the file**: the series are kilometres to thousands of kilometres
  off and have no Moon to the kilometre; the file is 32 MB and read by our own code.
- **Astropy or SPICE as a dependency**: both are permissively licensed, but a dependency the engine cannot
  carry (Rust, offline) makes the kit's numbers unreproducible there.

## Backwards compatibility

Every document at 1.7.1 keeps its meaning: `space` is absent, no asset is an ephemeris, and the three
`object3D` attributes default to `document`. The constructs are refused below version 1.8 (V17).

## Open issues

1. **One clock for the sky.** The physical sky draft's `date` should accept the keyword `space` so the
   dome light's sun follows the frame clock; that is a one-line amendment to the sky draft once both have
   numbers.
2. **The Earth's true orientation.** The IAU expression is a tenth of a degree from GAST and ignores
   precession-nutation and polar motion; an `earthOrientation="iau2006"` option with the IERS series is
   possible when a shot needs ground truth under a metre.
3. **Satellites and small bodies.** SPK files for satellites (`jup365.bsp`, `sat441.bsp`) and asteroids are
   the same format; TF4's "in the file" already admits them, but their rotation elements are not in rule 6.
4. **Leap seconds after 2017.** The table ends at the last announced leap second; a document dated after a
   future one would be a second off until the table is extended with the schema.
5. **Type 3 and type 13 segments.** Some SPK files (spacecraft) use other segment types; the reader refuses
   them by TF1 and a later draft can add them.

  seven kit cases described; the engine work filed as SR-87.
- 2026-10-25: four points the engine settled vendoring the draft (SR-87, scene-render 45ff742), adopted: with

  that names no asset (kit case `space-no-asset`); TF2 bounds a UTC offset to 23:59, checks 1972-01-01 after

  bound is the years −3000 to 3000 (`element_table`, `approximate_relative`, five table rows).
